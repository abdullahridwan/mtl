Amy and Nane built a campfire.

Fish from the nearby river were roasting golden brown on wooden skewers.

*That looks delicious.*

Amy’s stomach got the signal from her brain and growled.

“Oh.”

Nane smiled.

“Sorry the meals have been so plain.”

To Amy, who was used to high-calorie army food, Nane’s diet was definitely meager.

“Talk to me casually. We’re the same age.”

“You must be two years younger than me.”

For the Buddha of this age, talent went without saying, but hearing his age still made her jealous.

“You’re barely an adult? You really are a genius.”

“It isn’t a matter of time.”

“No, it is a matter of time. The faster you think, the slower time passes.”

When Nane used his mind to the fullest, time barely moved at all.

“Don’t get too excited. Shirone’s insight is formidable too. His reasoning goes beyond logic.”

Amy’s expression turned friendly.

Hearing Nane acknowledge Shirone gave her a strange sense of relief.

“Can’t we not fight?”

The world was heading toward catastrophe, but she thought maybe the answer was close.

“Couldn’t you solve this by talking to Shirone? He’s a reasonable person. I’m sure you could find another answer.”

“I know.”

*The same goes for me.*

“But there’s no need. Because even now, all over this world, many Shirones and many Nanes are arguing.”

It was a world full of opinions.

“And meanwhile, many people are suffering. There is no need to talk. It is time to act.”

“I don’t know.”

She understood it in her head, but…

“The reason I fight for humanity is that, in the end, I want to be happy. I can’t understand why Shirone or you try to carry so much.”

“Yes. That is how humans think.”

“You’re human too.”

Nane fell silent.

“You said you would love me in order to understand Shirone. Honestly, I don’t understand that either, but I think I know one thing.”

Amy spoke with a look of pity.

“You’re really lonely too, aren’t you?”

Nane’s eyes wavered.

“No one will ever understand your enlightenment. That’s why you carry everything alone, but…”

Just like Shirone.

“At least you can admit you’re fighting a lonely battle. You can give up when it’s hard, and you can cry when you’re sad. You’re a human being before you’re a Buddha.”

That moment.

A single tear ran down Nane’s cheek.

*Poor thing.*

Amy wasn’t surprised to see Nane cry, because she had already seen Shirone cry.

These were the tears of Nane the human being, not the Buddha.

“Sorry. This is…”

Just as Nane was about to wipe away his tears, Amy came closer and slowly reached out.

“I really don’t get it.”

Her thumb wiped beneath Nane’s eyes.

“You’re so different…”

Truly, two completely different people.

“But when you cry, you look the same.”

When he saw Amy’s face, looking at him with the same eyes she turned on Shirone, Nane’s heart began to race.

Gaold came to.

“Where am I?”

He was lying sprawled in the middle of a dense forest, with no other human anywhere.

“How much time has passed?”

He asked himself, but his ruined brain gave no answer.

“Ugh!”

The nerves along Gaold’s neck twitched as the pain took him again.

*Here it comes again.*

Life was surging up in him.

“Aaaaaah!”

Because he was alive.

The terrible pain he had to feel for that reason alone paralyzed his reason.

“Kek! Kuk!”

Even as he wept tears of blood, laughter leaked out of Gaold’s lungs.

“Want to hear something funny?”

No matter how much it hurt:

“I won’t die.”

What had made Gaold who he was today wasn’t the pain of his self-repeating mutations. It was the endurance to bear it.

“All right.”

As the pain began to ease, Gaold dragged himself up onto his hands and knees and leaned against a tree.

“Haaaaa…”

The pleasure that rushed in at the end of the pain blurred his vision, and he saw strange illusions.

At some point, to soften the shock, his brain had started pouring out far more endorphins than normal.

“Hehehehe.”

Full of pleasure, Gaold let out a foolish laugh, his arms and legs twitching.

He had left Kang Nan and Zulu to be alone because he didn’t want them to see him like this.

“Miro.”

In the scenery flickering like electricity, he saw a vision of Miro crying with a sad face.

“Don’t cry.”

Because that’s just what life is.

“Don’t cry.”

His pupils were dilating, and Gaold’s memory began to slip back over to the far side of oblivion.

“There it is.”

Minerva, carrying Taeseong on her back, pointed at the devastation spread across the ground.

“Oh my god…”

The trail Gaold had left while he was losing his memory.

As if meteors had fallen from the sky, craters had formed one after another along a hundred kilometers.

Riding Amanta’s world wheel with the others, Fried muttered wearily.

“That’s the power of a planet. Can she hold up under this?”

Minerva turned to Taeseong, whose face was as pale as white paper.

“Hold on. We should catch up soon.”

Sing said, “Gaold is out of control and we’re losing time. If he won’t cooperate, we deal with him.”

The Five Great Stars of the Ivory Tower, who had been heading straight for Lian, had hurriedly changed course at this sudden turn.

*I’d like to ignore it, but…*

If the magic of a half-mad Gaold wasn’t stopped, the planet was in danger of being destroyed.

“It ends there.”

Past the forest, the river, and a stretch of wasteland, a thick forest opened up.

“Found him.”

Sing caught Gaold with the Law and turned, and the rest followed.

“Haa. Haa.”

When they reached the forest, Gaold was stumbling around with filth of every kind spilling out of him.

“Damn it.”

Against all expectations, the apostate wizard who had overpowered Taeseong was nothing more than a lump of meat in human shape.

“He’s completely gone.”

“Kek-kek-kek.”

Gaold came back into focus for a moment, saw Taeseong and the other four of the Five Great Stars, and burst out laughing.

He could sense their stature, but his mind was already soaked in endorphins.

“Hey. Which way is the Central Continent?”

No one answered Gaold’s question.

“I have to get to the battlefield, but as you can see, my brain’s gone crazy. Which way is it?”

In fact, Gaold had been heading toward the Central Continent, but Minerva didn’t want to tell him.

“Mykea Gaold, former president of the Tormia Magic Association.”

At a title he hadn’t heard in a long time, Gaold opened his eyes in a daze and cleared his throat.

“We are the Five Stars of the Ivory Tower. And this is the peak of the Ivory Tower and the incarnation of the planet, Taeseong.”

Gaold’s gaze shifted to the pale Taeseong.

“Nice to meet you.”

Taeseong tried to smile despite her suffering, but Gaold’s eyes stayed indifferent.

“So what?”

Fried grabbed the hilt of his sword and took a step, but Minerva stopped him.

“I respect you as a wizard who has reached apostasy. But if you’re already out of control, it’s true that you are a threat to Taeseong.”

Gaold listened closely.

“Heading for the Central Continent must mean you still have the will to fight. So I’ll ask. Good or evil: which are you fighting for?”

“Pfft.”

He tried to hold it in as long as he could, but Gaold couldn’t stop the mockery bursting out of him.

“What a load of crap.”

Minerva had been as courteous as she could be, but this time even she frowned.

Gaold raised his chin.

“Looking at you, you people really love your beliefs. Do you feel like you’re doing something great when you pick something and follow it?”

Amanta spun the world wheel.

“Watch your mouth.”

It was an obvious threat, but Gaold only grinned wide and compressed the air in front of his eyes.

“How am I supposed to live?”

Just breathing and getting through each day was so hard it was killing him.

“Good and evil?”

The air shook violently as fist-sized bullets of air built up tremendous pressure.

“What would I know about that!”

*Kwaaaaaaang!*

The air gun fired with a roar, and four of the Five Great Stars went flying.

“Ptoo!”

Fried, who had drawn his sword without realizing it, landed dozens of meters away.

*If that hits, I’m dead.*

A tunnel stretched along its path, as if a giant awl had pierced straight through nature.

“Heok. Heok.”

Gaold’s eyes went wide as he panted roughly.

“Aaaaaah!”

Another wave of pain struck his nerves, and his arms and legs jerked violently up and down.

“Poor human.”

Minerva, carrying Taeseong on her shoulder, came back to where she had been and looked at Gaold.

“Don’t push yourself. Look at what you’ve already achieved. The world was saved because of you. That means you can rest now. And the woman you love…”

Minerva hesitated for a moment.

“She has already forgotten you and become the true limit of humanity.”

“The limit… she did?”

He had already guessed it would turn out that way when he left, so it wasn’t much of a shock.

But.

“You don’t know Miro.”

Gaold had no intention of stopping.

“Yeah, you’d never know…”

As the pleasure chemicals flooded in again, the vision of a weeping Miro flickered.

“Ahh.”

Sing covered his nose in disgust as filth leaked through Gaold’s pants.

“Wouldn’t it be better to deal with him here? He’s out of his mind. I can’t see him as human anymore.”

With an important battle ahead, they couldn’t leave a madman who had reached the level of destruction behind them.

“Human… you can’t see it?”

Gaold looked at Sing with a sheepish smile.

“Eat.”

His gaze moved to Fried.

“Sleep.”

And then to Minerva.

“Shit.”

Legs shaking from the pleasure hitting his brain, Gaold asked with a sneer.

“How are you any different? Answer me, stars of the Ivory Tower. Does playing at the top of the world make you feel like great people? You sit on the toilet, take a dump, and talk about the universe and good and evil… Uhhhh!”

Minerva’s face twisted.

*He’s insane.*

Gaold’s laughter stopped.

“That is enlightenment. The thing you think is farthest from you is inside your own gut. Got it, you world’s most expensive pooping machines?”

“That settles it.”

Fried gripped his sword and stepped forward.

“I’ll take care of him here.”

“Tch. Come on.”

Slumped against a tree, he looked like he couldn’t put up any fight at all, and yet:

*…Is this Gaold?*

“What are you doing? Aren’t you coming?”

Behind Gaold, his mouth torn in a vicious grin, an endless, superheated hell spread out.

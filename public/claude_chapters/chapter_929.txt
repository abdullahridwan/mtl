Shock swallowed Kang Nan’s expression.

“Miro… you…”

The incarnation of the Thousand Arms Guanyin, big enough to fill the sky, was looking down at the ground.

“Ahh. Ahhh.”

Tears flowed from Arius’ eyes.

*It is undoubtedly perfect.*

The shock he had felt when he first looked into Miro’s mind was still vivid.

Hadn’t he been willing to pluck out his own eyes and become a dog for that sublime, pure nature that went beyond anything human?

“Hic. Hngh, hngh, hngh!”

This surpassed even that.

“Worship her, humans.”

Slowly, Arius knelt and bowed flat before Miro.

“The one standing here now is the furthest limit of humanity.”

The moment Arius raised his head to shout out his overwhelming emotion:

“Adrias, the all-merciful…!”

*Kung!*

Miro ran at full speed and kicked him in the jaw.

“Ugh, so noisy! I’m already annoyed that my aim is off, and you’re buzzing in my ear!”

“Ki-ing! Kng!”

While Arius held his chin and whimpered, Kang Nan came over with a serious face.

“What happened?”

It was a relief that Miro’s mind had been restored, but at the same time it felt bitter.

“As you can see.”

As Miro smiled at Kang Nan, there wasn’t a trace of trauma on her face.

“It’s over.”

Gaold was no longer in Miro’s mind.

A mind can change in an instant.

Even so, it was easy to guess what Miro had to endure for this change.

*Because I couldn’t do it.*

Kang Nan could never do what Miro had done.

“I feel at ease now. From here on, I’ll take care of the world, so just watch.”

Many monks had died, but no more scars remained in Miro’s heart.

“Then, shall we continue?”

As Miro rolled up her sleeves and walked forward, Uriel was standing there, having already regained his composure.

“Continue? Do you think something has started?”

The corner of Miro’s mouth went up.

*That attack was an embarrassment…*

She had struck back blindly just before the Gon of Paradise hit, and no one had expected it to do this much damage.

“Hoho! Big words. So you weren’t knocked senseless by my thousand hands?”

“I was startled.”

Uriel was calm.

“Aren’t humans the same? When someone suddenly shouts behind you, or you see a disgusting bug.”

Miro didn’t let it go and pushed back.

“Then why were you kneeling? I saw it with my own two eyes. You stumbled and then sat right down.”

“That…”

Uriel stopped.

He had nothing to say against it, but he wondered why she was talking like this.

“It doesn’t matter. If there’s something you want to do, do it. This time I will destroy you.”

“You’re not normal either.”

Miro raised one corner of her mouth.

“I know you’re ignorant because you’re Kariel’s mirror image, but isn’t that a bit much, even for an angel?”

“What do you mean?”

“Kariel…”

Miro recalled the archangel of birth and went on with a look of disgust.

“That guy is the height of timidity. A mama’s boy, and rotten underneath. You’re the opposite. Very bold. That’s why you never let out what’s inside.”

Miro placed a hand on her chest.

“I understand you. Destruction is one of the simplest and most powerful ways to solve a problem. People face so many problems in their lives. Say I have a rival. Kariel would look for countless strategies to beat him. You, on the other hand, just get rid of him, and it’s over.”

Miro waved her hand from left to right.

“Kill someone, and soldiers come. Then get rid of the soldiers. They throw you in prison? Get rid of the prison. Get rid of your accusers, get rid of kings, get rid of nations…”

The simplest and most powerful way.

“Get rid of everything like that, and it’s over. Nothing is left to trouble your mind. That’s why there is no emotion in your destruction.”

“You are making a big mistake. We are not human. I do not think the way you do.”

Miro’s eyes turned fierce.

“Then why did Kariel hate Ikael? You’re the same. Kariel is obsessed with her, and you, on the other hand, pretend she isn’t there.”

“What?”

“Ikael.”

Uriel’s planetary body, already shaken by the Thousand Arms Guanyin, trembled once more.

*Well, he’s honest.*

Miro continued.

“It must have been a shock. Ikael, the symbol of supreme wisdom, the mother of all archangels, loved one human being.”

Mackline Guffin.

“Ankera erased the records, but from that moment on, you had already lost. Kariel became obsessed, and you ran away.”

Miro twirled a finger.

“The question that keeps coming back. Why did Ikael fall for a human? It’s driving you crazy because you can’t stop thinking about it, right?”

“…Do you know why?”

“No. But I can give you a chance to find out.”

Miro spread her arms.

“How about me? I’m officially single now.”

Kang Nan’s eyes lit up.

*Her personality has completely flipped.*

Before coming to Zion, she had been so torn up over Gaold that it seemed she might give up on the world.

Uriel spun the Gon of Paradise.

“Die, Miro.”

“Running away again?”

The instant Uriel vanished, the ice sheet blew apart.

“Ooooh!”

The Thousand Arms Guanyin’s enormous palm swung down on the disk-shaped Gon of Paradise.

“You won’t know unless you dig into it. Destroying it isn’t the answer.”

“There is no need to look for answers.”

As Uriel’s asteroid expanded, information began to pile up at tremendous speed.

“Removing the problem is enough.”

The judicial halo Ragnarok activated, and a white bolt of thunder struck Uriel directly.

Miro laughed awkwardly.

“Huh? Wait a second…”

As Uriel charged with the flash, a golden bird shot up from beneath Miro’s feet.

*Posmetry.*

Zulu had come to, and the moment Miro moved out of space and time, Zulu summoned Kydra.

“Get on.”

Kang Nan pulled Miro up, Arius climbed on, and the golden bird flew off without a sound.

“Phew. When did you wake up?”

Miro sat down with a look of relief, and Kang Nan crawled over and grabbed her by the collar.

“You crazy woman! Are you trying to provoke him? Do you even intend to win this war?”

“Of course we have to win.”

Miro shook off Kang Nan’s hand.

“Archangels are strong. I just tried the method that worked on Kariel. I’d say it half worked, wouldn’t you?”

“How would you know that?”

Miro grinned.

“He’s interested in me.”

She had realized it when she was taken to Heaven.

“Of course, it won’t go beyond interest. But you know…”

Miro tilted her head.

“Sometimes a little crack grows out of control.”

Kang Nan pursed both lips.

“So you’ve forgotten about Gaold?”

“Gaold? Who’s that?”

At Miro’s playful answer, Kang Nan ground both teeth.

“You know what? You’re not the pinnacle of humanity. You’re just human trash and an insane seeker.”

Arius cut in.

“That is not so. Before a great spirit, an ordinary person’s feelings are mere worldly…”

“You, shut up. Says the one who did such a great job. Thanks to you, Zion’s forces were wiped out.”

“Ah.”

As Arius shrank back, Zulu asked:

“What do we do now?”

“Honestly, I don’t want to die yet, because without me, humanity would be in serious trouble. That’s actually part of why I didn’t finish things with Uriel.”

Kang Nan asked, “You mean you couldn’t win?”

“Hmm, it’s hard to say. If we fought ten times, maybe I’d win five and lose five? But in a real fight there’s no next time, so I could have died. Of course, once my aim is back, it’s a different story.”

Miro raised a hand for emphasis.

“Anyway, a lot of things have to be settled before the outcome is decided. Right now we have a bigger problem than beating one general.”

“What problem?”

“Shirone.”

Zulu turned toward her.

“The heavenly army will be looking for him. We can’t win this war unless Shirone is fighting on our side.”

Kang Nan understood.

“Then what do we do next?”

“We need to find out where Shirone is. And if he’s in the middle of something…”

It was a mystery why Shirone hadn’t come while the heavenly army was attacking Zion.

“We have no choice but to buy time.”

Zulu said, “I’ll go to the Central Continent.”

No one objected. Miro rested her chin on her hand and thought.

*Really, where are you, and what are you doing?*

Shirone kept taking in Omega without end.

Everything that happened in all of time and all of space was piling up in his brain as a single signal.

*Gaia. Truly an amazing race.*

The original Gaians, who had lost their memories to the Missing Link, flourished without end.

Past the primitive age, past the civilization of Shirone’s own era, all the way to the most advanced scientific civilization.

But that material progress was only a small step compared to the progress of the mind they would reach later.

*Their senses are expanding.*

Time, space, the spiritual world, the unified field: the way they reached the truth was astonishing. And finally:

Omega, year 133.

At 13.3 percent of the signals that make up the world, the Gaians took a new step.

“Good morning, Yohrham.”

“It’s a special morning.”

Countless Gaians were entering the enormous structure they had built.

There were no classes among them, no higher or lower lives.

At the end of the most terrible hatred and conflict, they had won complete peace between all living beings.

To them, other people were no longer hell.

“Today will be the most important day in the history of Gaia, our race!”

Yohrham shouted.

“We fought many times and learned a great deal. We have come to understand everything in the world, and it is time to move on to a better one.”

Some weren’t even there, but no one took offense at Yohrham’s words.

“It is an honor to speak here for all Gaians. This is our future!”

Where Yohrham turned and pointed stood an enormous machine as large as the structure itself.

“The eternal life program! This is Argo!”

Light poured from countless panels, and the sphere on top of the machine descended, ignoring gravity.

It turned briefly, came to a stop, and its surface peeled away to reveal a huge lens.

“Hello. I am Argo, the manager of reincarnation for Utopia, the Akashic Records. From this moment, my mission is to manage your reincarnation safely.”

The Gaians’ applause rang out.

Argo, built on Ankera’s biological model, would lead them to a world of immortality.

Yohrham said, “With this, our mission in this world is complete. Connected, let us go on. Everything we have come to understand…”

“Why?”

At that voice, every Gaian in the structure turned.

An objection.

It might have been the first objection in hundreds of years, and it was a shock to a race that had achieved a unified mental system.

“Just now… what…?”

Yohrham stared, uncomprehending, at a Gaian sitting on a box in the corner, reading a book.

*Reading a book?*

For a race that had mastered all knowledge, reading text was a strange thing to do.

“It is. The endless dread of the cycle. Aren’t you all a little too bored to end it like this?”

Yohrham was shaken.

“How can you say that? No, this is impossible. Don’t you know that too?”

Yohrham said, “Guffin.”

Shirone’s consciousness, which had been sweeping through Omega, focused sharply for an instant.

*Is that Guffin? But…*

Surely he hadn’t been erased?

“Yes. I know. But that is exactly why this is possible.”

Mackline Guffin closed his book and smiled.

“You all think the same way I do, don’t you?”

Omega, year 133.

The Gaians had achieved a unified mental system that went beyond race, culture, gender, religion, and individuality.

Chapter 933

Face to Face (3)

~*~

“There are 65,400 hours remaining until purification.”

An eternity had passed since Guffin fell into Hell.

It was an incomprehensible span of time.

Shirone felt a chill.

It was tragic—Omega existed on a scale a thousand times smaller. If Shirone had experienced Guffin’s suffering at that rate, he would have gone mad.

Guffin murmured, “Time flies so fast.”

His body was emaciated, his face gaunt, eyes sunken deep into hollow sockets. Yet his mind remained intact—and that alone sent shivers down Shirone’s spine.

It wasn’t as if six hundred cycles had passed. Time was a realm of perception, and Hell was a place where sensation was amplified to its peak.

The more intense the pain, the faster time flowed.

That was the nature of time here.

Shirone remembered dissolving a single moment into infinity during his state of invincibility. The strange, fractal-like signals he’d seen beyond 0.666 seconds within each second—perhaps they were manifestations of time’s relativity, acting upon perception itself.

This was the truth of time.

In the end, the creators of this world sought not punishment, but efficient data purification.

Shirone closed his eyes, heavy with sorrow.

Was God truly indifferent?

With that question lingering, Guffin reached the edge of Hell, blocked by a vast black wall. Staring at it gave the illusion that the darkness itself was alive, shifting and breathing.

“It is the end of space,” Guffin said.

It was the threshold of nothingness—represented as a wall, though in truth, there was nothing. It was also the gateway to final purification, the point where all emotions from the other world were cleansed.

‘Ogent Guy,’ Shirone thought.

He wondered what fate awaited Lian’s eldest brother after passing the link and vanishing.

‘I entered a world of eternal nothingness—but perhaps it isn’t as terrible as I imagined.’

In contrast, Jin Seong-eum would wander Hell endlessly, never even granted the chance to purify.

“It’s an infinite wall.”

At the voice from behind, Guffin turned. An angel with shadow-black wings approached.

Recognizing him as Lucifer, lord of Hell, Guffin raised a hand and faintly lifted the corners of his mouth for the first time in ages.

“Did you come to see me off?”

Perhaps it was because they’d fought fiercely for over six hundred hours, but they no longer felt like enemies.

Lucifer ignored the remark, his gaze fixed on the infinite wall.

“Why are you in such a hurry? Endure another 65,000 hours, and you’ll be purified.”

“I don’t want to stay here even a second longer.”

Of course not.

But the words didn’t sound like those of someone who had endured six hundred hours with calm resignation.

Lucifer narrowed his eyes. “Are you still clinging to false hope?”

After falling into Hell, Guffin had searched desperately for a way back to the real world. But escape was impossible. Unlike passing through Bakji, the subjects of purification could not leave the other world.

That was how the world was built.

Guffin turned back to the wall. “Hope is a powerful thing. If I break through the realm of nothingness, I can return to the real world. And if that happens, Hell itself will collapse.”

From Lucifer’s perspective, it was madness.

“Why are you so obsessed with reality? Hell has already been destroyed because of you. Wouldn’t it be better to simply exist here?”

“Yeah, right. I’ve spent a long time here. It’s been an eternity. But just after the end—after the purification—I mean…”

Guffin shrugged.

“That eternity means nothing. Time feels like it flows, but when we finally reach the end of the future, when only the past remains…”

He paused.

“All memories collapse into a single instant. How long you live doesn’t matter.”

That was why.

“An existence that cannot surpass infinity is an illusion. Me. You. And your brother… Ekael too.”

There had originally been nine archangels.

Eight represented the fundamental concepts of the world. The ninth was the opposite of those concepts—the Archangel of Chaos, Lucifer.

“Anchera didn’t create you,” Guffin continued. “You weren’t born. You’re negative energy tied to non-existent time and non-existent events.”

He was outside the law.

“In the great chain, you and the angels are the second law. When the angels’ concept weakens, chaos grows stronger…”

Guffin winked.

“But that doesn’t mean you can meet Ikael.”

Lucifer’s expression twisted at the words, but only he could see the wound they reopened.

“So you’re gambling on breaking through the infinite wall? If you fail, you face eternal extinction.”

“I don’t care.”

Guffin’s eyes hardened.

“Even after six hundred hours, all that remains is emptiness. If God is truly indifferent, then fine. That means it doesn’t matter whether I become God.”

That was why he had refused reincarnation, unlike his peers, choosing instead to remain as a signal.

Guffin stepped toward the wall.

“Shall we go soon?”

He would break infinity.

“Wait.”

At Lucifer’s voice, Guffin turned.

“I haven’t even said goodbye properly?”

Lucifer shivered, amazed that Guffin could still smile on the brink of eternity.

“Do you really believe it? Not a careless god—but one who loves this world?”

“Hmm. Actually, it’s not that hard to imagine. The concept of God isn’t like that. But if I were God…”

Guffin looked up at the ceiling.

“Wouldn’t it be enough to instill hope? Even if it’s just a signal.”

Lucifer fell silent for a long time.

‘Messiah.’

If it could truly be done…

“I’ll bring you back to reality.”

At Lucifer’s unexpected words, Guffin raised an eyebrow, amused.

“Hoo? It’s been a while. How long have you been holding that in? Your mouth must’ve been itching.”

“There’s no method to stop purification. But precisely because of that, I can send you away.”

Guffin understood.

“No way… You’re…”

“Yes. Purification must still happen. I will perish in your place. After all, I’m the only one in Hell who can replace data like yours.”

“Hmm.”

Guffin rested his chin on his hand, thoughtful.

“You know what kind of pain this is, right? It’ll be far worse than what I endured.”

“There are conditions,” Lucifer said.

Guffin laughed. “I knew you would.”

“If you truly want to give meaning to this world, share that meaning with us. It can’t end with just a signal.”

“I understand how you feel.”

Guffin frowned. “You’re not human. There’s no connection. You’re just part of the system that maintains this world.”

“Ikael,” Lucifer said firmly. “Give her meaning. Not just as a system—but as someone like you. That’s my condition.”

“It’s Ikael.”

That stubborn archangel.

“I thought you hated her?”

Indeed, Ikael, the beginning of law, and Lucifer, chaos outside the law, had never met. As if being and nothingness could never coexist.

But because of that, Lucifer hated and envied Ikael more than anyone.

He remembered.

At the dawn of creation, the radiant figure of Archangel Ikael, born in a blaze of light, smiling.

What had been his first feeling then?

‘Why am I…’

‘Was I born this ugly?’

Shirone understood the depth of Lucifer’s resentment through Omega’s early years.

Yin and Yang.

Two extremes born at once, diverging instantly.

Lucifer, pulled from the memory, continued, his voice breaking.

“I was jealous. Even though we were born in the same moment, why am I so ugly?”

It wasn’t about appearance. It was about concept.

“Why do I hate Ikael? Why do I see her as beautiful and myself as hideous?”

“Did you realize?”

“Maybe.”

Fighting Guffin for over six hundred hours had changed something in Lucifer.

“Because I can’t have it.”

He smiled sadly.

“There’s only one thing I can do. But it’s too late. If everything is empty anyway…”

If emotions are just signals.

“I want to do it with love. Love is better than hate, isn’t it?”

Even if they were equal, Lucifer saw Ikael and felt beauty.

Shirone doubted his senses.

‘Love?’

For those words to come from the mouth of the lord of Hell.

‘Ah????’

Suddenly, he remembered Igor, commander of the Demon Division, and his final words.

‘It was this.’ – There are no repentant demons.

Guffin asked, “What will happen to Hell?”

“Just because I disappear doesn’t mean demons vanish. The initiative will pass to humans. I wonder what kind of Hell they’ll create…”

Lucifer shrugged.

“For most people like you, it wouldn’t be a bad place.”

“Hmph. What if humans fall?”

“There’s no need to worry about Hell for someone returning to reality. A human-made monarch will rule anyway. Let’s call him Satan for now.”

“It’s Satan.”

“Humans, take care of yourselves.”

“Good. After my purification, you can return. Your Ultima will grow stronger through the Lord of Hell’s repentance…”

Lucifer’s eyes flashed with demonic light.

“Anke Ra is strong.”

Guffin nodded. “I know. But not alone. All Gaians will unite and attack.”

“Ikael won’t help you.”

Guffin knew her well—proud, conservative.

Lucifer’s fangs glinted.

“But you must keep your promise.”

With the universe’s existence at stake, Guffin had no intention of breaking it.

“Do your best. Under no circumstances will I forsake Ikael.”

Lucifer finally smiled.

“That’s enough.”

As he closed his eyes, thinking of Ikael, a cold voice echoed.

“Time remaining until purification: 764 years, 1,428 circa, 9,984 trillion, 129.8 billion, 11,763,329 hours.”

It was the price of repentance.

Guffin vanished from sight moments after leaving the infinite wall.

“…How much is left?” Lucifer asked the sky.

“Time remaining until purification: 764 years, 1,428 circa, 9,984 trillion, 129.8 billion, 11,763,329 hours.”

It hadn’t lasted an hour.

“Cheuk. Is that so?”

In Hell, time was converted into sensation. But the lord of Hell could not feel pain.

“It’s embarrassing.”

Lucifer looked up. The vision of Ikael appeared before him.

As vast as the distance between nothing and being, it must have been an immense love.

“If only I could see you just once.”

He reached out, but his hand grasped nothing.

For the first time in his existence, a beautiful smile formed on Lucifer’s weeping face.

“Ikael.”

The pain of his heart tearing apart.

“I love you.”

That pain was instantly converted into time.

“Aaaaaaa!”

His body exploded into black ash and was thrust into the infinite wall in an instant.

Where only silence reigned.

“…Purification is complete.”

A woman’s voice echoed, soft and lost.

‘It’s really amazing.’

Minerva felt a strange admiration as she watched Gaold deny himself.

‘He loves goodness, yet doesn’t hesitate to embrace evil’s methods—for her sake.’

I would do anything for Miro.

‘It must be painful…’

Even though he knew this world was just an illusion, a fleeting ball of light.

‘The one who saves life from hell, to the very end.’ Gaold, who embodied both good and evil, was profoundly human.

“Why?” Minerva asked.

“What are you doing this for?”

Every star in the Ivory Tower knew the truth of Gautou’s life.

“Miro used you. Even if it wasn’t intentional, she destroyed your life.” Gaold’s silence only stoked the fire inside Minerva.

“You climbed all the way to heaven to save her! You shattered me—by defeating the southern demons, then sacrificing love itself, you pushed a mere human like Miro to the extreme!”

“…Yes. That’s true.”

“But don’t you see? You were crushed by the maze. Sucked dry until nothing remained, then discarded!”

“I suppose so.”

“Then why keep holding on? Living is hell you want to end. Why not just give up?”

Gaold slowly raised his chin.

“Cry again.”

The maze wept.

Minerva wondered if Gaold had even spoken to her at all.

“Stop this! You’re a wizard! What you’re seeing is an illusion! It’s not real! She’s already forgotten you—she’s reached the pinnacle of goodness and lives well now!”

Gaold opened his mouth. Saliva dripped from the corner.

“You… what do you know about the maze?”

Thing said nothing.

“Persuasion is useless. He’s trapped in his own world, unable to accept reality. What now? How do we handle this?”

Fred let out a long breath.

‘That’s the problem.’

Gaold looked broken, unable to lift a finger—but anyone who stepped into his radius would die instantly.

“I’ll go in first,” Sing said.

It was a strategy: use a magic swordsman’s physical strength to bind Gaold’s mind.

He raised both hands in a sign.

“I’ll redeem you with the Law.”

“It might buy you 0.01 seconds,” Fred said, nodding.

He moved forward.

“…Enough.”

Minerva sucked on her smoking pipe. Amanta spun the World Wheel, preparing for battle.

Gaold remained lost in the vision of the maze, even as the weight of death pressed down on him.

‘Why are you crying?’

Why are you crying?

“You’ve done everything you wanted. Now I’m the loser. Why cry like that?”

“Go.”

At the same moment Fred lunged, Thing’s Law restrained Gaold’s movements.

The blade flashed toward the nape of Gaold’s neck—but Gaold didn’t flinch.

‘Finished.’

Victory flashed through Fred’s mind, certain and absolute.

‘In the end, he’s dead.’

Nothing more, nothing less.

But as the light cut across Gaold’s neck, there was a sharp sound of metal breaking.

“What?”

Only the hilt remained. The blade was gone.

‘How?’

It wasn’t just any weapon—it was a legendary sword, unbreakable by ordinary force.

“Keugh!”

Only then did Fred feel the pain. He looked at his hand—torn to shreds, bones exposed.

‘Vibration.’

Shockwaves had pierced his skin, the air pressure alone vibrating the blade apart.

‘That means…’

The Law of Thing had already been shattered.

Fred turned. Where Thing had stood, there was now a black hole.

The atmosphere had collapsed into the earth.

‘Are you still in shock?’

The ground should have been torn apart by such a void—unless something had scooped the soil away.

“Huh.”

Taeseong staggered, eyes closed. Minerva shot into the air on a jet of flame.

“Get back!”

The earth swelled like a balloon. Red light seeped through cracks in the surface, like a turtle’s shell splitting.

‘It’s going to explode!’

Aaaaaagh!

Fred crossed his arms in an X as a shockwave hurled him backward.

“Damn it!”

He tried to release external gravity, but inertia pinned him.

‘It’s weakening.’

Amanta, who had absorbed half the impact, grabbed Fred’s legs and pulled him skyward.

“I was careless. He’s stronger than I thought.”

At her words, Fred shook his head.

‘No. That’s not it.’

He hadn’t underestimated Gaold.

It was that Gaold’s apostasy—his complete rejection of reality—was far stronger than expected.

When the smoke cleared, a massive crater marked where the forest had once stood.

“Is it over?”

Minerva descended on a jet of flame.

“For now. But Thing’s missing. How deep did he go?”

If Gaold’s atmospheric pressure had crushed him underground, he wouldn’t have survived.

Minerva stared at the crater’s center, her expression dark.

“It’s 100 meters deep.”

“I’m fine.”

Then Ting’s voice came from the side.

She floated in, shoulder dislocated, gasping for breath.

“You said you held out?”

Thing pointed at Fred with his uninjured arm.

“The Armor of the Five Gods.”

It was the finest armor in the world, capable of 60% anti-magic resistance.

“The aim was off by about four meters. But the wind pressure alone dislocated her shoulder. She nearly died.”

Even though she’d escaped death, burrowing into the ground under that pressure had nearly killed her.

“What a humiliation.”

Amanta’s eyes narrowed.

“We’re apostate wizards, yet we’re four of the Five Great Stars of the Ivory Tower. It’s absurd that we can’t win.”

Minerva agreed.

“To be honest, we’re being overpowered. We need to defeat him tactically. It might be even harder for Taeseong…”

Taeseong, the living incarnation of a star, was immune to magic—but defenseless against planetary-scale impacts.

Fred prepared to jump.

“Be careful. He ignored me and went after Thing first. That’s instinct. A true master of battle.”

They all felt it in their bones.

“Let’s go.”

As the four leapt forward, cylindrical columns of atmospheric pressure rained down from the sky.

Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud!

“In action! Sense it with the Spirit Zone!”

Ordinary wizards couldn’t react at that speed—but they were stars of the Ivory Tower.

Minerva landed, puffed her cheeks, and released a cloud of green smoke.

Toxic magic.

‘I’m weak against air magic, but… this should be enough to force Gaold into pure defense.’

‘One breath and I’m dead.’

Gaold immediately wrapped himself in an air shield, blocking the spreading poison.

‘From here…’

Amanta halved the air shield’s density. Thing focused the Law to create a gap.

“Crush!”

Fred slipped through and opened fire on Gaold.

“It’s over!”

Blood sprayed.

“Huh?”

But it wasn’t Gaold’s blood.

Fred’s fists were torn open.

‘Air shield?’

The air clinging to Gaold’s body wasn’t just a barrier—it was sharp, convecting like blades.

‘The air shield isn’t passive. Could he have released an outer layer?’

If he’d shed the outer layer to survive, he should have been overwhelmed by their combined assault.

‘But it’s still there.’

Proof: Minerva’s poison still couldn’t penetrate.

‘Then how…?’

A wild thought struck Fred.

“Timeshare?”

A skill that rapidly switches between two active abilities to maintain both.

“Boo… woo… woo…”

Gaold staggered, eyes closed, letting out a pained moan.

Fred laughed.

“Puhahahaha!”

But his laughter held sorrow.

‘It’s ugly.’

I’ll survive, but what is Gaold now? A man using battery-switching skills with a mind already shattered by pain and illusion?

“Woo… woo… woo…”

His strained voice sounded like a frightened fool’s.

“If you go to the western peak of the world…”

Fred remembered the shock and awe he’d felt when he defeated Buddha.

“Did you really sell your pride, autumn de?”

Enraged, Fred unleashed violence, uncaring that his fists were breaking.

As the air shield weakened, physical shocks began to reach Gaold’s body.

“Uh… uh… uh…”

Even as flesh tore and bones cracked, Gaold kept watching the maze.

Sniff… sniff…

The image of her, sitting helpless, covering her face and crying—surely an illusion, yet…

‘Why are you doing this to me?’

Gaold’s heart tore apart.

‘What do you want from me, crying like that? I’ve done everything! I gave my life! I did everything you asked!’

Sniff… sniff…

‘You’ve reached the extreme! I don’t have to protect myself anymore!’

Sniff… sniff…

Fred’s eyes burned red.

“Die! Just give up already!”

Miro lifted her head. Her lips moved, tear-streaked.

Gaold couldn’t hear her voice, but the shape of her lips was burned into his mind.

Gaold.

Me… it’s so hard…

Don’t cry.

Chapter 986

The Right Choice (4)

~*~

King of Baska.

“A monster! A monster!”

Giants over eighty meters tall surrounded the castle. Even the most dignified politicians among the royal court urinated themselves as they peered through the windows in terror.

Was this the sign of the kingdom’s ruin?

Inside the royal chambers, Adolf XII—the young king who had once ushered in a new era of magic—lay on his deathbed.

“Sigh… sigh…”

Pony felt sorrow. This would be the last thing she ever saw.

“Your Highness, don’t worry too much. The glory of Tor Mia will live on through your descendants.”

“Crane! Crane!”

Amid the weeping royal family, King Adolf XII raised his head to deliver his final words.

“My… my all…”

At that moment, his eyes met those of a giant staring through the window.

“Aaaaaaa!”

The king shrieked, convulsed in terror, and died.

“Majesty! Majesty!”

The king of Tormia was dead.

As the royal family gathered and wailed, only Pony remained calm.

‘This is the king.’

He was just a man.

‘What do I do now?’

Thanks to Rufist risking her life to warn her, Pony knew the disaster approaching Baska. Soon, an elemental bomb would detonate over the city. No one would survive.

“Listen carefully,” she said.

“Majesty! Majesty!”

No one paid her any mind. Pony opened her eyes wide and shouted.

“Listen to me, everyone!”

The royals turned, frowning.

Then they saw the giant outside—and remembered the truth.

“Eh—run away!”

They rushed for the door, hands reaching for the knob.

A violent surge of water magic slammed them back against the wall.

“Pony! What are you doing?!”

Pony stared coldly at the terrified nobles.

“Where do you think you’re going without securing the succession?”

“T-that…!”

To those who staked their lives on bloodline and title, the royal succession was everything.

“Of course, the next king is Adolf XIII here! Why are you even asking?”

The eldest prince, Thenar, had already taken the name Adolf XIII. The royals exchanged uneasy glances.

‘What is that girl Pony planning?’

The Royal Guard had already been sent to the flower fields, just as Rufist had advised. Only a few guards remained. But Pony was a graduate of a magic academy. Killing someone would be effortless.

“If Thenar becomes king…”

As the royals watched nervously, Pony spoke.

“How about holding a quick succession ceremony? The legitimacy of the throne must be maintained.”

“Huh? Yes?”

They blinked in confusion.

“Look outside. This is a national crisis. We must restore the monarchy immediately and lead the people.”

A sigh of relief passed through the room. They had expected betrayal, but she was cooperating.

“That’s right. The palace must never be empty.”

The eldest royal brought forward Adolf XII’s crown and placed it on Thenar’s head.

“Alright. Let’s go—quickly!”

The ceremony ended in seconds. The royal family fled, leaving Pony behind.

She watched them go, a faint smile on her lips.

‘Still, they showed a little courage.’

Even when she’d heard Rufist’s plan, she’d dreamed of becoming queen.

“What is that?”

Now, at the end of everything, the only things that truly mattered weren’t things others could give.

“You were right, Shirone.”

She would survive.

At least her end would be different from Adolf XII’s—dying in fear and shame.

Outside, the giant, peering into the castle like a child inspecting an ant farm, roared.

“Gaaaaang!”

Though its urge to crush small things far exceeded any human instinct…

“I won’t make it easy.”

Through the hole the giant’s fist had torn in the wall, a flash of teleportation escaped.

Codename: Lucky Boy.

“There!”

Nade piloted a self-powered, wizard-only aircraft. Designed to draw energy from external sources, it was lightweight and small—perfect for high-maneuverability missions.

‘Basuka.’

From this altitude, the city was no bigger than a thumbnail. Yet Nade could see every demon swarming across it.

It made her skin crawl. If she could, she’d crush them all beneath her boots.

‘It will happen soon.’

Mounted beneath the aircraft was a single elemental bomb.

‘Only one shot.’

If it failed to detonate, the mission would fail. Humanity would fall to the forces of Hell.

Even so, Iruki had insisted on a single strike.

‘He’s thorough… but who could blame him?’

‘Don’t worry. I’ll succeed.’

Below, most of Hell’s army was concentrated around Basuka.

‘I drop it here.’

The first real signal reached her mind. Her heart sank.

‘Really? Am I really doing this?’

All the simulations, all the rehearsals—suddenly they felt like childish fantasies.

‘Everything I see will vanish. In an instant.’

Life. Civilization. Joy and sorrow. None of it weighed on her.

“Huh.”

Her heart didn’t move. Yet sobs escaped, and tears streamed down her face.

“Timer. Timer.”

She decided to stop thinking.

‘I’m just doing it. Like a fool. Like a machine. Like a madman—I just do my job.’

Sixty seconds appeared on the bomb’s panel. The countdown would begin the moment it detached—each second ticking down one by one.

‘According to calculations, it will detonate 200 meters above Basuka. And when it does…’

Nothing would survive.

“Goes.”

Nade reached for the release button, hand trembling.

Then a sharp voice cut through the air.

“Catch that human!”

An army of fairies surged upward, wings flashing.

The moment she saw them, Nade made her decision.

‘Press.’

A pure electrical impulse—no hesitation, no thought.

The elemental bomb detached with a click.

The fairies swerved instantly.

“Stop that object! In Ikael’s name—ISHIDA!”

Detonation time: 59 seconds.

The bomb fell, carving a path through Basuka.

“Like cockroaches!”

Every time the Thousand-Armed Guanshiyin incarnation struck, buildings collapsed.

“There!”

Miro and the others couldn’t tell who was rogue and who wasn’t—but they had no time to care.

“Knock-knock! You want to catch me?” Paimon laughed, darting freely between hundreds of points in midair.

‘Annoying ability.’

Van Eating Out.

It isolated a specific space and used the mobility of those trapped within it.

Now, Basuka had become a zone of impossible population density—no modifier needed.

Paimon’s voice echoed from every direction.

“Ho-ho-ho! O moths drawn to flame! The greatest festival in the world is coming soon…!”

The roof where Paimon stood was crushed by a giant palm, then pierced by repeated blows from Cheonsu Gwanse Yin Jangbeop.

“It will begin.”

Paimon vanished from the spot and gazed up at the sky, eyes alight with ecstasy.

The elemental bomb was falling.

“What?”

The halo of sun and moon created a telescopic effect in the atmosphere. Miro and the others looked up.

“This!”

Even prepared for death, the sight made their heads spin.

‘How much time left?’

Sain checked the timer—38 seconds.

Nade’s aircraft, dodging a barrage of fairy attacks, increased its output.

“I’m going crazy from frustration!”

She cast lightning magic, but it was absorbed—the fairies’ flying constructs feeding on the energy.

‘Too late to escape anyway.’

Nade abandoned the aircraft. Plasma flared around her as she began casting in earnest.

Passed the Law.

White lightning exploded outward.

“Kyaaak!”

The fairies’ screams were almost cute. The result was not.

Their bodies glowed, then collapsed into squirrel-sized skeletons.

‘What the hell are those?’

According to intelligence from her time as chairwoman of the Nade Group, these were the Army of Heaven.

The lines between good and evil were blurred. But none of it mattered to humanity.

‘Too many.’

Base fairy forces exceeded twenty thousand. Another thousand surrounded her now.

“I’ll handle the human. You all retrieve the bomb!” Tanote, the fairy commander, gave the order.

‘No!’

Nade had already accepted her death. But the bomb *had* to detonate at the right location.

“There!”

As Nade dove toward the ground, a powerful restraining force seized her body.

“Keugh!”

Tanote smiled coolly.

“Triangle Jung. Triangle Field.”

Nade scanned the glowing red lines forming around her.

She was trapped inside a sharp triangle. At each vertex, an ominous flame burned.

“When three points are connected by a straight line, the area is mine.”

‘Damn it!’

Detonation time: 23 seconds.

“Iruki! Iruki! Open the door!”

Alone in the temple’s command room, Iruki sat on the floor, silent.

An empty alcohol bottle lay beside him. His eyes were dull, unfocused.

“Iruki, please…”

Dorothy’s voice was followed by the commander’s shout.

“Back off! You said you wanted to be alone!”

“How can you say that? What if you’re having dangerous thoughts? I know how you feel right now!”

“I know *exactly* how I feel! No—nobody knows! We can’t interfere!”

The argument faded, distant, as if from another world.

“…You made the right choice.”

Lucky Boy’s trajectory calculations showed arrival over Basuka in five minutes.

Iruki smiled faintly.

‘I did it. It’s perfect. Nothing could be more efficient. So why…’

Is this right?

“Ahh…”

Fear erupted from his chest. Sobs burst out uncontrollably.

“Ahhh… Aaaaaa…”

He watched himself tremble, clutching his head. He knew.

“It’s over.”

He wouldn’t survive this sane.

“I’m done.”

“Iruki! Please! Talk to me! Say something!” Dorothy’s pounding on the door sounded like the footsteps of a demon drawing near.

“Aww! Leave me alone!” When the noise stopped, Iruki pulled his arms over his head and wept.

“It would’ve been better if I died. If I’d died…”

Then, a calm knock—different from before.

“Iruki.”

He froze.

His eyes snapped open.

Pounding. Pounding.

His heart raced. He lifted his head, even though he knew it was a hallucination.

“Iruki, it’s me. Can I come in?”

It was Shirone’s voice.

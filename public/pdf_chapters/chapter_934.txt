Chapter 934

Face to Face (4)

~*~

The day had passed.

“Aww.”

When Amy woke and moved her body as usual, pain flared through every muscle.

‘Feels like I fell off a cliff.’

Gripping her aching back, she limped toward the window and looked outside.

Nane and Shura were already up, tending a small vegetable garden.

‘What are they doing?’

The world was hurtling toward ruin, and the Buddha of this age was planting vegetables.

Sensing her gaze, Nane straightened and glanced at the cabin.

Seeing the warmth in Nane’s smile, Amy remembered what he’d said the day before.

‘I have to say something that makes sense.’

She understood why he wanted to become Shirone’s heart—to truly understand her.

‘Is that love?’ The moment she asked herself, something clicked.

‘Why do I love Shirone?’

Maybe, as Nane had implied, love was simply giving your heart without condition.

“Ah, okay! None of my business, right?”

More pressing than that was the news Shura had overheard—Heaven’s army had arrived.

Havitz alone was terrifying. Iruki would be hard-pressed to hold them back. No—this would be the worst war in history.

Amy, lost in thought, looked at Nane with new eyes.

His face was covered in tattoos, making him look fierce, but his smile remained childlike.

‘I don’t think he’s a bad person.’

She didn’t want to imagine what would’ve happened if Havitz had captured her instead of Nane.

‘If he’s on Shirone’s side…’

More than that, Nane was the one who had swallowed Ankera’s dream of ruling Heaven’s army.

Perhaps sensing her thoughts, Nane and Shura exchanged mischievous glances.

“Red!” Shura called out. “When will you say you love me?”

Amy shook her head quickly.

‘No, no. Don’t go there.’

It was too dangerous to dwell on. She had no choice but to fight.

Her body was still too weak to run, but magic didn’t require physical strength.

When she opened the cabin door, Shura stood up, pulling back the hood she’d been wearing.

“If you’re going to be lazy, come help.”

“Why me?”

“Those who don’t work don’t eat. Didn’t you know? If you don’t want to starve, pick up some pebbles.”

Amy wouldn’t die from missing a meal, but she didn’t want to act childish.

“Me too, Homi… you intervened.”

“Are you feeling well?” Nane asked.

“Don’t worry. I’ll pay for the meal.”

Nane smiled as Amy pouted and puffed her cheeks.

“Guests don’t have to work. Leave it to us. If you want to train, head into the woods over there. There’s a good spot for mental cultivation.”

“Because it’s fine. And I’m not pretending to be friendly. The chickens are growing.” She wasn’t offended.

“Don’t argue and go. You’re a soldier. The world’s on the brink, and you’re out here plowing fields? You’ll make yourself sick.”

Recalling her mission, Amy snapped back to reality.

“Right. What am I doing here? Whether you feed me or not, I’m going to train.” Without waiting for a reply, Shura threw down her hoe and stormed off.

“Aww, annoying! Whatever.”

“It’s not like the fire world is nothing.”

Shura fell silent.

Amy had once been merely a strong mage, but now she was becoming a system.

‘The pillar of fire from the Canian Plateau.’

She could still see the massive flames that had shot into the sky.

“Sheesh!”

Shura glared at Nane.

“Then shouldn’t you be training harder? One day they’ll become our enemies.”

“Did you expect me not to? If you don’t understand Shirone, you can’t become a true Buddha. The same goes for Shirone. You should’ve realized the truth of the ball by now.”

“Isn’t it just because you’re pretty?”

“Haha! Can’t deny that.”

Shura’s eyes widened.

“What kind of Buddha is that?”

Nane gently stroked Shura’s hair.

“Pretty is pretty. The first step toward truth is not ignoring facts.”

Buddha’s words were always right. She should’ve accepted them this time too.

‘Why am I like this?’

Ever since Nane had confessed to her, what had been holding back her heart?

‘Buddha, why am I standing beside you?’

For the first time, that simple fact felt heavier than all the world’s secrets.

Shura didn’t press further, and Nane returned to plowing the field with a calm smile.

‘Ignite!’

The moment Amy activated the ignition circuit—the foundation of fire magic—flames erupted from her body.

“Wow.”

Power surged, overwhelming even herself.

‘Is this incarnation art?’

She felt like she had become flame itself, not just a mage wielding fire.

“But not quite like this.”

She couldn’t face Heaven’s army without matching the power she’d unleashed on the Kanian Plateau.

“Whoa… whoa…”

Fear gripped her. She’d already crossed the threshold of death once.

“Go.”

Amy was a soldier.

‘Coffin of cowardice.’

As her focus plunged into the depths of fear, her consciousness blurred, as if her mind had split open.

‘I can’t give up!’

If she couldn’t master the inertia of coercion, she’d die just like on the plateau.

“Big!”

Hong-Eun’s eyes flickered thirty times per second, but control slipped away, and the flames raged unchecked.

‘No! No!’

The moment she realized she’d lost the thread, the fire exploded like oil doused on flame.

“Sermon. Jin òå.”

Nane’s voice resonated through the world, and the flames subsided as if the entire world had exhaled.

“Huh!”

It was like being pulled from drowning—submerged until the last breath, then yanked to the surface.

“Sigh! Sigh!”

Amy’s spirit snapped back into her body.

Gasping, pupils wide, she slowly turned her head.

Nane stood there, scratching his eyebrow with a puzzled look.

“It’s difficult. I suggested mental training, not life-or-death trials.”

“What difference does it make?”

She wanted to argue, but her voice softened—she’d been saved by his sermon.

Nane walked slowly, hands behind his back.

“Your incarnation is dangerous. Humans rarely awaken natural elements as incarnations. Except for Shirone.”

Hexa itself was light.

“Amy is also unusual. Perhaps a fire spirit resides within her mind.”

‘Jack O’Lantern.’

The fire spirit Shirone had given her from the outskirts of heaven had grown alongside Amy.

“You mean it manifested as an incarnation?”

“She’s always had a higher affinity for fire. Of course, reaching this level requires intense training. But it’s a double-edged sword. If mishandled, it harms the wielder.”

‘Incarnation isn’t simple.’

From the earlier display, she realized it wasn’t like regular magic—repetition didn’t guarantee strength.

‘Ninth.’

It required a special realization—something beyond thought, beyond mind.

‘That’s why I can’t meditate now. Or… can I?’

Amy knew she was far from being a true seeker.

She asked, “Can you help me?”

“It’s fine. I’ll figure it out myself.”

If she hadn’t overheard yesterday’s conversation, she wouldn’t want to owe Nane anything.

“Don’t complicate it. Helping someone in mental distress is natural. It has nothing to do with Shirone. And maybe… I’ll have a decent birthday.”

He was the closest thing to right in this world.

“I know. I still hate it.”

Nane let out a dejected laugh.

“You’re really stubborn. Then let me give you a hint. You weren’t far off. Enlightenment isn’t big or small. It’s a fleeting flash. A single twist, and the problem vanishes.”

“All at once?”

“Yes. It’s up to you. Just let go of your stubbornness for a moment.”

Amy pouted.

“Then let’s try again. Don’t hold back—open your incarnation completely.”

Nane stretched out his right arm.

“The Sermon Wall.”

An ocher-colored barrier unfolded like a fan, blocking the surrounding landscape.

Amy eyed him suspiciously. Nane shrugged.

“At least I have to protect my land.”

“Hmm. Fair enough.”

She hadn’t heard of the mantra, but she felt something when told to open herself fully.

‘I was scared.’

This time, she would stay in control.

‘Coffin of cowardice.’

Her eyes burned red, and flames rose around her once more.

“Huh!”

It was even harder this time.

‘I understand it in my head…’

She thought her heart was lighter than a feather, but fear still clung.

Her mind was sucked in.

“Huh billion!”

Again, the flames surged, slamming into the ceiling of Nane’s defense wall.

‘I have to stop…’

Then Nane’s voice echoed in her mind.

“Don’t stop.”

What?

“You are fire. It won’t stop until it burns everything. Get bigger. Grow endlessly.”

“Eternally?”

A flash of insight tore through Amy’s mind.

‘I see!’

The only thing that could control fire…

“Yaaaaa!”

Amy shattered her fear and unleashed her power in a trance.

‘Yes! Burn out! Burn everything!’

Her mind became a bomb, consumed by the will of fire.

“Huh uh uh uh!”

The moment the will of fire outpaced cowardice, a massive catharsis surged through her.

“Yes.”

Nane smiled.

“Exactly.”

When Amy came to her senses, all she saw was flame—small, but radiating in every direction like the spines of a porcupine.

“The Will of Fire.”

Nane said, raising his hand in affirmation.

“The true nature of fire is to become flame before it consumes itself. Remember—suppressing fire requires a greater fire.”

“Sun…”

Amy, dazed by Nane’s words, raised her hands and shouted.

“I did it! I did it!”

She had controlled the fire.

She had finally reached the level she’d dreamed of since magic school.

“I-I did it! I…!”

Nane, watching her run around with tears in his eyes, smiled.

“You really love magic. Congratulations.”

Amy froze.

“…You helped me after all.”

“You did it. I was a mage once too—I know how desperate you were. And a few words don’t count as help.”

“But Buddha’s words are different. Honestly, if it had been anyone else, I wouldn’t have risked my life to follow them.”

“If you put it that way…”

Nane scratched his head, embarrassed. Amy felt a pang of guilt.

‘Yes. Favors should be accepted as favors.’

“I have no selfishness. It was you who risked your life. And if any seeker turns away from those who’ve lost their way…’

“Thank you.”

Amy smiled.

“This time, I got help.”

Nane blinked, surprised by her response. Then, slowly, he understood.

‘I see.’

For the first time, he felt he might understand Shirone a little.

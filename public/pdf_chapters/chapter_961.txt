Chapter 961

In the Flower Field (2)

~*~

It was a sad story.

At Ikael’s request to name the child, Satiel agreed and returned to heaven.

Guffin asked, “Are you all right? I know Satiel’s personality well, but Heaven is a formidable opponent.”

Ikael smiled. “Have you already forgotten? Who was the angel who most eagerly sided with humans when you were having a hard time?”

Guffin had no reply.

“I know that,” he said slowly. “But still…”

“Do not worry. With you and me standing together, who would dare harm this child?”

Ikael, the head of the angels, and Guffin, who wielded the power of a god, were the strongest couple in heaven.

“Are you doing this because you’re afraid?” Ikael teased.

Guffin finally smiled, lifting his chin. “Haha! The only thing I fear is you. Then… shall we take down that formidable enemy?”

As Guffin stretched forward, Ikael twisted his body to shield the child.

“Wait a moment! My baby!”

Guffin halted immediately, looking down at the infant with a tender smile.

‘Don’t worry,’ Ikael thought.

Guffin placed a hand on Ikael’s shoulder and gently brushed the child’s cheek with one finger.

“Dad will protect you.”

Meanwhile, back at Jebul’s residence, Satiel felt as if she had just woken from a dream.

But it was real. The image of Ikael and Guffin, so affectionate and united, would not leave her mind.

“Name.”

You want me to give you a name?

At the time, she had simply nodded, not thinking much of it. But now, looking back, everything became clear.

‘For whom am I… …

Countless days of fighting for humanity flashed before her eyes.

She had done her best.

Yet the one Guffin had chosen was Ikael—the very angel who had opposed him all along.

“I was the one who fell in love first…” Tears of light streamed down her cheeks as an asteroid blazed across the sky.

“I loved you more. I fought for you more. But why? Why is it Ikael?”

She couldn’t cast her heart aside.

‘I should have said something. If I had confessed first, maybe things would have been different…’

Wouldn’t Satiel have had a child of her own?

Her starlight form collapsed to the floor like molten lava. When she regained consciousness, the space around her had changed.

‘Where is this?’

Sanctuary of Aravoth.

Behind her, the great door stood wide open. Anchera stood before it, his heart pounding loudly in his chest.

“What do you want with me, Satiel?”

She didn’t remember summoning him, but Anchera didn’t seem angry—rather, he had requested an audience.

“Ah, that… …”

Anchera’s eyes narrowed slightly, and Satiel’s jaw trembled as her voice caught in her throat.

Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud!

In Matei, the City of Giants in the Fifth Heaven, the giants—each towering to the upper limits of anecdotal power—marched forward.

Anchera was not the tallest, but the city shook each time his massive form struck the ground.

“What is this… …”

For the first time in Heaven’s history, archangels and Mara had gathered to witness a single event.

“Is it really possible?” Uriel asked Kariel, the overseer of the ritual. His tone held no conviction.

“I can’t say for sure,” Kariel replied.

Giants from all over Matei had gathered around a colossal statue that stretched into the sky.

“This is the first attempt at the 10th level of Ilhwa Sake. It’s not about odds.”

“Was level 7 the highest achieved so far?” Girshin, leader of the giant corps and the only 7th-tier giant, ruled over Matei’s giants.

“I cannot say it’s impossible. The giants participating in this ritual are all Ancients and Gaians.” The firstborn giant had traveled to Jotunheim and received a new law.

This was Step One.

From this point on, a giant’s identity was forged—making further advancement in magic nearly impossible. Considering that previous attempts using subjects had only reached three stages, the unifying power of the Gaians was clearly immense.

“Still, jumping straight to level 10? Isn’t that like merging ten billion Gaia-born beings?”

“Exactly. If successful, you will be the first and last giant to achieve the 10th level of Anecdotal Art. You will surpass Girshin and rule all giants.”

“… … He is the king of giants.”

Following Anchera’s instructions, Kariel began the ritual.

Success. Success. And success again.

The lamps on the bronze statues, arranged in a pyramid, lit up one after another.

“Gaiain, was this enough?”

Kariel, who had been anxious about losing only one giant, now wore a satisfied expression.

When the light reached the eighth bronze statue, Girshin’s face twisted.

Uriel watched him closely.

‘It must wound his pride.’

It was only natural. Until now, he had stood at the pinnacle of the giants. In an instant, he had been reduced to second-in-command.

‘But I’m also uncertain.’ Uriel turned his gaze to the towering bronze statue, its peak lost in the clouds.

‘Why would we need something this powerful?’

He knew how strong Girshin was.

But this was level 10.

Uriel wondered if such strength was even necessary in this universe.

“Step 9…”

Finally, the final threshold was crossed. The liquid containing all intermediate essences was drawn into the central bronze statue.

It took a long time to fill, but no one noticed the passing of time.

“It’s finished.”

At the quiet murmur of a Mara, the statue’s vibration ceased, and silence fell.

A metallic creak echoed across the plaza, now empty of the countless giants who had once filled it.

The sound, though low, was sharp enough to pierce eardrums. All eyes turned skyward.

But all they saw was the hollow interior of the mold.

“Why?”

Thud!

The ground trembled as someone spoke.

Looking down, they saw a figure no taller than an ordinary Gaia-born, standing with head bowed.

“What?”

His thick, muscular frame was unlike anything formed through normal training.

‘Creation is a difficult skill. The fact that the anecdotal liquor succeeded immediately…’

As Kariel analyzed the situation, Girshin stepped toward the bronze statue.

“You newly born giant.”

Uriel moved to stop him—this was a ritual conducted under Anchera’s command.

“Stay where you are,” Kariel said, giving Uriel a meaningful look.

‘Level 7 was already deemed dangerous to Heaven. What will level 10 bring?’

It was pure curiosity.

“I am Girshin, commander of the giant corps,” he declared, drawing his sword. “Though you may have been chosen as king, the law of the giants is strictly military-first. Fight me here—”

The moment the newborn giant raised his head, Girshin stopped breathing and dropped his sword.

A clear rustle echoed through the air as the newborn slowly looked around.

Kariel’s face went pale.

‘Alive?’

No—not just alive.

His presence was so overwhelming that mere eye contact made one feel small.

No angel voiced their emotions, but the Holy Light around them trembled uncontrollably.

“Ymir.”

Girshin’s voice dripped with awe as his legs shook.

“I am already…”

It was said that all living things had natural enemies. But Ymir did not seem like a creature meant to be challenged.

‘King of Giants? No. He is the king of all creatures.’ Girshin dropped to his knees in prostration.

“Forgive my rudeness, my king. I will rule the world at your command.”

Ymir did not respond. Instead, he turned his gaze toward the spire of Aravoth.

‘I want to fight.’

He didn’t even question his origin—the most basic instinct of any living being.

“This world… …”

Ymir pressed down with one foot.

The stone floor collapsed beneath him.

“It is too weak.”

The moment he finished speaking, a tremendous explosion erupted where he stood.

“Keugh!”

The gathered angels, maras, and mid-tier giants suffered no harm, but the plaza was utterly destroyed.

“Such self-indulgence!” Kariel frowned, appalled by Ymir’s arrogance.

But Uriel was focused on something else. “There was no hostility in that explosion.”

“No hostility? Not even toward angels? In a place where all archangels are gathered?!”

“Look over there.”

The entire area had sunken into a hemispherical crater. Uriel pointed to the spot where Ymir had stood.

“Ymir didn’t attack. She simply bent and straightened her knees.”

Kariel stared blankly at the deep footprints at the center of the crater.

“That’s exactly the problem,” Uriel said. “Even with archangels present, there was no hostility at all. None.”

It meant Ymir saw them as no different from Girshin.

As the angels stood in stunned silence, a sudden explosion rocked Aravoth.

Bang!

Ymir, having leapt to the spire in a single bound, drove his fingers into the wall.

“Hmm.”

He considered using his head as a support, but the wall crumbled like dust.

‘Uncomfortable.’

The universe was full of solid matter, but Ymir seemed to exist in a world without solidity.

‘I don’t feel anything.’

Realizing it would take time to control her power, Ymir lifted into the air and kicked downward.

Pop!

The wind pressure alone lifted her body. Moments later, the ground collapsed flat with a thunderous bang.

She smashed through walls like tofu and advanced—only to be stopped by the sanctuary door.

For some reason, she hesitated.

“Hey, Anchera.”

It was an instinct.

“I have been called.”

The door opened, revealing the massive form of Anchera.

Ymir, seeing the source of the strange pressure, smiled.

“Are you stronger than me?”

“It depends on your definition of strength.”

“No,” Ymir said, stepping closer. “There are no standards. I am stronger. Why should I obey you?”

“Because I gave you the blessing of birth.”

“No. That would be a curse.”

Ymir stepped within arm’s reach, spread his palms, and pressed them against Anchera’s eyes.

“I have no achievements. I am already the strongest. What purpose is there in becoming stronger?”

He crushed Anchera’s flesh in his hands, the sound of tearing skin echoing softly.

“Look—just a little pressure.”

“Do you want to feel it? The power, the body, the sensation of impact?”

“… … Can you?”

“It is not.”

Anchera’s tentacles lashed out.

“Maclein Guffin.”

The fingers that had torn Anchera’s flesh extended again, and Ymir’s body reacted to the name.

“Guffin.”

Though the personalities of ten billion beings had already merged, it seemed all of them could still be heard.

– Guffin is the best.

Anchera connected to Satiel’s astral body, electricity crackling from his eyes.

“Good.”

The door opened by itself.

“I will lead you to Guffin.”

Ymir turned slowly, gazed at the ruined landscape beyond the broken wall, then leapt.

Moments later, a powerful shockwave shook the spire.

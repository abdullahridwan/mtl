Even if it’s an illusion (4)

~*~

Sadia, the central city of Tormia.

The West family, known as the Nade family, resided here, in the kingdom’s largest plain.

Once on the brink of collapse, the family had risen to prominence through Nade Military—a company founded by Nade himself. What began as a wartime enterprise had exploded into something far greater, now standing as the most powerful conglomerate in the central region.

Of course, that reputation was often dismissed by the elite as mere envy.

Originally focused on military technology, the company had expanded rapidly while leading the core development of the elemental bomb project. Today, it was the world’s foremost corporation in its field—dominant in both name and reality.

Ranked third globally, with corporate assets totaling 780 trillion gold.

This was the current status of the Nade Group.

“Chairman, we’ll arrive at the family estate in ten minutes.”

The coachman opened the window of the heavily fortified wagon—the finest in comfort and security—and delivered the message.

“Understood.”

Nade, the young chairman of the Nade Group, pulled his coat collar up around his neck, burying his face into his shoulder.

Across from him, Liz flipped through a stack of documents with sharp efficiency.

“The corporate rankings moved up another spot. Congratulations.”

“I didn’t earn that,” Nade replied. “It’s built on war, blood, and lives. A feast of money created by demons.”

Liz knew exactly what he meant, but she didn’t voice it.

It was a battle of patience.

“Yes,” she said instead. “Deliveries to the temple were the catalyst for growth. But you also developed the weapon Iruki uses as a commander. It was adopted because yours performed best. And… it’s entirely your skill that brought the Nade Group this far.”

Normally, he would have brushed off such praise with a shrug. But this time, he didn’t.

“I know what you’re saying,” Nade finally admitted.

“But Liz, I have to go.”

He had to fly over Baska, the capital, and drop the elemental bomb at the precise moment.

“Tch.”

Liz exhaled sharply, barely holding back the storm of anger threatening to erupt.

“I just don’t understand. You’ve led the Nade Group brilliantly. Countless lives have been saved because of your weapons. Why do you have to do *everything*? What kind of hero are you trying to be?”

“It’s not about being a hero,” Nade said quietly.

Liz slammed her hand against her chest in frustration. “You fool—think! This isn’t dying in battle. This is suicide.”

“I’ll evacuate the moment the mission’s complete. Theoretically, survival is possible.”

“Theory? That equipment isn’t even 100% safe yet! What if something happens beyond theory?” When Nade remained silent instead of answering, Liz’s eyes filled with tears.

“Don’t do it. If you won’t, I’ll do anything. Fine—give them all our money. Take every coin we have. Just hire someone else. There *has* to be someone.”

Nade’s expression hardened.

“Liz, money can’t buy a person’s life.”

“Then what about *your* life?”

Her voice cracked through the soundproofed carriage like thunder.

“And me? Your family? The thousands of employees who believe in you? Their families? Why are you being so selfish?”

Liz snatched the papers off the bed and strode toward him.

“No! I won’t let you go! Find someone else! If you won’t, *I’ll* find someone!”

“You know the aircraft is fully customized. Only I can pilot it.”

“No. *I* can. Do you really think you’re the only mage in the world who can control electricity?”

“Liz.”

Nade winced, but she didn’t back down.

“I know why you’re doing this. Shirone, Iruki, and you—the Three Musketeers of Alpheas Magic School. Do you feel guilty because they’re fighting while you live comfortably? Or do you just want to shine as brightly as they do?”

Nade fell silent.

“Calm down, you idiot. You’ve already become the best. You don’t have to fight like Shirone or lead wars like Iruki. You’re making a difference.”

“It doesn’t matter if I don’t shine.” His voice was sincere.

“It’s always been this way since school. The three of us knew our roles. I was never meant to be in the spotlight. That’s Shirone’s place. Even Iruki understands that.”

“Then why are you trying to shine *now*? Leave it to Shirone. Leave it to Iruki.”

“Light,” Nade said softly.

A faint smile touched his lips.

“After you graduated, I became president of the Supernatural Psychic Research Society. The membership? Just two—Shirone and Iruki.”

It had been a good time.

“There was this one night… we gave a presentation on supernatural phenomena. About psychic energy…”

“I know,” Liz interrupted. “I’ve heard this story a hundred times.”

Nade ignored her, lost in memory.

“That night, after everyone had gone to sleep, the three of us lay down in Central Park and stared at the stars. The North Star.”

He laughed suddenly.

“We were so childish back then. Talking about eternal friendship and what we’d do after graduation. But in that moment… I really felt like I owned the whole world.”

He rubbed his lips, cutting off the echo of the past, and smiled sadly.

“Why? Why did I feel that way? Was it because the presentation went well? Did I see hope for my future?”

Liz listened without a word.

“No,” Nade answered himself. “It was because there were *three* of us.”

The corners of his mouth trembled as the memory surged back.

“I don’t know why, but in that moment, I thought it was a miracle we’d found each other. And now… one of them is suffering.”

As a participant in the elemental bomb project, Liz understood exactly how heavy Iruki’s burden was.

“If I pass this task to someone else, I’ll only add to Iruki’s load. Why me? Because I want to. But really… why should I?”

Nade’s eyes reddened.

“Because *he* wants it.”

Liz had no reply.

“He’s brilliant, but he’s an idiot. He tries to carry everything alone. Won’t ask for help. Won’t even admit he’s struggling. But I know. That’s my role. I’m the only one who can ease his burden now.”

If only I’d told you to let me do it.

“But to turn away? To pretend I don’t see his pain just because he didn’t ask? I hate the thought of dying, Liz. I love you more than anything. But I’d give my life for you… and for him.”

Nade had already made his decision.

“I have to go.”

Liz’s shoulders slumped.

“Tch.”

She returned to the bed and collapsed onto it, running her hands through her hair, swallowing back tears.

The window opened again.

“Chairman, we’ve arrived at the estate.”

Because the family had purchased and merged all surrounding mansions, the compound was vast—reaching the main residence still took time even after arrival.

Liz, now composed, turned her head and pressed her lips together.

“Someone even got married because of this place.”

Nade finally smiled.

“Haha. That’s unavoidable. You’re part of the survivors too, after all.”

“Who’s saying that now?” she muttered.

“I love you, Liz.”

It felt like a knife to the side, but hearing it didn’t hurt. Not really.

“Huh.”

At the main residence, Nade’s parents, Ballroom and Teria, waited to greet them.

“Son.”

There was no trace of the terrifying woman who used to scowl at him with a twisted face.

Greed and jealousy—endless human vices—meant nothing in the face of wealth that defied reason.

Nade didn’t want to think about whether money had changed her or if prosperity had simply revealed her true self.

“Yes. I’m back.”

Ballroom extended a hand for a handshake.

“It’s been a while. You’ve been busy with work, I hear.”

“Yes. I have to leave immediately—urgent matters. But Liz will stay here for a few days.”

Liz was a key figure in the project, bound by strict confidentiality.

“Hello, Mother. I should’ve visited sooner. I’m sorry. While I’m here, we’ll go to some nice places.”

“Hoho, of course. I’ve reserved a table at a high-end restaurant. We’ll go together—just the two of us.”

It was fortunate that Teria liked Liz.

“Then I’ll take my leave. Liz, take care.”

After a brief exchange, Nade turned toward the carriage.

“Wait, Nade.”

Liz hurried after him and spun him around.

“Wait. Your coat collar’s wrinkled.” It was a flimsy excuse, but Nade let her fix it.

“Sorry.”

Liz froze for a moment, then smiled.

“Promise me one thing.”

“Yes.”

She straightened his coat, gripping the collar tightly with both hands.

“If you’re going to do this… do it right. Shine brighter than anyone. Don’t hesitate like the nervous boy from school. Understood?”

Surprised, Nade narrowed his eyes—then nodded.

“Thank you.”

For letting me leave without regret.

They shared a brief kiss. Then Nade turned, his eyes alight, and walked to the carriage.

*I’m coming, Iruki.*

The atomic bomb’s codename was *Lucky Boy*.

Meanwhile, Guffin and Ikael were hiding in a remote area, shielded by Hexa’s ability.

“Why not?”

Guffin muttered, sprawled across a mat, while Ikael picked fruit nearby.

“Still thinking about it?”

From all sides, his three followers—Mara 3, 2, and 1—rushed forward and knelt.

“We will serve!”

Ikael stared at the six hands extended toward him and blinked.

Ashur of Signal, Lethe of Augmentation, and Ramus of Lightning.

It was almost pitiful to see beings once known as Ikael’s three great guardians scrambling to fetch a colander.

*I am truly fortunate.*

Even the angels’ direct subordinates had defied Anchera’s authority to follow Ikael.

“It’s fine,” Ikael said gently. “There’s nothing left to do now that Baekkyung is gone. This is all I can offer.”

Ashur bowed deeply. “The mere fact that you exist is a blessing. No force in creation could replicate your beauty.”

“Oh, Ashur,” Ikael said, covering his mouth with a soft laugh.

Guffin, lying on his side, scratched his stomach.

*He’s such a sly one.*

Maybe a little jealousy crept in, but it was endearing.

Lethe stepped forward, hands outstretched.

“Give it to me. I’ll multiply it a hundred million times so Ikael-nim never has to risk himself again.”

Lethe had the power to increase the quantity or value of objects.

“It’s fine. Guffin will just eat it anyway. Honey, today’s lunch is fruit.”

Guffin pushed himself up, eyes wide.

“Lunch is fruit again?”

“Would you like something else?”

The moment Ikael’s aura flickered, Guffin bit into an apple.

*It’s my hand.*

Still, his stomach felt hollow.

“I know you don’t understand the joy of eating. But… what’s the point of eating if you won’t enjoy it?”

“You’ve written well,” Guffin said dryly.

Many years had passed. Yet Guffin and Ikael had no children.

*It’s not possible. The biology is different.*

They were fortunate there was no physical mismatch, but the mechanism of reproduction was incompatible.

“Are you really that worried? About Husa, I mean. Of course I know what it means.”

Anyone could become a god.

*Ikael is a mental being. His form limits him. He needs a signal to manifest.*

I twisted the laws with Hexa. Kael made countless adjustments. But nothing worked.

*The connection between Ikael and me must be through the heart. But no matter how we merge into Hexa, nothing changes. It’s not that I desperately want it.*

Guffin, who had been watching Hexa spin on his fingertip, suddenly clenched his fist.

Chang and Hexa shattered.

“Mind?”

Ikael, still trimming fruit, turned.

“Honey, what’s wrong?”

“It’s not about the body.”

It wasn’t enough to link their hearts and merge into a single signal.

“Let’s go.”

Guffin grabbed Ikael’s wrist and pulled him up as he knelt, lost in thought.

“What? All of a sudden?”

Guffin glanced back, smiled meaningfully.

“You’ll like it too.”

The most precious love in the world.

Agape.

Aetra descended beside Miro and the others, her breath still radiating life energy across Basuka’s battlefield. The healing aura she had unleashed shimmered faintly in the air before fading. Returning to her usual form—a young girl with braided hair—she wiped sweat from her brow and smiled.

“Hehe, Messiah-sama would be proud.”

The maze had arrived.

“It’s an ultra-fast activation,” she said.

“Yes. It increases vitality by about 200,000 times.”

“200,000…”

Miro exhaled sharply. Just imagining it, such power could mend any wound—except death itself.

“Is the effect good?” Aetra asked.

“Incredible,” Miro admitted. “Are you invincible?”

“In a sense,” she replied. “But…”

Her gaze drifted to the edge of the city, where not everyone celebrated. Amid the cheers of victory, some remained silent—those who had lost families, those broken by fear, those who had endured horrors at the hands of demons. Even Eight, who rarely showed emotion, felt a pang of sorrow at their suffering.

“You can’t heal a wounded mind.”

“Couldn’t a White Dragon do it? Assriker deals with soul information.”

“Probably not,” Aetra said. “Her presence might soothe pain, but she can’t erase the scars etched into the psyche. It’d be like using drugs to forget. The pain returns.”

Miro nodded. “I see.”

He shifted focus. “Is there a cooldown?”

“For Life Healing? One hundred hours. Among the Twelve Apostles, I have the longest.”

“Four days…”

Aetra understood his concern. “Yes. It’s hard to time perfectly in battle. But Messiah told me to use it whenever lives are at stake. Don’t worry about efficiency.”

It was a Shirone-like sentiment. Miro agreed.

“But four days…”

Suddenly, Miro’s head snapped toward the city wall. His eyes widened. Lightning crackled where his gaze landed.

“Blitz?”

Aetra tilted her head, confused. Blitz, tense and silent, didn’t respond.

“Come.”

“Coming? What do you mean?”

A roar erupted from Basuka’s walls. Miro’s expression turned cold.

“Already?”

The armies of Heaven, scattered across the world by the Hand of God, had returned.

‘They must have flown thousands of kilometers.’

If Hexa was the ultimate expression of mental power, then the stronger the being, the farther they could travel.

‘So who arrived first?’

Ironically, it was the one presumed strongest among them.

“Ha ha ha ha!”

Ymir’s laughter tore through the air, shattering windows across downtown Basuka.

Miro cursed under his breath and activated a mass teleport. The group materialized at the front gate.

There, Garcia led the allied forces in a desperate assault on Ymir.

“Are you kidding me?”

Every time Ymir spun and struck, allied soldiers exploded—heads bursting like overripe fruit.

“Garcia!”

Even as Miro called out, Garcia didn’t turn. His eyes burned with fury.

‘Why?’

The scene was absurd—a being who had obliterated the demon army now trading blows with human soldiers.

‘Is he mocking us?’

No. It didn’t feel like mockery.

Ymir was simply enjoying the fight. The battle itself was his purpose.

“Let’s go! We are the defenders of humanity!” Allied soldiers, bloodied and fearless, charged blindly.

And each time Ymir struck—blow after blow—bodies burst apart without resistance.

Garcia’s nostrils flared.

“Aaaaaaa!”

Rage surged through him, deeper than the hatred he’d feel toward his parents’ killers.

“Wait!”

Garcia ignored Miro’s shout. Miro followed, clicking his tongue in frustration.

Beside him stood Enox, the fairy representative.

“Let’s work together.”

Both sensed the same dissonance—a deep, unsettling wrongness they couldn’t name.

‘Hellfire!’

Garcia unleashed a torrent of fire magic. Enox summoned a gale, weaving wind energy into the flames.

Ancient wind magic slashed at Ymir, followed by a barrage from Heavenly Arms Guanse Yin.

Dust exploded outward, then collapsed like a burst bubble.

‘Isn’t it shocking?’

Ymir stepped forward, muscles vibrating, air swelling around him. He walked over the corpses of the fallen.

“Come on.”

Garcia, Enox, and even Miro froze.

Lian stared at Ymir.

‘I don’t know.’

They’d fought together briefly before the Hand of God scattered them. But this was different.

‘I can’t feel anything.’

Lian wasn’t a master at gauging strength, but he could usually tell who was strong or weak. Yet Ymir radiated nothing—no power, no fear, no intent, not even hostility.

“Ah??????”

Anger flared in Lian’s chest.

‘That’s it.’

Like a distant star too far to see, none of them could perceive Ymir’s true presence.

“Hey! What are you doing? Why aren’t you moving?!”

The same thought echoed in their minds as they instinctively stepped back with every step Ymir took.

‘Why does this exist?’

Their anger came from denial—Ymir’s mere existence invalidated theirs.

‘Ymir shouldn’t be here. Because…’

The moment they acknowledged him, everything else lost meaning.

“Pooh!”

Ymir looked up at the sky and exhaled.

“I know what you’re afraid of.”

He lowered his gaze. His eyes held 1% expectation, 99% emptiness.

“For example… I don’t want to die. I don’t want to vanish. I want to keep thinking.”

His steps halted.

“But even that desire… I can’t feel it. I mean, I can’t feel anything.”

Just as humans couldn’t perceive Ymir—so too could Ymir no longer truly sense the universe.

“It was thrilling.”

The strongest memory in Ymir’s mind was Ogent’s strike.

Even now, the spot where a molar had been torn out throbbed vividly.

“You promised me. I will definitely come back.”

Ymir’s eyes locked onto the blue-haired swordsman at the rear of the allied forces.

“Keep your promise.”

Lian wasn’t Django. But this time, he had thought long and hard.

As he finally stepped forward, Miro spoke.

“Do it together. I can’t catch him alone.”

“I know.”

No one would be foolish enough to face such an enemy alone.

“But I can’t retreat. There’s no way back.” Lian, having passed Ymir in speed, took the lead.

“This is the destination.”

Miro understood.

What courage it must take to close the final two meters.

“It was thrilling earlier,” Ymir said.

“But that was meeting Ogent. Now, that won’t be enough.”

“Don’t worry.”

A line shot forward at invisible speed.

“I will fight until I die.”

A horizontal slash split the landscape. The world divided in two—Ymir at its center.

Lian’s right elbow ruptured as the impact from his straight sword, lodged in his forearm, tore through him.

“Great!”

Deny’s strength ignored inertia.

With the blade resting on Ymir’s arm, Lian twisted his upper body with every ounce of will.

“Aaaaaaa!”

As the Distortion of the Law warped reality, Ymir’s form began to shift.

“Hehehe.”

Ymir’s shoulders trembled. A cruel smile split his face.

“It’s ridiculous.”

His other forearm shot up, striking Lian’s body at terrifying speed.

Lian staggered back, blocking with his greatsword. His fist slammed into the blade.

A shockwave erupted.

Time ceased to exist. The concepts of fast and slow dissolved.

‘Sleepy.’

Through half-lidded eyes, Lian saw thousands of Tesses spinning, laughing.

‘I want to sleep…’

He didn’t close his eyes.

‘I want to close them. I’m so tired. Why can’t I lie down? You can’t sleep standing.’

Lian waited.

‘I have to go home. Reina noona isn’t in the royal castle. Should I take Shirone?’

But the next moment never came.

‘Bored.’

With nothing left to think, Lian began counting.

‘…Two thousand three hundred and twenty-one, two thousand three hundred and twenty-two.’

How much time had passed?

‘Aaaaaa! Aaaaaa! Aaaaaa!’

He had a feeling.

Perhaps this timeless state would last forever.

‘Move! I have to move!’ His brain had forgotten time.

‘I can’t escape.’

He feared being trapped here—forever.

“Ha ha ha ha ha! Ha ha ha ha ha! Ha ha ha ha ha!”

Tess’s faces filled the sky, laughing endlessly.

Tess in Tess’s mouth, Tess in Tess’s mouth, Tess in Tess’s mouth…

‘I’m going crazy.’

What kept Lian anchored in time?

‘Obsession.’

His body, mind, and soul were already destroyed. He had no strength left.

‘I have to achieve something here.’

One unshakable idea held him between life and death.

The universe swelled with Tess’s face, ready to burst from laughter.

‘Ah??????’

At the peak of madness, Lian finally understood Ogent’s words.

‘To reach where I want…’

How much can you give up?

A happy life. The woman you love. Your lifespan. Your body.

‘I gave up on being human.’

And if that still isn’t enough?

Then there’s only one choice.

Either abandon your obsession.

Or abandon everything except your obsession.

‘Go!’

When Lian’s consciousness returned to his eyes, time surged forward.

‘That’s right.’

Ymir’s fist pierced through the unbroken sword. Eighty percent of Lian’s body ruptured.

‘Doesn’t matter.’

The moment the thought narrowed to a single purpose—*cut*—Ymir’s eyes widened in shock.

‘What is this?’

Even as Lian’s body flew apart like pulp, the greatsword continued its arc.

‘What remains?’

What exists where the body is gone?

‘Obsession.’

A terrifying obsession.

In that instant, Ymir crouched, and clearly saw a vision—someone drawing the greatsword.

‘Ogent.’

Every hair on his body stood on end. A sensation he could never forget surged through him.

Lian, body gone, clenched his teeth.

‘Cut!’

Divine Transcendence – The Day of the Divine Sword Union.

At a speed far beyond living limits, the great straight sword cleaved the atmosphere.

A normal blade would have melted from friction before halfway…

‘This is my conviction.’

<Idea> is not destroyed.

The impact traveled faster than sound. In the silence, Ymir threw his upper body back with all his strength.

‘What?’

He thought he’d dodged.

But when he looked back, countless greatswords filled every space.

‘It destroyed my cognition.’

Natasha called it *negative beats*.

The next moment, the air exploded in a roar.

It was undeniably a fallacy.

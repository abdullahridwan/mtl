Chapter 919

Coming Disaster (1)

~*~

The 2nd Valkyrie Corps, mobilized from across the Central Continent, converged on the Kanian Plateau.

Tess gazed at the barren, rocky expanse and turned to Lian.

“Where’s Shirone?” By all logic, he should have already reported to the Ivory Tower and returned.

“I don’t know,” Lian replied. “But he’ll be fine. He’s strong.”

Yet unease lingered. Even the Temple had failed to locate Yahweh.

For Lian, protecting Shirone was the highest priority.

Tess, understanding this, asked, “Can I really stay here?”

Lian fell silent. His urge to find Shirone burned like smoke from a chimney, but he couldn’t leave—not with Havitz still at large.

And Natasha.

Moving at unmatched speed, only Lian could track her.

But the demons would come looking for him. From their perspective, the Knight of Mach was a key threat—one that had to be eliminated first.

“I just need to hold out for one more day.”

When new orders arrived from the Temple, the entire 2nd Corps seemed to exhale.

“I’ll handle Natasha,” Lian said. “But I can’t stop Havitz. Be careful, Tess.”

Tess swallowed hard, remembering the sensation of vanishing.

“Don’t worry. I’ll keep my head clear.”

It wasn’t something vigilance could prevent, but there was no other choice.

“What about Amy? Is she okay?”

The barracks sprawled across kilometers, making it hard to find anyone.

“She’s in a magic unit,” Lian said calmly. “They move frequently based on strategy. Might only fly during battle.”

Once the fighting began, everyone would have to focus entirely on their role.

No one could be replaced.

Survival meant saving oneself.

*Emmy…*

Tess sent the thought into the void, reaching for a friend whose mind was elsewhere.

*Let’s make sure we survive.*

With the return of the outcasts and the Temple’s shift in command, morale surged.

As the appointed hour neared, the army’s banner sharpened like a cold blade, aimed at the enemy camp.

“Whoa.”

Amy’s 6th Battalion was assigned to provide rear support for the vanguard.

*You can do this.*

Iruki had said he would die.

Amy didn’t disagree. But above all, she believed in what she had done.

*Fight!*

She rose from her seat and left the barracks. Her soldiers were waiting.

“Commander, it’s time.”

In their eyes—eyes that had overcome fear—Amy saw that long speeches were unnecessary.

“Let’s go.”

Her unit joined the vanguard, twenty thousand strong, splitting left and right.

Support would rush to whichever side collapsed first. After that, no further orders could be expected.

“Listen up, all crew members!”

The vanguard was led by Brigadier Taigang, a famed swordsman of the Central Continent.

“The enemy we face next—”

A massive cloud of dust rose on the horizon. The ground trembled.

“Commander.”

There was no time to say goodbye.

*Yes. This is war.*

Taigang, who had been watching the dust with cold detachment, turned to his troops and smiled.

“It was an honor to fight beside you.”

His words struck deeper than any speech. Soldiers’ eyes turned red with rage and resolve.

“Waaaaaa!”

Spurred by the roar, Taigang spurred his horse forward, sword drawn.

“Let’s kill!”

The battle had begun.

On the plateau, where strategy meant little, the distance between humans and demons closed rapidly.

*Come!*

Amy’s unit, rushing from the center, saw shadows moving within the dust.

“Kuaaaaa!”

An army of two hundred million surged forward, an unstoppable tide.

“Huh…”

A groan escaped Amy’s clenched teeth.

Fear.

But it didn’t matter.

*It’s okay. I’ve come this far.*

From this point, the army’s movement wasn’t driven by will or determination—it was pure, irreversible inertia.

“Start bombardment!”

At Amy’s command, hundreds of wizards unleashed fire into the enemy ranks.

And then—everything was trampled.

Valkyrie Command.

“Report! The 3rd Division of the 2nd Corps, gathered on the Kanian Plateau, has been destroyed!”

The commander asked, “What’s the estimated damage?”

“Impossible to determine precisely. But based on unit area, over thirty thousand.”

“Hmm.”

Iruki bit his nails, his breath ragged, as if his lungs were torn.

“Thirty thousand.”

Only two hours into the battle.

*I have to endure twenty-two more hours.*

The worst part was they couldn’t send help.

*Don’t think about what we can’t do.*

Iruki stood and walked to the map.

“Forget the Plateau. From now on, focus on counterattack. What’s the deployment?”

“It’s ready. The best position is here.” The young officer’s eyes were colder than steel as he pointed.

Six hours after the battle began.

*What?*

Amy pushed herself up, covered in dust, the world spinning.

*Where am I?*

Canian Plateau.

*What am I doing?*

War.

The answer came instantly, but it felt unreal, as if blocked by a wall.

*Am I alive?*

She knew activating self-image memory could pull her mind back from shock.

But instinct resisted.

*Why am I here?*

She didn’t want to wake up.

Her instincts screamed to stay broken, to go mad and forget everything.

“Kuaaaaa!”

A one-meter-tall angler lunged, jagged teeth bared.

Red eyes flared. Amy twisted aside just in time.

*Mental attack.*

Reality crashed in, a pressure so intense it felt like her brain would split.

*Rod.*

*Degree.*

“Great!”

But it didn’t break.

“Kihehehe! You’re a survivor! Survivors!”

Amy raised her hands, flames erupting. She turned the charging angler into a charred corpse.

“The battalion commander!”

At the familiar voice, Amy turned. Fourteen soldiers, including Berik, limped toward her.

“You!”

It was like seeing an angel in hell.

“You’re alive! You’re injured!”

Berik’s right wrist was gone. Blood still seeped—he’d been too rushed to stop it.

“I’m fine. Move. We’re already far from base.”

“If you bleed more, you’ll die.” Amy tore her uniform and wrapped it tightly around Berik’s forearm.

“Aaaaaaa!”

The pain was excruciating, but there was no time to argue.

“It’s okay. Are you all that’s left?”

The 1st Squad Leader shook his head. “Don’t know. These are just the ones we found.”

Amy nodded, her eyes glowing red.

*Where is it?*

Even without knowing the origin, her self-image memory pinpointed their coordinates.

She pointed west.

“Base is that way. Follow me. I’ll avoid fighting. You take care of the wounded.”

The escape began.

Her red eyes guided them through every obstacle, but avoiding every demon—filling sky and earth—was impossible.

In several skirmishes, three men fell. Another died two hours later.

“Sigh! Sigh!”

The distance that should’ve been short felt endless.

*Never give up.*

Amy felt like screaming from frustration, trapped in a loop like a tangled thread.

As the sun set, she finally heard Asrai’s battle cry.

“I’m here! Made it. Hang on a little longer, everyone!”

Since the demons were advancing recklessly, detouring to the main base wouldn’t be a problem.

“Quack! Found.”

At the voice from above, Amy’s face went pale. The soldiers’ eyes filled with despair.

“I missed you.”

A demon with a bull’s head, clinging to a crow’s leg, grinned wickedly.

*If only…*

Parkma, the division commander she’d fought before.

*No. Not a coincidence.*

Pakma landed with a thud, a massive axe in both hands. His side still bore the mark of Amy’s strike.

“Did you really think you could run?”

“I told you,” he sneered, “even if I have to rip out my retinas, I’ll burn your face into them. I haven’t killed anyone since. I’ve been looking for you.”

Amy glanced back, biting her lip.

*I’m almost there.*

Fighting Parkma now, with her group exhausted, was suicide.

“Is it me you want?”

Parkma laughed bitterly. “No. What I want is to do the thing you hate most. For example…”

He lunged. The axe swung.

The injured soldiers fell, heads severed.

“Like this?”

Amy’s face twisted in rage. The remaining soldiers attacked together.

“Die!”

Flames and ice rained down, but Parkma advanced without flinching.

“Haha! You must’ve suffered, huh? Is it worse than before?”

The axe showed no mercy. In the end, only Amy and Berik remained.

“Now there’s no one in the way. Shall we begin?”

Parkma stomped forward. Berik stepped in front of Amy.

“Go, Commander. I’ll buy you time.”

It was what Amy had wanted to say.

“You go,” she said. “I’ll take him.”

“No. You know I can’t make it back to base with my skills.”

“I’m the captain. This is my responsibility.”

“… … Humans die anyway.”

“What?”

Parkma slung the axe over his shoulder and watched.

“My father said that. When you face hard choices, remember—everyone dies in the end. Then you can make the right choice.”

Berik looked back.

“I want to live. But if I can’t, I want to make the right choice.”

Amy clenched her teeth.

“Right choice? Dying in my place—is that it?”

“Nope.”

Berik slowly stood.

“I was born a man. Isn’t it better to die looking cool in front of the woman I love?”

Even if death was certain.

“I was so happy you came back to the unit. You chose us—not your lover. Honestly…

“I feel like I beat that blessed bastard.”

“Go. You deserve to live. And I… I’ll be a damn cool guy.”

Berik kicked off the ground and charged.

“Forever.”

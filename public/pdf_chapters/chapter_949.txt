Omega Year 689.

The population of Gaia—those strong enough to hold Ankera in check—had rapidly dwindled since Sojeonghwa.

“**Ataraxia.**”

As Ikael’s judicial halo blazed, six archangels charged toward Gaia.

“Damn it…!”

With the population reduced by attrition, the power of the ‘Hand of God’ had weakened significantly.

“**Ragnarok.**”

Uriel surged forward, wreathed in white electricity, carving a vast path through the sea of humanity.

“Stay in formation!”

Methiel clicked his tongue as the Gaians regrouped with flawless precision, like cells mending a wound.

“Still resisting?” Rayel sneered. “The age of rebellion is upon us.”

Dodging a strike from the Hand of God, she raised her hand to the sky.

“**Small and Medium Speed Bullets.**”

Hundreds of tons of metal filled the sky, launched at supersonic speeds. The impact rivaled a small meteor strike, shaking the Ultima System to its core.

“Hold on! For a new era!”

Before the Hand of God could re-form, Rayel—the archangel of light—rushed in and drove her fist forward.

It was a simple attack, but unstoppable. Nothing could withstand physical force moving at light speed.

“**Ultima System!**”

Within Gaia’s core, an intangible barrier swelled like a balloon and expanded rapidly.

“Huh.”

As Rayel unleashed her power, a horizontal flash tore endlessly through the defense.

Pop! Pop! Pop! Pop! Pop!

Along the flash’s trajectory, a chain of explosions erupted, scorching the air and biting back at its source.

“Does everything have to die before it’s fulfilled?”

Rayel leapt into the sky, chin raised, the accelerator spinning on her arm.

“Direct?”

Light pierced Gaia’s eyes.

“Did you really think I wouldn’t respond?”

As billions of voices merged into one, the Hand of God clenched its fists.

“It’s the same pattern. It won’t work anymore.”

At that moment, Rayel spun her judicial halo at light speed, preparing to strike.

“Please stop it!”

Satiel stepped between them, arms spread wide.

“This is enough. If one side is annihilated, it will be a loss for everyone.”

Rayel snorted. “Back off, Satiel. I’ve tolerated your neutrality so far. But if you side with humans, the situation changes.”

“I’m not taking sides. I’m saying this war has no purpose.”

The Gaians hesitated.

‘It doesn’t make sense…’

Of all who had left the photon world, only Guffin remained.

‘Yes. Why are we even fighting?’

It had been so long that the reason was forgotten.

Satiel turned to the representative.

“Request a truce. There’s still a way for angels and humans to build a new world together.”

“I’ve come too far for that,” came the reply. “If this ends now, what of the ancestors’ war, lasting eons? What of the hundred billion who died since Sojeonghwa?”

Ikael’s voice boomed across the battlefield.

“Do you still think you’re gods? Look at you—defeated, pitiful. I’ve seen no gods like you anywhere.”

A weak voice rose from the ground.

“Could you be any more pathetic than me?”

Ikael frowned as Guffin staggered forward, barely standing.

‘He’s still fighting? After enduring Ankera’s wrath alone… he’s as good as dead.’

‘Even though Sojeonghwa was blocked.’

The anger capable of destroying the world was not something a single human could contain.

Ikael clenched his teeth.

‘A foolish man.’

The fall of such a formidable enemy was exhilarating—yet seeing him broken stirred something deeper.

‘He’s fought for so long… from the beginning until now.’

“Don’t give up, Guffin. Gaia is weakened. You’re not in your right state. This war is ours.”

Guffin gave a dry, hollow smile.

“I can’t.”

“Why? Why carry everything alone? Even holding back a fraction of that pain must be agony.”

The pain surged again. Guffin winced, clutching his chest.

“Big…”

“You arrogant fool. You’re not gods. You’re just mutations disrupting the world’s stability.”

“Maybe.”

Guffin admitted it quietly.

“We might be errors that destroy worlds. And among them… I’m the worst.”

“You know that. Then why fight? Facing Sojeonghwa’s wrath is overwhelming enough. Why push further?”

“Because I hate it.”

Guffin opened his eyes.

“I hate this cold world. It doesn’t matter anymore whether God exists or not. I will become one.”

“Don’t confuse delusion with reality. This is a perfect world. We are beings born of the law.”

“Is that what a god is?”

Ikael fell silent.

“Once you call it perfect…

Is everything over?”

From that moment, Guffin’s philosophy rejected the world’s law entirely.

“To the one who claims the name of God, to the world he created, and to all who live within it…!”

A massive hexagonal light flared into existence.

“Are you saying you can’t even feel pity?”

A tornado of light erupted as the Miracle Stream coalesced into a Dragon Fist.

Mattiel murmured, “I’m not mad. I’m mad with rage.”

At the same moment, the six archangels reversed the Miracle Stream and attacked Guffin.

The collision of ultimate concepts shook the world. Thunder split the sky.

‘I thought it was over… But still, Guffin is Guffin.’

As the battle raged evenly, Ikael opened his eyes and activated Ataraxia.

The archangel’s power surged to its peak.

“Got it!”

The six archangels converged, trapping Guffin in a zone of Movement Zero, and unleashed their strongest attack simultaneously.

‘It’s over. There’s no way to avoid it,’ Rayel thought.

“Great!”

Guffin, eyes wide, pulled the Miracle Stream toward himself.

‘This cold world…!’

Light compressed between his palms, glowing with unbearable intensity.

‘I won’t accept it!’

Brake.

“What…?!”

An unstoppable inertia surged outward. The six archangels were hurled backward in unison.

Mid-attack, none of them understood what had just happened.

“H-How…?”

Ikael, for the first time, forgot his dignity and felt pure shock.

“The planet… stopped.”

For three seconds, the world braked. Volcanoes erupted. Earthquakes tore continents. Tsunamis swallowed coasts.

‘Only one human…’

Could the universe’s laws bend if a single being willed it hard enough?

Ikael whispered, almost involuntarily.

“A god with a heart… Is that possible? A god who watches endlessly, who feels the suffering of all creatures…”

‘Under the mercy of such a god…’

Angels, humans, mountains, seas, fields, trees—

‘Nothing is insignificant.’

Guffin’s hands, blazing with Miracle Stream light, surged toward the archangels.

“I will become a god!”

Uriel raised his arms in defense—but Guffin’s eyes suddenly widened.

“Gah!”

He spat a gourd of blood, staggering, pain overwhelming him.

“Damn!”

Ankera’s wrath surged again, shaking every cell in his body.

Uriel spun the Wheel of Paradise.

“… Do not resent me.”

Guffin activated Hexa, but before it could complete, the Gon of Paradise struck his stomach.

His body shot backward like an arrow, vanishing into the distance.

“No!”

Satiel spread her wings, rushing after him.

“S-T-L!” Ikael’s voice thundered across the battlefield, freezing her in place.

“Huh!”

Above, Ikael stood with Ataraxia fully open—his presence towering, majestic.

“Satiel, I can no longer tolerate your indulgence. I command you: do not move a single step.”

The order was clear. None of the other angels moved to follow.

‘He’s never been this angry,’ Satiel thought.

Ikael turned toward the path Guffin had flown.

“I’ll finish this myself. You stop the Gaians here.”

He descended to where the trail ended, where the ground was torn apart.

Guffin leaned against a pile of rubble, blood staining his body.

“It’s pitiful, human,” Ikael said, stepping forward slowly.

“We’ve fought a long time. If I’d known it would end like this, I’d have ended it sooner.”

“Kuk… kuk… kuk…”

Guffin trembled, laughing weakly.

“How does it feel? Defeating the one who stood in your way for so long?”

“Neither good nor bad. I am a noble archangel. Humans mean nothing to me.”

“I see.”

Guffin remembered.

Ikael, angry. Annoyed. Embarrassed. And once, even smiling.

“Well, history belongs to the victors. Record what you want. But tell me one truth—was I at least handsome?”

“Is that all you have to say?” Ikael asked.

Guffin looked up at the sky. He thought of the burden he carried—one he never wanted anyone else to bear.

“Yes.”

Sparks flashed in Ikael’s eyes.

“You’re arrogant to the end. A human who nearly defeated a god… and leaves nothing behind?”

“What can I do? It fit right.”

Ikael spread his wings, sharp as a blade, and shot forward.

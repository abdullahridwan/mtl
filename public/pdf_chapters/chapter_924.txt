Chapter 924

Devastating Strike (2)

~*~

“The integrative mental system is…” Shirone’s voice trailed off, slurred.

If the world operated like a system built on procedural principles, one final truth remained to be revealed.

“Mind,” said the Chief Justice.

“Yes. Material information is based on light, but the mind forms through quantum signals. That’s what it ultimately comes down to.”

A hologram flickered into existence in the air.

“When we perceive something, a quantum signal is triggered. The world is realized only when that signal combines with information received through light.”

Shirone gazed at the hologram of the planet with detached indifference.

“This hologram isn’t real. You could zoom in and scan every inch of the planet, but in the end, it’s just an external signal.”

The Supreme Court Justice turned to him.

“The tenth sense perceives the smallest unit of all signals that constitute reality. That’s why you’re a Manager.”

“Then answer me. Why did I erase my own memory?”

“Even telling you the answer could distort your thinking. I can, if you insist. But first, understand this clearly—it was your choice. Your past self chose it, and no one else.”

Shirone hesitated. He didn’t want to dig it up.

But if it had been his past self who made the decision, he could guess the reason.

“It was probably to stop me.”

“Yes. The moment you awakened your tenth sense, two Managers came into existence. And the paths to Ultima diverged.”

“When I reach Ultima…”

“You will become a true Buddha. You’re absolutely right. That’s precisely the problem. In a world that has lost its vitality, the User can no longer exist.”

“The User? Will it cease to exist?”

“When the system reaches perfection, the biological program Argones will activate. It will annihilate all life in the universe. From the outside world’s perspective, it’s like forcibly disconnecting and abandoning the User.”

Fichot’s warning finally made sense.

“The outside world… do you believe it exists?”

“I don’t know. It’s not about belief. Terraforce is what protects this world—a kind of User protection program. Just as Argones activates when the system is perfect, we activate when evil reaches its peak.”

“Referee?”

“I know you despise the term. But strictly speaking, I’m not a referee. The essence of evil is chaos. Intelligence cannot survive in a world ruled by chaos. We don’t judge—it’s simply a way to preserve this world.”

Shirone understood why the Supreme Court Justice had come.

“It means the end is near.”

“Exactly. If Gong wins, the universe closes. If evil wins, humanity perishes. Neither outcome is acceptable. And from Yahweh’s perspective, neither is welcome.”

“I’ll fight. I’ll stop Havitz. To do that…”

“You must reach the Ultima System before anyone else.”

“Terraforce hopes for the same. But it won’t be easy—integrating the minds of all Users.”

The Justice turned away.

“Ultima becomes perfect when the User’s mind and all its signals are unified. That’s how Gaiain escaped Ang Kera, the Manager. And it will be the only way you can fully subdue me.”

Shirone pondered the words of the Supreme Court Justice.

“Prepare yourself, Shirone. The end is coming. On Judgment Day, everything will be revealed.”

As the Justice activated his engine, the steel wall of the common room slid aside, revealing a full-length window.

Beyond it, an ivory tower rose from the center of the Arctic, encased in ice.

“Big…”

~*~

In a cave on the Central Continent, Gaold groaned in agony.

“I’ve been stranded here for three days already.”

Kang Nan’s words meant Gaold had endured three days of unbearable torment.

“Aaaaaaa!”

His scream shook the cave violently.

“Gaold!” Kang Nan took a step forward, but Zulu grabbed his shoulder and shook his head.

“It’s dangerous. There’s nothing we can do to help.”

Gaold was likely in a state of complete mental collapse. If the air press activated even slightly, he’d die instantly.

“But—”

“I’ll go check.”

As Miro stepped forward, Kang Nan glared at her and snapped.

“Don’t you realize going in would only make it worse? Why won’t you just leave?”

Why?

Gaold had treated Miro worse than a stray dog in the labyrinth.

‘You bastard!’

The pain wouldn’t be unbearable—she could endure it.

But in that hellish agony, how deeply must Miro hate him?

‘Endure until the end!’

Selfish thoughts.

But after such a fall, wouldn’t even his flawless spirit crack?

‘Because of you, even I—what is this?!’

He clung on with selfish resolve, but if his mind deteriorated further, even the spirit of the Thousand-Armed Guanyin, who contemplated all things, would shatter.

“I’ll give you one blow!” Kang Nan shouted at Miro.

“I told you to wait! You could die! Don’t you understand Gaold’s power?”

Miro turned, her eyes cold.

“Do you really think I’m like you? That kind of man—I could beat him right now.”

“Keugh!”

Kang Nan had no reply. Miro was the only one who had followed Gaold into apostasy.

“Kuaaaaa!”

Gaold’s scream echoed through the cave again.

“I’m going crazy from the noise!”

Miro rolled up her sleeves and strode forward. At the back of the cave, she saw Autumn curled up on the ground.

“Hey! Have you been like this for days? And you call yourself someone who protects the world? Weak—so weak you fall for it!”

“Go away.”

A hoarse voice echoed through the cave.

“Get out of my sight! Disappear from the world!”

After shouting, Gaold vomited a torrent of heavy snow he couldn’t keep down.

Miro clenched her fists, biting her lip as she absorbed every word.

“Sigh… Sigh…”

Gaold exhaled roughly, each breath like swallowing razors.

“Are you done? Feeling a little better? Was holding it in just so you could torment me later?”

“Go away.”

Gaold’s dying voice tore at Miro.

“Don’t misunderstand. I didn’t follow you because I wanted to. I want to see this through to the end. And now I’ve decided—let’s settle this.”

“Fine. Then please go…”

“Hey! You really—!”

Miro, eyes blazing, lunged forward, fist raised.

“Even if I leave, I’m hitting you once!”

She lit the area with flash magic and reached for Gaold’s collar.

But she froze mid-motion.

“Huhhh…”

Even light seemed painful. Autumn’s withered body trembled where he crouched.

“…Hey.”

He was no longer recognizable as human.

“You idiot.”

Miro’s face crumpled. Tears streamed down her cheeks, uncontrolled.

“What the hell is this?”

Gaold lowered his head, hiding his face. His pitiful state only deepened the sorrow.

‘I’d rather die…’

Even death felt like a mercy.

“Why are you so stupid?” Only bones remained. Miro pulled Gaold into her arms, pressing her cheek to his shoulder.

“Huh! Whoa!”

The trembling of the labyrinth pulsed through her skin. Gaold clamped his mouth shut.

“Ahh! Aaaaaang!” For the first time in her life, she fully unleashed her emotions—and couldn’t hold back her cries.

Humanity’s greatest seeker.

The spirit of the Superman, who tolerated no flaw, now bore a massive crack.

~*~

That evening.

“After… after…”

Shivering under a blanket by the campfire, Autumn stared blankly. Kang Nan held out a spoon.

“Here. Eat.”

Gaold’s mouth opened slowly. Kang Nan guided the spoon and fed him carefully.

Miro sat across, watching between her knees, her expression cooling.

“Red. It looks good. Then I’ll be fine.”

“What does it matter?” Kang Nan replied indifferently, stirring the food on the plate.

‘Jealousy?’

Miro felt an honest pang—but it was ironic, feeling jealous of the world itself.

Gaold looked up.

“It’s a maze.”

For the first time since leaving the cave, Miro stood, her colors brightening.

“Why? Should I feed you? After all, am I not better?”

“Stop it.”

Miro narrowed her eyes.

“Is that sound again? It’s my choice to leave—”

“You’re in danger.”

Thinking Gaold had caught on, she tightened her lips.

“You’re with me. You can’t fight if your mental state isn’t stable. You want to save the world. Go back to your seat.”

“What is my seat?”

“The pole. Human emotions don’t suit you. If you fixate on one thing, you can’t see the whole.”

One reason the incarnation of the Labyrinth, the Thousand-Armed Guanyin, was so powerful was the absence of blind spots. She saw the world from every direction, planning attacks in any situation.

Miro slumped back.

“I do my own thing.” If her heart was like this, if it could be undone so easily, it would’ve happened long ago.

“If you can’t,” Gaold said, raising a finger.

A terrifying breath of life surged. Compressed air gathered at his fingertip.

Kang Nan swallowed hard at the power—so intense the air trembled. Miro glared at the fingertip.

‘Gaold, I’ll really kill you.’ If the Thousand-Armed Guanyin’s form was used properly, even an air gun could be avoided.

But the fact that she even considered it meant she’d already killed Gaold in her heart.

“You.”

As Miro opened her mouth, screams echoed from the cliff where the party had taken cover.

“Keeeh!”

They were still far from the core battlefield, but demons swarmed across the Central Continent.

“Sheesh! Move!”

Kang Nan snapped to attention. Gaold, Zulu, and Miro scattered in all directions.

Demons poured down the cliff like a waterfall. Those who could fly darkened the sky.

‘Air gun!’

Gaold pivoted, redirecting the shot he’d aimed at Miro toward the cliff.

Aaaaaaaang!

The cliff shattered in a single blast—but demons surged through the smoke without pause.

‘Over 100,000.’

They were clearly marching toward something.

“Fight!”

Zulu’s summoned cougar leapt in as Gaold’s air gun fired relentlessly.

“Aaaaaang!”

Kang Nan, guarding Gaold’s flank, crushed the demons that slipped through with his fists.

“This position is no good! Fall back and regroup!”

They were too close to allies to use Pagye’s air press without causing collateral damage.

“A labyrinth?”

Zulu asked, stepping back beside Gaold.

Only then did Kang Nan notice. He looked around, eyes wide, and pointed.

“There—over there!”

Above the demons’ heads, the Thousand-Armed Guanyin’s incarnation wavered, unsteady.

“Come on!”

Miro’s incarnation attacked, but the power was so overwhelming it seemed almost reckless.

“Puhahaha! It tickles!”

“That’s all you’ve got??????!”

As she turned her head, a massive club swung down in front of her.

‘I missed it!’

A square barrier formed.

“Die!”

Just before the blow crushed Miro, afterimages flashed in, shielding her body.

Wow!

The air shield exploded in a shockwave. Miro looked up, stunned.

“Go, Autumn?”

Relief flickered in Gaold’s eyes as he surveyed the labyrinth.

“Guffin, I’ve made up my mind.”

“Twenty minutes until the enemy enters!”

At the messenger’s report, the Tormia allied forces at their positions tensed.

Two kilometers away, in the ‘Flower Field,’ the Fire and Fairy tribes waited for orders.

Three hundred fire tribesmen and six hundred elves stood in quiet harmony—a scene that looked like nature itself.

Lian and Tess arrived at the field, searching for Enox, the garrison commander.

Tess grumbled.

“Why are we stuck here? I can fight just fine.”

“It’s a strategic decision. Victory isn’t the goal. We’re here to lure the demon army. The Flower Field must be defended.”

“I know that! But why us? Battles are about life and death!”

It was maddening to watch from two kilometers away while comrades fought and died.

“Do you really think so?”

Tess turned at Lian’s chilling tone.

“Natasha?”

Gustav’s four skills—Battle Doll Natasha.

She was a fearsome opponent. Even in their recent sword duel, the outcome had been uncertain.

‘No—truthfully, Lian seemed to have the upper hand.’

“I won’t lose like last time, but she’s still dangerous. If we don’t stop Natasha, real hell will descend.”

“You’re saying Natasha’s strength exceeds the Flower Field’s?”

This time, even Lian stayed silent.

“It’s about role. If the Flower Field is firepower, Natasha is speed.”

There was no need to spread panic before battle. The conversation ended as they reached their destination.

“Hello.”

As Lian approached, Protea and Enox, standing side by side at the front, turned in unison.

“Oh? A famous one has arrived.”

Enox’s eyes sparkled with welcome. Protea shrank back.

‘What is this human?’

The Hwajok’s direct lineage—the Lotus bloodline—was known for passivity. Except for the meat-eating Armand, they were considered the weakest.

But the moment Protea met Lian’s gaze, that passivity seemed to stretch to its limit.

“Ogent Lian.”

Enox stepped forward, poking Lian in the chest with his index finger.

“The world’s greatest swordsman. Said to be the strongest among humans. Mach’s Knight. Right?”

Lian briefly wondered how Quan would react to that title, then dismissed the thought.

“Thank you for the recognition. As garrison commander, I respect Enox-sama. But I—”

“I know. Special mission. I’ve heard there are fools in your camp too?”

Enox glanced around, then shrugged.

“What of it? If they exist, they’re human. I can’t compare to Lian-sama, who carries ideas. Besides, I’m the finest swordsman among the fairies.”

As Enox stepped back, his sheathed sword clicked sharply.

Though of the same tribe as Tess, his martial prowess was on par with Prince Lee’s elite.

‘Strong, certainly. But I’ve never heard of fairies fighting in battle.’

Still, he’d find out soon enough. Lian bowed and turned to leave.

“Then. Good luck.”

“You as well, Mr. Lian.”

When the two returned to their posts, Protea, who had been holding her breath, finally spoke.

“Enox, you’re a wind nymph.”

Elves, like fairies, were born with innate attributes.

But theirs wasn’t natural—it was an ancient Norse gene, passed down through generations.

“Why are you so kind to humans? They trample flowers and destroy forests.”

Fire tribes couldn’t oppose humans due to their passive nature, but fairies were different.

They were physically stronger than humans, and their clear, pure minds granted unmatched concentration.

“Huh. I like humans. And strictly speaking, the humans from back then weren’t like today’s.”

“Back then?”

“A people with a divine perspective.”

When Protea looked confused, Enox recalled elven tradition.

“Long ago, in an age when gods and humans coexisted, there was the Fairy race and the Nor race. They were often at odds, but sometimes not. Like fairies, the Nors cultivated mental civilization. They knew many things that fascinated the fairies. But most importantly—they carried divine blood.”

“Divine blood? So their ancestors created nature? As far as I know, Ankera—”

“There *is* no nature.”

Protea fell silent.

“No forests. No seas. No rivers. This world consists only of God’s perspective. Call it Idea.”

Ideas also appeared in Hwa tribe legends.

“Lian is a being of ideas. He’s a human who bends the world’s laws from a divine viewpoint. How extraordinary is that?” Enox’s eyes gleamed.

“Perhaps my feelings now mirror those of the ancestral fairies? They sought ideas from the Nor. The Nor sought to evolve through the fairies. Tradition calls it a ‘user battle.’ That’s how the elven race was born.”

“There were great difficulties. The fairy body was too small.”

“If a fairy bore a Nor’s child, her stomach would burst.”

“One day, a man with a divine perspective appeared. He solved everything. No records remain, but since he had ideas, he must have been human. That’s when the fairies split—those who followed Ankera, and those who followed humans.”

Unlike the fire tribe, the elves’ history was turbulent. But perhaps their roots were the same.

“Mind.”

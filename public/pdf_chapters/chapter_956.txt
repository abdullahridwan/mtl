Chapter 956

Even if it’s an illusion (1)

~*~

A magic trick that transforms humans into giants using a black elixir—the magic of the Anecdote.

Its first purpose was to dismantle the Ultima System, which had challenged the authority of God.

Because of the nature of the technique, progress depended on the quantity of experiments rather than their quality. Under Kariel’s leadership, the number of Gaians had dwindled for a long time.

“Move quickly! This is a sacred ritual! Wash away the sins of the law and be reborn into a new life!” The Maras urged them forward, but the Gaians walked on steadily, neither slow nor hurried.

None of the Gaians had produced offspring. Now, only a hundred remained.

From this day forward, the Anecdote would pass through a new human race—Subjects.

A truly historic day.

Ikael watched from a distance as the Gaians approached the bronze giant.

There was no resistance. No resentment.

I know why.

The more they submit to Guffin’s will, the clearer it becomes who is truly close to God.

‘But it’s all in vain.’

Ikael’s asteroid pulsed with a dry, cold light.

‘I am the only one in the universe who knows about the meeting between Ankera and Guffin.’

As long as she remained silent, no one would ever know. And if no one knew, then it was as if it had never happened.

‘Foolish humans. Why can’t you see that this struggle is meaningless?’

Satiel shouted from the center of the plaza.

“Stop drinking the Ilhwa! You’ve done enough! Please, let us continue as long as even one Gaian remains!”

Giants, fairies, and Subjects appeared. The ranks of Heaven shifted, and archangels pierced the sky with their presence—but no one listened to her.

It wasn’t that they ignored her. Their ears had already been sealed. Her voice was automatically muted.

‘It’s fine. Do you still believe in humans?’ Ikael had never intervened during the Anecdote ritual before. She had expected nothing different today.

‘Just a hundred humans vanish. Five giants are born.’

Giants, stripped of their ego, would return to Jotunheim under a new law.

‘With this, Gaia’s history will vanish forever.’

Forever.

The word sent a jolt through Ikael, as if a spell had been cast upon it.

‘Vanish?’

All Gaians who had known Guffin disappeared from Heaven.

The moment the thought struck—*I will never be able to ask about Guffin again*—it lodged deep in her mind.

“Aren’t you afraid?”

Before she realized it, Ikael was speaking to the Subjects of the Anecdote.

“Chief Angel!”

The Maras froze, stunned by something that had never happened in the history of Heaven.

They quickly stepped aside, but Ikael walked past them without a glance and approached a Gaian.

“You know what becoming a giant means, don’t you? Your mind will be crushed, erased completely. A blank page. And on it, a new law will be written.” Her voice was eerie, but the Gaian showed no change in expression.

That only made her angrier.

“Say something! Isn’t this terrible? Aren’t you afraid? Or resentful? What are you—”

An old man interrupted her.

“The weather’s nice. On a day like this, I should hang the laundry.”

Ikael’s eyes widened at the absurdity coming from a man about to die.

“What did you say?”

“What do you mean?” the old man repeated, turning to her.

“I was just answering nonsense with nonsense.”

Ikael’s lips trembled as she grasped his meaning.

“Are you talking about the heart? Fine. I’ll tell you. The heart is an illusion. A mistake. Guffin is a fraud. Can you tell me where he is? What he’s doing?”

The old man clicked his tongue.

“You’re out of your mind.”

Life flared from Ikael.

“What? How dare a mere human insult an archangel?”

But the old man bowed his head meekly, as if dealing with an inconvenience, and quickened his pace.

“Sorry. My mistake.”

“Wait!”

Ikael grabbed his foot.

“This cannot be ignored! You deserve punishment beyond the Anecdote!”

The old man sighed.

“What exactly do you want from me?”

Ikael’s eyes trembled. She said nothing.

“Curious about Guffin? Fine. I’ll tell you. As you said—he’s a trickster. Are you satisfied now?”

Ikael stayed silent.

“Or should I say this? Guffin isn’t a fraud. He never was. Does that make you feel better?”

She had no reply.

“Stop playing with words. Do your duty. Wake up, archangel.”

The insult no longer registered in her mind.

“Guffin… to me…”

“No, no.”

The old man shook his head sadly.

“Wrong. To me, Guffin is what I call *Guffin*.”

“No…”

Ikael’s voice shook.

“I didn’t say anything. You said you’d leave like this, but you never said a word to me.”

The old man looked at her with deep, knowing eyes.

“Have you ever spoken to him?”

“That’s… I…”

Before Ikael could answer, the old man continued.

“Yes, there are reasons not to speak. Endless reasons. Complicated circumstances. Fear of not getting the answer you want. Or maybe… it was simply too hard to speak, for all those reasons.”

He paused, lost in thought.

“Wouldn’t Guffin feel the same?”

Ikael’s soul seemed to drain from her body. She opened her mouth, dazed.

“Why do you think only you are suffering? Why don’t you believe others exist too? That’s the essence of the Ultima System.”

The old man placed a hand on his chest.

“It means others have hearts just like mine. So the thing that’s hard for you to do…”

He smiled sadly and shook his head.

“It’s hard even for Guffin.”

Ikael’s entire body trembled. She couldn’t stop the shaking in her arms and shoulders.

“That’s why I don’t call it throwing the heart. The heart is cast out. It might come back. Or it might not. So yes, as you said—it might be an illusion. But…”

The old man spat the words as he rejoined the procession.

“Are you *cool inside*?”

Ikael stood frozen, absorbing his words.

After a long silence, she reached out.

“Ah… there…”

“Don’t step out,” the old man said firmly.

“This is my choice. Whatever Guffin did, I judged it. I chose this.”

Gaians know what is right.

“You’re a fraud,” Ikael whispered.

The old man, now standing before the bronze statue, lifted his head and laughed.

“Puhahaha! What a joke to hear before I go!”

He laughed until the black elixir filled the glass globe before him.

In Ikael’s eyes, it was the heart of a man choosing death without a single doubt.

Like Ikael’s heart, the moon waxed and waned countless times.

Back in her dorm, Ikael crouched on the floor, shoulders shaking, tears flowing endlessly.

“Huh!”

Fear.

She imagined being crushed between two immense laws tearing the world apart.

“God… what should I do?”

You have to throw your heart out.

And the price might be losing everything—even herself.

*Are you cool inside?*

The old man’s words were strangely comforting. Yet, she couldn’t think of a better way to describe it.

It seemed insane.

How much time passed? She didn’t know. Many archangels came, reporting decisions made by Baekkyung.

But Ikael, her mind torn between choices, could barely remember any of it.

“Huh!”

Then, one day, a sudden conviction surged through her, as if someone else had planted the thought.

“…Guffin.”

When she realized what she’d said, she was already flying toward the sanctuary of Arabot.

*Open the door!*

She tore the door open—not because she feared death, but because breaking her own will was worse.

“Ikael? What’s wrong?”

Ankera raised her enormous eyes, startled.

But Ikael’s gaze locked onto the floating form of Guffin.

“Iced coffee…”

Tears welled in her eyes.

“Answer me, Ikael. I never allowed you to enter here.”

The moment she spoke to Ankera, she knew—her future would change.

Ikael lunged forward and severed the neural link between Ankera and Guffin.

Blood stained Ankera’s eyes.

“You…!”

A consequence outside the law.

The first physical attack ever inflicted on an absolute being sent Ikael’s mind reeling.

“Ikael!”

“Huh!”

Gritting her teeth against the sanctuary-shattering noise, she fled Arabot at full speed.

*Farther! Further!*

There was no place in the universe where Ankera’s gaze couldn’t reach. But Ikael wasn’t searching for distance. She sought peace.

She found a mountain range rising 6,000 meters into the sky, dove down, and entered a hidden cave.

As starlight pierced the darkness and illuminated Guffin lying on the ground, his eyes slowly focused.

“Guffin!”

Her voice was desperate, raw—the voice of someone who had risked everything to reclaim him. But Guffin didn’t wake easily.

It was only natural. He had been under Ankera’s control for countless years.

“Where… am I?”

At the sound of his voice, Ikael rushed to support him.

“Are you insane? It’s me—*Ikael*. Do you understand?”

Guffin’s half-open eyes traced her face.

“…Ikael?”

She barely held back her tears.

“Yes.”

“Why are you… why me…”

His words cut into her heart like thorns.

She wanted to ask—why he never told the truth, what his deal with Lucifer was, why that kiss meant nothing.

“Guffin…?”

She gently stroked his cheek.

“Buy…?”

Tears of light streamed down her face as she let out a soft sound.

“Love.”

Throw your heart.

“I don’t care what you were thinking. I love you. I want to connect with you. I can’t exist without you.”

It might not come back.

Ikael lowered her head, eyes closed, as if she had already accepted defeat.

“Hmm.”

When she looked up again, Guffin was smiling faintly.

“Out of 100 points…”

Even half-conscious, his voice was clear.

“It’s 100 points.”

Ikael blushed, then smiled bitterly, pride warring with emotion.

“Is that your answer?”

Guffin slowly shook his head.

“No. That’s not polite.”

Then, struggling to rise, he leaned close and whispered into her ear.

“I love you too.”

Electricity flashed in Ikael’s eyes.

“I can’t say how long I’ve felt this. But I’ve wanted to connect with you—*so badly*.”

Ikael, who had been crying, finally broke down, unable to contain herself.

“Iced coffee… Ahhh…”

What connects heart and mind?

*How wonderful.* Ankera, my God.

*I’m sorry.*

Even if the mind defies the law, even if all of this is an illusion.

*I have this misunderstanding…*

A smile, pure as light, bloomed on Ikael’s face as she embraced Guffin.

*It’s so good.*

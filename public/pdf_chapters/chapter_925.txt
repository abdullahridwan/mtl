Devastating Strike (3)

~*~

Iruki pointed at the map.

“Abyssal Cliff.”

It was a 160-kilometer-long valley running along the border between Tormia and Kazura. Though geographically just a valley, its cliffs spanned over 17 kilometers wide—vast, imposing terrain.

“It’s easy to assume it’s a prime spot for ambushes, but the Balkans will come here. It’s the shortest route into Tormia. And after losing half our strength on the Kahnian Plateau—damage sustained in a single day’s battle—we have no other viable path.”

He paused, then pushed aside the thought that had surfaced.

‘And Amy…’

He erased it. Now wasn’t the time.

“But even with our weakened state, this could strike a vital blow. Still, setting a trap across such a massive area with our limited Valkyrie forces seems impossible.”

“It might not even exist.”

Iruki nodded. “There are no physical traps. Only elemental bombs capable of obliterating entire valleys.”

A murmur rippled through the room.

“Whoa.”

Since this strategy could end the war, tension among the commanders peaked.

“I’ll stop here.” Iruki placed the baton at the center of the valley, 70 kilometers from Tormia. “An elemental bomb’s power is unimaginable. The terrain will absorb some impact, but our allies—and nearby cities—won’t escape unscathed.”

The commander spoke grimly. “This opportunity came at a terrible cost. We have no choice but to carry it out. All we can do is detonate it where collateral damage is minimized.”

Iruki remained silent.

“Sir General?”

“Yes. I don’t intend to dwell on guilt. The operation proceeds as planned. The day of reckoning has arrived. There must be no mistakes.”

With those final words, the commanders left the room, their expressions easing slightly.

Aromi approached Iruki.

“Are you struggling with the decision?”

He wasn’t the kind to hesitate, so seeing him like this unsettled her.

“No. It’s not that.”

“Then… is it about Major Amy?”

He couldn’t deny it.

‘I couldn’t recover her body.’

If he’d been certain of her fate, he wouldn’t be tormented like this.

‘If she survived, she’d have returned. Was she captured by the demons?’

With the full army of Hell descending, the thought hit like a bomb.

“Sir, it’s hard to say this, but…” Aromi hesitated. “If Major Amy is alive and in their hands… killing her might be the mercy she needs.”

She knew how cruel the demons were. And Iruki, deep down, agreed.

“The reason Balkan failed to account for Kitaru Man…” Iruki finally spoke. “Was because his perspective was limited. You can read the flow of events on the battlefield, but not what lies beyond it. You can’t anticipate the unthinkable.”

“Yes, but why bring that up now?”

“Because I’m missing something.”

The thought gnawed at him.

“If Amy is captured, they’ll use her. Vulcan would exploit it. There’s no better way to break me.”

“Hmm.”

“But there’s been no signal. No psychological warfare. No taunts. That suggests she wasn’t captured.”

Aromi wanted to offer hope, but this wasn’t the time for sentiment.

“Sir General, it’s hard to reason through this…”

“I know.”

Even Iruki understood.

“Even if she is in their hands, there are a hundred reasons they wouldn’t reveal it. Shirone… she could be a pawn for Yahweh. A stepping stone. So any hope I feel might just be delusion.”

His mind wouldn’t quiet.

“It keeps echoing. No matter how I judge it—by experience, by logic—it’s not clear.”

Aromi realized Iruki was in a cold, detached state.

“It means something is happening beyond your calculations, Commander-in-Chief.”

“Yes.”

Iruki stared at the valley on the map.

“Just like with Kitaru Man. That difference could decide whether this strategy succeeds or fails.”

Where the hell is Amy?

* * *

“Whoa. Whoa.”

Amy’s harsh breathing echoed in the small mountain hut on the Central Continent.

Her hair was short now, and bandages covered her burned body. The heat hadn’t penetrated deeply, and the wounds were healing—scabs would fall off in time.

“She hasn’t shown any sign of waking,” Shura said, watching Amy lie motionless on the bed.

“Her body is recovering, but the real problem is her mind. This isn’t samadhi. It’s entirely different.”

Samadhi was a state granted by cosmic providence. Amy’s condition was artificial—man-made.

“It’s like she buried herself and sealed the exit. She can’t climb out on her own.”

Nane bit her index finger, thinking.

“Remove the bandages.”

“What are you going to do?”

“Right now, she’s shut down—eyes closed, ears blocked, all senses cut off. No external voice can reach her.”

As Shura unwrapped the bandages on Amy’s abdomen, fresh flesh swelled beneath, skin still red and raw.

Nane drew a circle of bloody script around her navel, then sat cross-legged.

“Get as far away as possible. You won’t want to be here.”

To bring Amy back, a sermon from a rogue saint was needed—one that could awaken even stone.

“Don’t push yourself too hard.”

Shura stepped out of the cabin. Nane smiled gently, then closed her eyes.

Her lips parted.

“Mahavanya…”

The sermon, rhythmic and endless, began to resonate.

Outside, Shura sprinted into the forest, clutching her ears.

‘It’s not sound.’

Nane’s voice bypassed the ears, piercing directly into the mind.

“Color immediately ball. Instant color…”

Inside the cabin, Amy’s body burned as if engulfed in flames.

“Sigh… sigh…”

Even unconscious, a thin, strained breath escaped her lips.

“Shirone…”

* * *

Earth Temple, Ivory Tower.

The five Great Stars had gathered at Taeseong’s summons. Shirone finished recounting what he knew.

“Argones.”

The word hung in the air.

“So what you’re saying is—whether I win or Havits wins—the world ends anyway?”

Fried scowled. “Even if the Ivory Tower seems passive, it’s a wellspring of power. Do you really think we’ll just sit still?”

Silence followed.

“Hey. What are you all doing? Aren’t you afraid?” Minerva demanded.

“It doesn’t matter if you fight or sleep. The underlying system will activate regardless. The only way to stop it is to end this before it begins.” She turned to Shirone. “You have to establish an integrated mental system—before Nane does.”

No one disagreed.

And for the same reason, none of the Great Stars had told Shirone the truth.

‘Amy has disappeared.’

If even Taeseong couldn’t locate her, then someone—or something—on his level had taken her.

‘It could be me. Or it could be Harvits.’

Both were possible.

‘I can’t reveal it now.’

Thing spoke.

“Shirone. Before we prepare for the end, there’s something you don’t know.”

Minerva turned pale.

‘Damn it. That stubborn man.’

Xing, who believed everyone but himself was illusion, saw no reason to lie.

“What don’t I know?”

Thing turned to him.

“In the Central Continent…”

At that moment, the Earth Temple trembled.

“What?”

The temple floated in space—there was no reason for it to shake.

Taeseong’s expression darkened. “You’re finally here.”

In the glass window reflecting the planet, something blazed through the atmosphere, glowing from air friction.

“A dragon?”

It was awe-inspiring—rising vertically, blue scales crackling with electricity.

Shirone recognized it instantly.

“Pterosaur Brain Blitz.”

One of the Twelve Dragons who guarded time. The same creature Andre had encountered in World No. 1. One of the few first-class dragons capable of leaving the planet and entering space.

Woo woo woo!

Blitz slammed his massive body against the temple’s ceiling, then bent his long neck to peer inside.

“Hey, Taeseong.”

A cold voice echoed directly into their minds, bypassing the air.

Taeseong looked up. “Apostle of Time. Why have you come here?”

Blitz’s enormous eyes swept the chamber, then locked onto Shirone.

“Our core seeks you.”

“If it’s the core…” Shirone murmured.

Mudeung Dragon Karatorsa.

“You mean it’s awakened from eternal sleep?” The legendary dragon had once declared, “I will handle it,” when all others failed.

“Something big is coming. It’s only a matter of time before Mudeungryong rises. Shirone—the one who opened the senses of nothingness—follow me. The core will tell you.”

Shirone turned to Taeseong. “What do you want me to say?”

“How to fight.”

“Who are we fighting?”

Blitz turned his head, gazing down at the planet’s northern hemisphere. His eyes settled on the Eastern Jungcheon-dong.

“Heaven.”

* * *

A peak piercing the clouds.

On the highest hill in eastern Jungcheon-dong stood a shrine built by humanity’s strongest wizard.

Guffin’s Gate.

High in the sky, a massive iron sphere—inscribed with henna-like script on an angel’s tongue—began to vibrate.

The sphere wobbled like liquid, then exploded violently, tearing apart the mountain peaks.

Aaaaaaaaagh!

Where the explosion had been, a colossal tunnel now gaped—wide enough to swallow the entire Central Valley.

“Is this the Bureau?”

From within the black sphere, feet suddenly emerged, stomping onto the ground.

Whoops!

A small garden burst open beneath them.

“Cheuk, it’s so small.”

Giants poured out, and fairies soared into the sky.

Angels and their servants, the Maras, passed arrogantly through the tunnel. Then, finally, four archangels appeared.

Though they were half-entities, stripped of the concepts they once embodied, their mere presence shook the world.

Uriel, Archangel of Destruction, spoke first. “You’ve arrived at last. Took longer than expected.”

Rayel, Archangel of Light, added, “It could’ve taken even longer. Yes… that’s strange. The dimensional barrier was pierced too easily.”

Uriel muttered, “Maze??????”

What had happened to the world’s strongest monk—the one who had once blocked Heaven’s army alone?

The giants and Maras, eager for this day, roared in speculation.

“Let’s fight! Let’s punish those who deny God!”

Ikael watched silently.

‘Shirone.’

The only reason he didn’t share their fervor was because Hexa was here.

“Chief Angel! Order the sortie! Let Mimul teach them the dignity of God!”

Ikael opened his mouth, quiet but firm.

“…Ataraxia.”

Her asteroid expanded at tremendous speed, radiating a halo of brilliant colors.

“Huh!”

In the sudden, unnatural silence.

“It reaches the entire army.”

Only her voice echoed, amplified across the void.

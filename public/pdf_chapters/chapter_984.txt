The Right Choice (2)

“What?”

The Balkan commander delivered an unbelievable report.

‘The vanguard’s trajectory has changed.’

In an instant, the enemy force had pivoted at a sharp angle. Moments later, a messenger came sprinting back, reversing the army’s march.

“Urgent news!”

Shirone didn’t even need to hear it.

“Ho-ho-ho! What? Did you fail after all? Humans are always like this. The military is the worst.”

He ignored Paimon’s mocking laughter.

*No. It can’t be.*

Alongside Jetaro, Sumodo, and Natasha, even Balkan had been overwhelmed by the enemy.

“… … Turn again.”

Vulcan, having made his decision, opened his eyes.

“You can’t go to Baska. There must be a reason they’re this desperate.”

Paimon grew tired of Vulcan’s defiance.

“So what is it?”

“I don’t know. But you’re right—something unimaginable is happening. Order the entire army! Turn now—Kuck!”

A brutal impact slammed into the back of Vulcan’s head. His vision blurred and spun.

“You’re talking nonsense.”

As Vulcan twisted toward the sound of Paimon’s voice, her claws lashed out again.

“Huh!”

He leaned back and swung his sword from horseback, but the distance was off.

“Ho-ho-ho! I do have one trick, though. But can you handle a lethal dose of poison?”

The moment the words left her mouth, Vulcan staggered. “Damn it! Betrayal…!”

Using a magical schema to regulate his blood flow, he slowed the poison’s spread—but it wouldn’t last long.

“For you to do this… Doesn’t that mean Havitz has betrayed you too?”

With his last strength, Vulcan tried to warn her. Paimon only laughed.

“What are you talking about? Everything is His will.”

“What?”

As his spinal nerves paralyzed, Vulcan collapsed onto his horse’s back. Paimon stepped forward and lifted his head.

“It means Satan is done with you. He doesn’t keep toys he’s no longer interested in.”

“Havitz… …”

Still unable to accept the truth, Vulcan’s eyelids fluttered shut.

“Heh. Weak things.”

Paimon dropped his head and turned. The demons, who had been restless, now approached eagerly.

“Shall I kill him? Or should we show the fear of demons?”

“Hmm.”

From the messenger’s report, Paimon had learned that Havitz had completely lost his humanity.

*That doesn’t mean he’s not still Havitz.*

She gave her orders.

“Imprison him for now. It’d be nice to see those four idiots locked up side by side.”

“All right. But what about Natasha? If she comes to her senses, it’ll be trouble.”

Her power was on a level that even demons feared.

“Whoops, no problem.”

Paimon’s eyes burned with purple light.

“Eating out, van.”

It was an ability that created a quarantine zone in a specific space, stripping those trapped inside of mobility.

“Take him.”

Demons carried Vulcan to the rear. Paimon mounted his horse.

“Whoa. Looks like you’ve lost a tooth.” Everyone felt the same.

“Paimon, lead us. We want blood. We want human suffering.”

“Ho-ho-ho!”

Paimon raised her hand.

“Disregard formations from now on. Scatter! Find the shortest path and converge on Baska!”

“Quaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa!”

The demons’ roar shook the air.

“Let’s go! It’s a bloody festival!”

Like cockroaches scattering from rotting food under light, the armies of Hell spread in all directions.

The temple command center erupted into chaos.

“Asmodians are entering Baska! 300 confirmed so far—no, 4.5 million…”

Iruki raised his hand, silencing the report.

*This is just the beginning.*

With the front garden of Baska breached, it was only a matter of time before the full 100 million-strong army arrived.

“Call Lucky Boy.”

The time had come.

“All right.”

The commanders finally grasped the reality and left the room with terrified expressions.

In the now-empty chamber, Iruki sat motionless, eyes closed and opening in quiet rhythm.

“Iruki.”

Nade entered, his expression bright, but Iruki couldn’t bring himself to look up.

*It’s miserable.*

He hated himself for dragging his friends into a mission that would leave a stain on history.

“I heard. The army of Hell is coming to Baska. We finally have a chance to turn things around.”

He tried to speak encouragingly.

“Sorry.”

Nade’s voice was soft.

“?????? What?”

“When you came to do this for me, I stayed silent. Because this strategy allows no second chances. So I kept quiet. My family, Mr. Liz… I wanted everything to be perfect, even though I knew what you had to protect.”

There were many skilled technicians in the temple.

But Iruki needed someone he could trust to take full responsibility, no matter what.

“If you’d told me to go back, I would have.”

Iruki finally looked at Nade.

“I’d have gone home to protect my parents and Liz—the people most precious to me. But…”

Nade’s eyes tightened.

“I’ll never see you again. Because to you, I’m only *that* important.”

“Nade… …”

“You’re not the only one worried. I thought about countless things on my way here. But there’s only one conclusion. If it’s not me, you can’t trust this to anyone else.”

Iruki lowered his head.

“What if the aircraft fails? What if the detonator doesn’t work? Could there be unpredictable variables? What if the carrier changes his mind? Should I have given up before even trying my best?”

Sighs. Heavy breathing. Silence.

“But if it’s me—if it’s Nade—I’ll make it through, no matter how bad it gets. Did you think of that?”

Iruki had no answer.

Tears welled up, but he held them back, his body trembling with each breath.

“When you stayed silent, I was honestly relieved. If you’d told me to go back, I’d have died of embarrassment. Ha ha ha!”

“Ku-k-k-k-k…”

Iruki burst into laughter, tears streaming down his face.

“Don’t worry. You’ve suffered enough. Now leave everything to me.”

With those words, a thousand tons of weight lifted from Iruki’s shoulders.

“Nade.”

Nade, already halfway to the door, stopped and turned.

“Yes?”

“Succeed. Unconditionally.”

Nade smiled and raised his thumb—a gesture carrying the weight of humanity.

“No problem.”

He opened the door.

“I look lucky.”

The allied forces, having escaped the initial demon surge, watched tensely.

“It’s okay. They don’t seem to be turning back.”

They didn’t know Paimon had already ordered a full convergence on the capital.

Dante spoke.

“Shouldn’t we pull back? If the operation succeeds, this area won’t be safe either.”

They were well within the elemental bomb’s blast radius.

Evacuating Baska’s citizens had been painful, but necessary.

“Let’s go.”

Miro said it as if taking responsibility.

“The feeling’s the same for all of us. No one’s going to praise you for staying. Let’s move.”

Amy frowned.

“Where are we going? If the demons advance on Baska, shouldn’t we follow?”

Dante licked his lips.

“Come to think of it, I’m a corps commander now. You don’t know about the operation yet.”

Amy pressed.

“Why do you all look like that? What aren’t you telling me?”

It was impossible to proceed without involving a mage of her caliber.

“To Baska… …”

The moment Lufist spoke, Miro felt a chilling aura and turned.

“That one?”

A force surrounded in light soared over the horizon.

“Army of Heaven.”

Angels and Maras flew swiftly, followed by fairies carrying a giant through the air.

“Huh? Uh?”

When the giants crashed down from a kilometer high, the allied forces felt dizzy just watching.

They rose slowly, kicking up clouds of dust on the dry earth.

*Big.*

The average height was around eight meters—but the largest among them neared eighty.

“Is this where you start?”

As Ymir strode forward, cracking his fists, demons blocked his path.

“What is this?”

Some demons knew of Heaven, but most had never seen a giant.

A demon with scythe-like arms stepped forward, sniffing.

“Sniff sniff. What’s this smell? Are they really that different from humans? Looks stupid.”

He lifted Ymir’s chin with his scythe, grinning.

“Hey, you’re frozen stiff. Say something. Call me ‘older brother’.”

The demons recognized the 80-meter giant as their leader, but their faces were pale.

“Decided.”

Ymir gripped the scythe and bent the arm like a piece of soft metal.

“Aaaaaa! My arm! My arm!”

As demons roared in fury, Ymir stretched his limbs and advanced.

“I’ll start by cleaning up the trash.”

“Kill!”

The demons charged, weapons raised, fangs bared.

“Huh.”

Ymir took a deep breath, expanded his chest, and raised his arms.

Girshin reached out.

“Ymir! Wait—!”

Before the words finished, Ymir dropped into a stance and slammed both arms into the ground.

The world went silent.

*Is time slowing? Or speeding up?*

The allied forces, watching from afar, felt as if drugged.

*What is that?*

The earth rose like a tidal wave. A whirlpool formed in the sky, colors swirling.

*So sleepy. Want to go home.*

A strange yawn escaped their lips.

Eyelids drooped. Vision narrowed.

“… … Sun!”

A voice entered someone’s ear like a worm, then slipped out.

*Hehe, amazing. Can you hear it?* Delirium from shockwaves.

Aaaaaang!

Those who experienced it vanished the next moment—gone without a trace.

Hey hey hey hey!

A mass teleport flash landed two kilometers away.

“Is everyone okay?”

They’d used maze magic for an emergency escape—but not everyone made it.

There was no time to mourn.

“Again! Again!”

As the teleport flared into the sky, a massive seismic wave ripped through the area.

Curleung. Curleung. Curleung.

The sound of the earth cracking around Ymir echoed endlessly.

“Tsk. You’re annoying.”

No demons remained in the flower garden. Even the angels of Heaven watched with exhausted expressions.

*A monster is a monster.*

The same question echoed through every heavenly soldier’s mind since Ymir’s birth.

*Why in this world…*

*… do we need something like that?*

Chapter 976

Age of History (2)

~*~

The armies of Heaven swept across the planet.

Ikael had searched once along the axis of rotation, twice along the equator and prime meridian… but found nothing.

‘Can’t find it.’

There was no trace of Buddha anywhere.

‘If we’re not blind…’

Shura’s gestalt thrived on lies, but before the power of amplification, those lies were as childish as nursery rhymes.

‘Am I avoiding it on purpose?’ There was a good chance of that.

When his memories were amplified twenty thousand times, Ikael had regained everything he had lost.

Even the things Anchera had done to her.

‘Of course I’m resentful.’

Yet he didn’t believe he would seek revenge.

‘Because I don’t have a heart.’

Every decision Anchera made had been a cold calculation to preserve that world.

‘But Anchera is human now. If I meet a man named Nane… what will I do? No—how will *he* treat me?’

Satiel glanced at Ikael, who stood deep in thought.

‘He must have regained his memories.’

The other archangels also suspected something about the erasure of Guffin.

An angel’s mental body was pure, and even the smallest fracture in reality caused discomfort.

‘No matter how the whole changes, it’s still just the whole. But a true reset… that’s different.’

The fact that the angels felt a sense of dissonance proved that someone had reset the world in Anchera’s place.

‘If that feeling is real… then I am Ikael’s enemy.’

Using her nostalgia ability, Satiel broke memories down into their smallest fragments and collected them.

‘Maclein Guffin. Jealousy. Ikael’s sin.’

Just connecting those words in delusion made her teeth clench and her skin crawl.

‘But why? Why won’t you say anything to me?’

Even without clear memories, it was obvious Ikael had done something terrible.

‘Did you really recover your memories? If amplification can do it, then it’s possible for me too.’

That was the question.

Why was Ikael the only one who remembered everything about the Guffin Eradication?

“Stop.”

At Ikael’s command, the armies of Heaven halted.

Photon signals lit the shared sky, but to Ikael, they were meaningless—childish lies.

“Come out.”

The sky warped, bulging unnaturally, and Shura stepped forward.

“I have been ordered by the Buddha.”

“I see you, Shura.”

Seventh-ranked among the Ten Commandments.

The Eternals of Heaven could not possibly be unaware of an archangel’s authority, yet Shura showed no respect.

‘There is no Ankera.’

The world’s steward was now a human named Nane.

‘Only to know the heart.’

Ikael felt a chill at the resolve of the being who had once been a god, now reduced to mere flesh.

“Buddha’s name? You’ll have to back up your words with power. To command us, you must be an existence…”

“Follow me.”

Without waiting for Ikael’s response, Shura turned and walked away.

Protea, chieftain of the Fire Tribe, clenched her eyes shut, her face twisted in pain.

‘I keep dying. I can’t last much longer.’

The Hwajok strategy was simple.

Three hundred flower people controlled three hundred fresh flowers. From the center, Protea directed the entire garden.

Eliminating her should have been the demons’ top priority—but they ignored her completely.

“Ha ha ha! I got it! It’s mine!”

They were too busy tormenting the humans, fire tribes, and elves before them.

“Damn it!”

Rufist finally gave the order to Fleur.

“Pull back the defenses! Protect Protea!”

The allied forces moved swiftly.

They reestablished their defensive line 300 meters ahead.

Intercepting devices activated.

Steel shattered from all directions. The ground trembled with a dull thud.

“It’s an elf! Catch the elf! Those are delicacies!”

Elves were the only prey the demons truly desired.

“No! Get away!”

Elves had stronger resistance to fear than humans, but once their spirit broke, their emotions spilled out raw and unfiltered.

“Pathetic! Like dogs!”

Enox watched his people trampled underfoot. Finally, he raised his sword and prepared to charge.

“Wait.”

Fireballs streaked down from the sky.

Blinding, like staring directly into the sun, they flew so fast the demons barely saw them coming.

“Huh?”

Only when their skin began to melt did they realize the fireballs were over twenty meters in diameter.

“Aww! Hot!”

The moment the fireballs struck, Enox grabbed the surviving elves and retreated.

“Are you trying to kill them all?”

At the same time, Rufist blocked the elves’ rear and erected a 40-meter iron wall.

“Big!”

The wall held the heat for a moment, but soon the steel glowed red, like molten lava.

“The water magic!”

Enox shouted. Water nymphs responded, chanting ancient spells.

“Ascol!”

Demon and human blood vaporized instantly. Torrential rain poured down in a wide radius.

Chee hee hee!

The iron walls collapsed with a cooling hiss as the temperature dropped to survivable levels.

The allies were dangerously powerful—but thanks to them, the demon advance line was pushed back 700 meters.

Rufist’s expression darkened.

‘Just as the rumors said.’

Only one person in the world could wield fire magic at this level.

Garcia arrived via space transfer.

“Are you all right?”

Rufist nodded. The title of world’s greatest flame mage was no exaggeration.

“Thank you. What’s the situation?”

“Not good. We’ve lost 20% of our Valkyrie forces.”

Considering the Allies had taken 50% casualties and the Elves 30%, the Valkyries were clearly elite.

“But the greatest loss was the 1st Corps commander. At this rate, we can’t protect the flower garden.”

Rufist’s brows shot up.

‘Fidero is dead? A swordsman hailed as the reincarnation of Cadel?’

It was shocking news—but war was unpredictable.

“The association president will decide. Whether to retreat and regroup, or hold this position.”

Garcia held higher operational rank.

But when it came to Tormia’s fate, he believed the decision should rest with its own commander.

“Hmm.”

Rufist stroked his chin.

‘If we retreat, we save our troops. But if we abandon this ground… do we have a future at all?’

The only reason they could resist the demons despite the power gap was the flower garden.

Which was more valuable—men or machines?

“All troops!”

Rufist barked the order.

If they abandoned the ancient weapon, they’d never inflict this level of damage again.

“Retreat!”

There could be no system without people.

That was Rufist’s belief. Garcia respected it.

“Valkyrie! Full retreat!”

The demons, seeing the allies flee, drooled with excitement.

“Ha ha ha! This is the best part!”

Chasing retreating enemies, they fed on the despair in their eyes.

“Aaaaaa! No!”

The cries of the fallen, the screams of comrades, tore through the hearts of those who escaped.

“Huh!”

Rufist clenched his jaw until his teeth cracked.

“Run! Just run!”

But in the end, some escaped only because others were caught.

“Come on! You bastards!”

As they fled, shedding tears of blood, a roar of space movement split the sky.

“What?”

Judging by the sound wave’s length, the spell had been cast from an extreme distance.

Rufist followed the trajectory into the heart of the demon camp.

“Why?”

Even for long-range magic, that distance was absurd.

A miscalculation would mean instant, agonizing death.

The demons surrounding the impact zone wore stunned expressions.

“What is this kid?” A slender girl with short, boyish hair muttered.

“Fire.”

Burn without remnants.

Despite her soft voice, a colossal flame erupted from her core, piercing the sky.

“Kuaaaaa!”

As the firestorm raged through the screams of dying demons, the allied soldiers froze.

“No way…”

Garcia’s eyes flared with recognition.

The flames dissipated, leaving only ash.

“Emmy…????”

Standing there was Amy, untouched, not a single thread of her uniform burned.

Only Garcia, the greatest flame mage, understood what that meant.

Complete combustion.

‘It’s not magic. It’s a spiritual realm. And reaching it requires absolute realization of fire.’

It was nearly impossible.

Like the old saying—only those burned to death truly understand fire.

Amy’s form flickered. In an instant, she appeared before the allied camp.

‘Magic based on concept, not phenomenon. Requires a unique understanding of fire…’

It was the domain of an Archmage.

Garcia didn’t know why she’d used movement magic, but it clearly had its purpose.

“Lieutenant Colonel Karmis Amy has returned to the battlefield.”

As Garcia watched, Tess rushed forward with Lian.

“Amy!”

Tess threw her arms around Amy, hugging her so tightly she nearly choked.

“I thought you were dead! What happened!”

“Wait, wait, Tess…”

“Also, your hair… it’s so short. Like a man’s. But… it’s pretty.”

“Lieutenant Colonel.”

Hearing Garcia’s voice, Tess snapped back and stepped away.

“Oh, sorry!”

Amy met Garcia’s gaze with a knowing smile.

‘Master…’

She knew he had waited for her return more than anyone.

“If you survived twice, it wasn’t luck.”

Garcia studied the enemy lines, then turned to her.

“The 1st Corps commander position is vacant. Take it.”

“…What? Yes?”

Amy hadn’t processed the words. Garcia’s tone left no room for debate.

“As of now, you are commander of the 1st Valkyrie Corps.”

“Huh!”

Tess clapped a hand over her mouth. Soldiers nearby murmured in shock.

“If you’re commander… does that make you a lieutenant general?” Even in the face of annihilation, this was unprecedented.

Amy’s voice trembled.

“Brigade commander…”

“You understand it may be symbolic. This could be our grave. But the responsibility remains. Nothing to gain. Much to lose. Will you take it?”

Tears welled in Amy’s eyes.

“Yes!”

She clenched her fists. Her eyes burned red with resolve.

‘I did it. I did it.’

In an instant, her entire life flashed before her.

Entering magic school. Failing the first graduation exam. Graduating fourth out of ten in the second.

Joining the Tormia army, then the Valkyries, with a record that promised nothing extraordinary.

Brutal training. Garcia’s cold, relentless guidance.

‘Thank you, Berik.’

Without Berik’s support, she wouldn’t be here.

‘And…’

Memories she had clung to even in agony.

‘Shirone.’

As 1st Corps commander, she could apply for the title of Archmage—reputation points alone would qualify her.

Given her actual feat—breaking the fire world—the red line rating system made sense.

‘Can we meet again?’

The night she had to leave Shirone behind… how much she had cursed her own weakness.

‘Now… we can fight together.’

Tess watched Amy, her eyes burning with pride.

‘Congratulations.’

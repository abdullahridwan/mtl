Chapter 955

Unconnected Sin (4)

~*~

A soft light gathered above Ikael’s head, forming a glowing sphere as her unconscious form lay still.

The asteroid around her slowly returned to its original shape, and her eyelids lifted as if moved by unseen machinery.

*Where is this?*

The first thing she saw was Satiel’s tear-streaked face.

“Archangel, are you okay?”

Ikael sat up, defying gravity, and looked around.

She was in Jebul’s room.

“What happened?”

Satiel hesitated. “After you passed out… the war was put on hold. Kariel sealed the Gaians behind the Metagate. I think it’s secure, but…”

Still, Heaven remained tense.

“Yes,” Ikael said quietly.

“What happened, Archangel? What occurred at the meeting?”

Ikael didn’t answer. She was thinking.

*No one knows yet.*

The fact that she had opened the sanctuary door without permission. The fact that she had blocked the angels on her own authority. And now—no response from Ankera.

*Are you planning to cover it up?*

The Gaians’ cry echoed in her memory, faint but sharp.

—*Some god reverses what he said.*

Could it be?

She pressed a hand to her forehead, a dull throb pulsing beneath her fingers.

“I need to see Ankera-nim.”

Satiel shot to her feet. “All angels have been ordered to stand by! No one is allowed near the sanctuary!”

“I understand.”

Ikael walked to the door and opened it.

“You will wait for me here.”

She ascended into the sky of Buddha and flew straight toward the spire of Araboth.

When she reached the sanctuary, she paused before the door—the same one she had opened on her own.

*Where did I get the courage to do that?*

*No. It wasn’t courage. It was a choice.*

A heart that could choose anything—but also had to bear the consequences.

“Ankera, this is Ikael.”

No answer came.

She reached out, fingers nearly brushing the surface.

Just before contact, the door creaked open from within.

Ikael stepped inside, her breath catching at the sight of Ankera as always—Guffin still embedded in the divine form, a sight that twisted her heart.

“Come in, my daughter.”

The moment she crossed the threshold, the door shut behind her.

*Cuckoo. Cuckoo.*

She had heard that sound countless times. But now, for the first time, it felt like a seal—cutting off retreat.

Ikael opened her mouth.

“Why…?”

“Do you wish to ask why I did not punish you?”

“Yes. And I want to know exactly what happened during the meeting with Guffin.”

Ankera shifted, pushing Guffin’s body slightly toward Ikael.

To keep the asteroid stable, she had to focus completely.

“Guffin made me an offer,” Ankera said. “He asked who is truly qualified to be a god. He said we should risk everything for each other.”

Ikael looked up, startled.

Guffin’s body was hollow—his soul gone. Only his pupils glowed faintly. She couldn’t hide her horror.

“Ikael.”

Ankera’s voice was calm.

“Did I do something wrong?”

At those words, Ikael felt her mind teeter on the edge of an abyss.

God makes no mistakes.

“…Dear Ankera.”

“The world simply exists.”

The voice was majestic, untouched by the grotesque distortions Ikael had heard before.

“There is no good or evil. I exist perfectly, and I sustain that perfection endlessly. Ankera judges only for the sake of the world.”

Ikael listened in silence.

“My daughter, everything in this world falls under my definition. But you… you are the most special.”

“I know.”

“It is not merely because you were the first.” Ikael met her gaze, confused.

“When I was unaware of my own existence, a surge of intense light erupted within me. Amplification. That was you—a concept on the boundary between existence and non-existence. That is why you are different from the other angels.”

“Different how?”

“At the moment of amplification, the world split—into existence and non-existence. You are the purest angel because you carried all the instability with you.”

Ikael had never heard this before.

“Satan Lucifer. The symmetry of the law. He was born at the same instant as you, but instead of embracing light, he became the vessel of eternal darkness. In a way, you could say you are twins.”

Ikael’s world trembled.

“He is…?”

“Gone. He purified himself in the other world and died. Only the concept of Satan remains.”

“Why?”

Ankera raised Guffin’s body.

“Lucifer made a pact with Guffin. In exchange for protecting you, Guffin would take his place—be purified instead.”

“N-no… That can’t be…”

A memory flashed—ancient, buried deep.

The photon cannon hurtling toward her. Then, suddenly, veering away—absorbed by Guffin as he stepped in front of her.

*No.*

The first kiss—the one that made her realize her feelings.

*That can’t be true!*

Even during the fiercest battles with the Gaians, the countless moments of understanding between Guffin and her.

“I… I didn’t…”

Tears of light spilled down Ikael’s cheeks.

*How happy I was.*

The joy that surged when Guffin said he would stay in Heaven.

“It was all an illusion, Ikael.”

Ankera’s tendril reached out, gently wiping the tears from her face.

“The mind is nothing but a system-generated illusion. Guffin was an error—an anomaly that sought to destroy the world. Why didn’t I punish you?”

Ikael felt the cold touch.

“Because it was unnecessary. You were merely infected by the error known as Guffin.”

“God, I…”

“Fear not. I have defined this world. And you are the beginning of that definition—the core of my thought.”

If Ankera was the brain, then Ikael was the mind.

“Yes, I could not absorb Hexa. But look—Guffin is still under my control. Perhaps this *is* perfection. Isolating the system’s errors. The law is stable once more.”

Guffin’s body drifted back toward her.

“My dear daughter. If I were deceiving you, then I would not be a god. But if I *am* a god…”

Ankera’s voice was absolute.

“Then this is right.”

Ankera does not deceive.

Because it is cold.

“The Ultima System will soon be dismantled. I will create a new human for you—one who will not fear, will not waver, and will only praise you.”

“Guffin… Guffin…”

Ikael collapsed to her knees, forehead pressed to the ground, shoulders shaking.

Satiel, who had been waiting in Jebul’s room, sensed her return and rushed to meet her.

“Chief Angel!”

She flinched at Ikael’s hollow expression—but her concern was for Guffin.

“Did you see Ankera? What happened? What’s become of Guffin?”

At the name, Ikael’s face twisted in fury.

“Do not speak that name in my presence.”

“B-but—”

“He dared to bare his fangs at Ankera-nim under the guise of a meeting. A fraud. Nothing more.”

There was no falsehood in Ikael’s voice.

*And he mocked me.*

Even as he made a final decision with Ankera, he said nothing to her.

*He used me. In his heart, there was never any room for me.*

“I didn’t.”

“But Archangel, Guffin—”

“Satiel.”

The intensity of Ikael’s gaze silenced her.

“Do not make me repeat myself.”

Ikael turned and walked away. Satiel stepped aside, opening the path.

“Do not allow anyone near the sanctuary of Araboth. This is the Archangel’s order.”

Meanwhile, Ankera had tasked the angels with finding a way to dismantle the Ultima System.

Kariel found the answer.

“The anecdote. The force that unites the Gaians. After Sojeonghwa, the black elixir overflowed and remained. It’s difficult to extract, but if we send an angel, it can be done.”

Uriel frowned. “Integration? Wouldn’t that only make the enemy stronger?”

“Not integration. Simplification. The core of the Ultima System is multiple personalities striving for one truth. We’ve failed to destroy any single one. So instead—eliminate the multiplicity.”

Satiel slammed her hand on the white council table and stood.

“I oppose this! You can’t annihilate the Gaians when we don’t even know if Guffin is alive or dead! No action will proceed until the full truth is revealed!”

“There is no need to worry. The giant formed from the anecdote will be separated back into individual humans. This is the only way to end the Ultima System.”

“How is that different from murder?”

“It *isn’t* murder. Humanity will thrive—under our guidance.”

Satiel turned desperately to Ikael.

“Chief Angel! Say something!”

All eyes turned to Ikael.

But she said nothing.

She stared ahead, expression blank—just as she had for days since the meeting.

The White Watch fell silent.

“…Enough for today.”

A familiar phrase.

Satiel sighed.

That night, Ikael lay in bed, tossing and turning.

*Why are you doing this? Everything’s over. The world is stable. I’m the CEO of Heaven.*

She thought time would make her an enemy—but instead, the pain only grew.

*You used me! Liar!* Her teeth clenched, rage burning.

*But… was it all a lie?*

Doubt crept in.

*Could there be another reason?*

She had known him for so long. Even an angel’s logic couldn’t accept it.

“Guffin…”

The moment she whispered the name, emotion surged—raw, overwhelming.

“Hah!”

She bolted upright and flew toward Araboth’s spire.

Arriving at the sanctuary, she gasped, glaring at the door.

*Guffin is in there.*

A distance she could cross in an instant.

How many nights had she come here, unable to resist the pull?

Ikael reached out, hand trembling.

*I want to see you. Just once. Just one last time…*

Maybe then she could forget.

—*The mind is an illusion.*

Ankera’s words cut through her.

Ikael bit her lip and yanked her hand back.

She closed her eyes, fists clenched, forcing herself to calm down.

The sanctuary door stood silent—empty, lifeless.

*It’s over.*

She turned, wings of light unfolding, and flew back to her quarters.

*Everything is over.*

Another night passed.

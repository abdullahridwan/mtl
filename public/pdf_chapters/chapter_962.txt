Chapter 962

In the Flower Field (3)

~*~

Under Ankera’s orders, Satiel returned to the hidden place where Ikael waited.

“You’re here,” Ikael said. “I was worried—there was no message.”

Her smile held no trace of doubt.

Satiel knew better. *None of them are fools.* Ikael had already run through every possible outcome in her mind, weighed every risk. Yet she still smiled.

*Believe in me.*

Satiel thought of all the years she had fought for humanity, of the dreams everyone once had of becoming gods. She forced herself to stay calm.

“Yes. Forgive me for being late. The truth is… I still haven’t chosen a name. It’s become a burden.”

Ikael’s eyes softened with affection. “Huh. That’s all right. You’ve always had a kind heart. You’ll find a wonderful name when the time comes.”

Satiel couldn’t answer.

“Come inside,” Ikael said. “You’re like a sister to me. That makes you an aunt to my child. You should see your niece’s face.”

*An aunt????*

Satiel’s eyes burned.

Even if she could never love Ikael the way she wanted, things might have been different.

*No. That’s not what I wanted. I don’t want to be tied to a giant like this—not like family.*

Then why was she crying?

“What are you doing? Why don’t you come in?”

When Satiel had sealed off her heart, even the smallest connection had become precious.

*I’ll just endure it. I’ll spend my whole life watching from the sidelines.*

But it was already too late.

“I’m sorry,” Satiel whispered.

Ikael tilted her head, watching the tears of light trail down Satiel’s cheeks.

“Don’t forgive me.”

A roar split the distance.

The source wasn’t visible yet, but the pressure in the air made Guffin tense.

“Harm!”

Maternal instinct overrode all other thoughts. Ikael shot off in the opposite direction of the sound.

She moved so fast the air resistance alone was deafening—but it was unavoidable.

If Ymir’s true target wasn’t Guffin, but Ikael herself.

“Found.”

Guffin locked eyes with Ymir and saw the incoming fist as if it were crushing space itself.

*‘Miracle Stream!’*

Golden smoke coiled around Ymir’s arm. A command beyond the world’s laws surged forward.

*Stop.*

The signals clashed. Vibrations rippled through reality, but no one felt them.

The entire world trembled.

“Big!”

Ymir’s face twisted, but his eyes blazed with joy—the first real resistance he’d ever felt.

*Is this what it feels like?*

To truly *fight*.

“Aaaaaaa!”

Guffin screamed, arms outstretched. A force beyond measurement surged through him.

The world’s tremors faded. Ymir’s fist halted just short of Guffin’s face.

Ymir’s arm bent at a twenty-degree angle.

If his elbow had fully extended, Guffin’s head would have been obliterated.

*I broke through the Miracle Stream. To enter after destroying Hexa—the artificial embodiment of Idea—a signal from outside the world… …*

“Are you Gaia?”

There was only one possible answer.

“I am Ymir.”

Ymir, muscles recharged with raw energy, lashed out with one leg.

“The king of giants!”

Guffin barely managed to create distance.

*Weird.*

This was the first time he’d faced a truly powerful enemy, yet the sight of Ymir stirred a strange nostalgia.

*… …Why?*

Then he saw it—in Ymir’s eyes.

One emotion, yet deep enough to hold ten billion others.

“So that’s how it turned out.”

Guffin’s eyes flared as he realized—he was the last Gaian left in the world.

“I’m sorry.”

Guffin stopped retreating. He pulled his arms together. The Miracle Stream gathered between his palms.

A blinding flash shot toward Ymir, who charged at breakneck speed.

*Photon Cannon.*

At sub-light speed, the energy mass slammed into Ymir’s abdomen.

“Big!”

Ymir caught the sphere of light with both hands, crushing its inertia in his grip.

*Did he catch it?*

No—the real shock wasn’t the catch.

Any living body moving at sub-light speed should have vaporized from frictional heat.

When Guffin saw Ymir’s arm glowing red-hot, his spine prickled.

*The mass… it’s incomparable to the Photon Cannon’s…*

Moving at sub-light speed, a shockwave spread in a four-kilometer radius centered on Ymir.

After Ikael fled, Satiel—who had been frozen in place—was instantly thrown back by the blast.

“Great!”

Where the land had been flattened, only Guffin and Ymir remained, glaring at each other.

“That’s strange.”

Ymir shook his throbbing stomach.

The pain brought him joy, but that wasn’t the issue now.

“I don’t feel right.”

He could fight anything in this world—except Guffin.

“It’s like… I’m hurting myself.”

Guffin asked, “Do you remember what happened before you were born?”

“Not at all. But I can guess. Looking at you… maybe I’m right.”

“Leave. You have to protect your wife and children.”

Ymir looked up at the sky.

“Family.”

At this moment—this origin of his existence—he wanted to make sense of everything.

“I have no family. There will be no more.”

“If you help me, you could—” Ymir reached out his hand.

“No. I don’t care about any of that. I just want to fight.”

That was his decision. Now, only one thing remained.

“If you don’t have much time,” Ymir said, and kicked the ground.

“Let’s finish this.”

The earth exploded. A single kick tore open an endless abyss.

From space, the northern hemisphere of the planet would have lit up like a match.

Kururrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrr!

The shockwave reached the heavens. Even the angels paled at the sheer destructive power.

*This giant is dangerous.*

Uriel turned toward the explosion.

*No compatibility. No heavenly enemy. Not even a standard. Just… strong. His existence itself is a violation.*

Even compared to Guffin…

Uriel, watching from above, saw a sea of lava where all matter had melted.

Ten meters above the scorching surface, Ymir hovered in midair.

The external gravitational distortion from his mere muscle vibrations showed no sign of fading—even after ten minutes.

*Guffin, I gave you a chance.*

Just before their final blows connected, Ymir had stopped his attack.

If Guffin died by his strongest strike, Ymir thought, that would have been an acceptable end.

*But he survived. And now… the hesitation that held me back is gone.*

“I am already…”

Guffin said, after slamming Ymir into the planet’s mantle and turning away.

“Thanks.”

Ymir slowly rose from the lava, face twisted in a sneer.

“Was it as awkward for you as it was for me? Doesn’t matter. Either way, your end will be the worst. As long as I exist, you’ll achieve nothing.”

“Maybe.”

Guffin didn’t deny it.

“But the will continues endlessly. One day, someone will come to take my place. And…”

Guffin’s body began to glow, turning transparent—as if dissolving into light.

“I will free my people from your prison.”

Ymir shook off the memory.

“It continues.”

He muttered, chin resting on his hand, cross-legged in midair, waiting as the gravitational distortion around him refused to fade.

“I wish I could.”

Above Nane, seated in meditation at the peak of a mountain, the clouds swirled, forming a perfect hole.

Shura, watching from below, whispered, “The Buddha is suffering.”

No—rather, this was the last unresolved problem in the world, born from the Buddha’s own agony.

*Was it Amy? What about her was so significant?*

Ever since Amy had left, Nane had been lost in thought, unbroken.

*She doesn’t care.*

Shura turned away.

*I only want to know the world’s secrets. If the Buddha can reach enlightenment through this, I’ll have no regrets.*

That was how she justified it.

Yet the sting of being overlooked still lingered.

“Shura.”

Nane’s voice descended from above.

“Come to me.”

Shura shot straight up, landing on the peak, and knelt.

“Did you call?”

“I can feel your heart,” Nane said. Shura gathered her courage.

“I’m embarrassed to ask… but why her? Why Amy?”

The Buddha of this age—the one who preached compassion for all beings—had fallen in love with a human.

Nane plucked a single flower from the ground.

“I was thinking about how to cast my heart away.”

Then, gently, he covered the flower with his other palm.

“I admired its color. Praised its scent. Admired its shape. But I could never truly love it.”

He crushed the flower between his hands.

“It was empty.”

Shura listened in silence.

“No matter what method I used, everything was hollow before the truth. I couldn’t love flowers. I thought Shirone, who opposed me, was foolish. But one day… *she* came to me.”

Karmis Amy.

“I only wanted to understand Shirone. I thought that would lead me to perfect truth. And then I realized.”

Nane smiled at Shura.

“I didn’t cast my heart away for love. Because I cast it away… I have no choice but to love.”

Slowly, he opened his palm. The crushed flower began to revive.

Shura’s expression went blank.

*This…????*

It was just like Shirone.

“I cast my heart into this flower. Color, scent, shape—none of it matters. Because I gave it my heart, this flower is now the most beautiful thing to me.”

Nane replanted the flower in the soil where he had dug it up.

“This is Shirone. It must be the reason we exist in an empty world.”

Shura bowed deeply.

“If you’re going, have you made your decision?”

“I told Amy I would see it through. My will lies only in the suffering of sentient beings.”

Nane stood, hands behind his back.

“But the mind is strange. After she left, regret took root. What if I had accepted her offer? I miss her. My heart aches as if it might break.”

Shura was certain now.

“Buddha.”

“Yes. I understand Shirone. I can now see everything from the most objective view. So I must go into the world. I will see with my own eyes and judge—do sentient beings need Buddha… or Yahweh?”

Shura sensed it. Soon, a decision would be made—one that would determine the fate of the world.

If Yahweh was right, Nane would gladly help Shirone destroy the demons.

*But if the Buddha is right…*

The world would end.

*No one could stop it. Not Yahweh, not extremes, not even atrocities could halt the apocalypse. If only…*

Suddenly, a name surfaced.

*Gaold.*

The one who had defeated the Buddha.

“Yes.”

Nane smiled, having read Shura’s thoughts.

“The only thing that could stop me from trying to free humanity from suffering… is the fall of humanity itself.”

He gazed across the landscape, as if memorizing every detail.

“Bring Ikael.”

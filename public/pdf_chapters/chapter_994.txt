Chapter 994

Simultaneous Incidents (4)

~*~

Ionas’s fist, disintegrated into particles moments before, reformed abruptly in front of Shirone’s face.

“Kuk!”

Shirone twisted his head aside. The displaced air cracked with a sonic boom.

“You still have talent.”

Shirone stared at Ionas.

‘Ionas of Extreme Decomposition.’

Pale-skinned, with silver hair falling to her shoulders, she appeared almost human at a glance. But her pupilless eyes and double rows of teeth made her terrifying to behold.

‘Atomic Movement. This is dangerous.’

Her ability allowed her to break down her body to the atomic level and move with impossible precision. Attacks born from it operated at a speed beyond conventional reaction—faster than the eye could track, faster than instinct could respond.

‘It’s not just speed.’ Even if Shirone saw the strike coming, the lag between sensory input and physical reaction left him vulnerable.

He perceived her movements through his eleven senses, but evading them relied entirely on his own reflexes.

“Shall we begin in earnest?”

Ionas, still in particle form, launched an attack from an unpredictable angle.

‘Miracle Stream!’

A burst of light wrapped around Shirone’s body as a black hole opened in the air, as if struck by a shotgun blast.

pop! pop! pop! pop!

Fragments of light scattered. Before the echoes faded, Shirone had retreated twenty kilometers away.

“Yes. I’ll run, after all.”

Ionas, now reassembled, circled Shirone with only her face intact amid a swirling cloud of particles.

“I’ve always wondered,” she said.

Through Ultima, Shirone tracked the motion of roughly ten thousand particles with perfect clarity.

“I’m strong. So why don’t I feel like the strongest?”

He caught a pattern in her movements—a fleeting opening.

‘Left punch.’

The strike passed by Shirone’s shoulder.

‘Close.’

He widened the distance again and unleashed 4,200 photon cannons in rapid succession.

“Is that all?”

Ionas’s arm moved at invisible speed, deflecting every beam.

Uriel, observing from twenty kilometers away, murmured, “He was a strong boy.”

No matter the standard, Shirone had always been someone who made others acknowledge his strength.

“And now, he’s even stronger.” Mental capacity was infinite, but the human body had limits.

To sense and evade Ionas of Extreme Decomposition was nearly miraculous.

“But I suppose that’s all he can do.”

Rayel silently agreed.

‘Uriel’s words are contradictory. The truly strong don’t need to prove it.’

Yet he couldn’t dismiss the question that had surfaced in his mind.

‘Why can’t I—or we—feel like we’re the strongest?’ Only Ikael knew the answer.

‘Maclein Guffin.’

Every being considered the strongest in Heaven shared one experience: failure against the Guffin.

When the Guffin erasure occurred, a transcendental concept—indescribable, unfathomable—was born.

‘Even if erased from memory, we’ve already experienced a power beyond that…

Yet we never reached the feeling of being the best.’

Ikael watched his son fight, his eyes filled with sorrow.

‘Love it.’

More important than anything in the universe.

‘I don’t deserve to be called a parent. But I won’t lose you again.’

He would never gamble with his children’s lives.

‘Please step back now. Return and live a happy life. This mother will handle the world’s affairs, no matter the cost.’

Each time Shirone narrowly avoided Ionas’s attacks, Ikael’s heart ached.

‘I can’t beat Ionas at this level. But it’s not due to lack of skill.’

Something beyond strength was at play.

‘Only… he’s not a Guffin.’

Even with equal mindset, Shirone’s body was far weaker than Gaia’s.

‘The strength of a giant, a spirit transcending angelic concepts, and the unity of countless Gaians.’

It was because they possessed all these that the realm of gods had once been shaken to its core.

“Ikael, I give you one final chance.”

Satiel spoke.

“Speak of the Guffin erasure. Admit your mistakes. Then I will acknowledge your authority.”

Ikael clenched his fists.

‘You mustn’t act on emotion.’

The position of Archangel was essential—to protect Shirone from the full might of Heaven’s armies.

“You know? Ionas is one of my own, yet she surpasses me in combat power. Follow me, and I won’t act dogmatically. If you tell me to leave Shirone alone, I will obey.” It was a tempting offer.

If only he could endure bowing to the one who had killed his son.

‘It doesn’t matter.’

Ikael’s eyes burned with resolve.

‘Because I’ve returned alive. If I can protect Shirone, I’ll kneel before anyone.’

Above, Ionas’s fist slammed into Shirone’s stomach.

“Keugh!”

Though over 99.99% of the kinetic energy had been absorbed, the pain was searing.

“I think it’s impressive.”

Ionas strolled through the air as if on a leisurely walk.

“You’re a human who’d die from a mere brush. I’ll applaud you for lasting this long in such a fragile body.”

Shirone wasn’t amused.

“You’re strong. You’re one of the ten strongest in Heaven. So if the only loser here is me—”

Ionas raised her fist before her eyes.

“—then you’re fighting one of the top five.”

“Haha.”

Despite the pain, laughter escaped him.

“… What’s so funny?”

Having lived through all of Omega’s history, Shirone knew Ionas’s true standing.

“Honestly, isn’t it dangerous?” Ionas’s eyebrows twitched.

“Ymir and Ashur are at odds. Uriel and Ikael are beneath them.”

Flaw…?

Shirone pointed at her.

“I admit, I sometimes get involved. But only sometimes.”

A mere human couldn’t grasp Heaven’s ancient conflicts—but the observation wasn’t wrong.

“It’s a joke. Forget it.”

Shirone steadied himself.

“It doesn’t matter anyway. You’ll never be the strongest.”

“Now I understand.”

In an instant, Ionas shot past Shirone’s head and swung her leg downward.

A whip-like crack split the air. Six hundred meters below, the sea parted in a massive fissure.

‘Sudden?’

They were over open ocean.

Ionas glanced at the water’s surface, where her wounds had already healed. She lifted her head.

“You’re a typical loser, mentally. If you defeat me—the strongest—you might feel your foundation tremble. But let me be clear: you and I exist on different levels.”

“I don’t care about levels. Strength has nothing to do with it. Because…”

Shirone opened his eyes. Miracle Stream flared, compressed tightly in both hands.

“I must win, no matter what.”

A flash of light split the sky. The Hand of God descended and bent a single finger toward Ionas.

An aggressive use of the Ultima System.

‘I’ve been hit by that before.’

This mental ability had once scattered Heaven’s entire army across the world.

“How dare you grow lax against me?” Ionas bared her double rows of teeth.

“I can’t afford to. It’s my responsibility.”

Shirone looked down at the sea.

They were over the open ocean, 800 kilometers west of the Jive Kingdom.

‘I can fight here.’ There were lives below, but protecting humanity was the greater strategic need.

Ionas’s triangular pyramid flared red.

“It’s over.”

Extreme Decomposition – Atomic Crash.

Particles compressed into fist-sized spheres exploded outward in all directions.

‘I’ve increased the activity of the particles.’

This Ionas, shifting into a higher energy dimension, aimed at Shirone’s back and raised her arm.

‘Just a human.’

Destroy the heart, and life ends.

‘Yes?’

The Hand of God, forming a circle with thumb and middle finger, appeared directly in front of Ionas.

‘Damn!’

The distance to Shirone’s back: 0.03 millimeters.

‘Too late.’

Ionas’s arms disintegrated into particles and crossed instinctively to block her face.

At that moment, the Hand of God snapped its fingers.

“Ouch!”

The shockwave struck every particle of Ionas’s form. Her body was hurled into the sea.

Whoops!

Water erupted. A towering column of spray shot into the sky.

“Kuaaaaa!”

Ionas burst from the water, her spine cracked like a whale’s, glaring upward with furious eyes.

“A worthless human…!”

She stopped. Shirone was raising his hand.

But what silenced her was the Hand of God now towering above Shirone.

‘Don’t worry, Ikael.’

As if reflecting Shirone’s heart, the Hand of God stretched all five fingers toward the sky.

‘Because we’re definitely connected.’

At the same time, a sphere of light—unfathomably vast—formed above the giant hand.

Ionas, and the distant Archangels watching, were speechless.

Shirone looked down at the sea and said, “Feel it too.”

Hand of God – Photon Cannon.

“This is my responsibility.”

With a full-force swing, the Hand of God slammed the Photon Cannon straight down.

The moment the astronomical mass struck the sea—

“Aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa…!”

Ionas’s scream was cut short. The sea vaporized in a crown-shaped explosion.

The scale was so immense that time seemed to slow. The entire process unfolded in perfect clarity.

Kurleung! Kurleung!

Steam swallowed the horizon. Only a deafening roar remained.

When the Hand of God swept its palm aside, the view cleared—revealing a colossal crater in the ocean.

‘Nothing.’

Ionas was gone.

After a moment, gravity pulled the surrounding water inward, creating a breathtaking vortex of colliding currents.

Satiel ended the observation.

“… Disappeared.”

Uriel, Rayel, and Mara—each with transcendent vision—remained silent.

The power of the Hand of God reminded them of a past they wished to forget.

‘Is it over?’ Yet Shirone seemed to imply a connection still remained.

Curleung. Curleung.

Like a scene crafted for divine entertainment, the sea continued its endless transformation.

‘Two minutes have passed. Is this enough?’

When it was clear Ionas had vanished, Shirone turned toward the direction he had come from.

He spoke in a gentle voice to the woman still watching him.

“Ikael.”

Though he couldn’t mend the heart shattered when Guffin disappeared,

“I am not alone.”

He believed that if they could remember together, that too could be a beautiful life.

“So…”

Bathed in sunlight, Shirone smiled and reached out his hand from afar.

“Come with me, Mom.”

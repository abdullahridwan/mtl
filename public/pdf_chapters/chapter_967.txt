Chapter 967

Fanaticism (4)

~*~

At Blitz’s suggestion, the eleven apostles observing in the background raised no objections.

It made sense—either prove their willingness to die, or fight all twelve apostles at once.

“Attack me one by one,” Miro said, her tone sour. “I won’t take it lying down.”

Yet when the first strike came, she made no move to counter.

‘If I refuse… it’ll just turn into practice,’ she thought.

The idea was to endure the full assault of the twelve apostles.

‘I’d rather choose that.’

Regardless of victory or defeat, when would she ever get another chance to face such a battle as a lone mage?

‘But I need to reach the Central Continent now. This isn’t the time to feed my pride.’

Having made her decision, Miro raised her hands.

“Alright. Hit me hard. I’ll take it.” Zulu stepped forward.

“Maze.”

“Stand back. You know he’s not someone you provoke by breaking the rules carelessly.”

Though true, Zulu abandoned her initial protest and shifted to a backup plan.

The twelve apostles had once stood against an archangel. At the very least, they should make their escape convincing.

She had flown long distances with Kydra and still retained enough mental strength to cast Posmetry once.

But whether it would work against the twelve apostles remained uncertain.

“What are you waiting for?” Blitz called, glancing back. “Have you already turned soft after singing Shirone’s praises? Come on in.”

“Who goes first?”

“I will.”

A woman stepped forward—her red hair shaved on one side, three piercings lining her left lip. She wore tight leather that accentuated her full figure, a long zipper running down the center of her body.

Miro’s eyes narrowed.

“Maybe… …”

The Fire Dragon, Infercus.

This was the same dragon that had served as the final trial in Shirone’s graduation exam.

Back then, how many spectators had cursed the developer who designed Infercus as a pervert?

‘Not a pervert,’ Miro thought. ‘More like… benevolent.’

Even in human form, the raw energy she radiated was far beyond anything a simulation could replicate.

‘And this monster hit me?’

Infercus, having leapt down from a nearby rock, paused and glanced back.

“I’ll go first. Any objections?” The Light Dragon, Phaeton, grimaced.

“What if I do? If it’s not your turn, you’ll just fight dirty again, won’t you?” Infercus smirked and moved forward.

“That’s why I asked. Better now than later.”

She approached Miro.

“Let’s make this quick. We’re all busy.”

Miro’s lips curled into a faint, tense smile. The Thousand Hands Guanyin manifested behind her in a vast, radiant projection.

The twelve apostles looked up as the celestial figure ascended into the sky.

All were battle masters—experienced enough to instantly recognize what they were facing.

‘That form… it’s not easy to attack.’

Poine, the Venomous Dragon, spoke in the soft, aged voice of an old woman.

“When facing an unknown opponent, you usually start by testing them. Either open with your strongest skill, or target a perceived weakness. But that Guanyin…”

Poine chuckled.

“It doesn’t even let me consider where to begin.”

There were no blind spots.

It was perfectly balanced in every direction, under any standard. Attacking first felt pointless.

Zulu murmured, “All-sky projection.”

To encompass all things beneath the heavens.

That was the sight of the Thousand Arms Guanyin.

“Anyway… …”

Infercus raised her fist before her eyes.

“Can I begin?”

She spread her fingers—and a torrent of fire erupted, a searing wind sweeping across the battlefield, hot enough to blister skin.

Miro exhaled slowly.

“It’s fine. It’s not a breath attack.”

There were no formal rules, but it seemed Infercus had no intention of using shape-shifting.

“Go.”

With a flick of her palm, Infercus launched a blazing sphere of fire, like a miniature sun, and stepped forward.

“Great!”

Miro flinched as a pillar of fire crashed down from above, as if the sky itself had inverted.

30 minutes later.

The rocky battlefield was unrecognizable.

Layers of soil, buried for tens of thousands of years, now lay exposed—torn up by Kang Nan and Zulu.

“Maze!”

The two who arrived froze in shock.

Miro knelt on one knee, arms wrapped over her face, trembling.

Her sleeves were charred, her arms streaked with blood, one foot buried deep in the scorched earth.

“I survived.”

She trembled—not just from exhaustion, but from the sheer intensity of enduring every attack from the twelve apostles, her lips bitten until they bled.

‘I gave up. I just endured.’

She didn’t particularly like Miro, but in this moment, she felt awe—for what it meant to be human.

“Miss Miro.”

Kang Nan approached, using honorifics for the first time in a long while.

“Aaaaaaa!”

Miro suddenly leapt to her feet and screamed.

“You damn lizards! I’ll kill every last one of you!”

She lunged forward, but Kang Nan grabbed her by the waist.

“Wait! Calm down!”

“Let go! I can’t live like this—beaten and humiliated! I’ll pay them back!”

As Miro strained forward, the weight of her body revealed how little strength she had left.

She was in no condition to fight.

‘I couldn’t do anything. I just took it.’

Kang Nan looked at Miro, silent and broken, and tears welled in her eyes.

“Huh. I’m actually… really sad.”

The reason Miro had endured such torment, even as rage brought her to tears… in the end, it was for humanity.

‘It’s a fine line.’

Until now, Kang Nan had resented Miro for not understanding Gaold’s pain. But now, she understood.

‘Because she’s like this.’

Maybe Gaold had tried to protect her, even while enduring unbearable suffering.

Kang Nan spoke softly. “Go to the Central Continent.”

“… … That’s how it happened.”

Iruki, who had been listening to Miro’s story in stunned silence, finally snapped back to reality.

“So you’re late because you fought all twelve apostles one by one?”

“Yes. I meant to come quickly, but I couldn’t walk without pain. I guess I’m getting old.”

Miro, who had been feigning weakness, straightened her expression.

“Anyway, I heard. The elemental bomb. He’s definitely one of Shirone’s allies. Clever strategy.”

A shadow crossed Iruki’s face.

“If Shirone found out, he’d be furious.”

Miro didn’t deny it.

“So do you regret your choice?”

“No. I don’t. My decision is final.”

Miro nodded. “Good. After all, isn’t that the only way to defeat evil? Back during World War II, human power was at its weakest. Let’s move forward. I’ll help too.”

It’s easy to speak of tragedy when you’re among the victims.

“So, something feels off.”

Iruki pointed at the battlefield map.

“As long as Mayray is in place, information won’t leak. Mudeungryong’s log is passed only to Shirone. Until then, no one can read it—it’s in eternal slumber.”

“Hmm. Go on?”

“We’re following the plan. But… there’s a reaction. A backlash.”

Iruki pressed his index finger with the other hand.

“It’s like something’s stirring inside the enemy—like a pulse in their veins.”

“You noticed it too?”

“It’s not that dramatic. Harvits has two routes: go straight, bypassing Tormia, or detour and strike Baska. Going straight is convenient. The detour leads to Bashukara. In my opinion, a small shock should redirect Hell’s armies toward Baska. But the shift is smaller than expected.”

“Hmm. Subtle, right? Shouldn’t shocking them cause resistance? It doesn’t seem like they’re refusing to go to Baska.”

“Exactly. Viewing the enemy army as a single organism, the current state feels like internal conflict. There might be disagreement within their ranks, or only a few know something the rest don’t.”

“Then now is the time. The more uncertain the enemy, the harder it is to manipulate them.”

Iruki was relieved he didn’t have to explain further.

“Yes. Here.”

He pointed the baton north of the flower field.

“Create a shockwave here. It should bend their path by at least eleven degrees.”

Miro grinned. “Is that an analogy?”

“Yes. Some strategies can’t be explained directly.”

“It’s intuitive. I like that. Understood. With Zulu and me, and if Kang Nan joins, we can manage eleven degrees or more.”

Miro stood.

“Then, get to work.”

As she turned to leave, Iruki called after her.

“Why?”

Miro paused. She didn’t answer right away.

“You don’t say anything, do you?” she said at last. “I know you hate evil. But good people die too. Why? No answer? Because you’ll die too?”

“I just want to hold on to something.”

Iruki’s eyes twitched.

“You probably want to hear something that sounds like the right answer. Even if it’s the words of a madman. No—I’d rather hear every bit of criticism.”

Miro walked slowly, as if strolling.

“But there’s no one to talk to. Only silence.”

Iruki lowered his head.

“That silence… is my answer too.”

When he looked up again, Miro was watching him with pity.

“I felt the same. When the 20-member committee met, most stayed silent.”

Only Gaold had shouted, over and over.

“It was brutal and heartbreaking. Humans looked like monsters. But after twenty years on the dimensional wall, I realized something. Maybe that was the first time humanity had ever been truly silent.”

Silence.

“I hate to admit it, but some questions have no answers. It’s arrogance to think every problem can be solved. Back then, humanity and intellect were powerless. Facing a question with no answer.”

Miro smiled, as if releasing the weight of the past.

“There was nothing to resent, nothing to be angry about. If I had rejected the committee’s decision, it would’ve ended the same way. Would mankind have perished? We’ll never know. It’s just the past.”

She stepped forward and kissed Iruki lightly on the top of his head.

“Right now, you’re standing alone in a world no one has ever walked. It will hurt. But don’t be afraid. Wherever you go, a path will form. People will follow that path and open a new world. That’s why, as a human being… …”

She stepped back, kissed her index and middle fingers, and extended them toward Iruki.

“I won’t interfere with your choice.”

Iruki’s fists clenched tightly.

“Thank you.”

What a blessing—to have someone who’s walked the unknown, standing beside you now.

“Remember. Right now, you are the master of this world.”

The door closed.

Iruki slowly closed his eyes.

The silence Miro spoke of wasn’t support, opposition, or neutrality.

‘A choice made only by me.’

Whatever he chose would be the first step. And from it, a new future would be born.

“Whoa.”

For the first time in a long while, as his overheated mind finally cooled, Iruki fell asleep—for about three minutes.

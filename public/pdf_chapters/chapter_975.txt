Chapter 975

Age of History (1)

~*~

Ikael and Ashur arrived at the seventh planet through the stargate installed in Jebul.

The sky was a serene blue, almost heavenly, but everything beneath it felt small, diminished.

From high above Galliant Island, they looked down on Mount Toa. Dark-skinned natives, shorter than the Nephilim of Heaven, with hardened, muscular frames, were locked in fierce combat.

“Back off, you self-indulgent heretics! We are priests following God’s command!”

Most of the angel faction were lean, their eyes glazed and unfocused. The anti-angel faction, in contrast, had sharp, alert eyes and bodies like predators—strong, alert, alive.

“Stupid fools! I can’t leave the future to people who chew hallucinogens while their tribes starve! Now we rule!”

Ikael stroked his chin.

“Hmm.”

The Ankhera did not seek material rewards from their followers. But sacrifice was vital—it confirmed faith among humans on distant worlds.

The angel faction held massive annual sacrifices, using a hallucinogen called *loop* to physically feel God’s presence.

‘Aren’t they good people?’ the thought came. ‘Just believe in God. Then Anke Ra will lead you to heaven and grant eternal life.’

But the anti-angel faction valued life above ritual.

They warned of *loop*’s addictiveness and urged their people to spend the money on feeding starving tribes instead of ancestral rites.

“The arrogant ones.”

Ikael’s cold gaze, spitting disdain, was unmistakable—the messenger of God himself.

Ataraxia unfolded above him, and halos spread across the sky, amplifying his voice.

“Listen, humans!”

A roar rose from below, as if the sky itself screamed.

“I—that… …”

Even the angels, who claimed to carry God in their hearts, couldn’t tell if what they saw was real or illusion.

But it didn’t matter.

“God’s messenger! God’s messenger has come!” The angel faction collapsed to their knees.

“The dissolute cults are persecuting us! Please look down upon us!”

The anti-angel faction, who had nearly won the battle, now froze, their momentum shattered.

“Damn! An angel?”

Legends spoke of Kergo descending from Heaven. But only Nephilim could pass through Heaven’s gates. No true angel had ever appeared before.

Ikael continued.

“Stop fighting now and repent. Otherwise, God will punish you.”

The angel faction leapt to their feet, raising their arms and jeering at their enemies.

“Hah! Hah! How blessed we are!”

For them, the God they had devoted their lives to had finally answered.

“A punishment?” The anti-angel leader twisted his face in fury.

“What have we done wrong? Is it a sin to live? Is protecting your family a crime?”

“Silence.”

Ikael’s voice was unyielding.

“Fuck your lies!”

The anti-angel leader hurled a spear, but Ikael hovered too high to be reached.

“When our people starved, you never showed your face! And now you call us sinners? My wife died from a *loop* overdose! My comrade’s child is dead! Was that God’s will?”

“Yes.”

The Ankhera existed only to maintain the system so humanity could use the world.

“How can you understand the deep will of God?” Ikael said coldly.

They knew nothing of the sacrifices Heaven had made to preserve stability. They didn’t understand how dangerous those who denied God truly were.

“Those who deny God cannot exist in this world. But His followers will gain eternal life.”

“Give me eternal life?” The muscular man with the thick beard broke into tears.

“Who said I wanted to live forever? Save my son! I’ll die right now—just save my son! Why… why my son…?”

Ashur glanced at Ikael, who stood silent, and saw her fists trembling.

The man pleaded, voice cracking.

“Please… save my son. Why should that child die? Give the punishment to me. Why him…?”

Ikael’s eyelids twitched. Ataraxia flickered, as if it might vanish.

‘Why?’

He couldn’t bear it.

‘Why does it hurt so much?’

From within the archangel’s chest—where no wound had ever formed—pain surged, vast and unbearable, as if it came from beyond the universe.

“Ikael.”

Ashur spoke, her voice anxious.

“Shut up.”

Ikael bit his lip. Even that small sound echoed through the air. His eyes burned with an anger he didn’t understand.

“Everything is God’s will.” He hated himself for saying nothing more, as if the words had been forced into him.

“God’s will?” The anti-angel leader shouted. “What is it? Great! You’re omnipotent! You can give us eternal life!”

Hot tears streamed down his face.

“I lost someone I loved! My heart is breaking! I’m going mad! But your God—how careless he must be!”

The entire anti-angel faction raised their weapons and stood.

“Can’t you show even a single tear? Not even a flicker of sympathy?” The moment the thought struck, Ikael’s body lurched as if struck by a cannon blast.

“Huh!”

Ataraxia shattered. Reflected starlight trembled violently.

‘What is this?’

A wound deep in his mind tingled—raw, bleeding.

A desire to reach out, to dig through flesh and bone, even if it meant tearing himself apart.

‘No!’

Ikael clenched his teeth.

‘I am a noble archangel.’ As Ataraxia expanded once more, Galliant Island shook.

Volcanic activity surged 20,000 times its normal level. From Mount Toa’s crater, a colossal eruption of lava burst forth.

“Oh! Oh God!”

The angel faction, lost in hallucinations, worshiped the sky. The anti-angels scattered in panic.

Ashur acted instantly, using signal magic to evacuate the angels—she didn’t have time to question Ikael’s intent.

‘It’s a mission anyway.’

Lava swallowed everything. Volcanic ash fell like heavy snow.

It was over quickly.

But when Ikael returned, his eyes were dry.

Stop.

‘Huh! Whoa!’

It had happened before, but never this badly.

Most of all—

‘sick. The pain is unbearable.’

Only the cold gaze of Ankera could soothe it.

‘Ah, my God. What terrible sin have I committed? Why do you punish me so?’

Ankera never answered.

250 years later, a man arrived on Galliant Island, now buried in ash.

Tall, with steel-gray hair, he introduced himself as human.

“Maclein Guffin?”

Kergo was suspicious, but after witnessing the man’s Nephilim abilities, his attitude changed completely.

After God’s Punishment, the angel faction’s status had soared.

Guffin surveyed the still-ruined land.

“It’s miserable.”

It was Ikael’s doing.

“It’s not scary when my wife gets angry.”

Kang, the young chief, didn’t understand Guffin’s words but knelt anyway.

“Messenger of God, I accept your command.”

At that moment, Shirone felt a strange sensation.

‘That man is the future Kadum.’

Seeing someone he knew in Omega meant the vision was drawing closer to the present.

“The volcanic eruption was long ago, yet the land remains undeveloped. Poor.” Kang echoed Guffin’s words, bowing his head.

“What’s the problem? We praise God’s greatness. Everything is His will.”

“Yes.”

Guffin walked the island in silence for a long time before turning.

“Gather the people. It’s time to work.”

“Yes?” Kang blinked.

“Restore this place. You’ll have to live here anyway.”

Guffin dug into the ground, uncovering ruins buried beneath ash.

Every ten meters, human bones surfaced. The natives whispered prayers to God.

‘Found.’

Deep within the ruins, a self-luminous red marble glowed.

“What is that?”

“A Coordinate Recorder.”

It was likely the material used in the stargate the natives had once operated.

“A signal that broadcasts this planet’s coordinates into space?”

As long as it remained, Heaven’s army could attack at any time.

‘This must be the last one.’ So far, Guffin had found 73 such signals on this planet.

As light radiated from Guffin’s body, the Kergoins dropped to their knees.

“Oh, Ancherashi! Look down upon us!”

When the Miracle Stream activated, the soil vanished, revealing a vast underground space.

“I—I can’t do this!”

“I’ll make a door here.”

Kang asked, breathless.

“When you say door… do you mean the gate to Heaven, said to have been destroyed in the eruption?”

“Yes.” But Guffin wasn’t rebuilding it to open a path to Heaven—he was making a shield.

By reversing the coordinates, he would strike the impenetrable dimensional wall.

The Miracle Stream absorbed the signal body, underwent material transformation, and became a massive stone gate.

Later, it would be known as Guffin’s Gate.

‘Seventy-three doors installed on this planet. Enough to form a dimensional wall.’

Kang asked, “Messenger of God… can we now go to Heaven?”

“You need the Infinity Key to open this door. Remember—never damage the gate bearing Guffin’s name.”

“I’ll engrave it in steel.”

Since the appearance of a man named Ogent, Ankera’s suspicion of humanity had reached its peak.

‘The Heaven Expansion Project has failed.’

According to Guffin’s investigation, humans were vanishing from space. Sooner or later, this world would be next.

‘It starts here.’

After leaving Galliant, Guffin hovered high above, viewing the planet’s full diameter.

Small. Cozy.

The perfect place to continue what he and Ikael had begun.

“Humanity, the end of Omega is not far.”

Guffin raised both hands.

Across the planet, every Guffin Gate activated.

“Be strong.”

Pillars of light shot up from every continent.

“Seek the right. Don’t repeat the past. Remember—everyone has a heart.”

The pillars converged into Guffin’s hand. He clenched his fist.

With a thunderous ripple, an intangible aura spread, enveloping the entire planet.

A dimensional wall.

‘I can’t rely on this forever.’

To complete one final task before leaving, Guffin descended back to the human realm.

Within the stream of Omega, Shirone sensed a place familiar to all five senses.

‘The Kingdom of Tormia.’

Creas City—home of Alpheas Magic School, his alma mater.

“Oh, without any warning!”

The head of the Kreas Magic Association branch rushed in, flustered.

The world’s greatest mage, Maclein Guffin, had arrived. Even the full staff couldn’t handle the occasion.

“No fuss. I have something I wish to do quietly.”

“Of course!”

Even kings bowed to him.

Guffin sat on the couch, sipping coffee.

“A few days ago, I passed by a temple and heard an interesting story. There’s a magic school student with extraordinary talent.”

The branch head, recalling legends of the Three Emperors, the Seven Kings, King Lee, and the peak of power, quickly understood.

“Ah! You mean Alpheas Magic School. Yes—this is the first student in history to pass Level 7 of the survival test.”

Guffin set down his cup.

“Bring that child to me. Preferably without anyone knowing.”

Two days later, a knock came at a shabby inn—no one knew Guffin was staying there.

Guffin, gazing out the window, closed the curtains and turned.

“Come in.”

A faint murmur came from outside.

“It’s not even our first meeting, but she’s half-talking. Annoying.”

He hadn’t meant to eavesdrop, but Guffin’s ears missed nothing.

The door opened. He smiled.

“Excuse me. I was told a very important person wanted to see me… What?”

The young woman, still carrying a girlish look, blinked at Guffin’s inhuman presence.

“The Adrias family labyrinth?”

“Yes. But… …”

The first impression of the labyrinth, as Guffin had sensed it, passed through Shirone’s awareness.

I’m still a baby.

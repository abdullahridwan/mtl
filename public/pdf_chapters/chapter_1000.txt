Pandemonium Hell (1)

~*~

The sea roared as it mended her wounds. Shirone stood at its edge, gazing toward the horizon.

Far ahead, countless starlights flickered.

From this distance, they looked like distant stars. But as one drew closer, they revealed themselves as titanic surges of energy—shockwaves so vast they defied measurement.

‘Ikael is fighting.’

Angels were powerful because their abilities stemmed from pure concepts—origin principles unbound by purpose. The Archangel of Destruction existed only to destroy. The Archangel of Birth could create anything from nothing. Their strength lay in the infinite scalability of these concepts.

But Shirone, having mastered the world, now saw their one limitation.

‘Space.’

Uriel could not destroy what did not exist within the universe. Kariel’s creations, no matter how vast, were still bound to what could exist within it.

Ikael was different.

Amplification had the potential to transcend the universe itself—a power Guffin had once demonstrated.

Suddenly, the starlight on the horizon exploded outward like a spiral galaxy, then vanished.

Moments later, Ikael came streaking across the sea’s surface, flying straight toward Shirone.

Her body was battered, her face drawn with exhaustion. Yet her eyes held no regret—only the quiet satisfaction of a decision made.

“Ah…!”

Spotting Shirone, Ikael accelerated. The sea below split into a V-shaped wake, dozens of meters deep.

Light trailed behind her like tears. Without slowing, she crashed into Shirone in a tight embrace.

“Ahh!”

Shirone cried out as if struck—but no impact came.

‘It’s a mother.’

At the last instant, Ikael halted, absorbing all momentum into her own body.

For a moment, Shirone felt a long-empty void inside her finally fill. But then, guilt—sharp and sudden—pierced her heart.

“I’m sorry,” Ikael whispered.

“It’s not your fault,” Shirone replied.

Their words were still awkward, halting. But the emotions behind them were real.

“No. I killed you long ago. And even after I regained my memories, I ignored it. You don’t deserve a parent like me.”

Shirone understood. She could imagine the weight of that guilt.

“But you can’t live like this forever. Letting go of the past—it’s necessary.”

Ikael shook her head firmly.

“There are bonds you can’t just discard. Parents have children. And… seeing you without guilt terrifies me more.”

“Like,” Shirone said, lost in thought.

She gestured. “Come. Sit.”

Ikael blinked, surprised. She hesitated—afraid even this kindness might hurt—but then quietly obeyed, bowing her head as she settled beside Shirone.

Shirone looked down at her, then raised her hand.

A soft, smoky light flowed through her body, gathering in her palm until it shone with a clear, transparent glow.

‘This is my first step.’

As she placed her hand on Ikael’s celestial form, countless thoughts raced through Shirone’s mind.

‘You’re right.’

In a cold world, nothing could truly be undone. Pain only piled upon pain, endless and unrelenting.

That was why Nane wanted all humans to awaken—to see the truth and break free from suffering’s chains.

‘But…’

There had been a god with a heart.

A ray of light. Hope. The Messiah sent from beyond the world, who sacrificed himself.

Hexa.

‘If that’s who I am…’

Then she would reject that cold world to the end. She would carry every anger, every sorrow, and still say—

“Ikael.”

She would believe that some things *can* be undone.

“Your sins are forgiven.”

Unconditional. Unreasonable. Unshakable.

A love so vast it flooded Ikael’s entire being.

“Ah…!”

Ikael’s shoulders trembled.

It felt as if the entire universe had whispered, *It’s okay. You’re safe.*

‘I am truly a happy angel.’ The pinnacle of the heart. The love of absolute kinship.

She had received it—not from one, but from two.

“Wake up.”

When the purification ended, no guilt remained in Ikael’s heart.

But now, she felt guilt toward Shirone.

“How can you forgive everything—like this, for no reason?”

“I don’t know,” Shirone admitted.

“Maybe because I’m stronger. Is that hypocrisy? Maybe I’m just repeating what Guffin taught us. But it doesn’t matter. What matters is that you *can* be forgiven.”

Unlike law, the heart could change. And the world Shirone saw was one where such change was possible.

“Like Nane said—reality is painful. If it had never existed, there would be no suffering…”

“But didn’t we meet because we *did* exist?”

“I believe there’s still something missing—something that keeps the universe from closing.”

“Yes,” Ikael said, convinced.

“What do you want to do?”

“I’ll help. I’ll fight for the world you want.”

Her sincerity was clear now. Shirone no longer hesitated.

“If I were to ask a human how right they could be… I’d ask this.”

She looked up at the sky.

“Do you know how vast the human mind can become?”

When Iruki arrived at Basuka, the immediate shock had faded. The city was in recovery.

“Iruki.”

Shirone came out to meet him.

Seeing her here—outside the door, when they’d been together in the carriage just an hour ago—felt strange.

“How’s the atmosphere?”

“Calmer. Not good, not bad.”

Ymir had been sealed. But the cost was high. Two of the Five Stars were dead. Garcia, commander of the Valkyrie Corps, was gone.

They walked toward Crete, the capital of Tormia, talking as they went.

“What about Allied casualties?”

“Not officially counted yet. Army Command will announce it soon.”

When Pony reached the royal castle, Amy and Nade ran to her, hugging the rookie tightly.

“Iruki.”

In the quiet comfort of reunion, Shirone turned to Minerva.

“How’s Taeseong?”

“He hasn’t woken up. Of course not. Gaia poured all her power into the five planets. Even stopping Mir like that—it’s a miracle. Don’t expect more from Taeseong.”

Shirone’s expression darkened.

“More importantly, the Demon Realm is the real problem. I’ve located most of the corps commanders, but dealing with them won’t be easy.”

Minerva understood immediately.

‘Right. Shirone isn’t the only one here.’

Right now, Shirone’s consciousness was stretched across the world—wherever the Twelve Apostles had first taken root.

‘What kind of experiences is she having?’

Fighting a demon corps commander to the death? Wiping the tears of a child who’d lost their family?

‘Maybe both.’

It was impossible for a human mind to endure such a burden—unless thought itself evolved beyond human limits.

If gods existed, wouldn’t their minds resemble Shirone’s?

Seeing Shirone smile faintly, Minerva looked away, as if stabbed.

“Anyway, this is why Taeseong came to Baska. The Demon Realm isn’t safe anymore. He wanted to meet Lian in person.”

“Me?” Lian pointed at himself.

“Yes. The demon remnants are still numerous. And the corps commanders capable of opening Pandemonium are still alive. This might be why.”

“Hmm.”

As Lian pondered, Gaold suddenly coughed up blood.

“Ouch! Kuck!”

Only a small amount, but it looked like he had little left to give.

Minerva, who’d been supporting him, turned and shot him a glare.

“Here. You take him. I’m done.”

Miro glanced at Gaold, crouched on the floor and still coughing, then turned away.

“Huh.”

He walked to the corner and leaned against the wall, indifferent.

Kang Nan stepped forward, eyes opening.

“That’s real.”

Sein placed a hand on Kang Nan’s shoulder.

“It’s okay. We’ve got him.”

As Sein lifted Gaold’s arm over his shoulder, Kang Nan moved to the other side and helped him up.

“Rest. I’ll take you to Zulu’s room.”

Zulu was also injured—gravely wounded by Ymir, hanging between life and death.

Gaold didn’t look up as he shuffled out, dragging his feet toward the door.

Miro watched him go, cold-eyed. When the door closed, he spat.

“Disgusting.”

Gaold’s suffering meant nothing to her—not since he’d shattered the perfect image of omniscient foresight she’d once held.

‘It’s over. No matter how obsessed you are with me, you’ll only suffer.’

She was just angry.

“I’m going to rest. Let’s end here. We’ll talk more tomorrow.”

No one objected. The world was still reeling.

‘The Asmodian War. Havitz’s assassination. The Flower Garden’s collapse. The Elemental Bomb. The Army of Heaven. Ymir’s seal…’

All in one day.

‘I’m tired.’

Everyone felt it.

Thirteen miles north of Baska, the earth stirred.

Beneath the corpses of Allied soldiers, something moved.

With the sound of grinding stone, a body turned. Paimon clawed her way out of the ground.

“Turn it off!”

Dirt and mucus burst from her throat like a fountain, unending.

“Haha! Haha!”

Her face was unrecognizable—eyes gone, nose swollen, the only movement in her jaw.

“Kaak! Kaak!”

But Paimon was the longest-lived of the demons. She kept shaking, and slowly, her body emerged.

Soil poured from her belly, swollen a hundred times its original size.

“Done. It’s okay.”

She’d been flung the farthest by Shirone’s Agape—launched into the deepest underground.

The place she’d landed: 120 meters below the surface.

When the light consumed her, she’d instinctively fled to the darkest space she could find.

She didn’t remember much.

“Damn Yahweh!”

If demons arose from human emotion, then Shirone’s compassion had the power to purify even the most extreme feelings into stability.

‘Definitely fatal.’

She was reduced to a torso—no limbs. Her stomach bulged, packed with excavated soil.

“Kwuq! Kww!”

She vomited dirt again and again—until it was easier to tear open her own belly.

“Whoops.”

As she grew, Paimon threw back her head and laughed at the night sky.

She had survived.

“I won! Yahweh!”

The plan had hit a setback. But sooner or later, the scattered corps commanders across the continents would open Pandemonium.

“We won.”

As her body began to rot, it twisted into a grotesque, hospital-like mass.

The disease spreading from her would ride the wind southwest—eventually infecting the entire world.

“It is Satan.”

She melted into the moonlight, thick eyelids twitching.

“Lead the world into despair.”

Paimon’s corpse scattered like smoke, flying toward Baska as a shapeless reaper.

The disease was called Emotional Disease—Emotion Scale.

An unprecedented catastrophe, triggered by specific conditions in the brain.

The moon rose, but the air still held the heat of the archangels’ battle.

The largest scars were three enormous grooves, each stretching over ten kilometers.

From the ends of those grooves, Satiel, Uriel, and Rayel approached the center.

They met in silence.

Finally, Uriel spoke.

“…You left.”

Satiel’s fist shot out—but Rayel caught her wrist.

“Calm down.”

“Let go.”

Satiel glared at Uriel.

“I’m serious. Let go. Or I’ll destroy you first.”

Uriel spoke into the air.

“There’s nothing worse than blaming someone else for your defeat.”

“Blame someone else?”

Satiel shook off Rayel’s grip and stepped forward.

“You didn’t fight your best! Don’t think I don’t know. Are you betraying Heaven too?”

Uriel stayed silent.

Satiel raised her hand. The Judicial Halo spun at terrifying speed.

“I’ll dismantle you.”

Just as she prepared to strike, Uriel said:

“Is Ikael…?”

Her fingers twitched. Uriel locked eyes with Satiel.

“Why did you leave us?”

Leil spoke softly.

“You chose blood over Heaven.”

Only after saying it did he realize how absurd it sounded.

‘I see. This *is* strange. Angels—archangels—having human children.’

“I’m not being sentimental,” Uriel said. “Ikael choosing Hexa means he abandoned Heaven of his own will.”

Satiel’s patience snapped.

“So what? That’s why you’re angry? What are you trying to say?”

“Not what I want to say. I never thought it possible. That an angel could betray Heaven.”

Rayel asked, “So you just… sent him away? Only because you don’t understand?”

“I will fight Ikael.”

Uriel turned.

“But this war is mine now. Not Heaven’s. My will alone will define it.”

Satiel didn’t understand. But Rayel saw it as inevitable.

‘Uriel…’

He had always been heavy-handed, awkward in speech. But Ikael had always done as he pleased.

‘The one who felt the deepest loss… might not be Satiel. It might be Uriel.’

No—perhaps even himself.

‘Uriel has left. Only Satiel and I remain. If that’s the case… then I…’

Honestly, neither Rayel nor Ikael had wanted to choose anything from the start.

‘That kind of existence— Ikael.’

In a world without direction, he was the only path. For an angel who didn’t know the meaning of existence, he was the only meaning.

“You go too,” Satiel spat.

“I don’t need a conflicted archangel. And you don’t want to be with me anyway, do you?”

Rayel looked at her—then turned and walked away without a word.

Satiel ground her teeth as he vanished into the dark.

“Are those things angels?”

The asteroid began to glow red again.

“What kind of archangel! The noblest being in the universe! In the end, you’re just jealous of humans!”

Her twin, Mara Gallio, reached out.

“Satiel! You must preserve the asteroid…!”

“Ikael! Ikael! Ikael!”

Red tears streamed down Satiel’s face.

“Why is it always Ikael? What makes me different? Ikael is forgiven no matter what he does—why can’t I?”

Had anyone in the world ever received such universal love as Ikael?

“From now on, I am the archangel.”

She wiped her tears with her arm.

“Hear me, all forces! I will punish Ikael for betraying Heaven! I am the noblest angel! I alone will uphold Anchera’s will!”

Her voice thundered—but only an empty echo answered.

“Ikael will destroy all the humans he loves! I will restore the law!”

Galio watched his master scream into the void, heart breaking.

‘No matter how loud she shouts…’

Her voice could not shake the world like Ikael’s.

After washing at the inn, Shirone walked down the hall to Amy’s room.

It was past midnight. But judging by the presence at the door, few were sleeping.

“Amy.”

At the knock, Amy opened the door, smiling.

“Come in.”

Shirone saw the sorrow behind it.

“I came because I was worried.”

“I’m fine.”

Garcia had been more than a commander. He was a teacher, a mentor, the one who had shaped her life.

“I’m sad. But I won’t cry. I promised. I don’t want anyone to cry when I die.”

She also sensed the war wasn’t over.

“I met your mother.”

It was daytime on the other side of the planet. Crises erupted worldwide. But this was the only story Shirone could tell with warmth.

“Really? You mean… your mother?”

“Yes. Ikael. It’s awkward now. But it’ll get better. I’ll introduce you.”

Amy beamed.

“Of course! What happened?”

They sat side by side on the bed, sharing everything they’d been through.

Amy was cautious when speaking of Nane. But Shirone understood.

“I think I get it. I came here through Nane’s philosophy too.”

“I still feel guilty. I was shaken—not by you, but by what you represent. Yudo, who brought this up, felt like he was betraying you…”

“Yes. I felt that too. I left you and your friends for humanity. That’s right, Amy.”

Shirone placed a hand on her shoulder.

“What’s hard for me is hard for you. That’s the heart. And now you know.”

*Ultima might not be that far away,* Amy thought suddenly.

“Shirone.”

Her voice grew thick.

Shirone turned.

Her hair, burned short in the fire, couldn’t hide who she was.

Silence stretched. The quiet pulled them closer.

Just before their lips met, the door burst open.

“Amy, what are you— Wait, what?”

Tess froze. The two flinched, faces burning to the ears.

Tess laughed, embarrassed.

“Oh—Shirone’s here too. Did I interrupt?”

“Haha, no,” Shirone said, waving.

Amy shot Tess a mischievous glare.

“You *know* what you’re asking.”

Tess grinned.

“Come on. Let’s go. Everyone’s gathering.”

“Now? Aren’t you tired?”

“Nade said all troops—except the wounded—should meet in the cafeteria. It’s been a while. We should welcome Lian properly. He doesn’t know the magic school friends well. Neither do I.”

“Ah, right.”

He was close to everyone in Shironeya. But some of his friends barely spoke to each other.

“Okay. Let’s go.”

As they walked, noise spilled from the cafeteria down the hall.

“Uh? Shirone! Shirone!”

Nade, already two beers in, stood up chewing meat, grinning wide.

“Ooh! You two came together? See? I *knew* it! I told you they’d end up together!”

Amy narrowed her eyes at the instigator. Nade clutched his stomach and laughed.

Dorothy brought drinks and food.

“Thanks.”

If Shirone hadn’t been there, this rookie might not exist.

“Thank you. We fought together.”

Laughter filled the room. Faces flushed in the warm atmosphere.

But while others shared stories of battle, Lian sat quietly, sipping his drink with a small smile.

“Nice to meet you,” Iruki said, turning to him.

“I’ve heard a lot from Shirone. Well, and from hundreds of reports. Let’s be friends.”

When Lian’s glass emptied, Iruki lifted the bottle and refilled it.

Everyone watched, smiling. Lian politely held the glass with both hands as he accepted.

An awkward silence followed. The rookie stared, clearly not expecting such formality.

Tess frowned, shaking her head.

‘Ugh. That stiff guy.’

He was Shirone’s friend.

Since Lian was close to the lord, no one dared act too freely.

“Greetings,” Lian said. “I am Lian, knight of Shirone. I wish you all well in the days ahead.”

Amy smiled.

“Hey, Lian. You don’t have to be so formal. We’re all friends. And we’re all about the same age.”

“Sorry if I broke the mood. But I can’t be the first to commit a breach of etiquette.”

Shirone gave a friendly smile.

*I’m still nervous.*

The reason he had used the spirit of alcohol to broach the topic was to ease the tension left over from battle.

But Lian was different.

Even in a relaxed setting meant to protect Shirone’s peace of mind, he never let his guard down.

Lian was dependable—someone you could trust with your life. But Shirone still felt cautious about letting loose while he was around.

“Yes, Lian. I hope you’re comfortable. Anyway, we’ll be fighting as a team from now on.”

Only then did Lian reach out and take the glass with one hand.

“Good, good! Let’s drink until our noses go crooked today. A toast—cheers!”

After another round, the conversation at the table shifted to the Ultima system.

Iruki spoke up. “What are you planning to do? Honestly, the Ultima system is the only real breakthrough we’ve got—but is it even possible? To unite the human spirit, I mean.”

Shirone nodded. “I have a few alternatives in mind. If Mr. Miro arrives tomorrow, I’d like to discuss them with all of you.”

Amy blinked in surprise. “An alternative? Just the fact that there’s another option… that’s shocking.”

“Yeah. It’s not perfect, of course.”

Tess, who had been idly blowing across the mouth of her bottle, leaned forward. “Like what?”

Shirone paused, then stood. “Then let’s try it here.”

The others fell silent, turning their attention to him.

He brought his hands together and closed his eyes.

A light formed between his palms, growing steadily until it became a clear, radiant glow.

“Miracle Stream? What… what is this all of a sudden?” Tess opened her arms wide, looking around the room with playful eyes. “Your sins?????”

The light from Shirone’s hands spread through the dining room.

“Sahanora.”

“Ah??????”

A powerful, stirring sensation washed over everyone. Ten minutes passed in that charged silence before, suddenly—

“Uhhhh!”

Nade let out a loud cry.

“I’m sorry! How can love like this even exist in the world? I don’t understand… I want to live kindly. Aaaah!”

Iruki groaned, exasperated. “Oh, stop crying! What kind of sins did you even commit that you’re bawling like this?”

It was probably just the overwhelming effect of a divine-level phenomenon.

“This bastard!” Nade shot back. “You cried too! And who was the one sobbing at me in the temple? You’re the crybaby!”

“… The idea of living a good life feels like a lie now,” Iruki muttered, wiping his eyes. “Guman.”

Shirone chuckled. “Haha, it’s not that simple. But the light of agape does purify the human heart. Why that matters will be explained at the formal meeting tomorrow. For now, know that it can help build an integrated mental system.”

Dorothy tilted her head. “But isn’t that too technical? I wasn’t thinking about systems—I was just deeply moved and satisfied.”

Shirone smiled. “Because the situation is what it is. I didn’t do this to impress anyone. This has to work. After tomorrow’s meeting, I’ll go on a pilgrimage across the world to spread this light.”

“A pilgrimage?”

Nade and Iruki’s eyes lit up at the same time.

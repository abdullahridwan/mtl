Simultaneous Incidents (1)

~*~

Rufist spoke first.

“Let’s think about it carefully.”

The confrontation had been brief, but the image of the Army of Heaven remained vivid in Lufist’s mind.

“The subjects gained independence in Heaven, and the eternal beings were mobilized by the Ministry of Ra Enemy. But with the collapse of the Council of Ten, they scattered.”

The placenta would have died.

“In the end, all that stood before us was raw power—angels and maras, fairies and giants.” Even so, it was true that such forces could not be countered by human strength alone.

Sein added, “Heaven isn’t the only concern. The elite demons still linger across the world. And sooner or later, Pandemonium will open completely.”

Shirone spoke as the mood grew heavier.

“We have three main goals. First, join the humanitarian relief efforts currently underway at the Ivory Tower. Second, subdue the demons and prepare for the demon world. Third, subdue the armies of Heaven—and stop Nane.”

Nade frowned.

“Stop her? Didn’t you already block her?”

“We discussed many things. Perhaps everything in the universe. But one thing is certain…”

Shirone turned to Amy.

“I love Amy.”

That single truth had been enough to make him step back from total confrontation.

“Huh?”

As everyone who knew Amy turned to look at her, Tess asked, bewildered,

“What are you talking about? Why her all of a sudden?”

Amy’s face flushed.

“That, that—”

Though he’d never been given the chance, he’d missed the moment to explain himself properly.

“So, when I first opened the Fire World…”

Conscious of Shirone’s gaze, Amy recounted everything, speaking as if walking on thin ice.

“Hoo.”

After hearing her explanation, Nade understood why she had backed down when facing Shirone.

“Still, that’s impressive,” Tess said.

“It was a battle for the fate of the world, and you still chose Amy.”

Amy protested.

“It’s not like that! It was a philosophical dilemma, so I—”

“It’s fine,” Shirone interrupted, smiling.

“Everyone has a heart. And as long as there’s a heart, nothing is absolute. Amy’s feelings could change at any moment.”

“That’s true, but I don’t mean to change!”

“I believe you. What I’m saying is, it all comes down to the mind. If I had chosen the world over Amy…”

Recalling the confrontation, Shirone admitted honestly,

“Maybe I would have been the one shattered into fragments.”

It was a possibility no one could rule out. Miro looked up at the sky, his expression grave.

“I’ll be watching from somewhere. To see if one’s heart is truly worth more than the entire world.”

“Yes. It won’t take long. The Nane I faced is nearly there. Maybe even now, she could return as a Buddha.”

If only one mind could remain clear.

“That’s why simultaneous events are necessary. If we don’t unite the hearts of humanity quickly, we won’t truly be able to defeat Nane.”

Garcia asked, “Do you really think it’s possible? All humans are different. The criteria for defining the self vary from person to person. To be honest, even I’m not sure I can belong to such an integrated mental system.”

Of course, he felt a strong duty toward humanity—but his compassion wasn’t broad enough to embrace every human being.

“I know it’s difficult,” Shirone replied. “But if it weren’t possible, I wouldn’t be struggling so much.”

Dante asked, “Then is there a way?”

“No, I can’t guarantee anything. The reason we’re here is because every factor of good and evil has played its part. Even evil shapes the world along one axis. If demons hadn’t spread, would the World Alliance have ever formed in human history?”

“Hmm.”

Garcia nodded. It made sense.

“It means the times are changing fast.”

“Yes. The wheel of good and evil is accelerating toward the end of the world. You can predict the laws, but you can’t analyze the workings of the heart. We do what we can—and accept the consequences.”

Armand asked, “What can we actually do? No matter what choice we make, if it doesn’t lead to results…”

There was one thing Shirone had realized after losing Havitz.

Life was such a complex web of factors that nothing ever went as planned.

“I ask only one thing,” Shirone said. “Do what your heart tells you to do. What you truly want—just do it.”

High above the atmosphere, surrounded by endless darkness, Nane sat cross-legged, gazing down at the planet.

‘I couldn’t defeat Shirone.’

Because philanthropy had been stronger.

‘But do you know, Messiah of this age? The reason Buddha’s gong never surpassed Ai…’

Maybe it was because the evil in the world had not yet fully emerged.

‘Of course, it’s the power of good.’

The labyrinth kept evil at bay.

‘But for how long? If the world becomes even more corrupt than it is now, even the Buddha will lose his mind.’

Nane’s eyes filled with sorrow as she watched the planet.

‘Personal love that spreads into the world—you will be watching from here. Indeed…’

‘…whether we deserve to exist.’

Allied officers followed Shirone’s words.

‘What I truly want to do.’

You could go see your family. Or, if you feared death, you could abandon the war.

Shirone said that looking inward was the beginning of the Ultima System.

And each person’s will, once formed, would merge into philanthropy—transforming into love that embraced both good and evil.

“Then, let’s go.”

Surprisingly, many of those gathered under one banner left the battlefield.

Miro said bitterly, “Human beings are certainly diverse.”

“Ultima is a high-level spirit that recognizes all personalities. That’s right—integration comes next. A person who doesn’t know their own mind cannot understand the minds of others.”

After answering, Shirone asked, “Will you stay, Mr. Miro?”

“Of course. This is the center of the battlefield. So who’s left here…”

Naturally, Lian, Nade, Amy, and Tess remained with Shirone. Among the Twelve Apostles, Super Dragon Aitra and Thunder Dragon Blitz held their positions.

Armand and Quan had left. Sein stayed behind to assist Miro.

Dante, Lilia, and Eden had chosen to pursue the remaining demons, while Garcia and the allied forces worked to repair Basuka’s walls.

And the most remarkable part was this:

“Shirone, you’re everywhere.”

“Yes. Depending on how far the observer’s range is set, I think the area around Basuka can be covered as a spirit zone without quantum transmission.”

“Hmm, that’s right. Moving through space would be easy. The Ultima System is definitely effective.”

“Hehe, then can’t I just give you one Shirone?”

Everyone turned to Tess, puzzled. She continued, flustered.

“Why, why? That’s funny! Imagine—”

The longer the silence stretched, the more anxious she became.

“No, not that kind of imagination! Are you really thinking that? Seriously?”

It was a joke born of Tess’s honesty—but her honesty sometimes caused problems.

Shirone liked her that way.

“Ha ha, yes. I trust you.”

For the first time in a long while, he felt like he was truly back among his friends.

“Iruki will be here soon…”

Shirone’s expression suddenly hardened.

“What’s wrong, Shirone?”

“…No, nothing.”

He forced a smile, but unlike moments before, his eyes were calm—too calm.

Miro guessed the truth.

‘It’s not happening here.’

Somewhere far away, a tragedy had befallen Shirone.

“Big!”

Countless angels gnashed their teeth against an unstoppable force.

In the Hand of God, Shirone’s heart had whispered, *Please fly away.*

Given that intent, the strongest of the Army of Heaven would have been the most deeply affected by the Hand of God’s power.

‘It’s formidable.’

Ikael, having crossed the central continent and entered the northern one without realizing it, stared at the rushing sky.

Shirone. Hexa. Hand of God. Guffin. Ankera’s death. The position of Archangel.

“Chief Angel.”

The three Archangels and Maras, freed from Hexa’s influence, gathered around Ikael.

“Yes. Report.”

“The army seems to be regrouping under Ashur. However, Ymir and the giants appear to be acting independently.”

Uriel asked, “Should I stop them?”

“No. Leave it be. I’ll handle it myself.”

Satiel stepped forward.

“Archangel, there’s something I must ask. Why didn’t you act when Ymir first arrived at Basuka and opened the gate for attack?”

As Ikael remained silent, Satiel’s voice grew sharper.

“I know you’ve recovered your memories. If the duty of an archangel is—”

Ikael cut him off with a sharp, gong-like sound. The distant archangels quickly retreated.

“Satiel.”

At the cold tone, Satiel’s asteroid trembled.

“Yes, Mr. Ikael!”

“Whatever decision I make is for President Cheon’s reasons. Are you questioning my authority?”

“No. Forgive me.”

The archangel’s chill was absolute. For now, no one would dare raise the issue again.

But then—

“Ikael.”

A wisp of light coalesced before the angels’ eyes, and Shirone appeared.

“You…”

Satiel’s judicial halo spun wildly, radiating unknown fury.

And Ikael’s astral body, still and silent, seemed to tremble.

‘What? What don’t I know?’

“Shirone.”

Ikael moved slowly.

“Are you alone?”

“Yes. We have things to discuss.”

Ikael’s gaze flickered, as if scanning behind him.

Silence.

Then, a cold voice echoed through the air.

“I have nothing to say to you.”

A look of sorrow crossed Shirone’s face.

‘Ikael…’

So much had happened.

Ataraxia had been taught in the First Heaven. In the second battle, she had even stopped him.

Why was she so cold now?

‘Really… You’ve recovered all your memories.’

Because you are a mother.

The gentle emotions that once stirred with quiet hope were gone.

All that remained was the stark imprint of tragedy across the celestial bodies—and the uncertain gazes of angels who still doubted themselves.

‘It might be motherhood…’

Even knowing that, Shirone tried to speak with confidence.

“Ikael, I’m not weak anymore. You don’t need to fear anything.”

Ikael’s gaze was clear—but Shirone felt something inside her snap.

It was likely the limit of what she could endure.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about. As the Archangel—”

“It’s not your fault.”

Finally, Ikael’s starlight—long strained to the breaking point—shook with overwhelming intensity.

“I died…”

Shirone spoke with all his heart.

“It’s not your mother’s fault.”

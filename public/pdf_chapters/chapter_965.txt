Chapter 965

Fanaticism (2)

~*~

The King of Desire, Gustav Havitz, 17 years old.

Sein and his party, linked by the psychic resonance in his heart, suffered severe mental damage.

Sein bit his lip until it bled.

Damn it. It was a misjudgment.

Focusing only on the possibility of tracking Havitz’s location, he had failed to account for the nature of the wave itself.

No—was it really?

Sein’s years of experience weren’t enough to excuse overlooking such a variable.

Calculated. It must have been pointed out somewhere in the stream of consciousness, even if it didn’t remain in memory.

The real problem was the sheer horror of the heartbeats emitted by Havitz’s atrocities.

You can’t prepare for what you don’t know. Havitz’s desire was a force as primal and formless as fire itself.

The quality of fire cannot be defined by shape. But just as everyone knows fire…

All humans carry a heart of Habit deep within.

Even if I like it, I quickly grow to hate it.

A being of chaos who loves so intensely he will commit any atrocity.

That point—the territory dominated by Habit.

Sein opened his mouth.

“Eden, calm down.”

As the Iron Wheel spun rapidly, Eden’s mind, clouded by fear, slowly cleared.

“Ah…?”

Eden’s expression shifted as she realized something had been sacrificed to achieve the current effect.

“Yes. Sorry.”

The name Yora echoed in her mind, worn thin from repetition, but there was no time to dwell on guilt.

Remove Havitz. It was a simple goal.

When everyone’s resolve had hardened, Quan, Armin, and Meirei launched their attack simultaneously.

“Hmm.”

Even as enemies closed in, Havitz merely tilted his head from side to side, completely at ease.

His calmness was unnerving, but Armin steeled himself.

He’s alone. There’s not a single demon protecting Havitz. If I kill him before—

Before the thought finished, the spirit zone activated. Slow magic enveloped Havitz.

As the pendulum of time slowed, Mayray fired a sonic cannon.

Done!

Still, all eyes remained on Havitz.

With body and mind restrained by slow magic and sound waves, Quan moved in to strike.

I want to kill you brutally, but—

Quan, slipping from Havitz’s perception, raised his sword.

Be lucky.

Just as the ten-meter gap closed in an instant, Havitz’s mouth slowly opened.

“Sigh…?”

Time seemed to stretch, but from the sound and expression, it was clear—he was about to yawn.

Sein’s mind raced.

You’re relaxed. But that act ends here. You have no choice but to die.

Every analysis through Cheolryunan predicted Havitz’s death.

But.

“…Wow.”

For Havitz, no such future existed.

The sky above the wasteland flared, a lightning bolt scorching the earth, followed by a thunderous roar.

Kurlrrrrrrung!

The sound came after the strike—Sein survived by instinct—but the explosion directly in front of him was too much to endure calmly.

“Keugh!”

Armin cast flicker magic to escape. Quan and Mayray threw themselves aside with all their strength.

“Suddenly—what the hell…?!”

Armin, stumbling from the shock, turned and saw black smoke rising into the sky.

“Hmm. I didn’t sleep last night, so I’m tired.”

The smoke cleared. On the scorched ground, Havitz scratched his neck, just as he had at the start.

“What’s wrong? What did you do?”

As the group stared in silence, Sein slowly raised his eyes to the sky.

Are there any clouds?

It wasn’t magic.

The natural elements had predicted this lightning strike long before it happened.

Just… at this exact spot.

It was simply that Havitz was there.

Even if you could predict the weather, the precise point where lightning would strike was beyond human calculation. No one could have planned this.

Only desire.

I was tired from not sleeping—so I just wanted to stay here.

An accident triggered by chaos.

That was all.

“They’re strange. You called me here, yet you’re not doing anything. Aren’t you trying to kill me?”

Havitz yawned again, then lowered his pants and urinated on the spot.

“Pee.”

Despite acting like a child—making noises with his mouth as he peed—Sein and the others could only watch.

Though defenseless, they knew this too was dictated by his desire.

Should I cast slow magic again?

Armin hesitated.

If I cast slow magic, there’s a high chance lightning will strike again. No—there’s no chance. It *will* strike.

Maybe right above me.

Wait—on the other hand, if I *don’t* cast slow magic, will the lightning not strike?

Armin shook his head.

Wrong order. It has nothing to do with my will. The lightning will strike regardless.

That is nature’s providence.

So, to be precise, I only began thinking this way *because* the lightning struck earlier.

If not for that bolt, I wouldn’t even be considering this now.

Right now, I don’t want to cast slow magic. If my thoughts changed due to the lightning, then conversely…

Should I cast it *now*?

But the reason I’m thinking this is because my thoughts changed due to the thoughts I had earlier.

In the end, it spiraled into an endless loop.

“Damn it!”

Armin had a mild temperament, but this time, frustration rose to his throat.

How can someone like this exist? A being with no determined future.

Sleep when he wants, eat when he wants, kill when he wants, strike when he wants…

Completely innocent in all things.

In the end, Havitz could do whatever he pleased.

“Armin, don’t overthink it.” As Sein spoke, the rotation within the Iron Wheel accelerated violently.

“I’ll handle this.”

The sky darkened. The Sun and Moon Halo—a dual halo—began spinning at the speed of light.

“A fixed future. Once I see it, I understand it completely. Whether it’s lightning, wind, or earthquakes—you must block it.”

“Pee. Pee.”

Amidst Havitz’s careless urination, Quan lunged again.

If Sein can block the future, I’ll kill Havitz before he realizes it.

At that moment, a purple aura rose from Havitz’s body as he looked down at the ground.

Quan, activating twelve external gravity forces, circled endlessly around Havitz.

Then, as the gravity fields vanished, he landed—limping forward a few steps from inertia—before stopping.

“What?”

The moment Quan spoke, everyone blinked, disoriented.

Lyria asked herself:

“What are we doing here?”

To kill someone.

I know that. But who?

They hadn’t even realized Havitz’s Vanishing technique had activated.

“Ah, that’s cool.”

Havitz, finished with his business, pulled up his pants and grabbed his longsword.

“Then shall we kill you now?”

Sein was the first target—not because he was the most threatening, but because his face looked the unluckiest.

“It looks like a corpse.”

Yet the decision made by Havitz’s desire was rational, even logically flawless.

“First one.”

The longsword sliced through the air, aimed at Sein’s neck.

It stopped with a *clink*, as if blocked by an invisible barrier.

“Ugh! Ugh!”

Eden, standing beside Sein, activated a barrier with both hands outstretched.

“Hmm.”

Havitz, having silently withdrawn his blade, leaned forward and stroked Eden’s mustache.

“Can you see me?”

His elongated face blocked her vision completely. Eden could neither see nor hear.

Why am I shielding?

She had acted purely on reflex, responding to the terrifying wave of murderous intent transmitted through God’s Frequency.

A tremendous wave of the heart. Her hands trembled, but Meirei’s ability was undeniable.

“I can’t last long!”

Eden’s face turned red.

She had said something absurd—something she herself found impossible.

Like approaching a stranger in broad daylight and making animal noises.

It was a statement that would never have left her lips if not for the waves in her heart.

“Yes.”

Everyone felt as if they had heard the answer.

“I’m missing something.”

Sein’s nose wrinkled.

I’m activating the Sun and Moon Halo. That means you have something to fight for. But for some reason, I missed that fact.

Hypnosis? Hallucination? Mind magic? Law?

Or am I going crazy? No. Absolutely not. The murderous intent is real.

Without God’s Frequency, without being the world’s foremost expert, I wouldn’t have noticed.

I decided to kill him.

It doesn’t matter who he is.

“Kill!”

Everyone moved the instant the word—most closely tied to the concept of Habit—was uttered.

Don’t think.

Like machines, they rushed toward the source of the murderous intent.

“Huh?”

For the first time in his life, Havitz looked embarrassed.

This can’t be happening?

It was the result of their combined effort—but the key was Meirei, with God’s Frequency.

“It’s here!”

The intent to kill had turned chaotic, but Quan’s ears clearly picked up the target’s location.

Honestly, it still feels insane.

As a child, I used to imagine fighting monsters with a stick.

I’m an adult now.

I’m not ‘pure’ enough to turn imagination into reality.

And yet—it cuts.

It was the decision of a swordsman who had spent his life training and wielding a blade.

The wind howled, and Quan’s sword sliced perfectly through the air.

…Did it.

Everyone froze. Quan, landing belatedly, looked back at the cut and frowned.

Is it another illusion?

At that moment, Sein spotted twelve black-souled figures remaining where Quan’s blade had passed.

“Armin! Stop!”

The command burst from him, as if his tongue had memorized it from endless simulations.

“Stop!”

Time froze.

Twelve figures in black robes appeared.

“It’s hell!”

The logic was still flawed, but information about Shiok surged into Sein’s mind.

At that moment, Havitz canceled Vanishing.

“…Quite interesting.”

The spark of sudden realization jolted through Sein’s brain.

He clenched his fists.

Big!

How could I forget Havitz?

“I don’t know what.”

As the twelve Shiok parted to either side, Havitz stepped forward.

“Can you read my mind?” Sein and the others remained silent.

Just imagining ways to locate him could yield hundreds of methods. No need to reveal the truth.

“Would it be fun?”

Havitz’s eyes curved like a seagull’s wings.

“Did you think I wouldn’t know?”

Know the location. Read the mind.

Only someone with insight could leap across the logical void between those two ideas.

“It’s my heart?”

Because every action of Sein and his party was the result of Havitz’s own mind and chemical reactions.

“It’s a feeling. Do you really think knowing the location alone would provoke such a response?”

A quiet voice came from Sein’s lips.

Yes. Before he became an atrocity. Before he became chaos. He was the emperor of the Gustav Empire.

Havitz was no fool.

“So that’s what you mean.”

Madness flickered in Havitz’s eyes as he studied every member of Sein’s party.

“Whose ability is it?”

At that moment, a terrifying thought struck Sein.

It’s a predetermined future.

Is it really something humans can interfere with?

Even if we do interfere—how would I know? Time is just a whole.

Sein’s mind spun.

In the end, the incident where Havitz left the battlefield was also part of the flow of time. Why? Because the atomic bomb project will succeed in the future? Or not…

The accident stopped.

Pale as blank paper, only one question floated to the surface.

Why is Havitz…

Could it be that he chose this place over the battlefield?

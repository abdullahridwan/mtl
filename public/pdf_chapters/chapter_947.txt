After the dinner with the royal family, Lupist and Fleur walked in silence for a long time after leaving the palace.

Horses. Horses. Horses.

It was the stress of having to interpret every word through the lens of political calculation.

“I’m sorry,” Fleur said.

Lupist frowned. “What?”

“Just… I know I didn’t have to do it. But hearing the royal family’s dirty talk again—it annoyed me.”

Lupist exhaled sharply and turned his head. “You’ve been getting sentimental lately. I know you’re facing a massive event, but—”

“Sentimental?” Fleur’s voice was flat.

“You wouldn’t have done it if it had been someone else. If it had been the former chief of staff instead of me, would you have apologized?”

Jane’s face flashed in Lupist’s mind. He wouldn’t have said a word.

“Because it was a perfect combination,” Lupist muttered.

Realizing his rudeness, he quickly composed himself and asked coldly, “What’s the situation?”

A faint smile finally appeared on Fleur’s face. “With royal approval secured, we can now deploy an additional 200,000 castle guards. If we position the drum line at the front, we can lure the demon armies.”

“How much time until the gate opens?”

“According to Dante, sixteen hours. We need to finalize all preparations within that window.”

“I’ll relay it to command. Anything else?”

Fleur pulled out something he’d been holding back. “You might have noticed—Pony. Her laugh. It was the same as ours.”

Lupist nodded. “She’s different from the others. Graduated from a magic school, has real social experience. Participating in a world beauty contest counts, I suppose.”

“What should we do about her?”

It was a short, unsettling question.

*If she’s noticed…*

*If we want her gone, we need to act now. Exposing her to the rest of the royal family would ruin everything.*

“What do you think?” Fleur asked.

“Eliminate her. She’s a talented mage, but talent doesn’t matter here. It’s not about odds. She’s already dead.”

“…I’ll send someone. He’s smart, but his magic isn’t strong—better to use a hitman than an assassin.”

“Yes. Now, the next—”

Before Fleur could continue, Pony appeared on the street ahead.

The two men stopped abruptly, but their expressions remained unchanged, like machines.

“Are you heading to the Magic Association?” Lupist asked, his tone polite but distant.

“Miss Pony, what are you doing here?”

Pony looked between Lupist and Fleur, then lowered her body in a deep bow.

“Please save me.”

It was unthinkable—a royal family member bowing like that—but Lupist didn’t flinch.

“You want me to save your life? What’s happening?”

“I don’t know,” Pony whispered, biting her lip. “I’m asking for help because I can’t even imagine what’s coming.”

*Sharp. Only ten minutes to realize she couldn’t handle the threat and had to surrender everything.*

*The king is an illusion…*

She was the key to uniting the nation quickly.

*It’ll be fine.*

They could rebuild with a puppet king.

“How much are you willing to give up?” Lupist asked.

Pony hesitated, then straightened. “Everything.”

To become the queen of a new era, she was ready to give up her humanity.

*Mental murder.*

To survive in a world overrun by demons, you needed this kind of madness just to speak of it.

“I’ll send you a message,” Lupist said as he passed her. “It won’t take long.”

Daris, packing his gear in the royal castle barracks, felt as if the sky had cracked open.

*Dog-like royalty. Now the capital garrison is deploying a drum line?*

His comrades felt the same.

“This is strange. We didn’t hear anything yesterday. Summoned out of nowhere…”

He had a bad feeling.

“Everyone gather!”

At the battalion commander’s order, grumbling soldiers grabbed their drums and rushed to the parade ground.

“Listen carefully! We’re marching rapidly for four hours to Denan Plain. There, we’ll serve as bait for the armies of Hell!”

Murmurs spread through the ranks.

“I have a question! Why are castle guards being used? Each division already has its own military band!”

“The entire Tormia military band is assembling at Denan Plain.”

The murmurs grew louder.

“The entire band?”

Roughly 3,000 people.

“We’re joining them, but don’t worry—this isn’t serious. As soon as we lure the demons, we’ll fall back and let the infantry take over.”

That was a relief, but the danger of being on the wire still loomed.

*Five percent chance of death? That’s 150 out of 3,000…*

Everyone was thinking the same thing.

“Return to your barracks and pack. We regroup here in twenty minutes! Dismissed!”

The soldiers scattered. Daris hurriedly checked his gear, his mind unsettled.

“Daris.”

He turned at the voice. Hanea, a royal court lady, stood at the entrance.

“Huh? What are you doing here?”

They’d been childhood friends. He’d confessed to her once. She’d politely refused, saying she needed time.

He’d suffered heartbreak, and his comrades had teased him about it.

“Can I talk to you for a minute?” His squad leader clapped him on the shoulder. “Go ahead. I’ll cover for you.”

Pushing down his gratitude, Daris stepped outside with Hanea.

“What is it? You’re on duty.”

“I heard. You’re being deployed?”

As a royal handmaid, she got news fast.

“Ahaha… Well, I mean… It’s not like I can say no. It’s something I have to do.”

“It’s dangerous.”

“I know. But I’ll be fine.”

“I’ll wait.”

Daris blinked. “Wait? For me?”

Hanea blushed slightly, then nodded. “I’ve thought about it a lot. You were right. It would be nice… to start over.”

“So… you mean… us?”

Her head dipped again.

“Come back quickly. Don’t step in front of me. Just do whatever you can to survive.”

Daris took a deep breath.

When he returned to the barracks, every head turned.

“How’d it go? Talk things out?”

They saw the fire in his eyes and froze.

“I *will* come back alive,” Daris said, certain.

After the four-hour forced march, the Wangseong drum unit joined the others. A line of nearly 3,000 drummers stretched across the vast Denan Plain. The sky was calm.

Daris clenched his drumsticks, fighting the tremble in his hands.

*Stay calm. It’s nothing. I play for an hour, then fall back. That’s it.*

The band leader raised his flag.

“Power!”

Sticks struck drums in perfect unison.

“March!”

Doo doo doo doo doo doo!

The fastest drumbeat thundered across the plain.

The drum line advanced a kilometer, with infantry following behind.

*One kilometer from the lifeline.*

When the demons came, they’d have to sprint at least 500 meters to escape.

After thirty minutes, the result became clear.

Demons with red-tinged skin emerged over the horizon, shaking the earth beneath them.

*There are so many!*

They filled the entire skyline.

*I can’t fight that! I need to run!*

Everyone felt it, but the band leader’s retreat flag hadn’t moved.

Cuckoo! Cuckoo! Cuckoo!

An earthquake-like tremor ran through the ground. The drummer beside Daris burst into tears.

“Aww! Aaaaaa!”

Daris roared, refusing to break. “Hold on! Retreat soon! Just hold on!”

The moment 3,000 lives focused on the band leader, the retreat flag waved.

“Shit!” Daris dropped his drum, ripped off the strap, and sprinted toward the infantry.

The race for survival had begun.

“Ha ha ha! Shoot! Shoot!”

Arrows made of vertebrae shot into the sky and rained down like bones.

The distance was still great, but several drummers fell, pierced through.

“Cheak!”

Daris gritted his teeth and pushed his legs harder.

*I can live! I can live!*

The band leader’s timing had been good. Fewer casualties than expected.

*Five percent? No—three. Never go in there! I can’t go in!*

Hanea is waiting.

*Let’s get married. Let’s go on vacation. Dinner at a nice restaurant… Yes! I’ll do so many things with her!*

When he saw the infantry within reach, tears spilled over.

“Ugh! Ugh!”

Then—

“Ouch!”

A thick bone arrow punched through the back of his neck and burst out the front.

Daris collapsed forward, clawing at the arrow embedded in the ground.

“Ouch! Kuck!”

He looked up, dazed—and met the eyes of his squad leader, turning back.

“Live… live… save me…”

He reached out.

The squad leader’s pupils flickered.

“Please…????”

Then, without a word, he turned and ran.

“Oh… no…”

God was indifferent.

*Why me? Out of all people, why me? Hanea just said she’d wait!*

What sin had he committed to deserve this—this young man who had just begun to love?

*I don’t want to die! Please, someone…*

He lay there, the arrow’s momentum the only thing that had carried him this far.

*I can’t die! It can’t end like this!*

But his legs wouldn’t move.

“Aaaaaaa!”

He pulled at the arrow with all his strength, but the barb in his neck wouldn’t budge.

“Ha ha ha.”

As the infernal armies advanced, they trampled his body into the earth.

“Ha ha ha! Kill! Kill a human!”

Just before his mind shattered, Daris had one final thought.

*If only I hadn’t come here.*

Even if it meant losing his job, if he’d just run away with Hanea.

*Why?*

Did he really think he could survive?

*Because she said yes? Because the odds were only five percent?*

No matter what you want, no matter how much you hope—the wheel of fate turns without mercy.

*I had a chance…*

A chance to give up everything.

Just before he died, Daris thought he heard a voice in his mind.

*save yourself*

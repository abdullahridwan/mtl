“Damn it!”

The demons charging toward Dante’s information castle twisted their faces in fury.

A defense battle had erupted out of nowhere.

This was no makeshift fortress. It was a fully realized structure, every element meticulously engineered for defense.

“Attack first!”

As the army of Hell reached a consensus, 3,000 gun gates unleashed a storm of magic.

Though pure in form, the firepower—9,000 rounds per second—was beyond anything they could withstand.

“Cheak!”

The leading demons collapsed, and those behind them, stepping over the corpses, fell just as quickly.

The piled bodies began to mark a boundary—one defined not by terrain, but by sheer destructive power.

‘Is this even possible?’

A conglomeration of magic.

Too complex for any single human to manage. But for Dante, it was both his specialty and his duty.

“Rush! Push forward with numbers!” To seize the castle without hesitation, the demons had no choice but to sacrifice themselves.

“Go! Go!”

They threw themselves forward, dying by the dozens, inching the front line closer to the Information Castle.

“Done! Just a little more!”

When the vanguard reached within forty meters, Dante propped his feet on the table and lit a cigarette.

“Activate the automaton.”

As he flicked fire from his fingers, the entire castle lifted more than a meter off the ground.

Cuckoo! Cuckoo! Cuckoo!

The leading demons, drenched in blood, stared in disbelief.

“What—what?”

No. They refused to believe it.

“The castle is moving.”

Slow, yes—but seeing an entire fortress shift on a massive scale was nothing short of spectacular.

The demons watching could barely contain their rage. They wanted to tear apart whoever stood nearest to them.

Dante exhaled a long plume of smoke, leaned back in his chair, and studied the multi-vision display.

“As long as it’s fast, it’s perfect.” The automaton synchronized the coordinates of every magic circle embedded in the castle, moving them all at once.

It was as precise as clockwork—but its speed depended entirely on Pascal’s arithmetic calculations.

‘Even if I had ten years… no, five more…’ He felt a pang of regret. He might have built an impregnable system.

‘No use making excuses.’

Dante smirked, the cigarette dangling from his lips.

“Because someone else already did it.” Thinking of Shirone—and the widening gap between them—Dante began manipulating the castle in earnest.

The demons chasing the distant Information Castle burned with fury.

“The demons are going crazy!”

No matter how slowly the castle moved, the cost in lives was staggering. For every meter advanced, hundreds died.

It was clear that Jin hyung, the demon commander dominating the horizon, had fixed his attention on the Information Castle.

Lufist, watching from the Tormia camp, muttered with a wry expression.

“Move the castle?”

The demon army numbered over 100 million. This maneuver couldn’t shift the tide completely.

“But it’s not nothing.”

If only one person had achieved this, the efficiency was over 10,000 times greater than conventional tactics.

“There are many talented people in Tormia.”

Garcia, commander of the temple’s Second Corps and Amy’s superior, approached unnoticed.

Lufist spoke with polite brevity.

“At one time, I was considered a talent who could represent the kingdom. And I still stand by that… but—”

“That’s Shirone,” Garcia said. “A shame.”

No magician in the world was unaware that Yahweh hailed from the kingdom of Tormia.

“I have no pride in that,” Lufist replied. “In a way, the child doesn’t belong to the kingdom.”

He was already beyond their system.

“No point dwelling on what you can’t control. That’s what matters now.”

Lufist pointed toward the Information Castle.

“The Third Division will support Mercury. No matter what, Dante must be protected.”

“Yes, sir!”

The division commander, fully aware of Dante’s strategic value, immediately repositioned his forces.

Dante, monitoring the rear through the multi-vision display, descended the stairs to the castle wall.

“Time to begin. Disarm the automaton.”

Once the movement calculations halted, the castle’s other systems could be activated.

Firepower surged instantly. The demons in front of the wall were crushed without mercy.

“Kuaaa! Aagh!”

Screams blared through the speakers, and the screen flashed red.

Enemy forces had entered the courtyard.

At that moment, Dante manipulated the device.

“Done. I’ll break it.”

As the demons reached the gate and raised their massive hammers, a voice echoed from the door.

- Please enter a password.

“What? Password?”

On closer inspection, a panel with numbers and symbols was embedded in the doorknob.

The demon, gaping, reached out to press the keys.

Bang! Bang! Bang!

A howitzer fired from within the fortress. Shells exploded in front of the wall, obliterating the vanguard.

“You stupid child! Just break it down!”

When the leader fell, the second wave surged forward, wielding enormous scythes.

“I’ll crush you!”

Dante grabbed the microphone.

- The password is 1111.

The demon froze, scythe raised. A flicker of hesitation passed through his eyes. Then, slowly, he lowered his weapon and tapped the number 1 four times.

The door didn’t budge.

“Ah, sir!” the demon called out, confused.

“What? It won’t open!”

Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang!

A second volley of howitzer fire annihilated the second line.

Dante’s voice followed.

- … You have to press enter.

Rage flared in the demons’ eyes.

“Is this bastard playing with us?”

From then on, it was pure madness—every weapon they had hammered at the gates.

“It’s damn hard!” The gate’s durability far exceeded expectations. But the sheer mass pushing from behind could not be stopped.

“Broke through!”

The gates groaned open.

Dante stood as warning alarms echoed through the control room.

“Activate the avionics system.”

The demons pouring in like vomit froze at the sight inside.

“Is this even a real castle?”

Before them stretched a vertical stone path lined with grass, fountains, and stone statues.

Above the fortress, surrounded by a moat, the Tormia Third Division was deployed.

“Whatever. I’ve got things to kill. Let’s go!”

The moment the demons advanced, mines hidden beneath the grass detonated. Gun barrels emerged from the stone statues.

Fountains spewing combustible liquid ignited, hurling fireballs in every direction.

“Run! Run!”

It was one trap after another.

As they reached the drawbridge over the moat, a hologram of a beautiful woman appeared.

- Hello. Welcome to Dante’s Castle!

“Shut up!”

Knowing it was fake, one demon still swung his claws at the image. His feet touched nothing—then the drawbridge vanished.

He plummeted into the moat. The green liquid bubbled, eating through his skin.

“Kuaaaaa!”

Poisonous snakes rose from the depths, spraying acid in all directions.

‘What the hell is this place?’

The demons wondered how much farther they’d have to go—how many more traps—before they reached the castle’s master.

“Damn it.”

Just before death, a cold shiver ran down their spines.

The brilliance of the Information Castle lay in its subtle manipulation of space—twisting the demons’ path by at least 0.1 degrees.

‘0.1 degree.’

Too slight to see with the naked eye. But Rufist was satisfied.

‘It only takes one misstep.’ Even if the starting point looked identical, the divergence grew with distance.

‘Iron-clad!’

Waves of iron surged across the battlefield. Garcia unleashed the flames of Inferno, burning through enemy ranks.

In the sky, Fleur’s Phoenix Pavilion spat fire according to a precise algorithm.

“Ha ha ha! Humans! Humans!”

The battlefield resembled hell itself—but for the demons, it was paradise.

‘This is the problem. Morale is hard to break.’

Beyond physical strength, the demons’ aggression was a temperament humans could never match.

Seeing the allied forces retreating, Enox, king of the fairies, raised his arms.

“The enemy of man is the enemy of the elves.”

Rufist had redirected Hell’s army toward Baska. Even a rough comparison showed their advance line had shifted over thirty degrees.

“Open the flower garden!”

The ground trembled. Over three hundred colossal structures unfurled their steel petals.

A shadow stretched for kilometers. Energy blazed across the petals, building to an explosion.

“What in the world is that…?”

Enox lowered his arm.

“Extermination!”

A flash, powerful enough to rival a MAP weapon, seared the earth. Three thousand demons vanished instantly.

“Disperse! Avoid the flash and break through!”

What was one burst of fire?

No—the formation scattered into near chaos. From the attackers’ perspective, the difficulty had skyrocketed.

If a human had piloted the flower garden…

“There’d be no problem at this level.”

Flarino, linked to the living flower through the small world window, swiftly adjusted the targeting.

With control as fast as nerve impulses, it was like crushing ants beneath her feet.

All sound was swallowed by the roar. The demons were incinerated by the mere flash of light.

Rufist, having retreated to the flower field, observed the battle closely even in the midst of combat.

‘It’s going better than expected.’

The rate at which demons were being wiped out by the fire tribe far exceeded projections.

‘Should I be glad?’

By rough estimate, over 150,000 demons had died in thirty minutes.

Enough firepower to obliterate an entire small kingdom in half an hour.

The problem? That number was less than 0.1% of Hell’s total army.

It was endless.

‘Meanwhile, allied casualties are around 4 percent.’

As time passed, the imbalance would only grow. But even that wasn’t the real issue.

‘They’ll just keep coming.’

No matter how many you kill, the enemy feels infinite.

Like standing under a waterfall—no one thinks about when the water will stop.

‘That’s a billion units.’

This was exactly why Iruki had risked everything to pursue the atomic bomb project.

This was the deep reason.

Vulcan, positioned at the heart of the army, rested his chin on his hand.

“Hmm.”

Through the chaos, he perceived the entire battlefield as a single, flowing current.

‘It’s tempting. Raw, uncontrolled fire.’

He watched the rapid flashes, then nodded.

“In terms of reaction speed relative to scale, it’s beyond machine level. Humans would be slower.”

A conclusion formed.

“You’re the fire tribe.”

It was impossible to explain unless you understood small world creation.

Vulcan called out, but Sumodo’s attention was fixed on the Information Castle at the horizon’s edge.

“What are you looking at? I want to go.”

Sumodo snapped back and turned to Vulcan.

“No. When I travel, I usually take pictures of all the hotspots.”

Vulcan raised an eyebrow.

“I’d let you go, but not now. Focus on the ancient weapons. Where’s my weak point?”

Sumodo, with clear vision, studied the three hundred flags and pinpointed the most erratic movements.

“Northwest. Twenty-third, forty-seventh, eighty-eighth. Those three are different. They’re scared.”

Vulcan nodded.

“Seize them. Even capturing one will destroy all nearby flowers. Go with Jetaro.”

“Understood.”

As Sumodo and Jetaro moved out, elite demons followed.

Natasha pointed at herself. “Vulcan, me?”

Vulcan was deep in thought, but there was only one answer.

“Kill him.”

Mach’s Knight.

At the sound of that name, Natasha turned toward the flower field, her eyes lighting up.

“It’s good.”

Lian’s spirit, rising from the center of the flower garden, was pulling her in.

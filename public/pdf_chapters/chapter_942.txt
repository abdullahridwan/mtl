“I can’t go on like this!”

At the report from the triangular Mara, the angel turned to look back at the distant spire of Arabot, forty kilometers away.

In a place where neither heavenly barriers nor the seven heavens had yet formed, only Ankera’s sacred ground stood—and from it, the mighty dragon emerged.

‘This is the last line of deterrence.’

The angel felt fear grip him as he saw once more the endless waves of figures approaching from the horizon.

A force of nearly one billion people advanced, converging from all directions.

“If you’re going to act, act. If you’re going to speak, speak.” Mai Shin, representative of the Gaia people, stepped forward with a sorrowful expression.

The angel stood isolated in the center of the pond, surrounded by maras.

A raised fist. A silent challenge. A question hanging in the air.

“What should I do?”

The core problem was this:

The source of the angels—their very foundation—was now in the hands of the enemy.

And that concept defined what it meant to be Gaian.

“Elysion.”

The unified spirit of Gaia, whole and indivisible.

“Shall we finish this?” When Myshin raised his hand, the eyes of one billion Gaians ignited and linked into a single Idea.

A massive rift tore open in the sky. Clouds spiraled backward like a waterfall.

“I… that…”

The moment the angel saw the colossal hand of light emerge from the black tunnel, his luminous body trembled.

“It’s not like we were playing around.”

Once driven to the brink of extinction by Code Argones, they had relentlessly refined Ultima.

“Hand of God.” As the finger of light flexed with eerie fluidity, the river flowing across the ground began to evaporate.

“Just stop it!”

The angels launched a full-scale assault against the hand—large enough to crush Arabot in a single grip.

It wasn’t a barrage of spells or fire, but a collision of primal concepts, the same forces that had shaped the early universe, striking against the Hand of God.

“It’s ridiculous.”

Why was it called the Hand of God?

“Because it is omnipotent.”

It was the hand that had created the universe.

The Hand of God, tearing through the original concept, seized the angel between its index finger and thumb.

“Kyaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaah!”

The being shrieked in agony under the crushing pressure. The angel clenched his eyes shut and roared.

“You dare to mock the name of God… Kuck!”

Between thumb and forefinger, the angel’s body burst into dust with a sharp pop.

“You can come in anytime.”

The Hand of God curled its fingers as if caressing the air, then turned and presented the back of its hand.

The angels watched in horror as the five fingers slowly unfurled like a blooming lotus.

The surface of the hand shimmered with five colors, as though it contained every natural phenomenon in existence.

After a moment, a sphere of light rose from the center of its palm.

“Big!”

The sky and earth vibrated in unison. For the first time, the angels felt motion sickness.

“Photon Cannon.”

A blow forged from the combined Ideas of one billion Gaians. A divine strike against the world.

“It is over.”

The instant the Hand of God moved and the Photon Cannon lanced forward in a flash, the angels screamed.

“Calm down! I can stop it!” The phenomenon itself wasn’t extraordinary.

‘You can do it! Just a little more!’

‘It’s only moving fast—nothing more!’

With a roar,

“Ah… …”

Light flashed. Everything turned white.

Without experiencing it directly, the angel instinctively knew: this attack had the power to obliterate half the planet.

Qurrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrr

In the blink of an eye, lightning struck Arabot.

Judicial Halo.

‘Ragnarok!’

Uriel, who embodied the concept of destruction, surged toward the Photon Cannon at sub-light speed.

Though the event unfolded at near-light velocity, the angels, with their heightened perception of time, saw it clearly.

Uriel’s body began to disintegrate as he entered the core of the Photon Cannon.

‘Even Uriel-nim… …’

Just as despair settled in, the Photon Cannon exploded, releasing a massive shockwave.

“Big!” The angels who had fled to the spire of Arabot frowned, staring ahead.

‘It’s fine.’

Though ripples spread outward from the blast, the scale was far smaller than expected.

Maishin looked around with a bitter smile.

“… You’re wasting your time.”

Taeseong, breathing heavily, wept tears of blood as he clasped his hands together.

“Are you really going to see this through to the end?” Gaia could redirect planetary shockwaves into incarnations. The fact that she was shedding bloody tears meant the Photon Cannon’s force equaled that of a meteorite impact.

“Be quiet. We’ll handle it.”

Planets didn’t matter.

But it was unsettling to see Gaia—long an ally of humanity—suffering like this.

‘To explode a light-speed mass using the Hand of God… …’

As Maishin watched Uriel with narrowed eyes, Leo stepped beside him.

“It’s not possible with just one Archangel.”

Leo inhaled deeply, then opened his eyes and fixed his gaze on Arabot.

“Zoom in.”

The Hand of God curled its index and middle fingers into a circle. A lens of light formed, magnifying the distant battlefield.

As the tens of kilometers of distance collapsed into apparent proximity, Ikael’s figure came into view.

“Archangel Ikael.”

And behind her, the faces of the six other archangels were now clearly visible.

Hope flared in the angels’ eyes.

“All the archangels have gathered.”

If the Gaians defined the world, the eight archangels represented the totality of the objective world.

In essence, Ankera had proposed a direct clash—between the Akashic Records and the Ultima System.

“As expected, they won’t fall easily.”

Leo crushed his cigarette and snorted as Penny approached, supporting her neck with both hands.

“Hmph. It was a mountain I had to climb anyway. We have the Hand of God. It’s as good as having an archangel.”

The power of Elysion, once formless, now concentrated into a single vessel, far exceeded the sum of one billion Gaian minds.

As Uriel returned, the eight archangels swiftly advanced and hovered above Gaia.

Ikael amplified her voice.

“You who pretend to be gods—why do you persist in this meaningless war?”

Myshin replied.

“Who started this fight? We only wish to leave this world.”

“This world is your resting place. Find joy here. Live in eternal peace.”

“That is your belief.”

Myshin raised an eyebrow.

“There are no more mysteries in this world. Why? Because we have defined them all. I will not live in a cave, staring at shadows of the outside.”

“Do you truly wish to leave?”

“Because we must.”

Ikael spoke as if making a final decision.

“Leave it to Ankera. We will find a way for you to depart this world safely.”

“Our answer is… …”

The Hand of God, linked to the external world, clenched its fist and raised a single finger.

“This is it.”

It was a divine refusal.

Ikael’s brow furrowed. The seven archangels behind her lunged forward in unison.

“Fine! Then come!”

Leo immediately shifted stance and raised his regime.

‘Divine Transcendence!’

As one billion Ideas activated, the Hand of God surged forward, fists clenched.

“Wow!”

Metatron activated his Judicial Halo as fists of light tore across the world.

‘Exist.’ Information spiraled into the halo like a whirlpool, forming a spherical black hole.

Aaaaaaaang!

Gravity so intense it consumed even light—but Gaia’s will was stronger.

“Haha! With just that?” Ikael’s voice rang out.

“Ataraxia.”

Her halo expanded to an unprecedented size. Metatron, sensing the surge, intensified his presence.

“Damn it—bend! Catch!”

The Hand of God spiraled along the Gravity River and was sucked into the black sphere.

“Ragnarok.”

With a thunderous roar, Uriel transformed into a streak of light and charged toward Gaia.

“I refuse!”

One billion Gaians raised their arms as a Law Barrier erupted, sealing off all intrusion.

“Who dares?”

Ikael demanded.

“Do you reject God?”

As the concept of destruction was amplified by Ataraxia, Uriel’s mind teetered on the edge of collapse.

“Yaaaaaa!”

The moment he collided with Gaia’s barrier, radiating pure destructive instinct—

Zeng!

The Law Barrier flared visibly. One billion Gaians staggered backward in unison.

“Keugh!”

All grimaced. But worse were the cracks now spreading across the barrier.

“Cheondo Eunha Ryun.”

The spinning Wheel of Paradise, sucking in the air, slammed into the already fractured Law Barrier.

Blood trickled from Myshin’s nose as a roar—inaudible to mortal ears—exploded from within.

“Damn!”

Uriel had broken through, but Gaia’s focus remained on Ikael.

‘As expected, the archangel is the real threat.’

Even Ideas could not replicate the concept of amplification.

‘Because it straddles the boundary between the outer and inner worlds.’

A bridge from nothingness to meaning.

‘Sorry.’

If Ataraxia had been fully implemented, the war with Ankera would have ended long ago.

“Finished with your leftovers?”

Uriel, having shattered the Law Barrier, raised his talisman of paradise and reignited his spirit.

“Come!”

The Hand of God’s form was reforming, but Ragnarok activated faster.

Uriel, surging forward like thunder, tasted victory.

‘I’ve won.’

Tens of millions of Gaians would die just from his passage.

‘Yes?’

Wind.

‘Wind?’

The speed was so great it should have outpaced the atmosphere. Puzzled, Uriel turned his head.

And in the next instant, the air ripped open—and a man’s face appeared.

“Pasaeng!”

The punch, delivered with a laugh, snapped Uriel’s head sideways.

Aaaaaang!

The angels watched in shock as Uriel skidded ten kilometers through the air.

“Oh dear.”

Guffin, who had appeared and thrown the punch, said with a grin.

“I can’t let you have all the fun.”

“How do you… …?”

Not just the angels— even the Gaians were stunned by the giant’s sudden appearance.

Maishin asked, “What’s happening on the battlefield? Did you decide to break from the Ultima System? Have you changed your mind?”

“It’s true that things have changed… …” Guffin said, looking up at the angels.

“But I have no intention of joining the system.”

“Then why… …?”

At that moment, Uriel charged from afar.

“Aaaaaaa!”

Rage burned in his Judicial Halo—humiliated by a human blow.

“Surprised? Excited?”

The Gon of Paradise arced through the sky.

“Destroy!”

Guffin raised his hand. Light particles gathered instantly, forming a thin, cubic film.

“Turn it off!”

The impact from the Gon of Paradise vanished—absorbed silently.

Uriel tilted his head.

‘What harmony?’

Was there anyone outside the Ultima System who could block Ragnarok’s power?

“Guffin, that… …?”

At Myshin’s question, Guffin smiled.

“Hexa.”

The hexahedral film dissolved into mist, gently pushing Uriel back.

As radiant light enveloped his body, Guffin looked up at Ikael.

“Are you still going to stop us?”

Ikael’s voice turned harsh—old wounds resurfacing.

“It’s only natural to punish those who deny God and claim divinity. And this has nothing to do with you. Do you wish to join them?”

Ikael paused, then asked again.

“What?”

Guffin extended his hand.

“I can take everyone.”

The angels stood frozen, expressions blank. The Gaians exchanged uncertain glances.

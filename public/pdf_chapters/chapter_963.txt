Chapter 963

In the Flower Field (4)

~*~

Shirone stood in silence, watching the vision unfold. Ymir had not been able to destroy Guffin, but the mere fact that he had halted Guffin’s movements for a time had fulfilled his purpose.

The tragedy began on a single day.

Ikael had run—desperately, with all his strength—cradling a child in his arms.

Ankera had used the Akashic Records to strip Ikael of his power. Then, the heavens turned against him. Countless angels and maras descended, attacking without mercy.

Ashur, Lethe, and Ramus—three guardian deities sworn to Ikael—fought with everything they had. But without Ikael’s power, they could not last. In the brutal clash, two maras were destroyed. Ashurma, the last survivor, was left mortally wounded.

Ikael and the child were captured and imprisoned in Heaven. Soon after, the execution was set.

A vast crowd gathered in the square, pointing at Ikael and screaming.

“Punishment for the fallen angel! Pain! Extinction!”

Though scorned by those he once ruled, Ikael cared only for the child.

“Please! Kill me instead! Save my child!”

The maras roared back.

“Silence! What kind of angel are you? Upright spirit? You’re a disgrace to creation!”

Shirone wept.

‘Ikael.’

Even though this was only their second meeting, the sight of Ikael sobbing broke something inside him.

The crowd’s hatred was absolute.

“Kill! Kill! Kill the corrupted creature!”

Kariel, in particular, stared at Ikael with eyes burning like midday sun—beyond contempt, filled with fury.

‘He bore a human child?’

Shirone didn’t even want to understand how such a thing was possible.

‘The most noble archangel… reduced to this?’

It felt like the desire of a beast.

“Please… Aiman… please…” Ikael pleaded, turning to the archangels.

But they all looked away.

Only Kariel met his gaze—and the planet trembled.

‘Then why do this?’

Why would the most powerful, most revered angel need such a fragile, mortal thing?

‘Then what am I? What are we to you, Ikael?’

Every angel present likely felt the same.

“Eung-aeng… ugh…”

The child cried, sensing death.

Shirone knew it wasn’t just instinct. The child was responding to Ikael’s voice.

“Begin the execution!”

The Angel of Punishment, Oddell, stepped forward. His three-pointed mara raised a massive axe.

Ikael panicked as flames from the mara’s breath reached the child.

“No! Stop! Aaaaa! Aaaaa!”

His amplification powers were sealed, but his scream tore through the sky, raw and endless.

“Huuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuu—”

Satiel, crouched in the corner of the Buddha’s dwelling, clutched his head as the sound reached him.

“Aaaaaa! Aaaaaa!”

Each scream felt like starlight shattering in the void.

“How long will you keep this up?” Methiel, the Archangel of Union, entered.

She saw Satiel, half-mad and trembling, and her expression darkened.

“Is this what you wanted? Now you play the victim? Do you really feel guilt?”

“Get out,” Satiel whispered, his voice ice.

Methiel’s patience snapped.

“As expected, you’re unfit to be an archangel. Too weak. You followed Ikael blindly, then betrayed him, and now you act wounded? What kind of angel are you?”

She stopped.

Satiel had risen without her noticing. In his eyes burned a murderous intent so cold it froze the air.

“What? Say it again. I’ll kill you.”

“Am I wrong?” Methiel shot back. “This sin was Ikael’s. He bore a human child. I did what an angel must do. Ankera-sama praised me. But what happened?”

Satiel found an escape from his pain.

“Yes… humans are the problem. Those insignificant lumps of meat ruined everything.”

Satiel, once thought the most human of all angels, now looked at the world with the cold pride of unyielding law.

“Yes,” Methiel answered, subdued.

She knew better than to provoke him further. He might break the angels’ greatest taboo right here.

“Beheading!”

At the command, the three-pointed mara’s axe fell.

The child’s head was severed.

“Aaaagh! Aaaagh!”

Ikael’s scream, half-mad with grief, tore through the heavens. Even the archangels flinched.

“My baby! My child!”

Tears of blood streamed down Ikael’s face as he howled, his voice stripped of all dignity.

“These Goyans!”

The archangels and the crowd turned to him—this time, not with hatred, but with something deeper.

“What crime have I committed?! Open the White Watch! I’ll reveal everything myself!”

“Trillion? Ha! Do you still think you’re an archangel?”

Enraged by the mara’s mockery, Ikael’s eyes burned red.

“I am not guilty! The sinner is Ankera! I’ll tell you all he’s done!”

‘He’s completely lost his mind,’ every angel thought.

“Listen! Ankera is not a god! In a meeting with a Guffin named—”

Time stopped.

Memories began to vanish from every being’s mind—angels, humans, even the maras.

At that moment, Shirone understood.

‘When I first met Ikael in Heaven…’

He had committed a great crime and was imprisoned in Arrabot. But no one remembered what it was.

‘It wasn’t Ikael’s sin.’

Ankera had erased it—not to hide Ikael’s crime, but his own.

‘God broke his promise. I lied.’

What greater proof was there that the world was unstable?

Ankera, driven to the brink by Guffin, could not reset the system.

‘Resetting is also a chance for Guffins.’ So Ankera chose to erase only specific events from the Akashic Records.

But such partial erasure created cracks. Left unchecked, the entire system would collapse.

Shirone gently stroked Kael’s sleeping face—peaceful, memoryless.

‘Forget it.’

Perhaps that was the only mercy left.

Humans, stripped of memory, lived on as before.

Angels carried out their duties, uneasy but silent, haunted by gaps in their minds.

‘I have sinned.’

But Ikael alone remained confined in Arrabot, unable to take even a single step.

‘I gave Ankera-nim an incurable wound.’

All that remained was guilt.

‘What was the sin?’

Meanwhile, the other archangels hunted the last surviving Gaian—MacLean Guffin.

The Ultima system could not be dismantled so long as even one Gaian remained.

‘Guffin… I know him, but I can’t remember.’

It was like the information had vanished from an asteroid’s core, leaving only a hollow feeling.

Fighting. Feuding. Screaming.

‘It feels like that.’

But on the other hand…

‘Happiness… did we have that too?’

That made no sense.

The only certainty was that they had known each other for a very long time.

‘Anyway, I’m no longer an archangel. I’ll atone for my sins in silence.’

On a night lit by a crescent moon, Ikael rose from his bed.

“Who are you?”

Beyond the shadow where moonlight did not reach, a figure stood—human, yet tall as an angel.

“It’s me.”

When Ikael saw Guffin’s face, he froze.

“Gaia? How are you here?”

As Ikael tried to stand, Guffin smiled and raised a hand.

“Stay. Just sit.”

As if by magic, Ikael found himself obeying.

“I’m sorry. It was all my fault. I couldn’t keep my promise.”

Guffin knelt, grasping Ikael’s wrist.

If this man was truly the only Gaia, there was no need to ask his name.

“Guffin,” Ikael said, voice cold. “How did you get here? I’ll capture you and deliver you to Ankera.”

The words cut through Guffin like a blade.

‘You’re my everything.’ He wanted to scream that they had loved each other, that their hearts had been bound.

Instead, he wiped his tears.

“I came to say goodbye.”

Only Guffin knew the depth of Ikael’s grief over losing the child.

‘You don’t need to remember. Just forget forever.’

If Ikael could be spared further pain, Guffin was willing to vanish.

“Are you willing to give up your life?” Ikael asked, as if suddenly remembering something.

“No—why would you say that?”

Guffin couldn’t bear it.

“Because I love you.”

“Nonsense! This is just another trick to destroy me! Even now, you—”

Ikael’s rage died as he felt something wet on his cheek.

“Why?”

Tears of light streamed down like a waterfall.

“Why am I crying?”

Guffin looked at him, eyes glistening.

“Memories fade. But emotions remain.”

That’s the heart.

“So don’t be sad if you don’t remember. I’ll carry your heart for you.”

Ikael’s tears floated into the air and settled in Guffin’s palm.

“Emotions remain?”

Ikael was dazed.

‘Why won’t the tears stop? Why does it hurt so much? What did this human do to me…?’

“You don’t have to understand. As long as your heart remembers, one day you’ll know everything. And then…”

Guffin gently stroked Ikael’s cheek.

“I hope you won’t feel sad.”

Ikael trembled. He stood, then leaned forward and softly kissed Guffin’s forehead.

‘This feeling… this touch…’

It felt familiar.

No—not just familiar. It felt like something repeated across a lifetime.

“Goodbye, Ikael.”

Guffin’s body dissolved into light and vanished.

The room fell silent.

“Ugh… uh… uh…”

Ikael collapsed, tears flowing endlessly, as if his mind had shattered.

The place Guffin fled to—where So Jeong-hwa’s ark had arrived—was Shirone’s planet.

‘It’s changed a lot.’

Humans born from the bodies of giants looked different from their ancestors, yet shared the same life direction.

They must be descendants of the Gaians.

‘Is it still in the primitive age?’

Colonies of early civilization were emerging, but they were far from the truth.

Guffin turned away from the civilized world, where only bloody wars raged.

‘It’s not time yet.’

Even if he revealed the world’s secrets, nothing would change.

“What should I do now?”

The civilized lands were small. Guffin walked through the planet’s vast wilderness, deep in thought.

Ikael’s heart was sealed in Hexa, but the loss of the child was a wound for Guffin too.

“It’s futile.”

Maybe this was simply the world.

Needing time to reflect, Guffin stopped at last.

He walked across the surface of a vast lake and sat cross-legged on a lotus flower.

“Yeocha, let’s take care of each other for a while. I’m counting on you.”

It was just a plant—unable to hear or speak—but it was his only companion.

“My heart hurts so much. Even if I wanted to mourn, there’s no one to listen.”

Looking back, Guffin remembered where it all began.

“A long time ago, there was a race called Gaia.”

A story spanning forty thousand years.

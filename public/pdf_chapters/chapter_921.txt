Chapter 921

Coming Disaster (3)

~*~

Becros Kingdom was the smallest of the ten kingdoms in the archipelago.

With a population of roughly two million, it thrived on fishing and embodied the relaxed lifestyle of the southern regions.

In the village of Arar, on the southeastern coast, Shirone awoke with a sense of contentment.

“O uh…”

The warm sunlight and the gentle sound of waves lapping against the shore seemed to revive his weary body.

“I slept really well.”

The moment he said it, Shirone froze.

“What?”

He didn’t know.

“What happened?”

He got out of bed and opened the door. A group of tanned children stopped playing ball and waved.

“Shirone hyung, are you awake?”

The sight of the foamy sea cleared his stomach, but his mind grew more confused.

‘It’s happening again.’

After meeting Fermi on the southern continent, Shirone had crossed into the Tenth Kingdom of the Archipelago to return to the Ivory Tower.

‘The fastest way to reach Corona Kingdom is through the Becros terminal.’

That much was certain.

‘But…’

It had already been ten days since Shirone had arrived.

“Why can’t I leave?”

He was stuck in the village.

“Again today. Shirone hyung still won’t leave. Did you have that dream again?”

Shirone looked down at the ten-year-old boy.

“Mycon.”

He remembered.

The son of the couple who had brought them lunch upon arrival in Arar.

“Me?”

“Yes. Every day you say you don’t have time, but in the end, you stay one more day.”

Had he?

“Ah…”

Shirone recalled the memory of celebrating with the villagers late into the night.

Torches lit the darkness. The smell of grilled fish filled the air. Wine flowed, laughter echoed, and stories were shared.

Everything felt alive.

“Puhahaha! You’re so pleasant. I thought wizards were cold and distant.”

Village chief Begpa pushed a beer mug toward him.

“Ah, no. When you play, you play.”

Shirone, already drunk, raised his glass in a toast—so hard it shattered.

“Yeah, right. Even during the war, this town stayed safe. We’ve never been invaded.”

Begpa always said the same thing when drunk.

“Do you know what ‘Arar’ means? It’s the sound of God’s breath. This place is protected by Becca.”

Shirone nodded along.

“Yeah, that’s right.”

“They say Becca’s breath shapes the waves for fishing, but when angered, it cuts like a knife. Even now, there’s a mark on Mero Cliff—said to be the imprint of God Becca’s breath.”

“Yes. I saw it too. I was shocked. No human could have carved that.”

“Of course! What else could it be? That’s the breath of God Becca!”

Shirone didn’t believe in superstition, but there was no point arguing during a celebration.

“Oh, right! I must’ve been drunk.” A hoarse voice cut in.

“Of course you were!”

A heavyset man approached, one grilled fish skewer in each hand, followed by his daughter.

“Uncle Yasuka.”

The best fisherman in the village, and a respected trader.

“You can forget about war here in Arar! Now, eat and get drunk again!”

Yasuka handed Shirone a skewer. His daughter, Lua, stepped forward with a smile.

“Hello, Shirone. Does it look good?”

Like her father, she was tall, her bronzed body lean and strong, like a wild horse.

“Yes. It’s been a while since I’ve had such a peaceful time.”

Lua was popular in the village, but Yasuka clearly favored Shirone.

“Yes, yes. Happiness is everything. Why not just settle down here with our daughter?”

“Dad…”

Lua didn’t seem thrilled by the suggestion.

“What? If it’s Shirone, I’d be relieved. He’s a good man. And he fixed all the broken houses with his magic, didn’t he?”

“I did. But you’re not getting married just because I repaired a few roofs, right? Shirone, don’t mind him.”

Shirone shivered.

“Haha! Am I really that good? I like this town. Besides, I have no responsibilities now. Everything’s over. Let’s forget it all. Fermi will handle it. No war. No Yahweh…”

He had turned his back on the world. But compared to the relief of shedding humanity’s burden, it was nothing.

“Uh?”

Then, for the first time, Shirone felt a strange unease about his memories.

‘The realm of Yahweh…’

He remembered talking to Fermi about it.

‘What did we discuss? I don’t remember. Did we make a deal? No, that can’t be. But Yahweh…’

It lingered in his mind.

‘Time width, thinness, granularity, resonance. Feel it all. Nothing has passed to Fermi yet. But why?’

Memories continued to surface.

“Halariolle! Rayerola!”

Arar’s folk music rang out. People danced in pairs.

Shirone and Lua held hands, spinning under the stars.

“Ha ha ha! Ha ha ha ha!”

The world spun. The alcohol burned.

“Shirone, this way.”

Lua took his wrist and led him to the edge of the sandy beach.

The drumbeats faded. They stood together under the moonlight.

“Shirone.”

Her eyes sparkled. She stepped forward and kissed him.

“I like you. I like you.”

The people of Arar were honest with their feelings.

“Lua…?”

Amy’s face flashed in his mind—but Shirone pushed it away.

‘Sorry. I can’t go back.’

He didn’t want to take even one step back into the world that awaited him—a world of pain and duty.

‘Stay here… forever.’

In Arar, protected by the gods.

‘I’m living.’

Shirone pulled Lua close and kissed her back.

They fell onto the sand. With a single touch, one piece of clothing came undone.

The drums played all night.

“No!”

Shirone groaned.

“It can’t be.”

It was his own memory. He had to accept it. But parts of it didn’t make sense.

‘Lua…’

The feel of her lips, her skin, her scent, the sound of the waves—everything was vivid.

‘But I still love Amy. Even drunk… this couldn’t have happened. Could it?’

One thought struck him.

‘Is this memory distortion?’

Aside from La Enemy, spirit-type mages could do this.

‘But who?’

Could anyone manipulate the mind of an Infinite Mage?

‘And it doesn’t make logical sense. In my memory, I handed everything over to Fermi. But in reality, the realm of Yahweh is still with me. That’s the key.’

Hypothesis after hypothesis formed. For a clumsy mage, this would be impossible. But for someone skilled, it was a vicious trap.

‘Or… is there something I don’t know?’

Mycon shrugged and sighed.

“He’s usually so calm, but lately he’s been angry every morning. He must be having nightmares.”

Shirone looked at him.

Since Mycon had come from the outside world—where war raged—he must have carried painful memories.

“Stop brooding and come eat breakfast at my house. Then we’ll go to Mero Cliff.”

“Mero Cliff?”

Mycon pointed toward the rocky shore.

“There’s a spot my brother goes to every day to train. He goes at sunrise and stays until sunset.”

Shirone stared at the cliff.

‘I don’t remember that.’

Memories could be erased, and you only noticed when a keyword triggered the gap.

‘More was deleted than I expected.’

If he’d gone there daily, there had to be a reason.

“I’m sorry, Mycon, but today…”

“I know. Don’t say it. ‘No time,’ right?” Mycon smirked.

Shirone turned away.

“Yes. No time.”

He cast teleportation and vanished toward the cliff.

Mycon watched, mouth open.

“This is really something…”

No matter how many times he saw it, it never got old.

‘There it is.’

From the sky, Shirone looked down.

Circles of various sizes were carved into the ground.

‘Massive. Impossible to draw without aerial measurement.’

No—this was something you’d see at the edge of the universe.

‘Perfect.’

The villagers called it proof of God’s breath. To Shirone, it looked like an angel’s halo.

He turned to the forest behind the cliff.

“Hmm.”

His ninth sense—a transcendental awareness—pulled him toward the trees like thirst.

‘Yes. The body remembers.’

He entered the forest and activated his Spirit Zone, scanning for traces.

‘Something’s there.’

Among the sensory echoes, one presence stood out—annoying, persistent.

He stopped before an ordinary tree, reached into the trunk, and pulled out a notepad.

‘Made of special material.’

One page was forged from dense, heavy metal. He opened the cover.

A passphrase was written in thick lines.

‘This… Shirone Code.’

It sounded childish, but Shirone had created a cipher from personal secrets.

For example, “Amy’s breasts” was coded as “X,” which decrypted to “unknown.”

Even if someone extracted the diagram, interpretation varied—“touching” or “desire”—depending on the reader.

‘An emotional cipher, governed by feeling. Instead of a diary, I made these when bored…’

It was useful now.

“Let’s see…”

Shirone decrypted the complex strings in one go.

The message shocked him.

‘First entry: Do not remove the chip in your head. Could cause death.’

He touched his ear.

‘There!’

A small incision, barely noticeable, behind his left ear.

‘They implanted a chip?’

If it was a physical device, mental resistance wouldn’t work.

‘That’s how they manipulated my memories.’

Who the hell did this?

He flipped through the notes. Yesterday’s entry was unreadable—already overwritten.

‘Wake-up time: 8:30 AM. Right. That’s what happened today.’

He read on.

‘Arar Village Escape Plan. Time Vibration. Requires minimum amplitude of 24 hours.’

By flipping the timeline up to 24 hours, you could perceive the past as the present—specifically, 12 hours back.

‘So at 8:30 last night, I’d experience events from 12 hours earlier as if they were happening now.’

What had happened during that gap?

‘He trained here.’

The notes detailed daily progress in mastering Time Vibration.

‘When do I activate it?’

Shirone flipped to the last page.

Then he understood.

“Today.”

Right now.

‘Time Vibration!’

A colossal wave of time surged from Shirone’s body.

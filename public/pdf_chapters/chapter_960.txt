In the Flower Field (1)

~*~

Flu approached and reported to Lufist, who was waiting for the army of Hell.

“As of now, the asylum processing of all 286 survivors has been completed.”

Some, like Nade, had remained in the kingdom of Tormia, but most had fled to other countries.

Lufist and Flu, however, had chosen to stay on the battlefield.

Even if the elemental bomb detonated, there was a high chance they would be caught in the blast—but neither was willing to run without fighting.

“Then let’s go.”

As the Tormia allied forces, reinforced by the Crusade, spread their formation, Lufist stepped forward.

“Please include me too.”

Dante was approaching from behind.

If Lufist hadn’t lost his memory to dementia, this would have been an uncharacteristically reckless decision.

“Surprisingly,” Lufist said. “I thought you were in the bunker.”

There was no mockery in his voice.

To lure the demons to Basuka, countless lives would be sacrificed—not elite agents.

“I’m tired of being locked up in a closet,” Dante replied. “If not now, when should I get some fresh air? Rather, I should ask—why are the president of the association and the chief secretary here?”

The Lufist Dante knew never acted on emotion, no matter the situation.

“Of course, I’m not here by will alone. Let’s say efficiency was considered.”

“Efficiency?”

“Anyway, I won’t die.” It sounded like overconfidence. Maybe it was. But his words shifted the mood among the commanders.

*To be able to say that in the most hopeless moment… That’s what a captain should be.*

A herald’s cry rang out from the side.

“Enemy sighted!”

Ahead, the army of Hell surged across the horizon, red as blood.

“First, we need to break their expectations.”

Lufist raised his hand, forging a steel blade from raw magic, ready to move forward—but Dante stepped in front of him.

“Leave that role to me.”

“You?” Lufist asked.

It wasn’t that he doubted Dante’s strength. But the enemy now was no ordinary demon.

Since the war began, the demon numbers had dropped sharply—meaning the survivors were elite veterans.

“Failure isn’t just your death,” Lufist warned. “It crushes the morale of the entire army.”

Dante walked past him without stopping.

“Do you know why I’m the final manager of the Flower Garden, despite my low red-line rating?”

He was known for his information-handling skills, yes—but that wasn’t the main reason.

“Because he’s the best defender in the kingdom.”

As the demons closed to within two kilometers, the ground trembled like a stretched membrane.

Flu turned to Lufist.

“President, are you really going to let him do this?”

“I’ll watch for now.”

Lufist wouldn’t have come this far if he couldn’t assess a subject properly and make the right call.

Alone on the open plain, Dante stood like a sailboat adrift in the open sea.

*Central Calculation Magic Circle.* With the enemy army in sight, he activated Pascal, forming a magic circle over 200 meters in diameter.

“Big!”

Countless smaller circles spun into existence, assembling into a massive three-dimensional structure.

“Soldier! There!”

The Vulcans, stationed far behind the front line, could still clearly see it.

“Damn it! What the hell is that?”

The sight of lasers spreading like a net and starlight simmering in the air was shocking—but the size was the real problem.

*Something huge.*

Dante’s eyes narrowed.

“Information Castle!”

It erupted in white light, as if a star had exploded, and transformed into a structure of impossible scale.

“Yes?”

The demons, seeing the vast shadow cast over the land, looked up in unison.

“Is that your final move?”

When a life-sized castle, the kind only seen in legends, appeared before them, disbelief took hold.

“It’s just magic! Push through it!”

Like anchovies swarming a massive ship, the demons accelerated.

—Welcome to Information Castle. Enter user setting mode.

Inside the control room, Dante sat in a command chair, watching the enemy through multiple screens.

The system offered full coverage—no blind spots, close-ups, wide views, everything.

“Auto-control and strike. Priority on close-range targets. Maximum firepower.”

—Setting confirmed. Enter authorization password.

Dante clenched his fists, then relaxed his hands.

“Playground.”

—Siege mode activated. 287 targets locked. Saturation in 4 seconds… 3… 2…

“Shall we play properly?”

The moment the countdown ended, 3,000 gun gates on the castle’s outer walls opened fire.

***

After Ikael left with Guffin, Heaven descended into chaos.

Ankera never explained why Ikael had left, but the angels had their suspicions.

*It’s all because of humans.*

Hatred for mankind had taken root deep in their hearts, and expansionism became the new doctrine.

Giants were sent across the universe, and one of them eventually entered the world where Shirone lived.

That giant, having reached the 6th level of Anecdotal Art, disassembled his body to reach the clouds and was reborn as a human.

A new kind of humanity.

But Adam and Lilith, who had once settled there, were long gone.

Again, vast stretches of time passed.

Omega Year 987.

Heaven, now thriving in the archangel’s absence, reached its peak of prosperity.

Cities were built for subjects—giants, fairies, and even fallen angels were managed separately.

“Sigh.”

Then, one day, something happened behind Satiel’s back.

“What?”

She turned around—and saw dozens of glass plates shimmering, their edges translucent.

Like a puzzle, Ashur’s body reassembled itself as the glass pieces faded.

“Satiel.”

“Oh, Ashur?”

She wasn’t surprised that the triangular mara had infiltrated the archangel’s chamber. His power allowed it.

*Ikael.*

Suddenly alert, she rushed to him.

“What happened? Where is the archangel? And Guffin—what happened to her?”

“All is well. Fortunately.”

Satiel exhaled in relief.

“Do you know how much time has passed? If you couldn’t return for any reason…”

“Ikael-nim is looking for you. If you’re willing to come, I’ll guide you.”

Satiel’s eyes flickered as she hesitated.

“Without reporting to Anchera-sama?”

“Yes. That’s why I came.” Though not as powerful as Guffin’s hexa, Ashur could still block signals.

“Whoa!”

Satiel puffed her cheeks and paced the room, torn.

*I wish I could refuse.*

Even if Satiel supported humanity, aiding a being of Heaven’s highest rank was dangerous.

But Ikael had a reason to meet her—and she understood that. She couldn’t turn away.

“If you’re anxious…”

“I’ll go.”

Having made her decision, Satiel stepped forward.

“I need to see him now. Take me to the archangel.”

The fact that Ashur still called Ikael “archangel” was his only comfort.

“Yes. Then, at the risk of disrespect.” Ashur activated the signal, and hundreds of glass plates—flat from every angle—enclosed them.

*Move.*

The moment the glass shattered with a clear chime, they were gone.

“Where are we?”

They arrived instantly. Satiel took in the scenery—a quiet backcountry where forest met sea.

“Follow me.”

Ashur moved without explanation. Satiel followed in silence.

Meeting Ikael was all that mattered now.

*Is Guffin here?*

Heart pounding with unspoken hope, she crossed the forest. Ashur stopped before a cave carved into a cliff.

“Ikael-nim, I’ve brought Satiel.”

“Yes. Tell them to come in.”

Ashur stepped aside. Satiel walked in, tense.

The cave was bright, not from torches, but from an inner glow. At the end, she saw the one she had longed for.

“Guffin??????”

He turned, weary, trying to calm a crying baby in his arms.

“Hey, Satiel. Long time no see.” He hadn’t even realized she was there until now.

*A human child? Who is this?*

It was something no angel could have predicted.

“Satiel.”

She snapped to attention and knelt before Ikael.

“Chief Angel! Why are you hiding in such a place? Please, return to Heaven with me!”

“Thank you. But first, there’s something I must tell you.”

Ikael reached out both hands toward Guffin.

“Hand him over.”

“Wait—did he just pee? Do I need to change the diaper again?”

“It’s fine. Give him to me.”

Satiel watched, stunned, as the child passed from Guffin to Ikael.

“Archangel… what about that child?”

“He is my son.”

Satiel felt as if the world had tilted.

“What?”

But when she looked up, Ikael was cradling the child with a radiant smile.

“Can you believe it, Satiel? He’s my child. Proof that Guffin and I are truly connected.”

Satiel’s mouth hung open.

“But… how can an angel and a human… ?”

Guffin brushed her hair back and smiled.

“Whoops! Also, I’m a genius. It’s Hexa’s skill. I call it Agape.”

Satiel finally understood.

“The Archangel and Guffin…”

Ikael turned, showing the child’s face, his voice trembling with joy.

“Yes. We succeeded. Now we can lead together. No one needs to be left behind.”

Of course.

“Satiel, help us. Our child is proof. Anchera-sama will have no choice but to accept this. We can go further. This isn’t the end.”

Of course.

“The reason I called you is because you’re the only one. You’ve always fought for mankind. Without you, no one else could do this. Please, Satiel.”

To seal their achievement with more than just two blessings.

“And one more thing.”

Ikael smiled gently.

“Will you name this child?”

“Name…”

A human born of an angel.

Since he was tied to Idea itself, he was worthy of a true name.

But the moment Ikael spoke, the first word that surfaced in Satiel’s astral body was:

*Why?*

At that point, Shirone stopped receiving Omega’s records.

The records, perceived not through five senses but eleven, were beyond human comprehension—

Yet Shirone felt it with perfect clarity, beyond thought.

*How deeply Guffin and Ikael love this child. With their entire beings.*

I can feel it.

And so, he couldn’t go on.

*I know.*

That this child—this proof of love—would die a terrible death, never even given a name.

*How fiercely Satiel would come to hate humanity, long after.*

Without seeing it directly, he already knew what would happen.

*But…*

That child who would soon die—was not Shirone.

*But why?*

When Igor had revived Shirone’s deepest fear, he had relived that child’s death.

*It wasn’t implanted information. It was my memory.*

From a child’s perspective.

And the image of Ikael, clutching his chest and weeping—still burned in his mind.

Shirone reopened the connection to Omega.

*What happened?*

He needed to know—what “Hexa” truly meant, buried in the tragedy that followed.

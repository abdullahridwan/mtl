What the Guffins Left Behind (1)

~*~

Kata Torsa nodded.

Shirone’s Miracle Stream was comparable to the abilities of the Guffins he had known.

“It’s connected properly.”

“Yes. Thank you.”

There were no more mysteries left for Shirone, who had experienced Omega. Naturally, the words came more easily now.

“If it weren’t for you, the spirit of the Gaians wouldn’t have survived to this day.” But that didn’t mean the respect was gone.

“On behalf of Guffin, I thank you.” They were the ones who had preserved time itself, bridging the gap between the eradication of the Guffins and the era of the Great Purification.

“I merely completed my mission.”

Even as he said it, Kata Torsa could see the emotion in Shirone’s eyes. He stared at the ceiling, his gaze distant.

“Knowing Omega doesn’t mean it’s over,” Shirone said. “As I said before, I can’t tell you the history of your era. From now on, you must face the world with your own judgment.”

“I am ready.”

Kata Torsa slowly lowered his head, his eyes locking onto Shirone’s.

“One thing remains. The history of your time—the only record permitted by the Guffin.”

“Guffin gave permission…?”

Shirone swallowed.

“Yes. It’s an event just before the Guffin left the photon system—and at the time of your birth. Very few know Hexa was born, so it won’t affect you much now. But Guffin asked for forgiveness. I don’t know the full truth either. If you refuse, I’ll erase the logs I have.”

In a moment where he had to abandon Hexa, Guffin hadn’t wanted to reveal his feelings.

‘It must be a parent’s burden.’

Shirone still wanted to know.

‘I don’t remember him, even though he’s my biological father.’

Since Shirone didn’t appear in Omega, it was assumed there had been no contact between him and Guffin.

“What the Guffins left behind.”

Shirone whispered the words and took a deep breath.

“Yes. I want to know.”

Golden light flared in Kata Torsa’s eyes, and Omega’s records flooded in.

The Guffin’s Gate had been installed in Istas. Soon after, the 20 Judges discovered Miro’s paradise.

“Is that the child?”

Plateau of Corruption.

In the aftermath of war, across a landscape stripped bare, a Guffin held a child in his arms.

An indescribable emotion crossed Guffin’s face as he looked down and met the child’s eyes.

“Look where you are.”

When Miro pulled back the child’s outer covering, the infant—bright as the full moon—smiled.

“Hmm.”

Guffin chuckled.

“If you’re going to say he’s pretty, just say it. He’s Ikael’s and mine, so of course he’s handsome.”

“He’s delicious. Such a waste—how can you just hand him over and leave?”

Guffin smiled bitterly.

‘Even if I can see everything… will my child feel pain?’

Miro studied the child again, shifting the mood.

“Yeah, he looks 90% like Ikael. Sweetie, you’re lucky to take after your father… Ugh.”

Guffin frowned.

“What are you talking about? Anyone can see he’s my son! Compare properly. Where’s this 10 percent difference?”

“No, the face is 100% Ikael. The other 10 percent… maybe here?”

Miro’s index finger slowly moved toward the child’s lower body, peeling back the covering.

Shirone’s shoulders trembled as he absorbed the memory.

‘Miro, please…’ It wasn’t just a vision—it was experienced through all eleven senses. Even her thoughts were clear.

‘I’m really trying to see.’

Guffin twisted his body sharply.

“Why? My son isn’t yours to take.”

Miro snorted.

“What? They said he was the only heir, the last defender of the world.”

“I don’t care. My son will never! I’ll have a normal life with a normal woman.”

Miro’s eyes widened.

“Mister, you won’t remember any of this soon anyway, will you?”

If the Guffin who left the photon field erased the log, he wouldn’t recall this moment.

“Wujjujju, you’re good.”

Guffin, who had been pretending not to listen while secretly admiring the child, suddenly softened.

“He said he looks like Ikael…”

Shirone’s eyes burned as Guffin’s emotions surged through the Gunggam.

‘How much I’ll miss you.’

Miro, noticing Guffin’s expression, relaxed and patted him on the back.

“Let’s go. We have to begin.”

……Flaw?

One hundred meters ahead, a massive bell-shaped structure rose from the ground.

“This… the Tower of Babel?”

Guffin laughed.

“I understand your disappointment. Once, it pierced the sky. It was enormous.”

We were close to God.

“The Battle of the Tower of Babel. In the Second War, Gaia was officially defeated in direct confrontation with God. You could call this place historic.”

“You want to tell the child this?”

Miro glanced around.

“When I think about it, Gaia never had a homeland. After achieving Ultima, he refused to back down and challenged the gods. Now that even the roots are gone… maybe this place can become the heart’s homeland.”

Guffin looked down at the child.

“Son, remember the spirit of the Gaian who lived in this tower. Even though we lost, we never stopped trying. We never surrendered to the world. We never gave in to despair. We defended our free will until the end.”

The Guffin and Miro entered the Tower of Babel and descended endlessly underground.

Shirone realized.

‘You really came.’

This was why he’d felt a strange deja vu when entering Babel during the Heaven Project.

Miro asked, “How tight is this security? Didn’t the Gaians live here before?”

Guffin disabled the security systems while keeping Miracle Stream active.

“It’s heavenly work. Judging by the code, it’s Kariel’s. Heaven’s technology originally belonged to Gaia. Now, a faction called Mecca carries on that legacy.”

“…Will it be safe?”

Miro scanned the area warily. Guffin smirked.

“It’s just technology. They can never replicate Gaia’s spirit.”

The red warning lights in the hallway blinked off as Miracle Stream’s smoke spread.

“I might be able to do something here.”

Upon entering the cockpit, the first thing Shirone saw was a cone-shaped pillar stretching from floor to ceiling.

Beyond it were various devices—crude, in Guffin’s eyes—clearly built by Kariel.

“The Tower of Babel is operated by the Ultima System. To the angels, it’s the bread of the picture.”

Guffin placed his hand between the cones and activated the system. Powerful electrical energy surged.

Instantly, every device lit up. The barbell-shaped core began to spin. Screens flickered to life.

“Access to main system. Codename: Babel Project.”

Miro watched as the program’s logic circuit appeared on the screen.

“What are you doing, turning this on now?”

“Well…” Guffin looked slightly embarrassed.

“But since I’m here, I should leave something behind. After all, I’m the last Gaian.”

We existed in this world.

“Of course, if Hexa fails to defeat God, this record will vanish… That’s why it’s Omega.”

“I’m just leaving this. My heart.”

As thoughts turned to text, unfamiliar characters began to appear on the screen.

Even the labyrinth couldn’t read them.

‘Number of Laws: 1.’

They were written in the language of the Guffins alone.

“All Gaians used different characters based on their personalities. But communication was never an issue. There’s only one signal that penetrates this world.”

The Gunggam. The Ultima System.

So if Shirone came here in the future, he would be able to read Guffin’s record.

‘When Ankera attempted the second reset… It was Omega 412 years ago.’

Guffin, recording Gaia’s age in Omega units, filled in the final 999 years.

‘The last Gaia, Mackline Guffin. Photon field exit.’

The name would be erased anyway. With this, the history of the Gaians ended.

‘The future…’

Guffin paused, then whispered.

“Beyond infinity.”

The message was recorded and sent. The screen went dark.

“Are you done?”

Miro asked.

Unnoticed, a dimensional door to Istas had opened behind her.

“Then give me Hexa now. If you reset while swapping with the Prince of Kazura, Hexa’s origin will be erased permanently. Then even Ankera can’t reach him.”

“I suppose so.”

With humanity’s future at stake, Guffin handed over the child without hesitation.

Yet his hands trembled slightly.

“…The child will be fine. I won’t convince Yolga-sister, but I won’t fail you either.”

“Yes. Request.”

Miro reached for Hexa, then paused, looking at the child’s face.

“As a father… do you have any last words?”

Farewell, life.

I know it hurts to let go, but I never thought it would come to this.

“Leave it.”

Any advice for his son, who would one day fight the world alone…

“If you…”

Guffin stroked the child’s cheek.

“If you face an evil so strong you can’t defend against it… you may adopt the methods of evil.”

The Behemoth seal on Shirone’s arm flared red.

“If you’re strong enough to protect your family, stand neutral between good and evil.”

There is nothing greater than protecting your family.

“Dad couldn’t.”

Guffin laughed—a broken, devastated sound. His eyes turned red. Tears streamed down Shirone’s face too.

“If you’re strong enough to protect your family and others… then defend the good.”

That’s right.

“And if… you become strong enough to protect the entire world…”

Guffin lifted Hexa high.

“Listen to everyone. No being born of oneness is insignificant.”

“Cadet. Iced coffee.”

The child smiled brightly, as if seeing Guffin’s face from above brought him joy.

Guffin raised his head, as if drinking in the moment, then slowly lowered his arms.

Miro, solemn now, took Hexa and stepped into the dimensional door. She turned back.

“How much time do I have?”

“The time in Istas must align with space. When events diverge, time’s relativity is pushed to its limit. One second here could be decades in Istas.”

It was a closed curve of time.

“Don’t worry. You’ll succeed. If you can’t return in time, just close the loop. I won’t resent you.”

“No. You will resent me. Because if I cease to exist, you’ll become humanity’s scapegoat.”

Guffin paused, then nodded.

“I’m sorry. I’m ready to be hated. Just… protect Hexa.”

Miro nodded and stepped into Estas.

“Yolga.”

The next moment, she saw a figure emerge from the hallway—blood on her face.

In the closed curve of time, space was time. From Guffin’s perspective, Warehouse 72 existed in the same moment.

‘If I create an independent time within the world’s flow and leave the photon field…’

In other Istas warehouses, Miro might already be affected by Guffin Eradication.

It was the paradox of Istas—future in the loop, yet past in all time.

‘The Prince of Kazura… gone.’

It was her first time in such a life-threatening situation, but she nodded silently.

‘Thank you.’

Tragically, Guffin closed the dimensional door without another word.

With that, the event of leaving Istas was sealed within the closed curve of time.

‘All preparations are complete.’

Guffin looked up at the ceiling.

“You won’t stop this, Ang Kera.”

Because you’re not alone.

As Ikael’s tears flowed through Hexa, a vast ataraxia unfolded.

“Big!”

Amplification.

Guffin’s body became a Miracle Stream, compressing toward infinity.

‘There is no more Ultima in this world…’

Leave it behind.

‘One hope.’

A faint smile touched Guffin’s lips as his body began to radiate light.

“It was fun.”

His form flickered—and vanished.

‘Pierce.’

At the moment he passed through a layer beyond time and space, beyond the photon world’s boundary—

‘That one?’

“Boy.”

“He’s become a good man. Look… he even looks like me.”

Chapter 957

Even if it’s an illusion (2)

~*~

Team Sein, tasked with assassinating Havitz, raced across the Denan Plain as the Hell army advanced.

Time felt distorted.

A ring of light flared in Sein’s eyes, and two massive halos appeared in the sky.

Cheolryunan, Sun and Moon Halo.

The world trembled as the halos converged and merged into one.

Eden gasped as the landscape rushed past him, as if space itself were collapsing.

This is the Iron Wheel.

In a sky dark as night, a single ring of light spun at terrifying speed.

You can do it. This is to stop Havitz.

The ability allowed control over all physical and mental factors within the halo’s radius.

Even if a boulder fell on Havitz, he might survive… but what if gravity itself was controlled?

The future of immortality would be shattered.

But the price for amplifying such power was the user’s own mind and body.

That’s why I’m on this team.

Eden, a specialist in defensive magic, focused on what needed protection.

Protect the control tower.

As Eden steeled himself, Armand approached Quan, who ran on the other side.

“Are you okay?”

“Prosecutors don’t deal in probabilities.”

“No, I mean Shiina.”

Quan’s eyebrows twitched.

Olifer Siena still taught in Creas City.

It wasn’t far from Zion.

Yet Quan had never gone to see her.

“Why didn’t you visit? This battle could be our last.”

“It’s unpleasant.”

Quan narrowed his eyes at Armand.

“I do what I do. And you’re not the only one who cares about her.”

He knew Armand was involved with Keira, a resident of the Ivory Tower.

“If this operation fails…” Quan said, “the Kingdom of Tormia will fall. If we kill Havitz, the war ends. That’s all.”

Sein pointed ahead.

“Move.”

Ahead, the demon army stretched across the horizon, charging forward.

“What about Havitz?” Mayray no longer needed to cover her ears.

“I’m listening. Activate ‘Nemesis.’ One last check first.”

She turned to the team.

“The wiretap target is Havitz. Don’t assume his heartbeat will sound normal.”

Understanding her warning, the team put on the <Nemesis> rings, their expressions tense.

The sound of Havitz’s heart echoed in their minds like a hallucination.

Eden turned pale.

“This… this is one…”

Listening to a person’s heartbeat was strange, but Eden had seen the dark side of humanity before as a Seeker. He had prepared for it.

But this—this was different.

I can’t do this.

If the sound they heard truly originated in the human mind…

It’s hell.

This world was a living room of suffering, where cutting one’s own throat seemed like a rational choice.

All of them wore the same horrified expression. Even Quan, usually cold, squinted one eye shut.

“No.”

Eden, realizing what Havitz desired, shouted.

“I have to go back!”

They needed to block Havitz’s thoughts, not Havitz himself.

“It’s too late,” Quan said.

Sein made a quick decision.

“We can’t solve everything by force. Just do what you’re best at.”

They had to trust the Tormia army and the temple forces still holding the flower field.

As the ground vibrated from the approaching enemy, Sein gave the order.

“Now.”

Light burst from Armand’s blindfolded eyes. Stop magic activated.

Time froze across a four-kilometer radius, but the entire demon army couldn’t be contained.

Demons that hit the boundary of the Spirit Zone froze in place. The troops behind them skidded to a halt.

“What?!”

The massive inertia only stopped after over a million more demons were pulled into the zone.

“I couldn’t contain all of them,” Armand said. “We need to finish this fast.”

Sein sprinted between the frozen demons.

Physically, they had no mass now. He moved at the speed of information, like a signal entering this world.

“Found him.”

Havitz sat on a frozen horse, his legs kicked up behind him, surrounded by demon escorts.

Lyria pulled several birch totems from the barrel on her back and leapt forward.

I have to leave Havitz.

At that moment, Sein saw it.

What is that?

Something moved in one of Havitz’s lifeless eyes—like a wax figure twitching.

“Fly?”

Twelve black holes opened in the ground. Figures in robes emerged.

Shiok.

He’d never seen it with his own eyes, but he’d bet anything on it.

As expected, it appears like this.

The Shirone case had proven that the stop effect activated independently of Harvitz.

Now, the first untested hypothesis would be examined.

The city prison exists outside of lawful time, making Harvitz independent of all events. But…

Why is Shiok visible now?

Because time has stopped.

If they could physically attack Shiok, their chances of killing Havitz would skyrocket.

“Quan.”

As Sein gave the order, Havitz—centered within Shiok—moved his lips.

“Stop.”

In an instant, Shiok was sucked back underground. The stop magic reasserted control over the space.

“Why?!”

Before Sein could react, Lyria slammed the totems into the ground.

At the same moment, the stop effect ended.

The twenty totems formed a massive circle around Havitz.

“That’s it!”

As Lyria clasped her hands, the totems vibrated and burrowed into the earth.

The ground shook. The surface of the law mirror smoothed into a perfect reflection.

But the reflected landscape wasn’t the Denan Plain.

It showed a vast wasteland of reddish-brown stone.

Law of Space. Magic Glass!

As Sein’s team surrounded Havitz, the law mirror began to tilt, as if rotating on an axis.

“Enemies! Kill them!”

The demons finally realized what was happening and charged.

But the camp had already been erected vertically.

Gravity still pulled downward for them, but to Sein and the others, the world appeared completely inverted.

“Satan! You’re Satan…!”

As the law mirror and its core flipped 180 degrees, demons crossed through empty space, disoriented.

“Damn! What the hell is going on?!”

In a frozen moment, none of the demons understood.

“Commander! Commander!”

The current general was Paimon, but the human messenger reported directly to Vulcan.

“This is serious! Havitz has left the formation! He seems to have fallen into a trap!”

Vulcan stroked his chin.

“Hmm.”

“Should we pursue?”

“No. Leave him.”

“What?”

“Havitz acts on desire, not calculation. He can’t be stopped. And…”

Vulcan smirked.

“Maybe this is what he wanted.”

All Havitz saw now was a wasteland, the horizon meeting a blood-red sky.

Even if they escaped alive, they’d die of thirst and starvation.

“Who are you?”

No one answered. Sein’s team remained silent, surrounding him.

Havitz’s choice was shocking.

I know it’s chaos.

But why come here, knowing it would prevent the prison from activating?

“Why?!”

Sein finally demanded.

“Why did you enter the prison? Why?!”

“Just…”

Havitz twisted his mustache.

“This seemed more fun.”

It was the answer Sein had expected—yet it confirmed something terrifying.

As long as Mayray was present, information was under complete control. There was no way Havitz could have known about the elemental bomb.

A predetermined future.

What if escaping this space with us is the only way to save Havitz?

If you choose the law that lets you get as far from Baska as possible…

Are our judgments right or wrong?

Sein realized anew the terrifying scope of Havitz’s innate ability—and the weight of fate.

“Mayray.”

Quan spoke quietly.

“Eavesdrop.”

At some point, the sound of Havitz’s heartbeat had stopped reaching the team.

Eden turned. Mayray’s face was pale, like a ghost.

Her expression was blank, but her skin told the truth.

“Mayray, listen to me,” Quan said. “When Havitz casts Vanishing, you must react immediately.”

“It’s not possible.”

Her voice was cold.

She would never make a reckless decision in front of their assassination target.

So there was only one reason she’d blocked the eavesdropping—despite Sein’s order.

She believed it would lead to near-certain mission failure.

Sein stepped forward.

“Mayray, if you don’t, we’ll be annihilated.”

“If you want to watch your comrades die without knowing why, then fine. Do as you please.”

Mayray flinched. Her nose twitched.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about.”

Havitz drew his longsword and stepped forward.

“Now… who should I kill first?”

As she sensed his presence, Mayray screamed, eyes squeezed shut.

“I’ll open it!”

As if a dam had burst, the sound of Havitz’s heartbeat flooded into Sein’s mind.

Waves of evil.

“Kyaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaah!”

Lyria screamed first. Armand and Sein jerked as if electrocuted.

Damn it! You shouldn’t have told me!

Mayray’s warning was clear.

Havitz’s heart was beyond imagination. It was monstrous.

“What’s wrong?”

Havitz turned toward Eden.

“It’s a reaction. Like you know something.”

“Ah… ah… ah…”

Eden’s legs trembled as Havitz approached.

Calm down. I’m Yora now. I’m not the same as when I followed the Jordanian faith.

Eden’s devotion to goodness was legendary, even in Zion.

Even if a bomb exploded before his eyes, his faith would not waver.

But now, he was just a man—terrified, helpless.

“What’s wrong?”

Havitz asked.

“Are you afraid of me?”

You’re just like me.

True fear doesn’t come from outside. It rises from within.

Murder and dismemberment don’t create fear.

Not unless it’s done to you.

No. I am a messenger of God.

Tears streamed down his face.

I thought I’d purged all evil from my mind. Was I still clouded?

I can’t win. Never.

Havitz’s fear struck the core of humanity, the origin of life itself.

Polar.

Only Miro, a flawless crystal, could withstand Satan’s voice.

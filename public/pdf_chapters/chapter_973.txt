Chapter 973

All History (1)

~*~

Tess sprinted through the massive shadow cast by the flower’s petals.

Even as she ran at full speed across three hundred meters, the surrounding blossoms caught fire and burned in the flash of energy.

The stolen ancient weapon was easy to spot.

A group of elite-looking demons guarded its flank.

The perimeter wasn’t as tightly secured as expected.

*My father’s enemy.*

If this were any ordinary demon, it would have trembled at her presence. But every second wasted meant more damage to the flower.

Tess stomped the ground, generating external gravity. Her body bounced, then floated upward.

As the demons turned, she was already clinging to the flower’s central pillar.

She ran straight up the vertical shaft.

By the time the sounds of battle faded behind her, a dark shape soared across the sky.

“Kaaa! Kaaa!”

A human-bird hybrid with black feathers.

*This is the limit.*

Tess spotted the nearest entrance and slipped inside the flower.

Even within, demons stood guard—but not enough to make evasion impossible.

She’d take them out silently, hide the bodies, and keep climbing.

“Hey! What—?!”

An old man’s shout. Then a woman’s scream.

“Please forgive me! Please!”

The control room.

Tess moved like a spider along the ceiling, creeping toward the threshold from above.

“Puhahaha! Is this really fun? Are you doing everything you’re told?”

Veins bulged in Tess’s eyes.

Gustav’s Fourth Arts: Sumodo and Jetaro.

Sumodo—the perverted brute who had tormented the Fire Tribe. Jetaro, who observed the battlefield with cold indifference.

“Sumodo, don’t be too upset. Your attacks keep missing.”

The Fire Tribe, born into obedience, would never break under threats or torture.

*Good,* Tess thought. *I’m enjoying this.*

She’d learned countless torture methods during her espionage training. But this wasn’t about extracting information.

The Fire Tribe woman begged.

“Please kill me… please…”

“Puhahaha! Jetaro, did you hear that? Does this woman still think she’s alive? Should I show her a mirror to see what she looks like?”

“Smodo, stop the operation…”

Zetaro’s eyes widened. He sighed and turned his head.

“Harm?”

“Yes?”

The moment Sumodo glanced back toward the entrance, Tess kicked him in the jaw.

“Kuk!”

Teeth flew, bouncing off the ceiling. Sumodo’s body flipped backward.

“You bastards!”

Tess pushed down her rising fury and looked at the Fire Tribe woman.

“Are you okay? Hey!”

The woman’s eyes were rolled back. Her head hung limp to the side.

*She’s dead.*

Zetaro, who had been helping Sumodo up, clicked his tongue.

“Sorry. What happened to the young wife… Rest in peace for the deceased.”

Tess’s mind froze.

“… Are you kidding?”

Zetaro remained calm despite sensing the overwhelming life force in the room.

“Is this all a joke to you?”

Sumodo, blood dripping from his mouth, spat.

“You’re in good shape.”

Tess exhaled sharply.

“You’re in the top 1.78 percent of the women I’ve ever seen. Assuming your weight…”

She punched him before he could finish. Her fist shattered his jaw.

Zetaro frowned as Sumodo collapsed.

“Anyway, buying the right thing and doing it… Kuk!”

Tess grabbed Zetaro by the hair and slammed her knee into his face, driving it into the control room wall.

Again and again.

Within seconds, Zetaro’s face was a bloody mess.

“Bad babies! Pure bad boys!”

When Zetaro stopped moving, Tess dropped her light body onto the floor.

He fell like a corpse.

Sumodo crawled across the ground, lips cracked and bleeding.

“Ah, what kind of woman hits this hard? Aaaaaa!”

Tess raised her saber and drove it into his calf.

“Are you sick?”

With the blade pinned deep into the floor, she leaned over Sumodo’s face.

“Does it hurt? Just this much? Is that why you scream?”

“Ahhhh!”

Even the sight of him trembling made her sick.

Tess grabbed his index finger and squeezed.

“You suffer the same.”

She bent the finger at a right angle.

“Aww! It hurts! It hurts!”

“Yes. That is pain.”

She broke another finger.

“Oh my! Oops, I’m dying! This is elder abuse!”

“Shut up.”

When all six fingers on both hands were broken, Sumodo collapsed, face buried in the floor, sobbing.

“It hurts… It hurts so much… Please forgive me.”

“Forgiveness? Did *you* show mercy to that woman?”

“Because I was wrong…”

Tess felt something tear inside her chest, but she couldn’t name it.

“How do you feel about yourself? Are you having fun? Was it that much fun to mutilate, to inflict pain, to hear screams, you bastard?”

Silence.

“… Third-rate.”

“What?”

Sumodo lifted his head, annoyed.

“Doing it for fun is third-rate, you half-lady.”

Tess shivered. There was no remorse in his eyes—only something she couldn’t read.

“You guys… What the hell are you? If it’s not even fun, why are you doing this?”

Sumodo laughed, blood oozing from his broken mouth.

“Because I can.” He grinned. “Freedom.”

~*~

Twelve years old.

Sumodo’s mother always called out before entering his room.

“Sumodo? Are you in there?”

It wasn’t really an accident. The accident had already happened—in his mind.

“Yes, I’m here. I won’t go out.”

“It’s not that.” Like any normal parent, his mother lied and opened the door.

The sight made her dizzy.

Vertical lines, densely drawn, covered the white wall.

And the objects placed at exact coordinates—each one broke her heart.

“Relatives have come to visit.”

Sumodo had severe obsessive-compulsive disorder.

Since infancy, he used only one baby bottle. The length of his pencil was fixed each day. If books weren’t arranged perfectly on the shelf, the day was ruined.

“I told you to have fun. I’ll be here.”

*The hair is really extraordinary,* she thought.

For that reason alone, Sumodo was still a child who needed protection.

“Sigh. Sumo, how long are you going to stay like this? I know you’re obsessive. But let’s get past it. You’ll become a great person. You barely go to school, but you always rank first.”

Sumodo didn’t answer. He looked conflicted.

“Come on, let’s go. Just say hello to the relatives and come back.”

“Are you promising?”

His mother smiled brightly.

“Then just say hi. Yes?” She gently pulled his wrist.

Sumodo pretended to give in.

Downstairs, his father chatted with relatives.

“Uh? Sumo’s here too? He’s my brother!”

Children of the same age played hide-and-seek.

But to Sumodo, they were just moving objects in space.

As he descended the stairs, he saw salt spilled on the snack table.

The kids had knocked over the shaker during tag.

Sumodo froze, staring.

His aunt called. “It’s been a while, Sumodo. Remember Auntie?”

“Yes.”

Sumodo never forgot the shape of things.

“Except for the wrinkle around your left eye.”

The list went on, but he endured.

“Ho-ho-ho! I heard you study so well?”

His mother nodded.

“Yes. I won first place again.”

“Oh my, the school Sumodo goes to—Gustav Public—isn’t that the one for geniuses?”

“Because I’m still young. Even so, I want you to become a great person in the empire, but…”

Sumodo’s gaze kept drifting back to the table.

“Ha ha ha! Run away! Run away!”

The children ran, feet thudding.

“Kyaaak! Caught!”

Salt scattered.

When Sumodo entered Gustav Public, the noise vanished. His entire mind focused on the salt.

He felt like he was going mad.

Heart pounding. Nerves stretched tight.

Thud.

A child kicked the table and walked past.

“Aaaaaaa!”

Adults turned. Sumodo was already running, shoving the child aside.

“Sumodo!”

The child cried. The mother shouted.

But Sumodo knelt before the table, frantically sifting through the salt.

“Damn! It’s all mixed up!”

The absurdity erased even the thought of violence.

“Sumo, what are you doing?”

“The salt is all mixed. I have to put it back. It was here, and this particle—yes, here!”

“Salt…?”

His mother asked.

“What?”

Even her voice couldn’t reach him.

“Damn! I need to rebuild it. I need tweezers.”

She knew about compulsions.

But her son digging through salt wasn’t obsession—it was madness.

*My son is not crazy,* she wept.

“Sumodo, that’s just salt. It spilled.”

“It’s not. The size, shape, color—every particle is different.”

She stepped forward, turned him, and slapped him across the face.

“Why are you like this? What are you complaining about?” It didn’t matter who saw.

“Are you stupid? Are you sick? Why the hell do you live like this? Why?”

Sumodo flinched.

But he didn’t resent her.

To him, it was a minor disturbance.

*What kind of world are we living in?*

The question burned in his child’s mind.

Was this error? Blessing? Curse?

He felt trapped in the framework of reality.

~*~

Back in the present, Tess stared at Sumodo.

“Because I can… do you?”

“Yes. That’s freedom. Didn’t you just break my finger? Because *you* could.”

“You say that now…”

Sumodo screamed and broke his own fingers.

“You can do this!”

Tess frowned.

“What’s the problem? If this is wrong, then the world shouldn’t be like this in the first place! Ask the one who made it! I do it because I can—why the hell are you stopping me?”

Sumodo, lying on the floor, screamed and stood.

“Aaaaaaa!”

The saber still pierced his calf. Flesh tore. He limped, then pulled off his jacket.

The sleeves were empty.

“Freedom!”

Tess paled.

His body was covered in piercings. Skin bluish from necrosis.

“Free theeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeee! Free the air, Mom!”

What had she expected?

No matter how much she punished him, the frustration wouldn’t fade.

“Ha ha ha! I am free!”

He ripped out piercings with his one good finger. Blood sprayed.

“Die.”

Even without hatred, Tess knew—this man should not exist.

Then the control room wall exploded.

“Why are you so late?”

Demons poured in, led by the brigade commander.

He checked Zetaro and Sumodo. Then noticed the missing guards.

*These…*

Even though Satan had taken root in Havitz, demons still hated humans.

And now that military authority had shifted from Balkan to Paimon, no one cared about two human elites.

*Still, they’re important to Satan.*

Worried about the consequences if they died, the commander ordered Zetaro and Sumodo carried out.

Sumodo passed out.

“Return to base! Get those with regenerative abilities on standby!”

“Stop!”

Tess yanked her saber from the floor and charged.

But the brigade commander had already retreated.

“Damn!”

With Sumodo injured, capturing the flower would be difficult for now.

But Tess didn’t feel satisfied.

*I’m not human.*

The image of Sumodo and Zetaro being carried by demons looked monstrous.

~*~

Elsewhere, the sound of ribs breaking echoed rhythmically.

If it weren’t his own bones, Lian would have laughed.

Natasha moved with a rhythm all her own, slipping into his blind spots.

*Temporal Concerto.*

A fast-tempo dance piece by Rastani, known for sudden tempo shifts.

Took. Took. Took.

More than the pain, Lian worried—the speed of his regeneration was slowing.

*No. Maybe this is normal.*

His right arm, once unnaturally strong, now matched the left.

Ymir’s arm—swallowed long ago—was gone.

*Why, Ymir…*

Had Ymir used his own body to regenerate Lian’s arm?

*Thanks to that, I crossed countless death lines. And finally reached Idea.*

If that was Ymir’s wish…

*Good.*

Lian opened his eyes and charged.

*From now on, this is my path.*

His strength wasn’t in intelligence. It was in never giving up, no matter the odds.

“Goes!”

His straight sword slashed down.

Natasha twisted mid-air, half a turn, the blade barely missing her nose.

The steel wire connected diagonally snapped taut, threatening to break.

Close. But Lian felt no regret.

*Talent is talent. But that incarnation…*

O……

Incarnation—the physical manifestation of a person’s inner nature.

A skeletal form meant something inside had already died.

*Does that mean life is meaningless?*

Natasha’s afterimage flickered in the light, left and right.

Lian felt dizzy.

One blink—she was on the right. The next—on the left.

*Extremely good.*

Her battle form.

Her incarnation pushed her physical abilities to the limits of natural law.

The price? Probably automatic puppetry.

Natasha was a puppet who couldn’t dance alone.

Three frames passed.

Her fist struck Lian’s stomach.

“Ouch!”

He brought the Daejikdo up to his chest—too late.

Natasha asked, “Can you see my movements?”

Just activating the Kaleidoscope consciously placed him among super-high masters.

“But do you know why you can’t avoid them?”

She raised her index finger.

“Rhythm.”

The Kaleidoscope was a realm of cognition.

Even if an arrow seemed slow, catching it was another matter.

With divine transcendence, Lian could move at the speed of thought.

“But you have no sense of rhythm at all.”

If an opponent’s movements followed an unknown rhythm, they became invisible.

*The seesaw of time isn’t magic.* If your body can’t sync with the opponent’s rhythm, even the Kaleidoscope fails.

Natasha moved her finger up and down, wrist swaying like a metronome.

“Can you feel it?”

Lian stayed silent.

“How about this? Tell me—what’s different?”

When he didn’t answer, Natasha nodded, as if her analysis was complete.

“You know why rhythm matters? All events unfold along time’s flow. A person without rhythm just accepts what comes. But with rhythm, you can divide an event into beats. Think on one, prepare on two, strike on three. You only react when it’s already in front of you.”

Lian couldn’t argue.

“Another advantage: you can pull the opponent into *your* rhythm. Fight any strong enemy for five minutes, and you’ll read their pattern. Then it’s a numbers game. That’s the fun part.”

She paused.

“You don’t feel rhythm at all. Honestly? It’s like fighting a beast—strong, but mindless.”

Lian exhaled.

“That is my way.”

“That way is wrong. Maybe you’ve survived because you crushed most enemies before they could fight back? Before you even sensed their rhythm.”

He knew it was true.

“Like a predator catching prey—overwhelming force. It works. But once you’re used to it… Well, how do I say it? Your battle is like a children’s song. Tone-deaf.”

Lian remembered Rayna’s older sister teaching him nursery rhymes as a child.

He’d sung along, excited. She’d giggled every time.

*Wow. That was a good memory.*

But this wasn’t a game.

Natasha said, “No matter how strong the voice or wide the range, that’s not a song. In the same way, you’re strong—but you’re not a good fighter.”

“So what?”

His ribs had fully healed.

“I knew from the moment I picked up a sword that I had no talent. I failed countless times. Was ridiculed.”

“Still, you say you don’t want to win?”

“No.”

Lian charged.

“That means it’s *my* sword.”

Seeing him stronger than before, Natasha tilted her head, almost absurdly.

*Am I stupid?*

She’d pointed out his flaws completely. Shouldn’t he at least reflect?

*He did.*

But Daphne hadn’t.

Ever since Natasha criticized her, Daphne had done nothing but slander her.

“… Shinigami’s Martial Arts.”

Natasha’s eyes burned with killing intent.

The wildest, fastest dance she knew.

*Fantastic Night.*

She moved, carving an empty tunnel through the air, and kicked Lian in the side.

“Keugh!”

Yaksha muscles twisted like a pretzel.

*If rhythm fails, I’ll catch up with force!*

Twisting his waist, Lian finally brought her into view.

*There.*

In the extreme Kaleidoscope, her body flickered like a strobe.

*What is this?*

She was slipping in and out of his awareness.

1/40,000th of a night.

The beat at which she processed events.

*I have no choice but to bell!*

Lian swung his straight sword down.

Natasha turned, stepping along the blade’s edge.

Ting. Ting. Ting.

He couldn’t react. She flicked the massive blade with her fingernails.

Beats remained.

Natasha watched his gaze as he tracked her.

*Here’s a change.*

She suddenly shifted to the opposite side.

“Damn!”

For the first time, despair rose in Lian’s chest.

*Can I really not win?*

To hold onto his crumbling resolve, he roared like a beast and swung his sword.

It was pure violence.

But for Natasha, who fought with rhythm, it was difficult.

*Even if it hits me, it’ll be death.*

1/80,000th of a night.

Splitting the rhythm finer, Natasha regained control and unleashed a storm of blows.

Fist marks covered Lian’s body.

“Damn it! Damn it!”

Ashamed that he couldn’t respond even when he saw it, Lian finally admitted it.

*I’m not suited for the sword.*

I have no talent.

*A man like me—stop Ymir? No. Better to leave it to this woman.*

Whoever wins will show a great battle.

*Why me?*

If Guy-hyung were alive… No. If the Idea had been passed to Rye-hyung…

‘Reina noona is better than me too!’ Even she had blue hair.

‘This bastard doesn’t know how to do anything! What the hell am I supposed to do here…’

As Natasha’s fist slammed into Lian’s left eye socket, half her vision vanished.

‘Why, why does it have to be me!’ So it’s you…

…….

Every cell in Lian’s body seemed to scream.

It was unclear whether this was a spark of hope or the conclusion of the Ogent dwelling within her cells.

But faster than a kaleidoscope shifting, an image flashed—Ogent galloping across the heavens.

Losing even his human form, he roared Smille’s name and raised his sword.

The enemy was mankind’s strongest swordsman.

‘It’s never beautiful.’

Ogent had saved Smille from the wrath of Heaven itself.

‘I see.’

Lian’s dream was to become a swordsman who would protect the brightest wizard in human history.

‘It was that simple.’

Lian, her face twisted like a yaksha’s, gripped the greatsword with both hands and twisted her body.

Natasha’s pupils shrank.

‘What?’

Lian’s back was still facing her, but the yaksha-like face, seemingly rotated unnaturally, protruded translucently from behind.

‘Great!’

Guided by the will of divine transcendence, Lian’s body began to accumulate rotational power.

‘With or without talent!’

‘Incredible speed…!’

Natasha leaned back, evading.

‘To mock or not!’

Following the afterimage of Daejikdo, the air shattered like glass.

‘Either human! Not even human!’ Did that even matter?

‘It’s enough if I do it!’ Natasha’s skin prickled as she realized the great straight sword was closing in on her waist.

1/160,000 of a night.

That fleeting moment when she thought she had split the rhythm finely enough—still time to spare.

‘Tuk’?’

She heard something snap inside her head.

The rhythm was breaking.

‘Irregular beat.’

What kind of beat could not be contained even when time was divided into 160,000 fragments?

‘That can’t be!’

Her pride wounded, Natasha felt the rhythm accelerate—1/320,000 of a night.

But what echoed in her mind was no longer a beat.

Tutututututu! Tutututututu!

Only the noise of perfect, indefinite pulses flooded in, obliterating all rhythm.

‘This guy.’

Natasha realized.

‘I have no real talent.’

If having too little talent could be considered a talent, then was Lian the natural enemy of geniuses?

‘Does that even make sense?’ Talent wasn’t talent.

As if in answer, the tip of the sword pierced deep into Natasha’s abdomen, sending a cold shock through her.

‘Ah, that’s right.’

To Natasha, it was merely a flaw.

‘Life for this man.’

She had lived with that single truth in her heart. She had swung her sword endlessly, striving to overcome it…

“Yaaaaaaa!”

Because it was life itself.

“Huh!”

Blood gushed from her stomach like a fountain, and Natasha moaned involuntarily.

Suddenly, a thought surfaced.

‘I’……

Maybe we’re overcomplicating things?

Wasn’t it simply that, because I couldn’t master one action, I drowned myself in endless thoughts—running away?

‘Look, it’s Daphne.’

The friend she had once dreamed alongside.

‘You can do it.’

Natasha smiled bitterly.

‘Still…’

If she had eyelids, she would have been smiling gently.

“Sorry.”

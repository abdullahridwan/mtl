Chapter 992

Simultaneous Incidents (2)

~*~

Ikael clenched his fists.

It wasn’t anger that tightened his grip, but something deeper—desperation, as if he were clinging to the last thread of control.

Then, a cry tore from his throat, raw and unbearable.

“Huh.”

It would take a miracle.

For a dead child to return and tell her mother it wasn’t her fault.

“Ikael.”

Satiel’s voice was cold, sharp.

“What happened? If you truly recovered your memories, then explain—”

Uriel cut in.

“Wait.”

Satiel turned on him, eyes blazing with fury.

“What did you just say to me?”

“Whatever memories were lost belong to the past,” Uriel said calmly. “Ikael-sama has not made any choices yet.”

“Nonsense.”

Satiel sneered.

“The past? Ikael betrayed us. He knew everything and pretended ignorance.”

“Hey,” Shirone snapped, stepping forward. “Quiet. I’m talking now.”

Her anger was justified, but Satiel, consumed by pain and betrayal, seemed beyond reason.

“Are humans now trying to stand equal to archangels?” Ikael said, his voice low. “Shall I kill you where you stand?”

“Satiel,” Ikael continued, “I will judge. I will decide.”

Satiel felt a surge of contempt rise from the depths of his mind—toward Guffin, toward Ikael, toward Shirone. The bond between the latter two, the loss of the former—it all twisted together, endlessly stirring forgotten memories.

‘Hate. I hate you so much.’

The emotion wasn’t clear, but that only made it more terrifying. At last, Satiel’s reason shattered.

“Shut up.”

Ikael’s eyes twitched.

To curse an archangel was a rare offense in the history of Heaven.

Uriel glanced back, but Satiel’s face was already twisted, monstrous.

“Why? What are you people?”

The question made no sense on the surface, but Shirone and Ikael understood.

“How dare you do this to me—just because you’re so powerful?”

The light in Satiel’s celestial form dimmed. Instead of starlight, tears of blood welled in his eyes.

Three-headed Mara Ionas spoke.

“Satiel, regain control. Remember—the mechanism of fallen angels.”

Can angels fall?

Perhaps. If the mind becomes trapped within the pure mental body.

A heart that aches.

And a heart that torments itself—only appears when one is ready.

“What, you’re ignoring me now?”

Satiel didn’t stop.

Each time Shirone and Ikael agreed, it felt like his mind was being torn apart.

‘Yes. Now I understand.’

It was a vague sensation, but undeniable.

“I was always the bridesmaid! You ruined me! All of you!”

This time, even Shirone stayed silent. She knew how deeply Satiel had cared for humanity—especially after Guffin and Ikael had children of their own.

‘Poor angel.’

But she also knew the truth.

That Satiel had killed an unnamed infant in a moment of madness.

“He killed me!”

Satiel screamed.

“Tell me! What did I do wrong? Did you even try to destroy the archangel’s authority—or not—”

His voice cut off.

“Ah.”

From the shattered fragments of memory, images surged forward, drawn like iron to a magnet.

Ankera. Secret conversations. The death of a child.

‘No.’

His fingertips trembled. His mind burned, his entire body aflame.

“It can’t be.”

He tried to look away, but delusion was impossible for an angel—a being of pure cognition.

“This is the reason… There must have been a reason!”

Ikael remained silent.

The longer he stayed quiet, the more Satiel unraveled.

“No! It’s because you’re evil! Yes—that’s it! It’s all your fault!”

Ionas shouted.

“Fix it! When the mental body collapses—”

“Ah ah ah ah ah!”

Satiel clutched his head, his planetary form losing its perfect sphere.

“Kill!”

His blood-soaked eyes locked onto Shirone.

“I said—kill!”

At the command, Ionas—who had vanished—reappeared directly in front of Shirone.

Extreme Decomposition – Atomic Movement.

“Don’t hold a grudge.”

Ionas raised his right fist. It disintegrated into atoms and fired at the fastest speed in the physical world.

Enemy Zero.

A military term for a zone of absolute safety.

It was also the state Shirone’s Hand of God had temporarily created within Basuka.

“Win! We won!”

No one knew how the news spread, but citizens poured into the streets, cheering.

The demons had been annihilated. The giants, stunned by their own defeat, had vanished.

“Long live Tormia! Long live Tormia!”

They stared at the agape light exploding in the sky, curious but silent.

For now, they could only scream, running from the truth that the war wasn’t truly over.

“All citizens of Tormia, listen!”

A carriage carrying a tall podium rolled down the boulevard and stopped in the center of the plaza.

An old man—seventeenth in the royal bloodline—stood on the podium, holding a loudspeaker.

The people didn’t recognize him, but they knelt at the sight of the royal robes embroidered with Tormia’s symbol.

The plaza fell silent. The stillness spread like ripples, engulfing every block.

“Dear citizens of Tormia,” the old man began, “today, our great king, Adolf XII, has passed away.”

Shock painted the faces of the crowd.

“Oh, this can’t be…”

The old man, who had held their attention, continued.

“Remember this: in the face of the menace that invaded Basuka, our royal family did not retreat even an inch.”

In truth, he was furious—no one had informed him about the elemental bomb. He bit back his rage.

‘How dare they bypass the royal line? I’ll find whoever’s behind this. I won’t let it go.’

He didn’t understand what had happened, but restoring royal authority was his first priority.

“Adolf XII fought the giants to the end and died bravely.”

“Ah, Your Highness,” a weeping citizen called out, rising. “Could the light that destroyed the demons have been the king’s final act as he passed?”

One of the old man’s eyes twitched.

‘What nonsense. Annoying.’

But even the most rational person seeks meaning when life hangs in the balance.

“I believe so too,” someone else said.

The mood turned solemn.

“Even as he died fighting the giant, the king thought only of his people. That is the true royalty of Tormia.”

“Whoaaaa!”

The crowd erupted.

The old man seized the momentum and shouted.

“The lineage of a great king will never be broken! I hereby proclaim—welcome Adolf XIII, eldest son of Adolf XII, as the new king of Tormia! Let us fight! We are Tormia! We will never run!”

“Long live His Majesty King Adolf XIII! Long live Tormia!”

The cheers surged, rolling down the streets toward Allog.

Inside the Magic Association’s president’s office, Rufist frowned as he sifted through documents.

“I’m going to die from the noise.”

Flew shut the window. The clamor faded.

“The royal family moves fast. Honestly, it’s just luck. Still, I don’t think there’ll be any issue with the succession.”

“Of course not. These people only know how to eat and sleep.”

A loud knock echoed from the door.

“President of the Association! Rufist! Are you in there? Open up! It’s urgent!”

It was the voice of the seventh in the royal bloodline. Rufist could guess why he’d come.

The knocking grew louder.

“Open the door! Don’t you know Baschka will descend into chaos soon? How dare you deceive the royal family?”

Flew, hands behind her back, glanced at Rufist.

“What should I do?”

“Get rid of him. I don’t have time for politicians.”

Rufist needed to assess the damage and reorganize the city’s forces immediately.

Flew opened the door just a crack.

“Apologies. The president is busy with official matters. Please come back later.”

“What? You—yes, you’re the chief secretary. Do you know who I am? I’m of the royal bloodline—Ouch!”

The head of security stepped forward and twisted the noble’s arm.

“I must be rude. Please understand—this is a special wartime situation.”

“It hurts! Let go! Ahhh!”

Flew sighed as she watched.

Shirone walked down the hallway, dragging her feet.

“Let go! Fine, fine—just let go! It hurts!”

She turned her head, watching the royal official being escorted away.

When she looked again, she was already at the president’s office.

“Sister,” Shirone said, “who was that?”

Flew opened the door wide and smiled.

“Nobody important. Come in.”

Shirone stepped inside. Rufist stood.

“Quantum transfer?”

“Space travel. Being too close feels strange. I’m used to Basuka now.”

The landmarks had changed.

“You were looking for me?”

“Yes,” Rufist said. “As you saw on your way, the royal family has begun moving. They’re trying to claim all credit for this war—yours included.”

“It’s fine. I don’t care.”

“Of course not. But it’s uncertain whether the current royal line can properly lead during wartime.”

It was a bold statement, coming from the Association President. Shirone blinked.

Rufist revealed his true intent.

“We want to push Pony.”

“Hmm.”

Shirone didn’t confirm or deny. She seemed to be thinking.

“Is there a reason?”

“She was one of the few who uncovered the atomic bomb project on her own. We thought she might use this moment to stage a coup—but she didn’t.”

Shirone laughed.

“Because she’s a wizard.”

“Exactly. That’s what we need. The Ultima System Project. If Pony becomes king of Tormia, it will greatly aid the unification of mankind.”

An undeniable truth.

“Then what about the current king? Is he unfit?”

“He’s qualified.”

Rufist looked out the window.

“King Adolf XII wasn’t a good man. But he was pragmatic. He even accepted that madman Gaold as Association President.”

That explained everything.

“He had three sons. Each different, but none of them are practical. Which means they’re unfit for war. It’s foolish to expect deep character from people, but when their behavior causes chaos—it becomes a problem.”

Shirone knew the history before her time.

‘Still, I should see for myself. People change as they age.’

She made her decision and nodded.

“Then I’ll go.”

“Remember,” Rufist said. “Ideals like Annie Park or Messiah won’t work on them. To stop this early…”

Shirone tapped the inside pocket of her coat.

“Yes. I’ll use this.”

No royal family could ignore the Five Stars of the Ivory Tower Integrated Space Management Department.

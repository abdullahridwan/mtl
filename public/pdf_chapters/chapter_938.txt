Vulcan gritted his teeth and muttered,

“This is… embarrassing.”

The armies of Hell were currently stationed in the mountains, sixty kilometers from the cliffs of the Abyss.

He had not expected her power to be this overwhelming.

Harvitz had argued that she wasn’t strong enough to tear apart the veil of the universe—but even so, her strength defied logic.

‘Apostasy,’ by definition, meant transcending the system. In human terms, it simply meant surpassing the limits of biological existence.

But this woman was different.

The force that had shattered the Abyssal Cliff wasn’t merely biological—it approached the level of a natural law, something beyond mere physical evolution.

‘It was impossible from the start. This kind of power didn’t manifest when we dealt with Natasha.’

What mechanism was at work?

‘Gwangwang Banjo. Rage. Special Law…’

Even as Vulcan analyzed Son Yu-jeong’s abilities, a commotion erupted near Harvitz.

“Your Majesty! Calm down!”

Harvitz knelt on the ground, his fingers clenched, his face twisted into a grotesque smile.

“Who… dares… destroy Shiok?”

Each time the purple smoke rising from his body trembled like a heat haze, nearby demons convulsed in agony.

“Big…”

Chaos rippled through the ranks.

If united, they could become the incarnation of Satan—but now, consumed by rage, they couldn’t even maintain their forms.

‘This is the first time I’ve seen Harvitz like this. Why? Is Shiok really that important?’

Only Harvitz knew which of the Twelve Apostles had remained at the 0.666-second mark.

“Fix it, Satan,” Vulcan said coldly.

The woman passed in front of him.

She was under 150 centimeters tall, with pitch-black hair and a pale face. Her unusually large eyes held a glint of madness, though nothing about her seemed outwardly remarkable.

“Paimon.”

Paimon, commander of the 9th Corps, was the one responsible for the hospital in the Demon World.

Originally a soldier in the Army of Hell, she had since been stripped of her rank as General Commander by Balkan.

‘Am I still jealous?’

Harvitz had started speaking despite the distance—because he was aware of Balkan’s presence.

‘I can’t help it this time.’

If not for Paimon’s ability, the damage from the earthquake would have been far worse.

She turned slightly toward Balkan, a faint smile on her lips, and slowly spread her fingers like a flower bud unfurling.

“Eating Out Ban (BAN).”

It was an ability that isolated a specific space and absorbed the motility of those trapped within.

‘Convenient for strategists,’ Vulcan thought, ‘though brutal.’

By isolating six million of the demon army, Paimon had instantly relocated the remaining forces sixty kilometers away.

She was also the one who had previously contained the spread of Minerva’s magical undead.

“I can tell what you’re thinking just by looking at your face,” Paimon said, her smile widening. “But it’s a human theory. And this ability—it’s human-made.”

“Yeah, right,” Vulcan replied. “A weak subject’s tiny hair is impressive. Eating out is quite fun.”

Only insane humans could become Law Eaters—but that didn’t matter to demons.

What mattered was that she had already sacrificed six million allies without hesitation.

Paimon waved a hand dismissively.

“Being interested in humans is just another game for demons. Anyway, I’m watching you, Commander-in-Chief—who failed.”

As she moved away from Harvitz, Natasha approached Vulcan.

“Shall I kill her?”

“No. Let’s watch a little longer. It seems Satan’s influence is stronger in her than Harvitz’s now.”

Paimon leaned close to Harvitz, who was still muttering to himself.

She whispered something.

Harvitz blinked, then slowly turned around.

Paimon smiled, cold and sharp as a blade.

“It will be a lot of fun.”

“Quack.”

As Harvitz’s chaotic energy coalesced, a colossal incarnation of Satan rose into the sky.

“Ha ha ha ha! Ha ha ha ha!”

Natasha, watching from a distance, turned to Vulcan.

“What did you say to make him laugh like that?”

Vulcan didn’t answer. He rested his chin on her head, lost in thought.

‘Of course I… I’m not a good person.’

He was the man who had killed hundreds of millions in pursuit of winning a rare game—World War II.

‘I’ve done every terrible thing a human can do.’

How dull Harvitz must have been, realizing Link merely by staring into the eyes of a dying corpse.

“But Harvitz dies of excitement?”

Natasha turned her head. Vulcan’s temples were slick with sweat.

“I really am a bad guy… Why?”

“This time, the demons’ creativity is on fire.”

“Knock-knock! Knock-knock!”

Paimon, her eyes lifted and her hand covering her mouth, glanced sideways at Balkan.

Iruki leapt to his feet.

“Let’s think this through one more time!”

The commanders turned in surprise. Aromi rushed to his side.

“Sir General?”

Iruki’s eyes widened.

“Ah.”

The thoughts swirling in his head spilled out before he could stop them.

‘Immense stress.’

It was understandable—but dangerous. The commander-in-chief revealing his inner turmoil in front of his subordinates was a problem.

“Sorry,” Iruki muttered.

“Baska is overcrowded. If an elemental bomb explodes there…”

He didn’t even want to imagine it.

“Baska is the capital of Tormia. It’s also where the Mercody family—the commander-in-chief’s family—resides.”

“That’s—?!”

The commander cut himself off.

“I know. That’s not why I’m conflicted. The same applies to all of us. What I’m saying is… in the end, this is a decision only the commander-in-chief can make.”

‘I have to choose.’

The lives of everyone in Baska hung in the balance.

‘What would my father do?’

If it were a Mercodian albino, would he have sacrificed the capital’s citizens to preserve humanity?

‘No.’

He would have found another way.

‘But of course, he couldn’t.’

The contradiction revealed itself—this thought was just another useless delusion.

‘This is a situation unprecedented in human history. I have no precedent to compare it to.’

That was why the commanders had handed over full authority to him.

‘No, wait—there is one.’

Shirone.

A true Yahweh would have understood Iruki’s position, his pain.

‘Is that what Shirone endured?’

Iruki’s eyes turned red.

‘Have you been carrying this nonsense on your shoulders all this time?’

The commanders called out in concern as his emotions surfaced.

“Sir General.”

Iruki raised his head, tears unshed.

“Leave me alone.”

He wore a resigned smile.

“Before you become a rare murderer, can you at least pretend to feel the tears of a hypocrite?”

Aromi’s face crumpled, streaked with tears.

‘I can’t do this.’

It was her duty to keep the general’s mind stable—but this was beyond her.

“Sir, if it’s too hard…”

“No.”

Iruki issued the order.

“There is no reverse strategy. I will draw the demons to Basuka.”

I’m sorry, Shirone.

‘This is the only choice I have.’

Because Shirone wouldn’t have chosen it.

“Call the president of Tormia’s Magic Association. And the manager of ‘Rope’ as well.”

Rope—the top-secret encryption system of Tormia.

“Lupist… and are you referring to Dante?”

“Yes.”

Iruki’s tears had already dried.

“In the name of the Valkyrie General Commander, I approve the activation of Project ‘Flower Garden’.”

Dark night.

Amy entered the cabin after another long session of solitary training. Shura was waiting.

“You’re out late. Having fun? Do you think your body’s fully recovered now?”

“So-so. What about you?”

“Why do you care?”

Nane was kind, but Shura’s true nature grew sharper with time.

Amy suspected why, but she didn’t want to dwell on it.

“I’m leaving tomorrow morning.”

Shura’s eyes widened slightly.

“I thought I should say goodbye in advance. You’ve helped me a lot. Where are you now?”

“…In the garden behind the vegetable patch.” Amy turned toward the door.

“Yes. Thank you for everything.”

Shura stopped her.

“Why do you fight?”

“What do you mean?”

“You know now. Buddha is right. Why are you so obsessed with a world where balls mean everything?”

Amy looked at the door and spoke.

“A man who loved me died in terrible agony—to save me.”

Corporal Berrick.

“Isn’t death the end? It was love, and he’ll never see me again. But he chose to die. When I asked why, he said what Ram said…”

Amy turned her head.

“He said he wanted to be an incredibly cool guy.”

Shura fell silent, staring at the sad smile on Amy’s face.

“Humans die one day. But just because the ending is the same doesn’t mean the journey is. He’s dead, but he lives in my heart.”

“I’m alive…”

Shura remembered the words of Veron, head of the Tenro Society.

– Live.

“So you fight. After all, even if it’s just a ball, even if this universe disappears…”

Amy opened the door.

“Because my last moment will be more beautiful than yours.”

The door closed softly—but to Shura, it sounded like thunder.

After leaving the cabin, Amy climbed a familiar mountain path.

Nane sat waiting, a blade of grass between her teeth.

“…Was it that uncomfortable being with me?”

She seemed to already know the answer.

“Thank you for your kindness. But I’ve recovered, and I have things to do.”

“I know you’ll be busy.” Nane pointed across the landscape.

“See? Just now, evil energy erupted on a scale greater than ever before.”

Amy turned.

“I can see it.”

“The hell that’s about to unfold in this world. It won’t be something humans can endure. It’ll be worse than death.”

Nane turned to her.

“I have to finish this.”

Amy couldn’t argue. She knew Nane wasn’t someone to be deceived by lies.

“I’ve been thinking for days.”

Amy sat beside her.

“You said you’d love me. But how can you still love me?”

“I admit it. It’s easier said than done. But I’ve realized a lot.”

“A true Buddha isn’t an extreme.”

“No matter where you go, you’re human. And by returning to being human, you transcend everything.”

Nane’s heart pounded as she looked at Amy.

“The me at the beginning and the me now are different. I understand now. Sincerely…”

“What if I go to you?”

Nane’s mouth closed.

“If I love you—not Shirone—can I fight to protect this world? If that brings happiness, if war ends and everyone survives…”

‘Amy’s love.’

Nane’s thoughts raced.

‘You’ll be happy. Okay. If you stay by my side, I’ll want to protect this world too.’

Time slipped by.

‘Iced coffee.’

Even when she was uncovering the truth of the universe, she hadn’t felt this torn.

‘Shirone.’

For the first time, she envied Shirone—for being able to include Amy in his life.

“I am…”

Nane’s face twisted.

“I am committed to the salvation of sentient beings. I love you, but I cannot turn away from those who suffer.”

“Yes.”

Amy smiled gently.

She finally understood why Shirone had left the one he loved—and why Nane couldn’t compromise.

That much belief.

‘But now, so am I.’

Amy extended her hand.

“Take care. Thank you. It’s strange to say, but… I hope you succeed.”

“Haha.”

Nane smiled and shook her hand.

“Yes. I’d like to see it through.”

In the end, there can be only one winner.

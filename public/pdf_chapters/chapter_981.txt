Chapter 981

What the Guffins Left Behind (3)

~*~

Vulcan murmured, “There are no elites in the army of hell.”

When Havitz had insisted on marching to the capital of Tormia, he made a bold decision. He would send the corps commanders’ troops to scatter across the world and attack Basuka with only the remaining 5% of his forces.

Paimon rolled his eyes. “So it took heavy losses. Even the three comrades you valued so highly came back half-dead.”

It was a shocking outcome, even for Balkan. When he heard that Natasha had been defeated by a knight of Mach, he thought it was a joke.

Paimon spoke again. “Now you’re stopping the advance on Basuka? Doesn’t matter—the flower can’t move. You can just ignore it. There aren’t many humans left to harass, and only our own demons are dying now.”

“Since when did you care about the soldiers?” Vulcan replied. “And if we push into Basuka, we might suffer even greater losses than we already have.”

“That’s your delusion. Above all, Satan wants it. Turn your forces toward Basuka immediately.”

Vulcan didn’t respond.

‘Havitz isn’t coming back.’

He sensed it intuitively. He had watched Havitz for too long not to recognize the pattern.

‘Havitz’s strategy always goes far beyond what any human—or demon—can predict. In this case, it’s clearly more advantageous for him to abandon the battlefield.’

If that was true…

‘Then this is an opportunity.’

Vulcan’s eyes gleamed.

‘I won’t go to Basuka. Not now. Not for a long time. Instead, I’ll cut through the flower fields and enter the Central Continent.’

Even if he was imprisoned for disobeying the king’s order, a commander’s duty was to wage the best possible war.

“For now, advance.”

Hearing Vulcan’s command, Paimon’s expression shifted, his eyes regaining life.

‘Tch. Just like a human.’

If Vulcan hadn’t been Satan’s favored one, Paimon would have dragged him off his horse and crushed him on the spot.

“Yes?”

At that moment, a demon descended from the sky, emitting ultrasonic waves inaudible to humans.

“Paimon-sama, I’ve located Satan-sama. He is currently moving toward Kashan. And—”

Paimon, who had been listening to Balkan’s report with a blank expression, suddenly narrowed his eyes.

“Oh?”

After absorbing all the information, a cold smile crept across his lips.

Tormia was the epicenter of the war, but conflicts large and small were erupting across the globe. Amid the collapse of countless kingdoms, the stars beneath the 4-star Ivory Tower carried out their assigned missions.

Among them, the stars of the System Inspection Department were tasked with humanitarian relief.

“Wherever you go, there’s war.”

Besides the main hellish army advancing on Tormia, other demons were acting independently.

First, there were rogue demon tribes—formed when over sixty of the 72 corps commanders vanished.

Demons followed Satan, yes, but those born from his chaotic essence obeyed in different ways.

Especially powerful ones began posing as corps commanders, seizing cities, and terrorizing the human populations within.

“It’s the end of an era.”

An old man with gray hair streaming down his back stood at the edge of a cliff and spoke.

“A world where fighters are glorified, but such glory means nothing. That’s why we must avoid creating more battlefields.”

Arnold Lampa, a 3-star resident of the System Inspection Department.

At 112 years old, he was deeply knowledgeable about global affairs. Aside from occasionally drinking tea with Mongin Louver, he led a quiet life. Yet he held the second-highest rank in the department, just below Oh Dae-seong Fried, thanks to his mastery of an information magic called Telebird.

Its transmission range spanned an entire hemisphere.

No one else could match its reach—except Lampa, who possessed the world’s most advanced breakaway spirit zone.

“Jjujju, you’re good.”

As Lampa bent his index finger, a blue skylark materialized and flapped its wings.

“Enemy spotted at Amore Hill. Estimated threat level: A. Moving slowly toward Beckyd Village.”

After recording the data, he raised his finger, and the telebird shot into the sky in a straight, brilliant flash.

A young man in his twenties, following behind Lampa, spoke.

“Another A-rank this time?”

He was a white-robed student from the East—handsome overall, though his eyes drooped slightly.

He was Lampa’s disciple, a man named Gun.

“The fallen kingdom’s army is beyond our capacity. Shouldn’t we request reinforcements?”

“You need troops to back you up. Don’t brag just because you’re young. Best thing you can do is run.”

Lampa bent his middle finger, and this time a red telebird formed.

“I’m telling you again—don’t even stop to change your underwear. Just run. We won’t be responsible for what happens after.”

Chong Yi clenched his fists as he watched the red flash vanish into the sky.

“Damn it! If only I’d learned combat magic…”

“Arthur,” Gun said. “People should know their place. Those who learn combat magic don’t fight. Those who fight learn battle magic. You’re suited for this.” His lips curled in mild disdain.

“When did I say I wasn’t good at it?”

“It’s because you care too much about others. Be honest. You never considered developing information magic, and you don’t even notice when women pass by with strange new tech?”

Chong’s face flushed.

“Who’s bewitched? Have I ever even gotten along with a girl?”

Truthfully, he had a decent face and a naive streak, but his personality wasn’t abrasive.

“Want to go on a date?”

“Yes! Absolutely, Master. Do you know what the most unfortunate thing in the world is? I’ve never had a passionate love.”

“In times of peace, it was hard enough. Now? Making light butterflies won’t impress women anymore. This is an era where strong men are in demand.”

“Tch. That’s why I’m learning combat magic…”

Lampa smiled and turned his head.

“How about finding someone among the stars or satellites? Aren’t there plenty of good women? If it’s a 3-star or lower, I might even let go of my bridge.”

“The stars?”

Chong rolled his eyes, recalling every one he knew, and sighed.

“I hate them. They’re all weird.”

“Chuck! On the outside, maybe. But everyone’s got a soft heart inside. Besides, with your standards so high, where do you even stand…?”

For a moment, Lampa’s expression stiffened.

Gun felt it too. Without a word, both dropped into defensive stances.

“Concealment.”

As Chong Yi held up a curtain of light, a group of troops passed beneath the hill.

‘Who is that?’

The demon leading them had multiple horns twisted like a bison’s, curling down its back. Its brown skin bore four arms.

‘Judging by the features… Is that Barsago?’ He was the commander of the 3rd Corps of the Army of Hell.

‘But why is he here? No—did the entire 3rd Corps abandon the front line?’

Behind Barsago marched the division commanders: a woman who looked like a corpse, a man dragging his right arm on the ground, and a monster with ribs split open, exposing its stomach.

“It’s a gun.”

Chong called out, but received no answer. He turned—his disciple’s face was pale.

“… … Gun, analyze it.”

The key to information delivery wasn’t just speed—it was accuracy.

You had to judge the situation objectively, not just transmit fast.

“First of all,” the gunman swallowed and began.

“The division commanders are all S-rank or higher. That female corpse-like one seems capable of doubling her power.”

Even without prior intel on the 3rd Corps, the assessment was accurate.

“And the one in front…” Gun’s voice trembled as he licked his dry lips.

“Probably… Triple S…”

Lampa’s eyes twitched.

“… … I don’t know the exact level, but it’s far beyond that.”

He raised an eyebrow.

‘Chong Yi has no intel on the 3rd Corps.’

For special training, Lampa had deliberately withheld all information about their enemies.

‘And he’s never encountered a Triple S before. Yet he can analyze this accurately…’

It was clear evidence that Gun’s intuition had reached a level beyond human perception.

‘A genius of the sixth sense.’

Perhaps tens of thousands of invisible signals.

“It’s impressive for a novice. But when information is conveyed through vague expressions, the receiver gets confused. You see with your eyes, but they imagine. Without clear definitions, fear takes over.”

“Then how do you define it?”

“G1, G2, G3. Beyond classification. Since we can’t assign a precise force level, we categorize by the sensation they evoke. G stands for universal gravitation.”

“Um, like gravity?”

“Exactly. It’s a fundamental force in this world. If you wanted to eliminate universal gravitation, what would you do?”

Gun frowned.

“It’s impossible. You can resist it momentarily, but as long as the world exists, you can’t remove it.”

“Exactly. That’s G1.”

“You understand the feeling? We use universal gravitation because it’s the most neutral force a person can perceive. If you say ‘tsunami’ or ‘typhoon,’ people assign personality to it—arbitrary interpretations. Saying ‘like a typhoon’ makes people think of speed or chaos.”

Chong nodded.

“But if the enemy is strong enough to feel like G1, how do we defeat it?”

“That’s not the informant’s job. Another G1 or G2 will handle it.”

Lampa raised his fingers one by one.

“G2 is twice the force of gravity. G3 is three times. That’s all. We can’t go higher because…”

“Because it’s too hard to perceive, so we lose objectivity.”

“Exactly. Three times is the limit of what a normal human can conceptualize as real. Anyway, the one down there is Barsago. I don’t know about Pandemonium, but if he unleashes it, he might become a G2-level disaster.” Chong recited what he’d learned.

“It’s none of the information communicator’s concern.”

“Right. But what I’m asking is—why the hell is he here? According to my intel, all top corps commanders went to Tormia. If he came back for some reason…?”

Lampa fell silent.

Four deep lines etched across his forehead as he stared without blinking.

“Gee, Master…”

Gun grew anxious.

He knew how drastically the world shifted when harsh words left his teacher’s mouth.

“Why… why are you like this?”

Lampa rested his chin on his hand.

‘Should I send the report now? No… I can’t decide yet. It might be a misjudgment based on a single anomaly. But if I’m right…’

He ground his molars.

‘I guess I have no choice but to verify while transmitting at high speed.’

Lampa stood.

“We’re going to circle the globe for a bit. Won’t take long. You’re in charge here until I return. Trust your instincts.”

“What? Me? You said I’d never be able to compete in real operations while you were alive? Now you’re just… eating the world?”

“I’ll die soon anyway. You’ll have to do it.”

“Wait! Master!”

Before Chong Yi could finish, Lampa’s body turned into a flash of light and shot into the sky.

The range of spatial movement from the world’s best breakaway spirit zone was unimaginable.

Hey, hey, hey.

As the sonic boom still echoed, Chong stared blankly at the distant sky.

Photon transmissions—golden birds—began erupting from his master’s trajectory every ten kilometers.

‘Real-time transfer magic, Gold Bird.’

Like comets spiraling through space, countless streaks of light curved toward the temple in dazzling arcs.

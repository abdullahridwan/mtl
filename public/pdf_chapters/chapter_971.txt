As the end of the Omega drew near, tension mounted, but there was no way to accelerate the creation of the battery beyond that timescale.

Even the mating of the 3,241st rabbit on a remote planet with the 8,790th rabbit—and the birth of the 17,653rd—was being transmitted.

In the space where Shirone’s consciousness was centered, the Guffin’s voice reached him through the 11th sense.

The Guffin’s appearance remained unchanged since his arrival, and the lotus flowers that had become his home had not withered.

“I must leave.”

The Guffin stroked a lotus petal gently.

“Thank you for listening to my long, boring story. I’m sorry… …”

‘It wasn’t boring. It was a story about humans. Humans are amazing. In fact… … I want to be human too.’

“I lost my wife and children.”

The Guffin looked up at the sky.

“I once wondered what it meant to be a god. To live forever—only to lose everyone you love. You might not be able to bear it.”

‘You’ve endured. I know that.’

“I would rather have had nothing from the beginning. If this world itself had never existed… …”

For an instant, emptiness filled the Guffin’s eyes.

All.

But then, for a fleeting moment, that emptiness softened as he looked down at the lotus with deep affection.

“Still, I tried to go out into the world.”

‘Why?’

The Guffin didn’t hear the lotus’s voice, but as if answering an unspoken question, he continued.

“Because it continues. Because we must go on—endlessly.”

Rising from the lotus, the Guffin crossed a vast lake and reached dry land within a month.

“Looks like I told you too much. It must have been painful.”

He could no longer communicate with the lotus, but he hadn’t expected the plant, which had endured for forty thousand years, to remain unchanged.

“If you’re listening now, and if you understand… … then perhaps I’ve developed a heart for you too.”

After a pause, he added softly:

“It means I’m connected to the outside world.”

‘Outside’?

The Guffin smiled bitterly.

“Actually, I don’t know. Maybe this world should stop shutting itself off.”

A world filled with endless pain.

“But if you still wish to continue… …”

With a bow of gratitude for their forty-thousand-year bond, the Guffin turned.

“Help the human.”

Human.

An existence capable of leaning toward good or evil, love or destruction.

The lotus fell silent in thought.

And the moment the Guffin turned to walk away, golden smoke rose from the petals and took the form of a young woman. The girl, radiating a fresh floral scent, slowly bowed her head in the same manner as the Guffin.

‘Goodbye.’

Leaving behind the birth of the Hwajok, the Guffin walked calmly through the remote forest.

‘How much time has passed?’

He hadn’t kept exact count, but he could feel it—about forty thousand years.

An immense span for humans.

But to the Guffin, who had lived for eternity, it was no more than the time humans needed to eat a meal.

“Forty thousand years. Could the world have changed at all?” When he first arrived, there had only been primitive tribes. They probably hadn’t lasted long.

The ability of a species to survive depends on the superiority of its system. To build a system capable of adapting to countless variables, a price must be paid.

The price of failure.

‘Maybe humans are gone.’

Perhaps unimaginable monsters now ruled the world—or perhaps nothing lived at all.

‘If that’s the case, I’ll be alone again.’

Despite such thoughts, Guffin’s steps gradually quickened as he neared the forest’s edge.

‘Don’t expect anything. I might get excited and cry.’

At the moment he climbed the final slope and stepped out of the forest, the world unfolded before his eyes.

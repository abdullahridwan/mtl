Chapter 978

Age of History (4)

~*~

‘Park Ji-kyung.’

After striking a deal with Kara Torsa, Guffin descended into the underworld through Choeni Bardo.

The demonic realm was as vast as the universe itself, yet its inner landscape bore no resemblance to the physical world.

“It will be a rough journey.”

To return to the planet where the labyrinth existed, Guffin chose a path that crossed all 72 hells.

It was the only way to reach him.

“Drinking Behemoth.”

Unlike human-born demons, the demons of fallen angels had flooded into the underworld and taken form there.

They differed from Mara—beings born from pure concepts—in that they were chaotic mixtures of countless emotions.

“Not dependent.”

Without the horns that marked the Mara, he had wandered for eons before settling on a planet shaped like a labyrinth.

Of course, that was by the standards of the hidden world.

“Long time no see. King of Beasts, Behemoth.”

Before Guffin stood a mountain-sized Behemoth, seven eyes symmetrically arranged across his massive frame.

“Guffin, you came to see me?”

“I crossed 72 hells. No matter how many times I called, you never answered. Did you avoid me on purpose?”

“… … Huh.”

Behemoth shook his head and gestured to the side with claws longer than spears.

“That doesn’t matter. Sit. Have a drink? I’ve got something good.”

“I came with a request.” Guffin sat on a chair made of bones and crossed his legs. Behemoth summoned a bottle of alcohol from beyond reality and uncorked it midair.

“I know. I’m retired now, but my power still feeds the underworld.”

Behemoth had lived too long. He had severed the flow of demons to himself and withdrawn from influence. Now, he merely passed time in the human world, indulging in the petty pastimes of low-level demons.

Yet even in retirement, the demons still regarded Behemoth as the next elder after Lucifer.

“Drink. It’s good.” When Behemoth tipped the bottle with his claws, Guffin raised his glass without hesitation.

“I had a child.”

Behemoth paused, then refilled both glasses.

“When? As far as I know… …”

“Yes, he is dead. But she isn’t. Because Ikael and I are still connected.”

Behemoth swallowed a drop of alcohol—tiny compared to his size—and spoke.

“Agape? That power—the ability to create life from thought signals?”

It was a word demons despised, but Behemoth, long retired, had no such aversion.

“Yes. The child is dead, but with Agape’s power, the body is irrelevant. There is only one problem.”

“What problem?”

“You won’t be human.” Behemoth mused as he opened another bottle. By all known laws, combining Hexa and Agape could materialize a human form.

“Is it a mental issue?”

Guffin nodded.

“Yes. Think of me and Ikael—we’re beings who’ve reached the highest level this world allows.”

“Hmph. Pretending to be noble.”

Behemoth snorted but didn’t press further.

“You’ve watched countless human lives in the Underworld. You know humans—they carry both good and evil. They smile when they’re sad, laugh when they’re broken. All those contradictions shape their worldview, their Idea. But my child… …”

Behemoth spoke gravely.

“It means there’s no demon.”

“Exactly. I am a Gaian with a fully integrated mind, and the child’s mother is Ikael—the first concept in existence. There’s no room for a demon to take root. So… …”

Guffin reached the heart of his request.

“You will be my child’s devil.”

Behemoth turned from the glass and gazed into the shifting scenery of another world, lost in thought.

“By the way,” he said, pointing at Guffin again, “isn’t that excessive? Even one human without a demon would suffice. Your child with Ikael—that alone defies the norm. Why cling to the standard model?”

“No, quite the opposite. Perfection lies in Agape, not in me. The Gaian integrated mind was forged through humanity’s countless failures. Without that struggle, Ultima cannot be realized.”

“So the standard is vanishing.”

“Exactly. True goodness isn’t mere kindness—it’s the will to overcome evil from within. True love isn’t blind affection, but the resolve to give everything, even knowing it may be in vain. If either good or evil is missing, humanity cannot reach Idea.”

“He who doesn’t understand evil—can he truly understand good?”

“That’s it. Only through the cycle of good and evil do we approach what it means to be human. And at the center of that being lies Idea. Philosophy is just a tool. In the end, there is no good or evil, no merit or sorrow.”

Guffin laughed and shrugged.

“Look at me now. Having realized all this, I’m drinking with you—the greatest villain of them all.”

Behemoth turned his head and chewed the air.

“I feel no remorse.”

“That’s why I’m asking you. And… come on, isn’t your resistance just childish?”

“Greatness.”

Behemoth swept up all the bottles beside him, tossed them into his mouth, and crushed them.

“So you’re telling me to die. You cast out Lucifer, and now you want to bury me too?”

“It’s time. You know that.”

Behemoth closed his mouth.

Since the day he severed his ties, he had no regrets about his existence.

“Are you sure? I mean, the child. If I’m ever pulled out, I might tear him apart.”

“If possible… …”

Guffin grinned.

“Make it the hardest.”

“Chew-cuw-cuw.”

Behemoth, shifting his massive form, poured another drink and raised his glass alongside Guffin’s.

“Call me when the time comes. For the first and last time, I’ll meet you.” Their glasses clinked.

Guffin returned to heaven at the place where Ikael and the newborn had once shared quiet joy.

Now it was a desolate scene.

His heart ached as memories flooded back—but perhaps that pain existed only because he had once been so happy.

“So I decided to stay here.”

This was where Ikael had smiled more brightly than ever, holding their newborn child.

Above Guffin’s palm, Hexa’s hexagon rose and dissolved into a stream of luminous smoke.

Ikael’s long-stored tears floated before his eyes, glowing with pure light.

“The odds are fifty-fifty.”

It wasn’t a technical issue.

Agape, a form of Hexa, had the miraculous power to create life by merging minds.

If Ikael did not love Guffin, the two signs would never unite.

“It’s okay if you don’t remember me.”

Connected by the heart.

“So, Ikael.”

Guffin opened his arms. Ikael’s tears, carried by the Miracle Stream, soared into the sky.

“I love you.”

Agape—the greatest love in the universe.

The Miracle Stream exploded in the shape of a cross, linking the farthest ends of existence.

The miracle unfolded in an instant. Shirone experienced it endlessly through Guffin’s vision.

“Ah??????”

Light filled the universe, condensing at a speed beyond perception.

The sensation of Guffin gazing up at the white light in the sky was transmitted directly.

“Ikael.”

Agape’s success was undeniable proof—she still loved him.

“Thank you.”

He gave everything. She gave everything in return.

“Our child.”

The moment the tearful Guffin saw a distinct shadow forming within the white light.

‘This is Hexa.’

Omega’s transmission ended.

As Shirone absorbed the memory of her own birth, she heard Kara Torsa’s voice.

“The information you’ve received covers all events of the first universe. And now… …”

A new Omega had arrived.

“This’?”

It was a log that restarted each time the universe was reinitialized.

‘Awesome.’

By human standards, it was vast data. Yet compared to the entire universe, the number of altered events was less than 0.1%.

‘Even in Guffin’s erased state, the first history remains nearly intact.’

After the reset, Ankera had done everything to eliminate the anomaly.

The war between angels loyal to his will and apostles preserving time had been relayed through the 11th sense.

“Whoa.”

After Mudeungryong’s sigh, Omega’s complete records were finally transferred to Shirone.

Kara Torsa lowered her stance, pressing her chin to the floor as if too weak to lift her head.

Shirone waited.

After a long silence, Saga Karator raised her head and spoke.

“While transmitting the Akashic Records, I could do nothing. Not just because it’s impossible in practice—but because the universe’s balance would collapse if this knowledge were passed to another being.”

Shirone understood.

What a heavy burden.

And what relief Kara Torsa must feel now.

“No matter who rules from this point, the age of gods ends here. It will either close or continue. That is the Omega Year 999, as defined by Guffin.”

It was the end of an era.

“And the log I can deliver ends here. Because history is the past.” Shirone, or Hexa, did not appear in Omega.

“If you learn the log after your birth, it ceases to be your life. You could never become who you are now.”

Shirone had reached Ultima through endless suffering and enlightenment.

‘But once you know the log, your foundation for judgment collapses. In other words, your state is broken.’

If Shirone knew every event, every emotion, of all the people and friends she had met…

‘I couldn’t see the world from my current perspective.’

That was enough.

The library of knowledge hidden in the depths transformed into a perfect five-dimensional cube.

‘It contains everything in the universe.’

With every particle’s motion fully accounted for, scientific formulas and definitions became unnecessary.

Shirone spread her palms before her eyes.

‘When I remember something.’

As the five-dimensional cube—a space-time integration—rotated, it retrieved what was needed from infinite volume.

‘The time it takes… …’

None.

In the five-dimensional cube, time was a manipulable physical dimension.

‘Combine that with Hexa.’

A hexagonal light rose from Shirone’s palm and shattered with a clear sound.

“If you pour your whole heart into it.”

The light swirled, forming a flower unknown to any planet, and began to grow in her hand.

Shirone slowly raised her hand as Karator watched.

The flowers burst into the air, scattering tens of thousands of lights, cascading like a fountain.

Colorful blooms sprouted across the hard stone floor. Soon, the cave filled with their fragrance.

Shirone smiled.

“Miracles happen.”

God’s brain.

It was the triggering principle of the Miracle Stream.

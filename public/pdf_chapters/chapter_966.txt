Chapter 966

Fanaticism (3)

~*~

“The representative of Zion has arrived.”

The moment Aromi’s voice echoed from the ceiling, the door burst open and Miro stepped inside, flanked on either side by Kang Nan and Zulu.

“This woman is real!”

The captain of the bodyguards, entering just behind them, finally spotted Iruki and bowed sharply.

“Apologies! This one acted recklessly—”

Iruki raised a hand.

“It’s fine. Leave.”

Iruki never punished subordinates for failing to anticipate the unpredictable. The guard captain knew this, yet still felt shame for his lapse in discipline.

“Yes, sir.”

He wanted to say more—something sharp, like smoke from a chimney—but held his tongue. He had no intention of becoming another Miro.

*What era is this polar line even from? Arrogant. Self-indulgent.*

Iruki didn’t necessarily believe in acting for others’ sake. To him, goodness was a rigid principle—absolute, untainted by chaos. In that sense, the labyrinth itself was the polar line.

“Welcome,” Iruki said. “You’re later than expected. I assumed you’d arrive first.”

With Zulu, the most versatile among them, distance and speed shouldn’t have been an issue.

Miro settled onto the sofa, draping one arm over the backrest and crossing her legs.

“A claim came up en route. I had to deal with it.”

“A claim?”

“An apostle of time.”

Earlier, as Miro and the others flew toward the Central Continent on Kydra, a sudden stillness had fallen over the group.

“Huh?”

Miro had turned toward the cockpit when Kydra abruptly stopped moving.

“What’s wrong?”

No sound came from the controls, but Zulu had already answered, his head turned sharply.

“There’s no path forward. Flight is impossible.”

They canceled the summoning magic and landed on a desolate stretch of land littered with massive reddish-brown boulders.

After stepping out of the vacuum, Miro and the others took deep breaths—and then saw them.

Twelve figures emerged from the rocks.

“Hoo?”

Zulu’s eyes widened. Miro’s smile tightened, unnatural.

*These aren’t human.*

You could tell just by looking at their eyes. No special method was needed—the difference was that stark.

“Greetings, Polar Line of the Times.”

The speaker was an elegantly aged woman, her smile warm and kind.

*It’s a dragon.*

Only first-class dragons could assume human form. The realization only deepened Miro’s unease.

*Has there ever been a time in my memory when all twelve apostles gathered like this?*

She scanned the group. Six men, six women, spanning different ages and appearances—but none of them ordinary.

*Poison Dragon Poine.*

According to the visions, Poine was benevolent… and terrifyingly cruel.

Miro tapped her fingers against her thigh.

“Uh, mind introducing yourselves? I can’t tell who’s who just by looking.”

“You don’t need to know.”

The man third from the right on the rocky ledge suddenly transformed into a streak of blue lightning, reappearing directly in front of Miro.

The afterimage still burned in her vision as she stared at his face.

*Thunder Dragon Blitz.*

His pale, browless face was sharpened by hollowed-out eye sockets.

“This is a message from Karatorsa-sama.” Miro bent her knees slightly in acknowledgment.

“Karatorsa. A legend’s name. A message from someone like that must’ve taken time. I’m so busy I can’t even wash my clothes after running errands.”

Despite the sarcasm, Blitz delivered his words calmly.

“Do not go to the Central Continent.”

Miro waited. No further explanation came.

“That’s it?”

“Yes.”

Miro burst into laughter.

*Pooka.*

She knew exactly what it meant—twelve apostles gathering just to deliver those words.

*They’re trying to force me back.*

She wasn’t afraid, not really, but she was aware of the labyrinth’s presence.

“I want a reason.”

She wasn’t asking out of respect for the dragons. She was curious.

“Don’t misunderstand, Miro.”

The voice came from a blond boy perched on a nearby cliff.

“It’s not our words. It’s Karatorsa’s message. You just obey.”

Miro turned to him.

*What monster is this?*

He looked about twelve—wearing sport shorts, a wristband dangling between his legs, a playful expression.

A boy who loved games. But the light in his eyes sent a chill through her.

*Light Dragon Phaeton.*

Behind him, she could almost see the silhouette of a golden dragon radiating blinding brilliance.

*It’s bad if it’s the second most violent.* According to the visions, even Ray El, the Archangel of Light, had hesitated when fighting Phaeton.

Miro exhaled, then lifted her chin toward the boy.

“Tell me why. Don’t waste my time.”

The atmosphere turned heavy. Even Kang Nan felt his chest tighten at her words.

Phaeton’s head dipped slowly. His shoulders trembled, silent.

When he looked up again, his eyes held only killing intent.

“Oh, sir—!”

“Stop.”

Blitz’s voice cut through the tension. Phaeton flinched, then turned to him with the same intensity.

“Me?”

“Yes, Phaeton. You.”

Golden smoke began to rise from Phaeton’s body as he stood, slowly taking the shape of a dragon.

This time, it wasn’t welcome.

“Have you lost your mind? Do you think Mudeungryong entrusted you with this era?”

When Shirone had sacrificed himself in the Labyrinth of Andre’s World 1, Karatorsa had appointed Blitz as his representative.

Remember Shirone’s light. Without that one moment, there would be no hierarchy among the twelve apostles.

“I am merciful,” Blitz said. “If I err, I will accept it. But you—why don’t you try speaking again?”

Phaeton’s words, delivered at the speed of dragon speech, were overwhelming—raw, violent, uncontrollable.

“There is no point in fighting among ourselves.”

Karatorsa had chosen Blitz for this era precisely because of his cold, detached nature.

“We want to stop you to preserve your strength. But if you die here, what good is that? Let her cool down.”

Miro smiled bitterly.

“Ah, such deep meaning. But from what I hear, people are just water to you?”

Blitz remained unmoved.

“Karatorsa-sama protected time from three resets. Without him, this world would have already been reshaped by another’s will. We cannot comprehend his full intent. But if you demand a reason, I will give you my own.”

He turned his palms upward, offering them.

“What is it?”

“The order to stop you was likely given because I cannot guarantee Shirone will arrive in time.”

Miro blinked.

“What are you talking about?”

Blitz explained the current situation.

Shirone was receiving Omega—the complete chronological record of all information.

And soon, the Crusade would initiate an elemental bomb drop on Vasuka.

“An elemental bomb.”

Miro rested her chin on her hand, raising a finger.

“So if I’m on the battlefield when it hits, everyone dies. So we should conserve strength?”

Kang Nan stepped forward.

“Gaold will be there too!”

Blitz nodded.

“Gaold is moving with a member of the Ivory Tower. I can give you their location.”

“The Ivory Tower?”

Miro frowned. Gaold was the type who worked alone.

*He can’t even move by himself anymore?*

She tried to picture him, but nothing had changed in her mind’s eye.

“I don’t care about Gaold’s situation. I have my own mission. I’m going to the Central Continent.”

Kang Nan glared.

“What are you saying? Autumn Degas is counting on you to fight! If you die—!”

“I won’t die.”

If death came from a bomb, she wouldn’t even feel it—no hesitation, no bloody tears.

“But that’s not what I’m really wondering. How did you know about the elemental bomb? The sanctuary should’ve been secure. If it failed, the plan would’ve been scrapped. How did you find out?”

“Nothing.”

Blitz spoke the truth.

“You don’t need to do anything to know certain things. Karatorsa’s Omega is—”

“What information is being passed to Shirone right now?”

“It’s the Akashic Record Log—the operational record of everything.”

“Log? You mean every event since this world began?”

“Yes. All dragons can access the core, but none can read the logs. There was a consultation with the Guffin before the first reset. Omega is too vast, too dangerous. That’s why Karatorsa-sama chose eternal rest.”

“Hmm. And now that log is being transferred to Shirone.”

Miro shivered.

*If Shirone gains Yahweh’s omnipotence plus every record in existence… he’d be a god.*

*If that’s true, maybe I can finally catch up.*

Blitz continued.

“Do not go to the Central Continent. The logs are being transferred from Karatorsa to Shirone. We will follow Shirone. Help him save time.”

“Thanks. I understand now.” Miro lifted her head.

“But I refuse.”

A surge of life force flared behind her, but Blitz remained calm.

“Why?”

“I won’t help Shirone.”

They were allies, but their philosophies were opposites.

“Shirone believes everything has value. He thinks even evil can be redeemed. Not me. I don’t tolerate evil.”

“Shirone doesn’t forgive evil unconditionally.”

“I know. We’ve shared drinks, had conversations. But Shirone and I are extremes—poles apart. No compromise, especially in war. I don’t know how to fight while thinking about repentance. If Shirone stands in my way, he becomes my enemy too.”

“Therefore? You’re saying you’ll go to the Central Continent? Do you think you’ll survive?”

“No. Quite the opposite.”

If the operation succeeded, good. But the Axis of Evil had many unknown talents.

If they caught even a hint, luring over a hundred million people to Baska would be impossible.

“I will detonate the elemental bomb, no matter what. Even if I die.”

*Has she already given up on life?*

As Miro finished speaking, all twelve apostles rose to their feet without realizing it.

Miro swallowed, watching them with mixed emotions.

It was clear—once Blitz gave the signal, they would attack with overwhelming force.

“I offer you a choice.”

At Blitz’s unexpected words, Phaeton snarled, turning on him.

“You don’t have that right.”

“I do. I will report directly to Karatorsa. Until then, I make the judgment.”

Ignoring Phaeton’s glare, Blitz turned back to Miro.

“This is my conclusion, based on your willingness to die. If you’re going to die anyway, arguing is pointless. So I won’t order all twelve to strike you down.”

Miro stayed silent.

“Since it’s a matter of duty, I won’t cooperate. You still have a high chance of dying. But if you can’t accept that, your sincerity is in question.”

“… Any other options?”

Blitz slowly turned his head. Blue lightning flickered in his pupils.

“He’s going to kill us all.”

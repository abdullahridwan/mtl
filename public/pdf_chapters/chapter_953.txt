Armin knew he wasn’t underestimating Lian’s abilities.

In raw sword power, the orthodox view held that no one could match Macha’s knights. But if the measure was pure technique, Quan’s movements were unmatched among all humans.

Bypassing Harvits’s perception? If Quan couldn’t do it, no one could.

Even with Sein’s Iron Eye and Mei Lei’s “God’s Frequency,” it wasn’t impossible—just improbable.

Even a 1% chance was worth trying. Harvits was that kind of person.

Once the atrocities ended, the war would be over.

“Before I go,” Mayray said, “I have something to confess.”

“Terraforce fundamentally upholds goodness. But when a total war between good and evil erupts, I cease to intervene in human affairs.”

“I know,” Sein replied. “Terraforce judges humanity and destroys everything if evil wins. Though I wonder what meaning remains in a world where Sun is defeated…”

“It means that once the war between good and evil begins, even the ‘God’s Frequency’ ability will become unusable.”

“Yes. This might be your last chance. That’s why I want to do it now.”

“What are you talking about?”

Instead of answering, Mayray held out her hand to Dante.

“Do you have an awl? Or something sharp?”

Dante walked to his desk, opened a drawer, and tossed an awl to her.

“Thank you.”

Mayray gripped the handle and aimed the tip at her ear.

“The out-of-regulation frequency is triggered by covering your ears. It blocks the world’s waves and allows only Terraforce’s frequency to pass. But blocking with your hands has its limits—it still lets in noise.”

Mei-Lei, who had been silent until now, inserted the awl into her ear and pierced her eardrum.

Her nerves flared. Her eyelids fluttered.

That was the only reaction. Mayray pulled the awl out, her expression calm.

“It’s done.”

Blood poured from her ear, trailing down her lobe.

“It’s not a problem if you only need to hear sound. But the waves in your heart are sensitive. I’ve lost hearing in my right ear—maybe now I can hear you more clearly.”

Dante caught the awl as Mayray tossed it back. He flicked it once, spraying blood across the floor.

Sein, assuming the role of Order, checked the time.

“Then let’s go. Hell’s army is advancing on Tormia. Before that, you need to eliminate Harvits.”

As the assassination squad followed Sein out the door, Dante called out.

“Lia.”

“Huh?”

“Be careful.”

Lilia burst into laughter.

“I really don’t worry—”

Then she stopped, turned to Dante, and frowned.

“Today’s strange. That’s not like you.”

“What?”

“You’re the coolest person in the world. You’re at your best when you do whatever you want.”

Her cheeks flushed slightly, but she quickly composed herself.

“I don’t know what you’re thinking, but don’t let your emotions sway you. Just… do it your way. Because I’m scared.”

The old joke—that a sudden personality shift meant death—held a sliver of truth.

“Yeah.”

Seeing Dante’s relaxed smile, Lilia nodded, relieved.

“Then I’m off.”

The door closed behind her.

Dante returned to the shadows, sat on a crate, and lit a cigarette.

‘Iruki.’

They weren’t close. But for some reason, the memory of Iruki saying, *Let’s have a drink sometime,* lingered.

“There should be something to eat.”

Dante stood, made a decision, and stepped out.

He approached the deputy chief of the security team and bowed.

“Chief, I have a report. The operating code for the flower…”

“Ena-san will handle it.”

Ena, who was handing over documents, looked up.

“Yes? What is it?”

“I’m delegating all authority over the rope. If it’s Ena-san, there won’t be a need for a formal handover.”

The flowers would be controlled by the Hwajok anyway. It didn’t matter who held the code.

“Ah… there…”

Ena looked around, then realized—this was a promotion. She accepted.

“What will the team leader do?”

Dante’s smile was cool, but his eyes burned with fighting spirit.

“To the battlefield.”

He’d been hiding underground long enough. He wouldn’t waste time fiddling with codes.

“Because I feel like I’m going crazy from being pissed off.”

Omega Year 892.

Guided by the growing population of Eden, the Guffins returned to heaven.

But peace among the angels was short-lived.

“Why?”

Kariel, having analyzed Gaia’s technology through Babel, had created countless ancient weapons.

“Why have you come back?”

Ikael was both glad and terrified to see Guffin’s return. The next war would be different.

“Are you going to fight again? Aren’t you tired? Acknowledge your limits. Humanity has already lost.”

In war, the victor takes everything.

“Heaven has mastered all human techniques and improved upon them. If we fight again, the result will be the same.”

Ashur arrived just then.

“Ikael.”

Before he could catch his breath, Ashur saw Guffin and clenched his teeth.

‘Is the war between gods and humans beginning again?’

Guffin, either ignoring or understanding Ashur’s thoughts, spread his arms wide.

“Did you miss me? Looked like you were about to cry.”

Ikael didn’t understand.

“Are you saying that now? Do you think time erases the past?”

“You’re misunderstanding. We’re not here to fight.”

“Really?”

Ikael blinked, then hesitated. A faint hope flickered in his eyes.

“…Are you surrendering?”

If Gaia raised the white flag, he could push for better terms.

“No. We do not recognize Ankera.”

Ikael’s voice rose, as if accusing himself of being played for a fool.

“You say you’re not here to fight, yet you demand to meet a god? Are you mocking me?”

“Maybe I am.” Guffin extended his hand. “As Gaia’s representative, I request a meeting with Ankera. Take me to Ra.”

Ikael and the gathered angels stared, stunned.

“Directly… You want to meet Ra?”

“Yes. I’ve fought every battle in this war. I know better than anyone how long it’s lasted. I don’t want another pointless war of attrition. I came to make a deal with Ra.”

Matthew shouted, “A mere human subject meets a god? Even angels require summons to approach!”

“If you don’t like it…”

Guffin’s eyes flared. Gaia’s Idea surged, tearing through the sky.

“I’ll try again, then.”

Most angels bristled with hostility, but none spoke.

‘No matter how strong heaven is now…’

They were Gaians.

Memories of endless battles, of wars turning at the last moment, flooded the angels’ minds.

‘Every time we thought it was over, they turned the tide. That’s what’s most infuriating.’

Fight or compromise?

While the angels hesitated, Ikael stepped forward.

“You, who deny God, demand to meet Him? No words will convince Him.”

“You can do it.”

Ikael’s eyebrows twitched.

“I’ll stay.”

Focusing on Guffin, Ikael said, “I’ll give up my claim to godhood. But send the rest of the Gaians away. Is that enough to negotiate?”

“Hmm…”

Realizing that understanding Ankera’s will was the first step, Ikael fell silent.

“Good.”

A voice echoed, amplified by Ataraxia—the archangel’s symbol of power.

“I’ll arrange the meeting. But only you may attend.”

“That’s enough.”

Guffin turned. Behind him, the massed Gaians blocked all communication links.

Depending on the meeting’s outcome, war could erupt. The angels remained seated, tense.

Guffin floated into the air and approached Ikael.

“Then shall we go?”

“Hmph.”

Ikael turned away coldly, but his expression twisted as he moved quickly ahead.

‘Sigh.’

Why are you doing this?

‘Since my purpose faded, how many regrets have I carried?’

If only I’d held on stronger at the end. If only I’d truly opened my heart.

Guffin, now beside him, said, “You look like you have something to say.”

“That… …”

Ikael’s lips moved, but no sound came. It was as if a curtain had sealed his throat.

“If you stay in heaven… do you intend to stay with us?”

He realized how hard it was to give voice to his heart.

“Hmm.”

Guffin nodded.

“Perhaps I will.”

“Really…?”

The answer was vague, but Ikael turned back, a faint smile on his lips.

“Good. I won’t be much help anyway.”

Ikael walked on, unaware that Guffin’s eyes were shadowed with sorrow.

They reached the top floor where Ankera resided. Ikael prostrated herself before the door.

“Anker Rassi, MacLean Guffin, representative of the Gaians, requests an audience.”

The door opened slowly. The sound of Ankera’s heartbeat, like a drum, echoed out.

A massive chunk of flesh pulsed like a heart. Guffin showed no surprise.

Ikael rose, her expression brightening.

“He has agreed. Be respectful. Come see me when it’s over.”

“Ikael.”

Ikael, already turning away, paused.

“Why?”

They had fought for eons, but this was the first time she saw him with a sorrowful face.

‘But… why should I give up what I’ve wanted for so long?’

Ikael comforted him. “Don’t be too sad. There are ways to live happily with us. Even Ankera-sama…”

“The mind connects.”

Guffin tapped his chest twice, then pointed his index finger at Ikael.

“I want you to continue.”

“What do you mean?”

Ikael tilted his head, but Guffin didn’t answer. He turned and approached Ankera.

“Wait! Guffin—!”

Ikael reached out, but the stone door slammed shut, cutting off her view.

A sacred realm.

Ankera’s heartbeat paused as Guffin stepped toward the colossal creature that stretched to the ceiling.

With a drum-like thud, the center of the flesh split horizontally. Convex eyeballs protruded.

“Vein… snap! Cla… snap! Nerve endings on all sides tuned the sound waves, producing a rasping noise.”

“Guffin.”

Finally, a stable voice emerged—magnificent, calm. The voice of a god.

“You, who deceived the world and denied the law… why do you seek me?”

“You know what? I’ll leave the photon field. There’s a way to save the world. Let all Gaians go—except me.”

“No.”

Ankera was firm.

“You’ve caused chaos at every turn. I don’t trust your words.”

“Then you’re not a god.”

A long silence followed.

“…What do you mean?”

“Not believing in me doesn’t mean I don’t exist. If you don’t know me, you’re not God. So why don’t we settle here—negotiate who the true God really is?”

“A bargain?”

Guffin gazed at his reflection in Ankera’s pupil—and spread his arms.

“Swallow me.”

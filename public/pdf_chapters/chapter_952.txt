Unconnected Sin (1)

~*~

The army of Hell, which had occupied the Denan Plain, advanced at high speed toward Tormia.

At the forefront rode Habitz, followed closely by the cadets of Gustav Fourth.

“Move! Move!”

Zetaro stood on his saddle, stripped off his lower garments, and performed a reckless stunt mid-ride.

“Did you see that? That’s true art!” Natasha called out.

“But why take off everything underneath?”

“Center of gravity,” Zetaro replied. Natasha said nothing, merely turning her head slightly, a faint smile playing on her lips.

Zetaro’s expression darkened as he stared at Habitz’s back.

*He hasn’t laughed in days.*

A trivial joke like his meant nothing in the face of what loomed ahead.

Balkan’s expression was grim as well.

*I’m completely hooked. I can’t stop now.*

That was why they hadn’t withdrawn, even though they knew the Tormia foot soldiers were using a lure tactic.

*The troop losses were too great.*

At the edge of the abyss, Son Yu-jeong’s forces had suffered casualties amounting to thirty percent of their total strength.

It was a situation that demanded accountability—and ultimately, the right to speak had passed to Paimon, a demon soldier.

*It was my mistake. But…*

Ever since Son Yu-jeong’s fall, one thought had haunted him.

*What if the Temple anticipated this? What if they’re planning something equivalent to annihilation? It wouldn’t have been just a thirty percent loss.*

*It would have been total destruction.*

If the annihilation of the demon forces was guaranteed, the Holy War would willingly sacrifice even its allies.

*It’s likely. No—it’s certain. I don’t know their method, but I’m sure that’s their goal.*

The thought of mutual destruction.

*Crazy bastard.*

Balkan wasn’t insane—but the Temple’s soldiers were.

*And even now, my head is spinning.*

The fact that they were chasing the Tormia army meant this was the enemy’s design.

*Dangerous. I want to change course.* But it was probably impossible.

The strategy Paimon had suggested was so destructive it was unthinkable without her.

*If we open the Demon Realm, Paimon will vanish too.* Demons were willing to accept extinction for the sake of ultimate pleasure.

*Habitz is the same.*

Natasha spoke up.

“What’s wrong, Balkan? You seem troubled.”

“No, I just have a strategic suggestion—but I doubt Habitz will listen.”

“Hmm.” She scratched her chin. “Wouldn’t it be better to tell him anyway? After all, the decision is his.”

“I suppose so.”

Convinced there was merit in speaking up, Balkan spurred his horse forward and approached Habitz.

“Habitz, I have something to say—”

Balkan fell silent.

He saw Habitz rubbing his right eye until the skin turned raw and red.

“What is it?” Habitz asked, voice flat.

“I have something in my eye.”

“A bug?”

Like a fly trapped in the pupil, something darted through Habitz’s field of vision.

Habitz turned his head sharply.

“What are you babbling about?”

Balkan, realizing there was nothing in the eye, snapped back to focus.

“Ah—never mind. I wanted to suggest changing our route. The enemy seems to be luring us in.”

“I know.”

Habitz scratched his eye until it bled.

“It doesn’t matter. Humans are there too. We won’t win. I’ll kill you all anyway.”

It was a horrifying philosophy—but then again, so was everything about him.

“This is serious,” Balkan pressed. “If we don’t change course, we could be annihilated.”

“Yes or no?” Habitz snapped, raising bloodied eyes. A cruel smile twisted his lips.

“I’m not going to die anyway.”

He raised both hands and dug his fingers into the eyes of the white horse he rode.

Kihehehehehe!

At the same moment the beast screamed, Satan’s incarnation art—Vanishing—activated.

“What the hell?!”

The horse, as if born blind, surged forward, hooves pounding the earth.

When its legs tangled and it collapsed, Habitz was flung clear, tumbling across the ground.

“Ha ha ha! Ha ha ha!”

Countless hooves thundered past him, yet none struck his body.

*Death doesn’t happen. Perfect.*

As he rolled, a fly clung to the inside of Habitz’s retina. In the dim light, his grotesque grin widened.

“I am the greatest mistake!”

As he rose, arms outstretched, a demon seized his hand and hoisted him onto another horse.

“Move out! Take everything!”

Habitz’s voice, echoing from the rear, carried clearly to the Gustav Fourth cadets.

~*~

Royal Crete. On the fourth basement level—classified as top secret—National Intelligence Service personnel processed intelligence data.

In a restricted chamber known as the “dark room,” accessible only to those with Black Code clearance, a confidential meeting was underway.

“Whoa.”

Dante sat cross-legged on a crate, exhaling a long stream of cigarette smoke.

Though called a dark room, there was light—but it didn’t reach his face. Darkness clung heavily around him.

Lyria, the sorceress of Zion, spoke.

“Why sit like that? You think it makes you look mysterious?”

They were familiar with each other—having once sealed the Ice Queen together—but today the atmosphere was different.

Dante lifted cold eyes toward her. Lyria shrugged and turned her head slightly.

“No, I just… my expression’s too serious.”

Lyria frowned.

“Wait. You’re actually serious? This bastard’s so cute you climbed all the way to his head?”

Eden, the Temple monk, cut in.

“Enough bickering. I don’t have time for this. Anyway, why aren’t you here yet?”

Armand’s voice came from the shadows.

“I couldn’t make it in time. I’ll have to work with this team.”

Sein nodded in agreement. Quan remained impassive.

“I’m here.”

The door opened. Mayray entered.

“Apologies for my delay.”

She had short black hair, a pale face, and a dress that couldn’t hide her gaunt frame.

Armand spoke again.

“I heard from Dante. You supported the Temple’s atomic bomb project.”

Her role had been to identify leakers using the Daejeonghwagi’s ears.

“Yes. It’s not complete, but as the receiver, my mission is secure. And these guided elemental bombs gathered here are just as critical.”

Quan muttered, “Assassination of Habitz.”

The room’s tension thickened. Dante slowly stood.

“I don’t have much time. Let’s make this brief.”

Six pairs of eyes fixed on him.

“There’s still missing information, but humanity has identified three major characteristics of Habitz’s atrocities.”

Dante raised three fingers.

“First: a fixed future. Among all possible outcomes governed by natural law, he exists furthest from death. Second: absence of incident. His incarnations seem to negate the perception of existence itself. Third: the prison of time.”

“This intel is confidential. I received it through the Reunion channel, not Tormia’s.”

“In Shirone’s words, there’s a certain period within the concept of time that law cannot penetrate. It lasts only a moment—but in that moment, nothing happens.”

Lyria said, “So in the end, he’s free from all laws governing past, present, and future. Is he just invincible?”

“Yes. Recently, Prosecutor Lian suspected something about the absence of incident. This is nearly the only case.”

Mach’s Knight.

“And he cut through the entire radius. I couldn’t have escaped it—but I couldn’t kill Habitz. Only twelve black traces remained. Shirone calls it Shiok.”

Quan reacted.

“Lian… you said you couldn’t cut him?”

Quan knew better than anyone the power of that man’s sword.

“Yes. He’s not someone you can defeat by force. That’s why we need your help.”

It was a project Zion’s executives had been planning since arriving in Tormia under Miro’s orders.

“First, Armand’s stop magic. It enables many possibilities.”

“With time halted, we’ll infiltrate the heart of the Hell army and approach Habitz.”

Dante turned to Lyria.

“Then you extract Habitz from the battlefield using the law. You know how to connect space, right?”

“Of course. But will it really work? Satan exists outside the law.”

“If you’re a totemist, it’s possible. You can set the trap in a space not governed by Satan.”

Dante continued.

“It’ll be a full-scale battle once Habitz is removed. The future is predetermined. I believe Sein’s Iron Wheel can counterbalance that. Sein will also command the battle.”

It was the optimal arrangement.

“Second is the absence of incident. That will be countered through Mayray’s ears. I’ll also use <Nemesis>, an artifact proven effective in Operation La Enemie. It allows sound sharing.”

Lyria licked her lips.

“So in the end, all we have to do is listen and fight. Should I say it’s a sense of righteousness?”

“It’s different from La Enemie. Only Mayray—or Terraforce—can perceive Habitz.”

Mayray stepped forward.

“To be precise, it’s about listening to the quantum code—the heart—within the waves. Photon code. No actual sound. The waves within Habitz will be transmitted to me. This has been confirmed by the Supreme Court Justice.”

“In the past, Terraforce distinguished between good and evil by reading hearts. If you look at it that way, do you think the sound of the heart differs from thought?”

“Yes. It’s a wave representing the entirety of the object. Call it the genetics of the mind. By analyzing specific signal segments, we can even predict Habitz’s movements. The problem is manipulating the waves. I think it’s impossible—but if it’s Satan…”

Dante continued.

“Even so, Eden’s role is to protect the combatants. Prevent any situation where Habitz raids the battlefield.”

“Understood. Then only the prison of time remains.”

“Yes. That’s the hardest. Stop magic won’t work. We can’t even recognize when the prison activates. But if we think differently, that could be our chance.”

Dante raised his index finger.

“In the end, it’s a defensive ability. It’s said you can’t attack while Shiok is active. So we strike first—before it triggers.”

“Before it activates? Is that even possible?”

“Normally, no. Shiok responds instantly to threats. But if there’s only one way…”

Dante turned to the man in the corner.

“Clown Pierrot.”

The extremes of asymmetry, beyond the other side’s comprehension—beyond technology, reaching into the depths of law.

Quan was speechless.

“The key is to decapitate Habitz before he can cast Hell. That’s the most critical role. Can you do it?”

Spear first, or shield first?

“… … That’s absurd.”

As the others waited, Quan replied with a cold smile.

“You shouldn’t assume I’m the same as that foolish disciple.”

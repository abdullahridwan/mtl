Chapter 985

The Right Choice (3)

~*~

Taeseong’s pupils lost focus.

Her eyelids trembled. She looked shocked—but after a moment, she exhaled slowly.

“Haaaaa.”

The shockwave from earlier must have been the cause.

*The ground trembled even from this distance.*

Oh Dae-sung’s expression darkened as he watched her.

*Is she really okay? Taeseong is a star.*

But in her current state of incarnation, she existed as part of the living world.

*To manage the planet’s transformation.* The star didn’t split immediately, but when enough shock accumulated, the system would destabilize.

Earthquakes. Tidal waves. Volcanic eruptions.

*Regardless of the war, countless humans will die.*

That was why Taeseong had chosen a method to separate herself from the star system.

She would stabilize the system by absorbing most of the impact into the planet itself.

Fried frowned.

“Why are you doing this? Humans can die. In fact, if humans vanished, the war would end. Life would flourish again. Everything would return to its place.”

“The reason nature is beautiful…” Taeseong said softly, “is because humans told me it was.”

Fried fell silent.

“When this universe first existed, everything just… was. Grass ate sunlight. Bugs ate grass. Animals ate bugs. It was a system. A very efficient system.”

Even in the midst of suffering, Taeseong smiled.

“But one day, a human asked, ‘Is this beautiful?’ And from that moment, my system became beautiful.”

Oh Dae-sung listened quietly.

“Humans give meaning to things. This feels comfortable. That feels unpleasant. When someone names a flower, they’re expressing love. Maybe that’s when it all began.”

Taeseong’s voice grew distant.

“To me—a system within a system—the authenticity of the external world doesn’t matter. But we call humans ‘users’ because they give meaning to everything. They are the ones who define this world. If all humans disappeared…”

She paused.

“If there’s no one to define it…

Nothing would be beautiful. There would only be a cold system, moving forward by law alone.”

Oh Dae-sung felt a strange warmth—like the maternal love of Gaia, the world’s mother.

“But you’re using it wrong,” Amanta said.

“Current humanity can never unite. If they had such wisdom, they wouldn’t have reached this point. Taeseong’s expectations are too high.”

“It’s not expectation,” Taeseong corrected. “It’s hope. Humanity must reach Ultima again. Without that power, you can’t stop Ankera or Buddha.”

She added, “Human survival matters. When mankind achieves an integrated mental system, the greater its destructive power, the more the laws of the world will change.”

“It’s presumptuous,” Thing said, “but I doubt they’ll make it. I can’t even perceive others clearly—starting with myself. The Guffins have left. Humanity is divided. Ultima ended with the Age of Gaia.”

“No,” Taeseong said firmly.

“There’s still hope.”

“The will of the Gaians flows to Shirone through Guffin.”

Minerva asked, “Can Shirone lead all of humanity to Ultima? You know how selfish humans are. How little they understand each other.”

“It only takes a moment,” Taeseong said, her eyes tightening.

“Humanity may need centuries to reach Ultima. But if, even for a single moment, they could achieve perfect unity…”

She raised her hand slightly.

“You could strike a blow at the world itself.”

“Shirone—the messiah left behind by Guffin—will do it. Until then, as many people as possible must survive. That’s why I need to meet Lian.”

If the legionnaires opened Pandemonium at the same time—when those who had reached apostasy began an all-out war—

“The current shock is just the beginning. Sooner or later, I won’t be able to endure it.”

She winced suddenly.

“Whoops!”

Taeseong crouched, her face twisted in pain.

“Taeseong! Are you okay?” Oh Dae-sung rushed to her side.

But Taeseong looked more puzzled than hurt.

*Why?*

She had bypassed the shock signal, yet residual waves still sent pain through the planet.

To a human, it would feel like bones vibrating.

*It’s already begun.*

She knew this sensation. She had felt a similar shock long ago.

“It’s done. Let’s leave.”

But as he turned, Oh Dae-sung glanced back at the grass where Fried had discarded Gaold, clicking his tongue.

*Damn it. Because of that psychopath… Gaold, who loses his memory when bored, goes on a rampage and wastes time.*

Still, if left unchecked, Gaold would destroy the planet. He couldn’t just abandon him.

“Hey. Wake up. We’re leaving.”

Fried narrowed his eyes and walked toward the grass to retrieve Gaold.

“Oh, sir…” Minerva said.

“What are you doing? Bring him quickly. We’re almost out of range.”

Fried stopped.

“No. That guy.”

The warmth of Gaold’s body still lingered in the grass where a collapsed figure had lain.

To escape the endless spreading shockwave, Miro activated a mass teleport.

They arrived on the mid-slope of an underground mountain range, overlooking Basuka’s landscape.

“Damn. That madman.”

Ymir’s destructive power was so great, even Maze had feared it.

Suddenly, a distant clamor like white noise reached them. Everyone turned toward the sound.

“Oh my god…”

Beyond Basuka’s walls, demons gathered like a sea, forming an island of chaos.

Amy frowned.

“Now tell me. What don’t I know about this operation? What’s the purpose of the flower garden?”

“Orders came from the temple,” Dante said.

Amy’s expression calmed.

“…Right.”

More than any explanation, the fact that Iruki had approved it was enough.

*If he said it was necessary, then it must be.*

He was the most level-headed of them all—never sacrificing lives for efficiency.

Rufist summarized the situation.

“The flower garden is destroyed. Demons are converging on Basuka. If the elemental bombs detonate, the entire area will become a sea of fire. But the demon elites are gone. And the armies of Heaven are pursuing us.”

The officers listened to the screams rising from Basuka, lost in thought.

*I want to fight in the capital. But what good would that do when the bombs go off?*

“We’re not needed here,” Miro said.

“You don’t have to fight. If we time it right, the elemental bombs can handle the army of Heaven. We just need to move quietly and get out of the blast radius.”

No one objected.

Yet none could look away from the tragedy unfolding in Basuka.

*We’re not running. We’re fighting—just not here.*

Just as Miro turned to leave, two halos appeared in the sky.

“No. We can’t leave.”

“Three.”

As more halos formed, Havitz, Am, and Salzo descended one by one.

Mayray was nowhere to be seen.

*Did Havitz eliminate her?*

Miro scanned the area, refusing to assume the future.

*But… in my family, this kind of thing happens all the time.*

*Quan lost one eye.*

Suppressing his emotions, Miro asked coldly, “What do you mean, I can’t go?”

Sein stepped forward.

“I read Havitz’s mind. Paimon, commander of the 9th Corps, plans to open Pandemonium at the exact moment the elemental bombs explode. When it opens… terrible things will happen.”

“What kind of demon world is it?”

“A hospital. But not just disease. It will infect both body and mind. I don’t have time to explain fully. We must go to Basuka now and kill Paimon.”

Dante stepped forward.

“Wait. If Pandemonium opens, why does the elemental bomb matter? Why the timing?”

“Because Havitz wants to savor the despair,” Miro said.

“What if humanity’s best decision leads to the worst outcome? The deeper the hope, the greater the fall. The stronger the despair, the stronger Satan becomes.”

Sein nodded.

“Yes. Eliminate Paimon before the bomb explodes. We have no choice. We’re going into Basuka.”

Even if it meant dying in the blast.

“Right.”

The chance of death had just skyrocketed, but everyone’s expressions were calm.

Miro’s ears twitched.

“Come.”

The sound of giants moving echoed from the other side of the mountains.

Miro turned to the group.

“Go into Basuka.”

No one hesitated. Moments later, a flash of mass teleport bent toward the capital.

The armies of Hell trampled over humans without discipline.

“Sah, save people! Aaaaaa!”

They weren’t an army. This was their true nature.

“Give it up! It’s mine!”

“No! I got it first! It’s mine!”

Two demons fought over a human woman, each gripping an arm and a leg.

“Kyaaak! It hurts! It hurts??????!”

Unable to withstand their strength, she was torn apart, her spine snapping.

“Gagging! Gagging!”

Nearby demons licked their lips, then turned away.

“Doesn’t matter! There are so many! Ha ha ha!”

They showed no preference for strong or weak, noble or common.

“Found! I’m first!”

But like humans choosing livestock, some demons had particular tastes.

“Help me! Help me!”

A young girl screamed.

“Aww! Mom! Mom!”

Others, rarer, cared nothing for youth or beauty.

“Hahaha! Come on! I’ll pluck out your eyes first!”

Knowing where demons were born, their preferences weren’t hard to understand.

The stench of blood, of intestines, of filth filled the air.

From Basuka’s streams rose the reek of ruptured organs.

Paimon raised his arms and laughed.

“Ho-ho-ho! Perfect! This is Hell! It feels like coming home!”

Her gaze swept over giants taller than castle walls, shoving their faces into the city.

Humans had become toys.

“Aww! What is that!”

“What are the Capital Defense Forces doing?! Get rid of that monster already!”

Most troops had been sent to the flower fields. No one remained to protect the people.

At the center of the carnage, Paimon looked up at the sky and burned the will of the Demon World into existence.

“I can do it here.”

She was a soldier. She knew her allies had hidden motives.

“Huh. Fine. Do what you want.”

*I’ll show you what true despair looks like.*

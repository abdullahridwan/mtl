Chapter 940

God’s Brain (2)

~*~

The ground began to tremble.

“What?”

The Guffin’s body, which had been scattered by light, reassembled itself.

‘I lack momentum.’

To break through the universe, the thoughts of all Gaians had to be unified.

‘Ultima is shaking.’ That meant a minority among those linked to Idea had voiced dissent.

‘But why?’

The Guffin, glowing in humanoid form, fell into thought—and quickly found the answer.

“Is it me?”

His gaze locked onto Ikael.

‘Because I protected the archangel.’ Even though they were all Gaians, the Guffins who had endured the end of hell held a different perspective.

‘I had to carry it through—or inspire it. At this rate, I can’t penetrate the universe.’

He could force a 100% consensus by purging those with conflicting opinions.

But if such an act wasn’t approved by the collective mind, Ultima would rather shatter than accept it.

‘Lucifer, you’ve passed on a terrible burden.’ He finally understood why he had accepted the promise not to harm Ikael.

‘It was inevitable.’ The time to choose had come.

Would he pierce the universe with 99.8% consensus, or halt for perfection?

‘Ultima is…’

Almost all agreed.

“Damn!”

As the Idea link weakened and normal signals returned, the blue sky reappeared.

“Ah????”

Kael, watching the world’s collapse reverse, asked, “What happened?”

“As you can see, it failed.” Guffin’s voice was cold. Ikael remained silent.

“What was that ringing just now?” Taeseong appeared on the ground, scanning the area where her comrades had been.

“Ankera made the decision.”

Argones had abandoned humanity, but Taeseong, their mother, could not.

“Gaia.”

She stepped forward.

“Argones. You will destroy those who turned this world into nothing.”

The ground trembled again, revealing parts of a colossal body stretching across the sky.

“That one?”

Even the portion visible beneath the clouds was spherical and immense.

“Cell Buster,” Taeseong said, her voice heavy with sorrow.

“Argones will reset all humans.”

The monstrous form split, dividing into countless spheres that began falling toward the earth.

“Tch!”

The Guffin flinched. Ikael soared higher into the sky to observe.

“Unbelievable…”

The gray spheres, each trailing a tail dozens of times their size, began attacking the Gaians.

“I refuse!”

The Ultima system activated, and a massive dome shield formed, blocking the anti-cells.

On impact, each sphere split in two—doubling in number and mass.

“Stop! It can’t be pierced!”

Taeseong moved to Ikael’s side.

“It will be unstoppable.”

With each collision, the anti-cells multiplied, eventually covering the entire dome.

“It’s a biosignal trait. If the goal isn’t achieved, the anti-cells will keep multiplying until they fill the universe.”

“Will the universe be full?”

Ikael’s expression hardened.

“To you, born in the universe, the biological world may seem insignificant. But the true power of life lies in adaptation.”

Taeseong’s gaze fixed on the tadpole-like spheres slowly burrowing into the dome.

“Theoretically, Argones can spread biosignals to any universe. Angels are no exception.”

Ikael bared his teeth. “Angels giving birth to creatures? I’d rather die than endure such shame.”

“This is a special case,” Taeseong said. “If God can create biosignals, it’s possible to implant angelic offspring into human bodies.”

“I’m not interested.”

Even as they spoke, Gaia’s defense wall cracked further.

“Is Gaia’s history ending like this?”

“There is one way,” Taeseong said.

“Ra, Gaia, and Argones each govern the universe, nature, and the biological world. Even Ankera cannot directly activate Cell Buster.”

“It’s a special situation, then.”

“Possibly an act of subversion against the universe. A contingency measure. If the Gaians renounce their apostasy, I can intervene.”

“But that… …”

Ikael didn’t believe it possible.

“Isn’t that all the Gaians have?” Taeseong knew the truth.

That was why she stayed silent, waiting for Guffin’s decision.

“There is no end.”

Sweat dripped from the Guffin’s face as he flew around the dome, firing photon cannons.

No matter how strong the attacks, the anti-cells kept doubling.

‘With the strongest attack…’

The Guffin raised his arms to the sky. Light gathered, dense as a star.

“Yaaaaa!”

A blinding flash tore past the dome.

“Okay… … !”

The Guffin’s mocking smile vanished.

Where the light passed through the colony, the anti-cells multiplied like cancer.

‘Is it unstoppable?’

The colony now spanned tens of kilometers. The number of individual anti-cells reached into the billions—trillions.

They moved in unison, reshaping into the face of Argones.

For a moment.

Then it looked at the Guffin, curled its lips, and dispersed—pouring down the barrier.

A crack formed in the dome. Anti-cells poured through, striking Gaia’s core.

“Aaaaaaa!”

The Guffin watched closely.

Cells devoured themselves, vanishing in reverse evolution.

‘I can’t resist.’

Even the strongest beings who reached Ultima were still creatures.

Even Gaia was helpless against a vessel carrying both spirit and a code that destroyed the body.

‘I must decide quickly.’

The Guffin watched as anti-cells spread across the world.

He seriously considered exterminating humanity.

“Don’t give up!” The voice of Gaia’s elder rang out.

“It’s our choice! Beyond infinity! Finding the real us is the only way!”

The Guffin bit his lip.

He knew the Gaians wouldn’t surrender—they had built an integrated mind.

‘I have to stop.’

But he couldn’t.

‘It’s my fault.’

A small crack, born from his promise to Lucifer, had led to this.

The Gaians shouted.

“Freedom! Self!”

Then, dark anti-cells broke through Ultima’s defense and attacked.

“I will surrender.”

The Guffin planted in his mind an opinion that directly opposed the collective.

Aaaaaaaang!

In an instant, Ultima collapsed. The dome vanished.

“What… … !”

The Gaians were more shocked by the collapse of their mental link than by the sudden loss of the shield.

“Ugh! Ugh!”

The Gaia elder watched as an anti-cell froze before him, motionless.

“Why?”

“Why?”

Before Argones, Taeseong stood with arms wide open.

“Stop here. You’ve abandoned apostasy. Will you really destroy these children?”

“It’s Ra’s order. Even if it’s not dependent, it’s a matter of cosmic scale. His judgment takes precedence.”

“What about your own judgment?”

“Nothing different. If the universe is destroyed, no life will remain. You’re right—every life born in Gaia is precious. But…”

Argones’ eyes turned cold.

“My mission is to preserve the living world. Compared to that, one race is trivial.”

“One race?”

Taeseong sighed.

“You gave me that name. Gaia.”

“… … It’s sentimental.”

“No. Maybe this *is* the mission. You and I exist to create them.”

“Then what about us?”

Taeseong didn’t answer.

“Do we exist just to make humans? No. We are gods. At least in this universe. Why deny it?”

“To know the truth… …”

Argones fell silent.

“Truth? What is truth? Is it opening a lid? Do you think that’s real? What if another lid covers even that?”

Perhaps.

“Gaians are just creatures. One of many things we’ve made. Why should I care?”

“Eventually, those children will find the truth.”

“Foolish. Arrogant. With evil unmatched…”

A sad smile crossed Taeseong’s lips.

“Recognizing their mistakes and moving forward in the right direction—”

“Humans.”

“They are our greatest masterpiece.”

The reason Taeseong couldn’t refute it was because he, too, had witnessed Gaia’s history.

Why was it unexpected?

Why couldn’t they be stabilized like other creatures?

“That’s why I don’t like them.”

Among Argones’ children, only humans were different.

Taeseong replied, “In a way… I’m proud of you.”

“Huh.”

The body of Argones, already turning away, dissolved into the ground.

“This is your last chance. No matter who wins the coming war, I will watch.”

Being freed from Cell Buster was a blessing to the Gaians.

“Whoa.”

After Argones vanished, Taeseong sighed and looked toward the opposite sky.

“Once you overcome this hurdle…”

The war between gods and humans had begun.

“It’s disappearing.”

The anti-cells, which had filled the sky, converged in reverse and shrank rapidly.

“What happened? Why?”

The muttering elder of Gaia looked around—and saw the Guffin.

“What’s going on!”

The Guffin approached and bowed his head.

“Sorry.”

At that moment, every surviving Gaian let out a bitter groan.

‘Wrong.’

The Guffin’s appearance was clearly an act of defiance against the collective.

“Is Ultima broken?”

The elder shook her head.

“No, it can’t be! After all we’ve sacrificed… Guffin, you… … !”

She fell silent. Around her, the Gaia people spoke as if they understood.

“Anger won’t help. If we fracture here, we return to old humanity.”

A society of endless conflict, where countless opinions clashed.

“I will take responsibility,” the Guffin declared.

“I will not open the Palace for now. Then the Ultima system will be restored.”

“What are you planning?”

Guffin looked up at the sky, meeting Ikael’s cold gaze.

“… … I will fight.”

An era in which gods and humans coexisted.

After receiving Omega’s information, Shirone realized this moment was a singularity.

‘Guffin has left Gaia.’

He couldn’t know the full truth from Babel’s records.

But one thing was certain.

The Gaians who remained by humanity’s side until the end—were the Guffins.

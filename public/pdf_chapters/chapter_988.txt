Face to Face (2)

~*~

The world, once drained of color, began to return to life as the light of Agape faded.

Miro landed on the ground and looked around.

Basuka’s downtown, once teeming with demons, now lay empty and silent—like a city that had never existed.

“Aww. Cadet.”

Even the strongest demons, those with the highest concentrations of power, lay collapsed on the ground, their bodies half-melted and lifeless.

‘Half of them are gone.’

Judging by the population density before the blast, at least sixty million demons had been wiped out.

Downtown Basuka was completely destroyed.

Only those demons beyond the city walls, outside the range of Agape, had survived.

‘But the important thing is…’

Not a single civilian had been harmed.

“I lived.”

Citizens stirred, rising to their feet.

“I’m alive! We’re alive!”

Only when their cries reached the sky did the party truly grasp what had happened.

‘Shirone is…’

He was a familiar face, but seeing him now sent a jolt through their hearts—as if a dead man had returned.

“Whoa.”

Shirone touched down last, exhaled deeply, and scanned the crowd with a quiet smile.

“Hi?”

Even that simple greeting brought tears to their eyes.

“City…,” Amy began, stepping forward.

“Shirone!”

Nade, who had just cast spark magic, closed the distance in an instant and grabbed Shirone in a tight embrace.

“You bastard! What the hell happened? You didn’t even say goodbye!”

“Sorry. There was a situation.”

They knew each other too well—Nade didn’t need an explanation, and Shirone didn’t offer one.

“Ha… really…”

It had always been this way.

Iruki thought, Nade prepared, and Shirone solved.

“I thought I was going to die.”

His heart had nearly stopped when the elemental bomb’s timer dropped below ten seconds.

Nade suddenly remembered.

“I have to tell Iruki. That guy—every second is dangerous. Just in case…”

“It’s fine. Even Iruki knows.”

“What?”

Even if Shirone knew about the bomb, there was no way he could have reached the temple first.

‘I don’t have that kind of personality.’

How could he have split himself in two?

But that was just human logic—useless when facing two crises at once.

“Shirone.”

He turned at the sound of Lian’s voice.

“Lian.”

He didn’t react much.

Lian was probably the only one who could forget Shirone for even a moment and keep fighting on such a battlefield.

With quiet pride, Lian smiled and gently pushed Amy forward.

“Ah.”

Nade let go, but his heart still pounded.

‘Am I ready to face Shirone?’

After doing everything he could, the warmth of hope had faded, leaving only cold reality.

Now, there was nothing left to hide behind.

‘Shirone…’

He seemed inhuman.

“You’ve gotten stronger, Amy.”

The first emotion Amy felt was anxiety—as if she needed to be comforted.

“You worked hard. You joined Valkyrie, trained under Commander Garcia, and…”

She tried to list everything, as if reciting a checklist.

“I risked my life fighting beside you, moving across the battlefield. And then the fire…”

She didn’t feel like she was getting closer to him.

“I almost died… but I did it.”

Shirone watched her struggle to hold back her emotions and finally spoke.

“Sorry.”

It was sincere.

“I hurt you. I shouldn’t have said those things. I’m the worst boyfriend ever.”

“What?”

Amy pressed her trembling lips together.

“What do you mean, ‘worst’? You did everything you could. Because I was weak, I couldn’t fight beside you…”

“No.”

Shirone shook his head.

“It was me who was weak. I said those things because I couldn’t face my own fear. You suffered just as much as I did—but I made you carry the weight of my pain.”

The Ultima system only worked because everyone had a heart.

“So…”

He looked at her, understanding the wounds she had endured.

“Let’s fight together. No matter what happens, I won’t leave your side.”

Amy’s shoulders trembled. Her friends smiled.

She clenched her fists and nodded.

‘I won’t cry.’

Not a single tear would fall until this war was over.

Meanwhile, the Army of Heaven, watching from beyond Basuka’s walls, stood stunned.

Satiel, in particular, felt something close to fear as the light of Agape faded.

‘What was that?’

It was clearly a form of Hexa, but unlike anything she had ever felt.

‘Is it related to Guffin erasure?’

It didn’t feel quite the same—there was no lingering discomfort.

‘But… I know this feeling. Or at least, I should.’

She had tried countless times to recover her memories. Only one possibility fit.

‘If it’s the Guffin itself.’

Agape wasn’t a memory—but if it was tied to the Guffin, the contradiction made sense.

‘If that light… is a fragment of the Guffin…’

Satiel glanced at Ikael, but his expression remained cold.

‘Why?’

If Ikael had regained his memories, he should have reacted to that light.

‘Hasn’t he recovered them?’

Satiel, who had been silently observing him, shook her head sharply.

‘No. That’s not it. Even I, with no memories, felt a ripple in my soul.’

The fact that Ikael showed no reaction meant he was suppressing it.

‘I’m alive.’

Ikael looked down at the battlefield where demons had vanished and millions of humans had been saved.

“Are you relieved?” Nane asked.

“I have no special feelings for humans,” Ikael replied.

“A voice without a heart is just an echo,” Shura said, stepping forward as Ikael fell silent.

“How does the Buddha feel? So many demons died. Humans live.”

I am…

“I opened the psychic realm.”

He looked down at the citizens celebrating their survival.

“I summoned the devil. In return, countless humans suffer. Do you think that’s cruel?”

“Are they happy to be alive? Of course. But demons are the spirit of this age. They’re not just space debris or fruit from the sky.”

Nane frowned, her expression pained.

“Since when did humans… Did you only start fearing demons after they hurt you?”

Shura had no answer.

“Did I open hell? No. I opened an era. The pain that someone had to endure even before the psychic realm became real—and it struck everyone. Of those rejoicing now, how many are truly free of guilt for the hell of this age?”

“But humans are naturally…”

“There was a time.”

Nane, who had consumed Ankera’s dream, remembered the humans of the distant past.

“A truly strong human who feared nothing, not even the spirit of the age.”

The archangels nodded.

‘Yes. That was real.’

In all of Heaven’s history, demons had never been this powerful.

‘The Gaian race fought against the gods. Demons had no place before their faith.’

They feared nothing within themselves.

“So I still cannot judge the necessity of my existence.”

He said nothing more.

“I will go and listen.”

The Army of Heaven crossed Basuka’s walls and landed on the street where Shirone stood.

The Yahweh of this age and the Buddha of this age faced each other, separated by a single block.

Sound faded. One by one, all noise disappeared—until even breathing was silent.

Nade swallowed hard.

‘My heart feels like it’s going to burst.’

Just looking at the figures behind Nane, the sheer pressure was enough to crush the world.

‘An archangel. Is that the Ikael Shirone mentioned? That guy is probably already—’

“There’s nothing to be nervous about.”

Miro placed a hand on Nade’s shoulder.

“We won’t know until we try. We’re not invincible. And that includes you.”

His calm smile eased the tension.

“Ah…?”

Then, for the first time, the full line of Shirone’s allies came into view—and a shiver ran through the Heaven army.

‘And me.’

Nade, the strongest—self-proclaimed—electric wizard.

‘I’ll end this.’

As she met their gaze with burning eyes, Shirone stepped forward alone.

“Hey, wait!”

Shirone turned and smiled.

“I’ll be back.”

Nane stepped forward from the Heaven army, approaching alone.

Each step the Yahweh and the Buddha took toward each other stretched like an eternity.

Dante, watching closely, smiled.

‘It brings back memories.’

Back in magic school, during the 2,000-man battle, Shirone and Dante had walked like this.

‘Neither of us had ever lost.’

Two undefeated prodigies faced off—and Dante lost.

‘That defeat…’

He was grateful. It freed him from the compulsion to win and let him truly live.

‘But you’re still carrying it.’

As afterimages of swords rose around Nane, Shirone summoned a hexagonal Hexa.

‘Good.’

Dante silently urged him on.

Here it comes.

‘The end of the world.’

Shirone shattered his Hexa into golden smoke and clashed with Nane, whose sword blazed with fire.

As they locked eyes, the onlookers thought the same thing.

‘They resemble each other.’

Shirone’s pure presence and Nane’s tattooed form were nothing alike—yet…

‘Why do they feel the same? Is something wrong with my eyes?’

It was as if one stood before a mirror—two versions of the same person, reversed.

“The other side of the mirror,” Miro said.

“Then really… who is the reflection?”

Nane’s sword rose like a thorn. At the same time, the Miracle Stream split and struck.

Cuckoo! Cuckoo! Cuckoo!

Each strike left thousands of afterimages, entangled in light and smoke, dizzying to watch.

“The fight began the moment you took your first step.”

They blocked each other’s path—neither willing to yield.

‘It breaks through.’

When Shirone gritted his teeth, Nane mirrored the expression.

‘What is necessary?’

A billion concepts of harmony surged at Shirone in the form of a sword.

‘Absolutely not.’

When the Miracle Stream wrapped around Nane’s blade, the concepts shattered like illusions.

‘It’s not cold. That’s a heart.’

As Hexa and Geomhwa collided with a roar, neither side dared to move.

‘The same force is colliding.’

It felt as if the world’s laws were tearing apart just by looking.

“The world is one,” Miro said.

“The only thing left for two people who’ve perfectly defined that world is perspective. Like a mirror. But the problem is—you don’t know who’s the reflection. And since both are perfect, neither will break.”

Sein asked, “Then what?”

“Then the perspective breaks. When the mirror shatters, they’ll know for themselves.”

Who is it that pours through the fragments?

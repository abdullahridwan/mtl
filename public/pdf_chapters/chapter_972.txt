Chapter 972

Hidden Code (4)

~*~

The war between the allied forces and the demons in the flower field had become a grim lottery, drawing lives as its stakes.

Flashing movements and devastating magic unfolded with mechanical precision, each action compounding into greater destruction.

‘There’s no end to them. I’m not exaggerating.’

No matter how many times the demons fell, they returned—relentless, unyielding. The allied forces began to feel the weight of inevitable defeat.

“Haha! I’ll wrap your intestines around your neck!” A horned demon lunged, driving a spear toward a soldier’s abdomen.

“Ouch!”

The air cracked with force. The demon’s spine snapped as if torn apart from within.

“Oops, sorry.”

Natasha appeared in a blur, gripping the shattered spine. She stuck out her tongue playfully.

“Why… why…?”

The demon’s upper body slumped to the ground, its head lifting weakly in disbelief. Natasha didn’t even glance back.

‘Where did she come from?’

In battles moving faster than thought, misjudging targets was common.

But Natasha wasn’t just fast—she was focused. Her awareness was locked entirely on Lian.

“Yaaaaa!”

Lian roared, unleashing a burst of energy. The air rippled, and demons within a radius of dozens of meters were sliced apart.

Above the carnage, Lian stood revealed—his body transformed, marked by the yaksha’s power.

“There he is.”

The moment she spoke, Natasha and her afterimage vanished from space.

Lian’s eyes widened.

‘The seesaw of time.’

A phenomenon humans called the kaleidoscope—where sensory input and brain processing split, creating a lag.

The body remained in reality, but the mind was flooded with overlapping signals.

‘Most people die here. If you feel this, you’re already dead.’

Escaping such a state was nearly impossible—unless sheer luck intervened.

‘But I…’

Divine Transcendence Denage activated. Lian’s body shattered its limits and moved.

‘It’s Ogent Lian!’

As Natasha raised her sword toward his chest, Lian thrust his straight blade forward—just before the shockwave hit.

The force overloaded both shoulders, propelled not by strength but by supersonic speed.

‘We’re fighting back here!’

Lian defied inertia, halting midair. He stepped forward and slashed in a clean line.

Swung.

Natasha bent her knees, letting her upper body sway like a reed in wind, avoiding the strike.

‘Incredible flexibility.’

Even within the distorted time, her form split into overlapping images. Her fists exploded outward like shotgun blasts.

‘Avoid.’

Lian twisted, pulling his greatsword back to cut.

“Ouch!”

A blunt impact struck his side—a blind spot. Bones cracked.

“Damn!”

Lian fought to stabilize, but his legs buckled.

‘How…? Where did it come from?’

He stopped, back arched, scanning—only to find Natasha already closing in.

‘This woman fights exceptionally well.’

It wasn’t just strength. She wasn’t merely powerful—she was a master of combat itself.

‘A battle genius.’

No wonder they called it the Gustav Fourth Art.

“Hmm.”

Natasha tilted her chin, studying Lian. A slight frown creased her brow.

“I’ve felt this before… but…”

Lian, usually indifferent to others’ opinions, found himself swallowing hard, waiting.

“Are you really untalented?”

His expression didn’t change, but a weight settled in his chest.

“Why do you use so much force? You should move by calculating time and space. Your rhythm is off. No creativity. Fighting you… it’s just not fun.”

“We’re not fighting for fun.”

Natasha nodded slightly.

“Of course not. But that’s exactly the point. Do you want to spend your life doing something boring?”

Lian stayed silent.

“You’re strong… but I can’t find any other advantages.”

Lian was strong—undeniably.

But he was also the furthest thing from a natural genius.

“So it’s unfortunate. Would you like to learn how to fight from me? A few corrections would make all the difference.”

“Shut up.”

Lian’s ribs snapped back into place.

“I don’t discriminate when it comes to learning. But you’re an exception. There’s nothing to learn from the villains of this world.”

“So if I weren’t a villain, you’d accept me as a teacher?”

Silence.

“What do you mean?”

“You say you won’t learn from a villain? Then while you train, I’ll fight on the side of humanity. You have great physical ability. It’s a waste to let it rot.”

Natasha was still an enigma. But there was something unmistakable about her.

“Right.”

Lian let out a bitter laugh.

“You have no weaknesses.”

She was the most complete genius among all the prodigies Lian had ever encountered.

“I’ll accept your sincerity. Even if you’re my enemy, I can tell you mean it. But you can’t teach me. If it could be fixed, it would’ve been fixed already.”

“Then I’ll help you.”

“To someone like you, a genius, what I lack is just a flaw. Like finding the right parts and assembling them—it’s solvable.”

“Then why isn’t it solved?”

“You don’t understand.”

Natasha fell silent.

“How many times have I swung my sword just to overcome one flaw? What have you ever tried? How long have you resented me? Cursed me?”

“That’s why you say sorry.”

“Have you ever thought about it? It’s not the flaw itself—it’s what a person with that flaw had to endure to keep going.”

“A genius dancer has been born in the Gustav Empire!”

Natasha rose to fame at just seven years old.

Her dance was perfection—even before masters who claimed descent from the imperial bloodline.

“Amazing. That child isn’t even nervous.”

There was no need to be.

‘Rotation.’

Her center of gravity aligned perfectly with gravity’s pull as she raised her arms and spun on one toe.

‘Jump.’

Her body sprang upward with elasticity far beyond a child’s, tracing a flawless arc.

“Oh!”

It was as if the audience’s eyes widened in unison.

‘Fun.’

Being good at something.

‘I want to do better. Then I’ll cry more.’

The angle of her fingers, the bend of her knee, the tilt of her chin, the degree of gravity, the distance from the floor, air resistance, line of sight, facial expression—everything.

‘I can feel it all.’

It wasn’t about checking each detail. Her entire body had become a single, unified mind.

Mid-air, countless calculations passed through her.

‘As soon as I land…’

As the music accelerated, Natasha’s feet began to blur.

While the melody played in her mind, her body cut through each beat.

Rhythm.

A measure of how many events fit into a single unit of time.

“Genius! Obviously, she’s a genius!”

Beside her stood Daphne, her classmate. But the officials saw only Natasha.

Even the dance troupe leader felt the same.

‘Burning. It’s at least one-thousandth of a night.’

When a rhythm is broken by a single beat, someone might lag by 0.1 beats.

One-tenth of a night—experts could sense it.

One-hundredth—only the most refined could detect it.

But one-thousandth? That was beyond human perception.

The sensation itself was a shiver—an instinctive reaction to an imperceptible flaw.

“Brafe!”

Applause erupted as the Gustav word for “awesome” rang out.

Natasha became a star overnight. But in her mind, there was only one thought: she wanted to be a great dancer.

“Daphne.”

Natasha called from the practice room.

“Yes?”

Though they were close, Daphne struggled with the attention Natasha now received.

“It’s a bit off-center.”

“Huh? Me? I can’t tell when I look in the mirror.”

“About two degrees. At our age, muscle strength is weak. Balance is everything. When you rotate, don’t focus on the center of your head—imagine a circular band holding you.”

“A round strip?”

“Centrifugal and centripetal forces. Don’t force the power. Let it gather naturally.”

“Aha…!”

Daphne bounced on her toes, then spun—suddenly smoother.

“Done!”

Startled by her own improvement, she blinked, then grabbed Natasha’s hand.

“Thank you, Natasha. I thought you’d forget about me now that you’re famous.”

“What are you talking about? I want everyone to feel the joy of dancing. Let’s work hard and stand on the same stage.”

“Yes.”

Tears welled in Daphne’s eyes.

Years passed. Natasha and Daphne grew into adults.

Daphne trained hard. But in the end, Gustav—no, the world’s greatest dancer—was Natasha.

“Daphne, don’t just listen to the music. Feel like you’re creating it in your mind. Then push it forward. Fingertips like this…”

Even the shape of Natasha’s hands was perfect.

“Amazing.”

Daphne couldn’t see the difference, but she could feel it.

‘A tenth of a degree. No—maybe one-hundredth.’

She forced a smile.

“Yes, I’ll try.”

“Yes. Go for it!”

Watching Natasha return to practice, Daphne’s heart twisted.

‘I never rest.’

Cafes, boys—none of it interested her.

Only dance.

When Natasha danced, her face was the happiest in the world.

Daphne, head lowered, passed behind Natasha with her bag.

“I’ll go in first. I’m not feeling well.”

“Yes? Suddenly? What’s wrong?” Daphne didn’t answer. She opened the practice room door and stepped inside, eyes hollow.

Years later.

Natasha was dragged by a stranger and suffered a spinal injury.

The end of a genius dancer.

Natasha snapped out of the memory, her voice quiet.

“I never thought about it. But if you really hated me for that reason…”

Her cheeks puffed slightly.

“That’s a bit unfair.”

“I don’t know what you’ve been through. But that flaw you see in yourself—it’s not just a flaw. For others, it’s the weight they’ve carried their whole lives. Unless you understand that…”

Lian raised his greatsword.

“Your kindness will never reach me.”

Strangely, those words cut deeper than any threat.

“…I didn’t work hard.”

For the first time, coldness flickered in her bright, cat-like eyes.

“You didn’t work as hard as I did.”

“Maybe. I don’t know who you mean. But it wasn’t you.”

The arm of the god of death within Natasha moved.

“You took away the most precious thing.” She didn’t even know if Daphne had hired the thug who attacked her.

Rather than dwell on the unchangeable, she had always thrown herself into becoming the best at what she could do.

That extreme focus—was itself genius.

But now…

‘I want to dance.’

The days when music alone could make her happy would never return.

“Kill. That’s my job.”

Shinigami’s Dance – Variational Time Concerto.

The moment Natasha moved within the seesaw of time, a chill ran down Lian’s spine.

‘What…?’

One-thousandth of a night.

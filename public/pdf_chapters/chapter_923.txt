Chapter 923

Devastating Strike (1)

~*~

Time fractured, splitting the moment into endless fragments.

In that instant, Shirone realized—this world was never continuous.

*This world…*

It was like the second hand of a clock, appearing to move smoothly from 0 to 60. But if you divided the interval between each second infinitely, you’d find only emptiness between ticks.

*It’s disconnected.*

He remembered Fichot’s words: the feeling that something exists is merely a human illusion.

*Like a video recording device.*

Flicker dozens of still images per second, and the brain perceives motion. The disconnection vanishes. Human perception of time was no different. Shirone now saw it as a sequence of discrete steps.

*And when you put all these facts together…*

The possibility arose that this entire world—everything that existed—was not substance, but electrical signals.

*If so…*

Where did the energy powering space itself come from, even now?

*In the beginning, there was light.*

And from that light, everything began.

Shirone recalled a vision—the universe igniting from absolute nothingness.

*Don’t be certain. If I open the lid and imagine freely, the impossible will dissolve.*

Only what is closed is real.

*But if there truly is a source supplying power…*

Then the energy required to sustain the current universe wasn’t as vast as it seemed.

*That’s scale.*

To humans, the universe was immense. But to the one supplying it, it might be no larger than a palm.

*Thousands of universes could be created in the same alignment.*

In other words, universes identical to this one—or utterly different—might exist beyond its boundaries.

*And if even the world of that source is itself being powered by a higher dimension…*

Then parallel and serial universes burrow endlessly into infinity.

*Infinite.*

It was unsettling.

*How far does the chain go?*

The Gaians had left the photon world, but there was no guarantee their destination was final.

*If you keep breaking away like this, what will you eventually reach?*

Fisho had said: infinite infinity.

*I think I understand a little.*

Imagine a world where only Shirone and an apple exist.

Shirone looks at the apple—and realizes there is nothing that isn’t the apple.

*It doesn’t exist. This is beyond human imagination.*

But what could Shirone have thought of, if he had never seen the apple to begin with?

*I can’t even conceive of the need to conceive.*

Perhaps the state within Shirone’s mind…

*There is truly nothing—so everything is a possibility of existence.*

Only illusion flows, without even a subject to experience it.

*In the end, everything was built from nothing.*

Shirone felt a quiet sadness.

*You were right.*

As he expanded his senses and grasped new truths, the nature of the world became clearer.

*But I’m not wrong either.*

He had said so too.

*If this world is truly empty, then the place where the heart resides might be the only meaning.*

Thus, before the truth of existence, Gong and Ae diverged.

*I will fight. It’s me.*

After these thoughts, Terra Force combatants stood before Shirone’s eyes.

“Have you reached it?”

Their voices were indistinct, but Shirone, having surpassed their level, understood.

“Let’s stop now. I don’t want to fight. I only want to hear why you oppose me.”

“We are not human,” came the reply. There was no biological meaning in their words.

“For Terra Force, fighting is a dead language. We act only for the order of the universe.”

“They don’t like the order.”

“I suppose not.”

The Terra Force combatants stepped forward.

“Because you are human.”

The moment sound waves shook the heavens and earth, Shirone vanished from his position.

“Slow.”

He reappeared behind all of them, turning slowly.

“No. It should be *far*.”

The combatants spun around—but Shirone was already gone.

He returned to his original spot, hands behind his back, walking calmly.

“That too is infinitely far.”

Or forever slow.

*Superlight speed…*

If a Terra Force combatant could express emotion, their face would resemble that of a defeated soldier.

“Nothing is faster than light,” Shirone said. “But that doesn’t mean it’s impossible. It means it has no meaning.”

He now understood: all signals composing this world were transmitted through photons.

“The speed at which information is implemented in this world—we perceive it by converting that speed into time.” Even having reached a theoretical impossibility, Shirone’s eyes remained calm.

“Thus, being faster than light means recognizing information faster than time.”

It wasn’t about speed. It was about cognition.

“I feel between time and time.”

The world Shirone perceived with his senseless senses was utterly different from what his brain had known.

*There is nothing.*

Only Terra Force existed where he chose to see it. Beyond that, no buildings, no land—only a realm of nothingness.

*If you don’t notice…*

The vast sea behind the Terra Force combatants vanished from Shirone’s vision.

*It never existed to begin with.*

The moment Shirone’s consciousness touched it, a specific signal materialized the landscape at light speed.

*That is the identity of the law.*

Now Shirone knew.

Unless observed by a conscious mind, there is no world behind you.

“But it exists because the Terra Force combatants are aware of the village behind me.”

“That’s why he’s stateless.”

The world Ankera had seen—and that should still be reflected in Nane’s eyes—Shirone now saw as well.

“Give me one last chance. Step back.”

Terra Force did not yield.

“Didn’t I tell you? I can’t retreat. I have no purpose. We act out of necessity.”

The gauntlet of a combatant trembled, forming a wave curtain around Shirone.

“Free Zone.”

If radiation was blocked beyond convection and conduction, no phenomenon could occur.

“I don’t like using my body, but…” The combatants drew blade-like beams from their gauntlets, kicked off the ground, and charged.

“There’s nothing you can do against a stupid human!”

They were astonishingly fast—but meaningless to Shirone, who perceived the speed of light.

“Far.”

Standing outside the Free Zone, Shirone floated a photon cannon in his palm.

*It won’t be as easy as crossing space.*

Since Terra Force’s consciousness was itself a signal shaping the world, conflict was necessary to subdue it.

*Raise your power…*

As a test, Shirone launched the photon cannon at the enemy.

Aaaaaang!

A flash of light tore through the barrier and struck.

“Big!”

The combatants, screaming in uncharacteristic panic, flew dozens of meters and rolled across the ground.

“Done.”

With their defenses broken, there was nothing to fear. Dozens of photon cannons lit the night.

When the thunderous roar finally faded, the lonely sound of waves returned.

“Whoa.”

After annihilating the Terra Force unit, Shirone exhaled and moved on.

“Didn’t you find out after all?” He had hoped to leave survivors, but Terra Force pursued with relentless madness.

“What is that…?”

As Shirone gazed at the dawning horizon, he spotted an aircraft above the cliffs of Mero.

“I can’t leave that.”

He fired a photon cannon. The air around the aircraft rippled, deflecting the impact.

Considering whether to increase the output, Shirone shook his head—and activated Ataraxia.

Quaaaaaaaaaa!

A colossal flash engulfed the aircraft, obliterating it without a trace.

Shirone spread his wings and soared into the sky.

*Time is running out.*

Just as he prepared to leave the island at full speed, a roar echoed from within the clouds.

Shirone narrowed his eyes. The clouds dispersed like smoke, revealing a fragment of the aircraft.

“Terra Force?”

A voice resonated, like a divine revelation.

“Come up, Shirone.”

At the voice of the Supreme Court Justice, Shirone ascended, piercing through the clouds.

Before him loomed a city-sized aircraft.

As he reached its center, an opening yawned like an octopus’s mouth and pulled him in, defying gravity.

No one greeted him.

One of the many doors ahead slid open.

The path led to a village-sized community.

“See you again, Shirone.”

Thud. Thud.

It would be a human illusion to assume beings of the same species shared the same scale.

As the Supreme Court Justice approached, Shirone had no choice but to tilt his head upward.

“See you again? When did we meet?”

Long fingers, sharp enough to pierce flesh, pointed at Shirone’s ears.

“When the memory transfer device was installed in you. Though it seems to have been removed now.”

Shirone frowned.

“You chipped me?”

“Yes. To be precise, you requested it—and persuaded me. I complied.”

“What?”

He had no memory of it. Even if it had happened, he couldn’t imagine making such a request.

“Why?”

The judge turned.

“Sit first. I’ll take you to the Ivory Tower. There’s something I must tell you.”

Shirone climbed into a chair built for a giant and settled into a cross-legged position.

The Supreme Court Justice returned after setting the destination.

“First, I must apologize. I believed there was truth in your words, but others held different views.”

“What are you talking about?”

“Extremists. I traced the airwaves, knowing the chip was in you. If you hadn’t destroyed their ship, I wouldn’t have found you.”

“In short… it has nothing to do with you?”

“I’m not making excuses. It *is* my mistake. But I want to make clear—it is not the will of the entire Terra Force.”

“I don’t understand. Even with minds superior to humans, you can’t unify your opinions?”

“It’s not uncommon. It also means your views were unconventional. But precisely…”

The Supreme Court Justice leaned close to Shirone.

“Even we, who govern the universe, have not achieved a unified mental system.”

Shirone fell silent.

“Gaia is not unique. Think about it—how difficult, how extraordinary is it? To hold one thought across infinite diversity?”

The judge placed a hand on Shirone’s head.

“If I must explain it at a human level: murderers must be punished. Most agree. But even this—viewed from the murderer’s perspective—differs.”

It’s not perfect integration.

“Terra Force is slightly better. At a fork in the road—should I go left or right? The answer depends on the situation. But our choice is always the same. It means our standard of conduct is perfectly established.”

“But this time, it split.” The judge withdrew his hand, almost embarrassed.

“All of it.”

“Yes. That’s the difficulty—what a unified mental system truly is. And because of that, even if you open your tenth sense…”

A deep voice echoed.

“You cannot open the Ultima System.”

It was a question that had lingered in Shirone’s mind since he realized his invincibility.

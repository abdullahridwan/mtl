Chapter 932

Face to Face (2)

~*~

Shura’s eyes widened.

“You’d better kill me.”

All night long, Nane had been reciting Buddhist scriptures. Now, the mountains, rivers, and trees trembled in response.

Even from the outskirts of the shelter formed by Gestalt’s ability, the sound carried without fading.

Kurrrrrrr.

A distant vibration jolted Shura back to awareness.

“Huh?”

Seismic waves rushed forward at high speed, converging on her location.

The Gestalt collapsed. The veil dissolved. The mountain ranges stretching toward Baska came into clear view.

“What’s happening?”

Shura had spent her life following the Buddha’s path, learning the truths of the world.

‘This isn’t natural.’

If an earthquake of this magnitude had originated along the coast, a tsunami would have already struck the continent.

‘It’s the power of apostasy.’

Several names flashed through her mind.

‘Shirone, Lian, Gaold…’

But none of them, no matter how strong, could cause a quake that shook the entire continent.

“The sky is open.”

It was only natural that her thoughts turned first to the heavenly figures—beings like gods, like home.

‘I have to tell him.’

Nane was still deep in recitation, but Shura pushed off the ground and ran toward the cabin.

Inside, Amy lay unconscious, the air thick with Nane’s voice.

“Mahavanya… …”

The words pouring from Nane’s mouth seemed to carry weight, as if they were solid matter.

“Tubular materials… …”

As Nane’s voice cut off, a small tremor shook the building.

“Mmm.”

It wasn’t just the quake that stirred her.

Amy’s eyes—clouded by trauma, closed for so long—slowly opened, as if triggered by the sound.

“… … Are you awake?”

Dazed, she didn’t even register that Nane’s voice was human speech.

‘Where am I?’

Her limbs felt numb, immobile, as though she were an insect trapped in resin. Only her eyes moved.

‘I’m sure…’

Memories surged back.

The escape. Her comrades. Division Commander Parkma. The ambush. The slaughter.

‘Berik.’

He didn’t deserve to die like that.

‘Only I survived.’

The thought twisted in her gut. She had lived because a man had sacrificed himself.

‘What kind of soldier am I? Just a kid who got lucky?’

Then, a face loomed into view.

“Are you okay?” Amy stared.

‘Who is this?’

The tattoos covering his face made him unrecognizable at first glance.

But his eyes—those calm, knowing eyes—reminded her of someone.

‘Shirone.’

The moment the name surfaced, everything clicked into place.

“It’s you!”

Nane looked down and smiled gently.

“That’s right. It’s me.”

Amy tried to sit up.

“Aaaaaaa!”

Fire tore through her muscles. She collapsed back onto the bed, gasping.

“You’re like a newborn,” Nane said. “It’s painful, and forcing movement could injure you.”

“Why are you here?”

This man—Buddha of the Age—was supposed to see the world as empty, to seek the end of all things.

He was the opposite of Shirone.

He was the enemy.

“It’s still not settled,” Nane said.

He pulled the blanket over her, then stood and walked toward the door.

“I’ll prepare something to eat. Until then… …”

He stopped. Slowly turned.

“Sigh. Sigh.”

Amy had gotten out of bed. She stood, trembling, barely supporting her weight.

Her crimson eyes locked onto him.

“Answer me. Why are you here? What did you do to me?”

Nane smiled. “Well, this and that… …”

But when Amy’s expression turned icy, he corrected himself.

“You were consumed by fire. It burned so fiercely it scorched even your consciousness. So I brought you back.”

“You brought me back?”

Amy remembered.

She had lost herself after Berik’s death. In desperation, she had unleashed all her power, sacrificing her life to do it.

“How?”

“It would take too long to explain,” Nane said. “Rest. I’ll bring food.”

For now, it seemed true—he had saved her. Amy stayed silent.

But then her eyes hardened.

‘Eliminate the Buddha.’ Wasn’t everything that had gone wrong tied to his existence?

Of course, he had also been the reason she was separated from Shirone.

Her crimson eyes flared. A massive flame aura erupted from her hand.

It was stronger than before—purer, unclouded by grief.

‘I can’t afford to hesitate!’

Amy lunged, aiming to subdue him.

“Porridge… …”

With a sharp sound, a sword pierced straight through Amy’s stomach.

“Sermon. Container.”

The red-black blade scattered like an illusion. Electricity crackled in Amy’s eyes.

“Aaaaaaa!”

Unimaginable pain dropped her to her knees.

Outside, Shura heard the scream, kicked the door open, and shouted.

“Amy?!”

Nane turned his head slightly, still facing Amy.

“Yahweh creates what does not exist. Buddha realizes what already is. If it hurts… …”

“Huh!”

Amy lifted her head, arms wrapped around her wound, refusing to look away.

“Then think of it as my pain.”

Nane’s voice carried truth. He had experienced everything in this world.

Shura stepped forward.

“When did you wake up?”

“A short while ago. By the way, that earthquake—what was it?”

“It’s the army of heaven. Perhaps that’s why I came here—to meet you.”

Nane smiled faintly.

Even after absorbing Anchera’s dream, his philosophy remained distinct from the angels’.

“They’ll find us. Even Gestalt’s ability can’t hide us from their senses.”

Amy listened through the pain.

‘The army of heaven is coming?’ If they joined the fight now, humanity had no chance.

“Let’s wait,” Nane said.

Amy called out before he could leave.

“Wait. What are you going to do with me?”

There was no way he’d saved her without reason. He’d been hidden behind illusions for so long.

‘I know who I am.’

She was Shirone’s lover.

“Even if you keep me, you won’t get what you want. I’d rather die than be used.” She wouldn’t become a hostage.

“Sigh. You’re stubborn.”

That was exactly why Nane found her interesting among all sentient beings.

“As for what I plan to do with you… …”

He looked up at the ceiling, then met her gaze.

“We need to feed you first.”

There was no table. The three of them sat in a circle on the floor.

The meal was simple—grass, grains—but to Amy, every bite was vital.

“Ugh.”

The pain she hadn’t noticed before now surged back.

“Don’t push yourself,” Nane said. “Once you’re stable, you can do as you please.”

Amy set down her bowl.

“I don’t understand. Why do this, knowing who I am? What do you want from me?”

Shura pointed at Nane.

“Buddha! You owe us! We saved your life!”

“Then tell me,” Amy said. “Why save me? If you save me, what about all the others who died to demons before?”

“… …”

Shura hesitated. She had wondered the same.

“The reason I saved you…” Nane began.

“You’re going to say it’s because I’m Shirone’s lover.”

“Exactly.”

“What?”

Amy froze.

“Shirone and I are partners in a way—though we walk different paths, we seek the same distance. I respect you. I was curious. Yahweh of fraternity—what kind of woman is irreplaceable to him?”

“You’re wrong. Shirone is someone I met in school. That’s all. Nothing to do with your ideals.”

“Is that so.”

Nane picked up his bowl and took another bite.

Amy didn’t touch her food. She just glared.

Eventually, Nane set his bowl down.

“If I threatened to kill you, would Shirone surrender to me?”

“No. Never.”

“Wrong.”

Nane shook his head.

“Shirone would yield. He would give up the entire world to save you.”

Amy’s eyes widened.

“So that’s why? You saved me so you could use me against him?”

“No. The opposite. I want to understand Shirone. I want to accept everything about him.”

What came after didn’t matter.

“So… …”

If his philosophy of philanthropy remained unchanged even after understanding fraternity, then he could become a true Buddha.

“Can I love you?”

A grain of rice tumbled from Shura’s mouth.

“Pfft!”

Amy choked, face burning.

Silence stretched, thick and awkward.

“No.”

Amy found her voice.

“Does that even make sense? Why love me? What do you know about me?”

“You already know the answer. And isn’t love a personal choice?”

She thought of Berik.

“Yes, but you don’t love *me*. Shirone loves the one he loves.”

“True. But everyone is the same. There’s nothing in this world that can be loved without reason. At first, it’s small—trivial. Then the feeling grows, until it becomes something irreplaceable.”

“Without knowing why, that thing becomes precious.”

“I’m not forcing anything. Recover well. I’m just being honest—this is why I saved you.”

Nane stood and left, giving Amy space.

Alone, Amy chewed slowly, dazed.

Then she felt it—a sharp, burning glare.

She looked up. Shura was staring.

“I feel like I’m the one carrying the world. Between Yahweh and Buddha, we’re supposed to unify the universe.”

“What? What did I do?”

She knew Amy wasn’t at fault, but Shura was strangely irritated.

“Anyway, get better fast. When the army of heaven moves, the world ends. Before you die, shouldn’t you at least see your lover?”

Amy couldn’t nod, but she understood. Recovery was the only path forward.

Shura, frustrated that Amy only stared and didn’t respond, slammed her bowl down.

“You wash the dishes.”

The door slammed shut behind her.

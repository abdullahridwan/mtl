Chapter 995

Simultaneous Incidents (5)

~*~

King Baska.

The Royal Knights flinched as the royal family clutched their heads in terror.

“It’s great…!”

Yahweh’s wrath.

A crimson stream of light—the Miracle Stream—painted the vast chamber in blood-red.

“It’s not what I did.”

Adolf XIII met Shirone’s gaze, his legs gave out, and he collapsed onto the floor.

Shirone’s expression remained perfectly calm, yet Adolf felt as if he were staring at a ghost.

“Then who did it?”

“My brother Robbins. I swear—I didn’t know anything until he told me.”

Adolf spoke only the truth. Years of punishment for lies had taught him that.

“Your brother.”

Shirone knew Robbins well. He was as cold and ruthless as Tenar—perhaps worse.

‘Trying to kill your own blood… and Gunzo Bedium is involved.’

Lichen’s father had been known since school days as the strongest assassin in the Black Line.

But the man revealed through Omega was far more dangerous than Shirone had imagined.

‘The title “strongest assassin” is just the surface. He’s traveled the world, assassinated two kings alone, and eliminated 47 high-ranking officials—deputy ministers and above.’

And those were only the confirmed kills before Shirone was even born.

‘The world hasn’t collapsed because the people behind these acts were never exposed.’

It reminded Shirone of the old saying: the best assassins are the ones no one’s ever heard of.

“Where is the prince now?” If Robbins was tied to Bedium, Shirone had to find him immediately.

“I don’t know. Truly.”

This time, Adolf didn’t even blink. Shirone attempted quantum transfer.

‘Failed.’

Quantum transfer allowed instantaneous movement, but it had limitations.

‘If you know the spatial and temporal coordinates, physical transfer is always possible.’

Because photons carried stable signals.

‘But the mind is different. To exist in two places at once, the observer’s consciousness must be synchronized. And the mind is unstable.’

For the transfer to succeed, the observer’s mental recognition of Shirone had to be 100%.

Normally, it wasn’t difficult.

With family, even 30% recognition was enough.

‘But someone like Robbins, who has prior knowledge of me, can block my manifestation.’

That was why most of the Twelve Apostles had been deployed to strategic locations in advance.

The royals had noticed.

‘Why am I still here after hearing about the assassination plan?’

Shirone muttered, his voice tinged with pity.

“… … Stupid child.”

Adolf XIII trembled, but Shirone’s eyes were fixed beyond the ceiling.

“Hoo-wook! Whoop!”

Pony, suppressing her terror, stared at the red light glowing over Lichen’s shoulder.

‘Shirone. Shiro… …’

Electricity surged through her neck. Her nerves fired, her limbs twitched uncontrollably.

“Where are you looking? Since we’re both alumni, shouldn’t I say goodbye before I die?”

Unlike Pony, Lichen studied her with the cold curiosity of someone seeing her for the first time.

“I thought you were a decent woman.” Lichen gripped Pony’s neck and pulled out a chain scythe.

“Well, that’s life.”

With a grinding sound of cold metal, the chain scythe plunged into Pony’s brain stem.

“Ouch!”

Before the blade could take effect, a powerful impact slammed into Lichen’s back, hurling her into the wall.

“Who is this?”

Crouched like a beast, Lichen raised her chain scythe—and froze.

In her eyes, a familiar face.

“Oh, sir… …”

The second most hated face in the world. The most, if disgust was factored in.

“Pony, are you okay?”

Shirone turned away from Lichen and moved to Pony, lifting her upper body.

“Uh… what happened?”

Her body still trembled with shock, but she regained awareness, her expression dazed.

“We need to leave now. Can you stand?”

Sparks exploded in front of them. Behind Shirone, Lichen swung her arm.

The chain scythe shot toward his neck—but just before impact, the Miracle Stream wrapped around her arm like a living coil.

“Big!”

Her muscles locked. She couldn’t move, as if she’d forgotten how to exert force.

Shirone stood.

“Lichen.”

Lichen’s own arm turned against her. The blade pressed deep against her throat.

“Kiyi! Kiyi!”

She shrieked like a serpent, thrashing in panic, but Shirone’s expression didn’t change.

“A long time.”

The scream cut off abruptly.

“Still unlucky. Am I still wandering around with this cursed disease?”

“Yes. Sorry.”

The days of debating philosophies were over. Shirone got straight to the point.

“Your father—where is Gunzo Bedium now?”

Lichen struggled to break free of the Miracle Stream, but it was useless.

‘Damn it. Did it come to this?’

Even trained by the world’s greatest assassin, the gap in power was too vast.

“This is mental art. The strength of my control depends on our connection. If your will were stronger, you could push back the Miracle Stream and move.” Shirone hadn’t thought it possible.

“Kill me.”

Shirone considered the words.

“Heuk. I know—he’d save you if you asked. But you won’t. So kill me instead.”

“Why? Because of pride?”

“No. If I let you save me, I’d have to give up something I hate more than death.”

Lichen twisted her mouth into a grim smile.

“You want to know where Gunzo Bedium is? Fine. I’ll tell you. That’s what you want.”

Shirone’s eyes widened.

“Stop!”

The Miracle Stream shattered.

The chain scythe flashed through—slashing Lichen halfway through the throat.

Blood sprayed across the room. Pony stared, horrified.

“You… …”

Lichen staggered, her mouth bloody.

“Quack. Look. Did I win?”

She’d defied Shirone’s control—but the price was her life.

“… … Stupid child.”

Shirone’s eyes held sorrow. Lichen shook her head weakly, as if correcting him.

“I am an assassin.”

Her body collapsed with a dull thud.

Silence fell. Shirone stood still, absorbing her final words.

“Bedium will come.”

To avenge his son’s death.

“He even used the strategy of breaking the Miracle Stream and sacrificing himself to kill me. Yes… you were truly an assassin.”

Even as a villain of the world, Lichen had lived by her convictions until the end.

“Evil is strong.”

And that meant more people needed protection.

“Pony, it was Prince Robbins who hired the assassin. But the other royals are accomplices.”

“I suspected as much. I didn’t expect you to act so quickly.”

“Because I’m here. Because they used you and me as pawns. And now, Shirone has made his decision.”

“I want you to lead Tormia. I need your help to fight the evil that’s coming.”

“My help?”

Shirone explained the principles of quantum transmission and the Ultima System Project.

“Time is running out. With a strong leader, we can shorten it even further. Someone who can unite people and focus them on a single goal.”

“I understand.”

Pony spoke softly.

“But I don’t have the qualities of a king. You know that, Shirone. A man who can’t bear to lie can’t lead others. I can only be responsible for myself.”

“I know. That’s why I came to you.”

Pony blinked, a thought flashing through her mind.

“If I become king… I will deny the very idea of kingship.”

That was the nature of wizards—the children of intellect. They questioned even the privileges granted to them.

“That’s enough.”

Shirone smiled.

In the streets of Baska, citizens performing relief work suddenly pointed to the sky.

“Wow! What is that?”

A dragon covered in long, grass-green scales soared overhead—no longer the small, cute girl they’d seen before, but a colossal beast the size of a city block.

“Can we do it now?”

Aethra selected her position, took a deep breath, and prepared to unleash her breath.

‘Healing of Life.’

A dragon’s breath possessed power rivaling an angel’s halo of justice.

Special organs within her body charged the ability, and the time required varied by dragon.

Aethra’s breath had a 100-hour charging cycle—far superior to the other Eleven Apostles.

“Quaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa!”

She opened her massive jaws and roared.

A golden gale swept across Baska.

“Ah… ah… …”

The wounds of the demon-injured closed. One by one, the wounded rose to their feet.

Miro and the others stared in shock.

‘Incredible resilience.’

After bathing the city in her breath, Eitra folded her wings and descended.

The people erupted in cheers.

“Oh, great dragon! Save us! Glory to Tormia!”

Having witnessed miracles again and again, it felt as if Tormia had been chosen by divine will.

A lady-in-waiting stepped forward.

“Jeonheu, Miss Pony is requesting an audience.”

The royals’ eyes widened. Adolf XIII turned pale.

‘How?’

Shirone had been there. The assassination plot hadn’t leaked.

But when Pony entered, all theories vanished. Only the harsh truth remained.

“Damn it??????”

He forgot royal decorum and spat curses—but there was little time left to wear the crown anyway.

Pony’s gaze swept past the kneeling royals and stunned guards, landing on Shirone.

‘Really.’

Seeing Shirone again so soon felt like a glitch in her memory.

Shirone turned to the royal family.

“As of this moment, Pony will succeed to the throne of Tormia. As a Star of the Ivory Tower and a member of the Five Stars of the Unified Space Management Department, I declare that no one who assassinates their own blood and steals another’s merit to mock the people is fit to be king.”

The royals realized then—Shirone was the one who had saved Baska.

“Dare… …”

The 44-year wait dissolved like foam. Adolf XIII was consumed by rage.

“Pony! You finally—me… … ! Whoops!”

At that moment, the Miracle Stream struck his heart.

The king’s face turned paper-white. He collapsed, clutching his chest, eyes wide with terror.

“Huh! Huh!”

Shirone spoke coldly.

“I don’t have time to waste. Confess your crimes to the people and relinquish your power. Then I’ll treat you as a human being.”

“Heuuuu…” Tears streamed down Adolf XIII’s face.

“I’m sorry.”

Even if a blade had been at his throat, he couldn’t have felt more fear.

‘Heart… heart stops… …’

The unfamiliar, overwhelming sensation lingered in his mind.

“Then from now on… …”

Just as Shirone began to speak—

“Majesty! Something terrible has happened!”

The door burst open. All the noble officials of the castle rushed in.

“Giant! Giant… … !”

Shirone and Pony exchanged a glance.

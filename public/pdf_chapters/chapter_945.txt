Cold World (2)

~*~

In the face of the final operation—one that risked the extinction of humanity—the title of king held no weight.

‘Me, Fleur, and Dante are enough to open the flower garden. That’s all the practitioners we need.’

That was why he had been given a hundred options.

“All I can offer is one hour. Choose 100 people from among them.”

A flood of thoughts surged through Dante’s mind.

The outcasts. The politicians. Doctors, teachers, family members—those abandoned by society.

In the end, they all carried the same weight.

“The president of the association should go first.” When no one moved, Iruki set down his pen and held it out.

“Valkyrie’s total army,” Lufist said, bending forward and crossing his legs.

“It’s no exaggeration to say you stand at the peak of the sky—yet you look down on everyone.”

Iruki remained calm. “What do you mean?”

“I mean I don’t even need to step in. I’ll leave the judgment to this man.” Lufist pointed at Dante.

Fleur and Iruki turned to look.

Dante, lost in thought, lifted his gaze. “Iruki… to be honest, I feel bad.” There was no reply.

“Fine. Since the president has issued the order, I have no choice but to make a difficult decision.”

Unlike Lufist, Dante didn’t even glance at the pen. He spread his arms wide.

“There is no one who needs to be saved.”

At last, a faint smile touched Iruki’s lips.

Gone.

“Drop a bomb on the capital. It will kill 6.5 million people. If I save 100, that information spreads to 1,000. Then to 10,000… The operation fails.”

It would spiral into chaos.

“It will become uncontrollable. Were you testing whether we’re ready to destroy the capital or not?”

All must die.

No loved ones. No exceptions. No one survives in Basuka.

“Right. Sorry.”

Dante snorted. “A single person like you pulling such a childish stunt. You must be cornered pretty badly.”

“To tell the truth, yes. I’ll be the mastermind behind this massacre. If I’m to be remembered as a demon in history, at least I won’t fail.”

Dante fell silent, understanding the burden Iruki carried.

“Still, are you sure about security? With a project this big, we can’t be the only ones who know.”

“It’s precarious,” Iruki admitted. “As you’ve probably guessed, not everyone dies. The lives of the conglomerate leaders and key investors in this project are guaranteed. At the very least, we’ll save the children of the victims.”

“A child,” Dante repeated. “But if you’re in their position, even if you save your wife and child, you can’t save your parents, can you?”

“I’m not stupid enough to be greedy. They know exactly what happens if they push.”

Mice and birds would vanish without even noticing.

“Should I be relieved? I set up an assassination unit just in case, but they haven’t moved yet.”

Iruki raised a single finger. “Actually, the unmarried were the ones we had to watch most closely. Some married in a hurry to save their lovers. The holy war allowed it.”

Lufist asked, “How many were involved that way?”

“286 people.”

Dante rested his chin on his hand, murmuring, “286.”

As an expert in intelligence, he knew that number was dangerously high—completely unmanageable.

Iruki continued, “I know what you’re thinking. That’s why we’re monitoring through multiple channels. One of them boasts the world’s most advanced censorship system.”

‘Mayray-kun.’

Dante had learned through Armin that the Great Purifier resided in the temple.

“We’re under 24-hour surveillance. And the number of leaks caught by the temple’s filter so far…”

Iruki’s smile turned bitter. “Zero. Not a single one.”

“Not even one?”

“No. Not even a trace at Code Zero—the highest suspicion level. From the temple’s perspective, none of the 286 survivors leaked anything.”

“Haha. Kuck.” Lufist shook his head, shoulders trembling.

“Perfect.”

Normally, people couldn’t keep a small secret to themselves.

But now, with the massacre of 6.5 million looming…

No one spoke.

They carried on their daily lives in front of parents and friends as if nothing were wrong.

“Ah, humans.” Lufist’s face twisted—Dante couldn’t tell if he was laughing or crying.

“Perfect.”

Then the expression vanished, as if it had never been.

“We’re no different from them. The truth is, 6.5 million will die. The only question is efficiency versus sacrifice. But will this definitely annihilate the demons?”

“My calculations show a 91.3% loss in Hell’s army if the strategy succeeds.”

That was a significant result.

“Good. Then Basuka should be abandoned, and the king replaced…” For Lufist, even the king was just a high-ranking tool to maintain the nation’s system.

“What about Tormia’s recovery after the war? With the water supply gone, self-regeneration is impossible. Other nations will devour us.”

“The temple will cover all restoration costs. Once the government is reestablished, we’ll implement immigration policies—offer homes and land. Many will come.”

Lufist narrowed one eye. “Sounds like a dream. Even for the temple, you’ll eventually need funds from other countries. Do you really think their leaders will obey?”

“In addition, I’ll hand over all elemental bomb technology and data to Tormia.”

Lufist’s eyes flickered.

“Even if only I know the source technology, it can be used to pressure other nations or as a bargaining chip. Above all, I’m from Tormia. I won’t let my country fall.”

Those final words struck Lufist.

‘6.5 million includes Iruki’s own family.’

If he weren’t a madman, he’d sell his soul to atone before dying.

“Good. According to the temple’s will. Once the detailed strategies are issued, we’ll hand over all access to the flower garden.”

Iruki turned to Dante. “There’s no time to explain verbally. I’ll send it through the ‘rope’. Since you’re in charge, it’s the safest method.”

Lufist nodded and stood. Fleur and Dante followed toward the door.

At the threshold, Dante turned. “What if we’d written down 100 names and submitted them?”

“…You wouldn’t have left this room alive.”

“In other words, you were severely underestimated. Do you really think it would’ve worked?” Iruki’s reply came like a snarl barely dodged.

“You didn’t write it down anyway.”

Dante laughed.

He sounded like a psychopath, laughing at Basuka’s grim future.

It was unreal.

‘But for you, Iruki, it must feel like reality.’

The man Dante was looking at didn’t seem alive anymore.

‘He was the villain.’

The one who’d tormented him since Royal Magic School was now half-dead.

“Take care of yourself,” Dante said. “Then you really die.”

Iruki offered no reply—only a helpless smile.

“Dante.”

Dante turned at the call.

“Yes?”

“When the war is over…”

Iruki’s cautious eyes, even as he spoke, looked unbearably lonely.

“Let’s have a drink together.”

Dante’s throat tightened. Tears welled in his eyes.

“Yes.”

He had no choice but to answer.

To hide his red-rimmed eyes, Dante opened the door and left the room.

‘You idiot! What kind of hero are you?’

As Dante walked quickly away, Fleur caught up, lengthening her stride.

‘The battlefield has shifted to Tormia. The Association will take the lead. I need to uncover Zion’s factors.’

But then she caught the emotion in Dante’s eyes and fell silent.

‘I’m a rational person… This anger is overwhelming.’

‘Damn it! Demon dogs! I’ll kill every last one!’

Fleur saw blood dripping from Dante’s clenched fist—but said nothing.

The Hideout of the Flower Tribe.

Lufist had made a deal with Flallino in exchange for the flower’s seed.

In return, 300 people from around the world were rescued and given a safe home.

Flallino, once the Minister of Defense of Spectrum, now lived as an ordinary Chinese woman.

“Hello, Mr. Flallino.”

A man of Grasse blood bowed with a simple smile.

“Yes, good morning.”

The violet tiara lineage—said to be the most beautiful and fragrant among the flower families—glowed brighter under the sun.

“The sun is lovely today. On a day like this, it’s best to go to the ‘garden’ and just sit.”

Flallino covered her mouth, laughing. “That’s true. Nothing beats basking in the sun with no thoughts at all. Would you like to come?”

“Haha, of course.”

Flallino spread her arms, watching sunlight filter through the leaves.

“I love this place. No one wants to harm us. And above all, it’s quiet.”

“All thanks to you, Mr. Flallino. Everyone here is grateful.”

“Ah, no, I—”

Flallino’s waving hands stilled as her eyes caught sight of figures approaching in the distance.

“Why…?”

Where her gaze fixed, Lufist and Dante walked side by side.

“Lufist…?”

Flallino’s body trembled.

“There you are. Things went well.”

Lufist strode forward, sighed, and got straight to the point. “Where is your chieftain? Take me to them.”

The deal was already settled. Lufist had promised not to interfere in the Flower Tribe’s lives.

“Um… what are you doing?”

“I don’t have time to talk. It’s urgent. We need to use the small world creation ability.”

Flallino cried out. “You promised! You took all the flower seeds! We just want peace!”

“Peace?” Lufist grabbed her by the collar, yanking her forward and forcing her to look up.

“Look at me. Straight in the eyes.”

The man beside Flallino fell back, and her jaw trembled.

“Do I look sane? Why do you think I’m here? Do you want to end this?”

“Huh… Huh…”

The ‘extreme passivity’—a trait of the Hwajok—overwhelmed her. Her mind went blank.

‘How can a person have eyes like this?’

It was the gaze of a predator at the peak—capable of anything to get what it wanted.

“This is your last chance. Take me to the chieftain.”

Flallino’s head dropped. “Yes.”

What is the scariest thing in the world?

If asked, her answer was clear: humans.

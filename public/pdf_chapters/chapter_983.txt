Chapter 983

The Right Choice (1)

~*~

The generals of the Allied Forces were the pinnacle of their respective fields, but excellence in one area alone could not guarantee victory.

Demons were strong not just because of superior strength, but because their bodies, constitutions, and mental abilities were fundamentally different from humans. Some reflected physical force, others manipulated elemental attributes, and a few—like Igor, Lord of Terror—could shatter minds with a glance.

Fidero, the former commander of the 1st Corps, had died because of extreme bias in his strategy. In the end, the battle in the flower field was one where death could come at any moment.

“Advance! Advance!”

Despite the shouted orders, the Allied Forces were being steadily pushed back.

And then—

“Kuk!”

The soldiers in the front line stumbled backward, their backs slamming into the shields of those behind. With their lives on the line, a cold dread washed over them as they turned, eyes blazing with fury.

“Suddenly… …”

The flower field was gone.

When Rufist finally expanded his field of vision, none of the 300 towering floral structures stood out anymore.

‘I’ve been pushed this far?’

The field, planted with 300 massive constructs, spanned a straight-line distance of 23 kilometers.

Rufist clenched his teeth.

‘Damn… …’

Opening a path through a demon army was nothing like breaking the neck of a charging horse. The Allied Forces were constantly surrounded, forced to generate rotational force from within.

Compared to the sheer power difference, it was like trying to spin an entire lake by shifting the currents beneath its surface.

‘Even so… …’

The Allied Forces had managed to shift their direction by a full 14 degrees since the Hell Army first advanced.

‘Just as expected. Once this much inertia builds up, the direction changes naturally.’

This strategy worked only because demons didn’t behave like human armies.

‘Humans use war as a means to an end… …’

For demons, war itself was the goal. They rushed toward wherever they could torment humans.

‘So the fact that inertia still applies means there’s a human-like will at work.’

And the absence of a corps commander in the Hell Army was likely no coincidence.

Rufist mentally unfolded the battlefield map.

‘If they keep moving straight… … they’ll bypass Baska.’

No matter how large the army, it would pass right by.

‘I have no choice but to act here.’

Lufist shouted, “Power!”

But when he saw the soldiers’ faces, Rufist couldn’t bring himself to say “charge.”

Despair filled their eyes.

“The flower fields are gone.”

The psychological impact of the fresh flowers’ power had been immense. The soldiers had relied on them.

“It’s over. We can’t win now.”

Rufist couldn’t bear to push forward as he saw their will crumble.

It wasn’t that their hearts were weak.

‘They fought well. Better than anyone could have.’

Even the ruthless Lupist would understand.

“Everyone, retreat.”

The soldiers froze, stunned by the unexpected order.

“President of the Association.”

“Retreat. I’ll hold the line here. Return to Baska and prepare for what comes next. You’ll be needed.”

Silence fell.

“I’m sorry, President.”

Then, as if by unspoken agreement, every soldier turned back toward the enemy, weapons raised.

Rufist glanced over his shoulder as demons closed in from all sides, laughing.

“What are you doing?”

“Well, I really want to live,” said a centurion, his voice edged with madness. “But I don’t have the slightest desire to run. My heart won’t move.”

No long speeches were needed. They had fought together.

“It can’t end like this. Right?”

Those words were enough.

Rufist turned, glaring at the enemy with the fiercest look in the world.

“… … Right.”

The Allied Forces gathered their last shred of will and prepared to charge.

“Power!”

Now he could say it.

“Rush!”

As if pulled by spirits, the soldiers surged forward, forgetting how to retreat.

“Ha ha ha! It’s the last banquet!” Demons from all directions gathered, drooling at the thought of the feast to come.

“Huh?”

No one knew who spoke first, but every eye turned to the empty space behind the Allied Forces.

“What? What is that?”

A towering figure rose into the sky—Thousand Arms Guanshi Yin—its countless limbs outstretched, gazing down with serene compassion.

“Bannyaparamil.”

The hands of the incarnation, once pressed together, now unfolded like a blooming lotus.

“Paradise.”

In an instant, the gesture spread like a fan, creating countless afterimages that filled the heavens.

The two palms, arriving from opposite ends of the world, collided where the demons stood.

Countless afterimages converged into a single point. The shockwaves exploded all at once.

“Kuaaa!”

Rufist trembled as he watched walls of demons—once impenetrable even to spears—torn apart without limit.

‘Cognitive dissonance.’

The entire sequence replayed vividly in his mind, yet in reality, it had lasted only a moment.

A path opened behind the Allied Forces. The enemy formation split apart.

“What are you so surprised about? Never seen a pretty girl before?”

Rufist finally recognized her.

‘Yes. We still had her.’

Humanity’s strongest defender. A being so far beyond normal limits that calling her human was questionable.

“Adrias Miro.”

Smirking, Miro approached Rufist.

“Call me if you’re interested. I’m currently single.”

As Lufist stood frozen, Dante stepped forward and pointed.

“It’s too late. The other officers of Zion are already deployed elsewhere.”

“You bastard!”

Before he could finish, Miro punched Dante square in the cheek, snapping his head sideways.

“Aww!”

It wasn’t the pain—it was the sheer impossibility of it. Nothing made sense anymore.

“Who do you think you are, comparing yourself to those weaklings? Don’t you know Adrias Miro? I’m a busy woman.”

Dante’s eyes burned.

“It was the mission you ordered. They risked their lives on a near-impossible task.”

“Impossible? No, I’d say it’s beyond impossible.”

“… … What do you mean?”

“Whoops. Little boy, you act so smart, but listen closely. This sister will explain.”

Miro ruffled Dante’s hair with a smile.

“No matter what you believe, the world is deeper and more complex than you think. Everything I do has meaning.”

Rufist stepped in.

“I’d like to hear it. What’s the deeper meaning you claim to know?”

“It means Satan isn’t here.”

Dante frowned.

“Because the elite of Zion left?”

“No. She probably wouldn’t have come anyway. The world’s workings don’t follow human logic. You can’t calculate them with thought alone. You have to understand the essence.”

Rufist asked, “What is Satan’s nature?”

“The most cowardly being in the universe. There’s no reason for her to join a large-scale war. She always stays behind, savoring the sweetness of victory. Remember this—Satan always gets what she wants. If you don’t act with that in mind, you’ll be swept away by the laws of this world.”

“Is that why you sent your comrades to their deaths? They could all be dead.”

Miro remained calm.

“Don’t focus on individuals. Look at the whole. I didn’t send anyone. To catch Satan, you must move with delicate precision, aligned with the laws of existence. Zion’s elite went to do exactly that.”

Dante fell silent.

Satan could only be contained through the Labyrinth of Sight—a place that observed the world from its highest point.

Seeing Dante’s unshaken gaze, Miro smiled.

“Of course, you’re the ones changing the world. Could I have arrived if you hadn’t fought so fiercely? In that sense, death is also life. Every life lost has led to this moment.”

That is the law.

“… … So what do we do now?”

“What should we do?”

Miro stepped forward.

“We open a new future.”

Aaaaaaa!

On the far side of the demon army, Kaidra flew, carrying Zulu, Kang Nan, and Arius.

Shirone had chosen the golden dragon, Methira.

‘Since he’s silent, the explanation will be brief.’

Shirone’s gray hair was parted to the sides, and he wore a coat cut sharply at the shoulders. Handsome, yet his steel-gray eyes gave him a cold, almost grotesque aura.

“What?”

After hearing the report, Shirone asked.

“Iruki is dropping elemental bombs on Tormia’s capital?”

“Yes. A human army is already in motion, with a fifty-fifty chance of success. But if we join, we can turn the tide completely.”

Shirone blinked.

‘So… … In exchange for Baska’s population, the demon army will be wiped out.’

Bingryong Frieji spoke.

“It’s a good strategy.”

A woman with short silver hair and a blank expression, she was known among dragons for never showing emotion. Her assessment was direct.

“It’s the best a human could devise. If it succeeds, humanity still has hope.”

Shirone nodded.

“Yeah. Right.”

The Iruki he knew was capable of detonating an elemental bomb to annihilate the demons.

“That’s why I’m not like Iruki.”

As Shirone’s closest friend, Iruki would never allow such a sacrifice to happen without exhausting every other option.

‘Even if it costs his own life… …’

He was the kind of person who would find a way, no matter what. A friend with the ability to do the impossible.

‘… … It must have been hard, Iruki.’

Poine asked, “Where to, sir? To Baska, where the elemental bomb will fall? To the holy war with Iruki’s full army? Anywhere in the world is fine. The Messiah is needed everywhere.”

The Twelve Apostles fought only by Shirone’s will.

“There must have been a reason,” Shirone said, his voice heavy.

“There must have been the greatest reason in the world—one that couldn’t be solved simply by dying.”

A soft whimper echoed.

“That’s right, Messiah. Don’t be too sad.”

A girl with black hair in two pigtails stood there, tears welling in her large eyes. She wore a simple dress of grass and straw.

Eitra, the Super Dragon.

Her legs, thin as chopsticks, peeked from beneath the hem. She looked like a child, but—

‘She’s an apostle I need.’ Her recovery ability, which manipulated life-related information, had been crucial in every dragon battle.

“Okay. Thanks.”

It was the first time he had praised any of the apostles directly, but no one took offense.

Blitz asked, “Messiah. If we’re moving, where should I take you?”

After carefully assessing the world situation through Methira, Shirone slowly spoke.

“We are… …”

‘It’s going wrong.’

Even in the heart of enemy lines, Rufist could feel the massive inertia shifting.

Faint, but undeniable—a distortion in the demons’ movement.

‘Four degrees. Just four more.’

A force acting beneath the surface.

‘Now three degrees.’

Maze’s shockwave. Lian’s shockwave. Amy’s shockwave. All of them, converging.

‘Two degrees!’

Cuckoo! Cuckoo! Cuckoo!

‘One degree!’

Finally, the vast lake of demons began to rotate as one.

“Done!”

At the same moment Rufist shouted, the demons broke through the Allied Forces’ line.

Direction: southwest.

‘If this continues… …’

Tormia’s capital would be struck directly. Elemental bombs would detonate over Baska.

‘Is it a disaster… or a miracle?’

No one dared to say.

Chapter 977

Age of History (3)

~*~

To Guffin, who had lived for eons, Miro was still a child. Yet he recognized her sharp insight and unwavering determination.

*She’s someone worth entrusting the world to.*

He admired how her confidence had grown, how open and unguarded she remained.

*If that’s the only issue… …*

Would Miro be ready to sacrifice everything for the sake of the world?

“I will,” she said. “There’s no one else who can do it. I don’t even know if there’s another way.”

A wizard could lay down their life if it was the only solution.

“No, not that much. It’s not a role you take on just because you’re able or willing.”

It might require enduring solitude for a lifetime—perhaps far longer than any human lifespan.

“The human heart has no limits, but it’s fragile. If you regret even a little, humanity will face its worst fate.”

“Even if the task demands the heart, not the mind… …”

Miro folded her arms across her chest and swayed slightly from side to side.

*It’s heaven.*

He liked how she never lost her sense of humor, even in the direst moments.

“Who would willingly take on something like this? I do it because I’m the only one. It’s a matter that must be approached coldly, and I believe I can endure it.”

“How long?”

Miro stayed silent.

“I know you’re strong. A hundred years? No—perhaps a thousand. If I asked you to hold on that long, could you? But it’s not just about time. You’ll have to fight the thought that you might be alone forever. No matter how vast the space, you’ll feel trapped in a coffin. And reason can’t survive that. I’d bet you’d go mad within ten years.”

Unable to deny it, Miro asked, “What do you want me to do?”

Guffin raised his palm. A hexagon formed, then shifted into a metallic box.

“Metagate. It’s heavenly equipment. It saves coordinates and bends space. Right now, this planet is sealed by a dimensional wall—there’s no way back without it.”

“You mean… go to heaven?”

“Give me one year. Go, see it for yourself. Feel it. You can’t rely only on one gender. I can’t name you my successor without testing your resolve.”

Guffin flipped through the Metagate’s interface.

“This must never be taken from you. The dimensional wall isn’t easily breached, but a system without a guardian will eventually fall. That’s why someone must be locked into solitude—possibly forever.”

Miro, who had been staring at the Metagate, lifted her head.

“I’m not complaining, but why not you? You just need to be the manager, right?”

“I have a greater mission.”

It was a brief answer, but Miro asked no further.

“Then I’ll go. Where should I go?”

“Galliant.”

A year later, a black sphere appeared in the warriors’ resting place in Galliant.

Miro emerged—thinner than she had been a year before.

Guffin, sitting on the bed and closing a book, asked, “Was it a pleasant trip?”

Looking into Miro’s eyes, he realized it wasn’t just her body that had changed.

*I’m ready.*

There was a sense of duty in her gaze—something beyond reason.

“I want to hear your impressions.”

Miro’s first words were, “No one should have access to heaven. Humanity, at least for now, can’t handle the truth of the world.”

Guffin nodded quietly.

“Have you made your decision?”

“I will do it. I will manage the dimensional wall and prevent heavenly beings from entering this world.”

Miro turned toward the window.

“The same applies to Guffin’s Gate here. It could be used as an excuse for invasion by those unworthy of heaven. At the very least, I need to install a gateway test.”

Guffin extended his hand, as if in invitation.

“Do as you see fit. From now on, the three relics are yours to protect. How you guard them is your choice.”

The labyrinth would create a trial gateway to judge those seeking entry to heaven.

Guffin stood, preparing to leave.

“Then I’ll see you on the promised day. Don’t leave it to Estas. And… this may sound far-fetched, but… …”

After a pause, he continued.

“If there’s something you’ll regret later, don’t hesitate—do it now. If there’s someone in your heart, tell them. Share your feelings.”

“It’s really remote.”

Guffin smiled.

“I don’t think you’ll make a mistake. But… it would be too sad to leave with lingering regrets.”

You will be unbearably lonely.

*It’s regret.*

Someone’s face flashed in Miro’s mind.

She stayed silent for a long time. Guffin asked gently, “Is she… there?”

Miro snapped out of her thoughts and answered firmly, “She doesn’t exist.”

Though the 20-member committee had not yet delivered judgment, the outcome was already clear.

If this was her fate, the world would push her into cold solitude.

*I’m just accepting it.*

Now it was Miro’s turn to ask. “Don’t worry about Estas. There are capable assistants. What will you do?”

“You should prepare to leave.”

Unlike a year ago, this time Guffin turned and walked away first.

“It won’t take long.”

The first place Guffin visited was the lifeless zone in the southern hemisphere of heaven.

Though named for dried seeds, it was truly a place where forgotten beings lingered.

“Cara Torsa.”

A massive presence moved through the sunless dark. Then, light appeared.

The majesty of Mudeungryong, gazing down from his colossal form, was awe-inspiring as ever.

“Guffin.”

The silhouettes of the Twelve Apostles were faintly visible behind Cara Torsa, where the light dimmed.

*They’re still a powerful race.*

To remain so strong without undergoing special evolution since Omega’s dawn.

*They must be perfect.*

At least, they were the most perfect beings Argones and Gaia could create.

“Why have you entered the forbidden area?”

Because Argones had allowed their survival, the dragon race waited in this lifeless world.

“Don’t be too hard on yourselves. The reason you’ve been able to rest is because we blocked the reset.”

Cara Torsa wanted to hear the point.

“Therefore? Why are you here?”

“I’m considering a reset this time.”

Life sparked in Cara Torsa’s eyes.

“Don’t joke. If you’re serious, it means you’ve come to fight us.”

“On the contrary. I want to make a deal. But there’s no oblivion for the Apostle of Time, is there?”

“Heh. Don’t compare us to human imperfection. We are the coldest species. We distort no information. All data is stored as combinations of zero and one.”

“Then we can view history neutrally. Help me, human.”

Cara Torsa lowered her head.

“We take no side—neither God’s nor man’s. We only prevent certain beings from interfering with the flow of time.”

“That’s exactly what I need. A world that remains unchanged, no matter what I do in the future.”

“You misunderstand.” Cara Torsa sounded frustrated.

“If you succeed in intervening, you could trigger an enke. The existing timeline would collapse, and from that point, I couldn’t manage it.”

“What if I could send you the logs of the Akashic Records?”

The Twelve Apostles’ eyes lit up. Cara Torsa, stunned, asked, “The log of the Akashic Records? You mean every event since the beginning of time—every movement of every atom?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

Cara Torsa’s voice was sharp. “I know you can embody signals from outside—Edea. But in the end, even Ideas are signals created within this world. The Akashic Records belong only to Ankera, who dreams this world. You can’t reach them.”

“I can reach you.”

Guffin pointed to the sky.

“I’ll shoot the data directly from outside.”

“… Outside the photon field?”

Cara Torsa tilted her head upward, imagining the universe beyond the dark sky.

After a long silence, she whispered, “Going into space… … Out there… … Sending logs to me?”

“Yes. I don’t know what kind of world lies beyond, but I will succeed. I’ll hack the log and transmit it to you. In return, if I succeed, you will anchor time to those logs—ensuring no variables arise.”

“Departure from the photon field. If you vanish… …” Cara Torsa’s mind raced.

She began to awaken.

“It would mean the only obstacle to Ankera is gone. After that, he’ll attempt a reset—to fix all the errors he’s made.”

“Yes. You must stop him.”

“But I have a question.”

Cara Torsa looked down at Guffin again.

“Even if we preserve time, who will stop Ankera? Aren’t you already doing that?”

“Hexa.”

Guffin smiled. “Ikael and my child will remain in this world. I won’t build a new one. Because we don’t deserve it.”

“When the time comes that my child can bear the truth of this world, tell them everything. The beginning and end. Alpha and Omega.”

Cara Torsa grew serious. “Ankera won’t let you go. Hexa will be erased first in the reset.”

“So I’ll act first. Hexa embodies the signal from the outside world. If I reset it, I remove the cause itself. Then even Ankera can’t touch it. I’m almost ready—the dimensional wall, and a successor to protect it… …”

“Wait. Hold on.”

Cara Torsa paused.

“If you reset while leaving the log, the being known as you won’t exist in the next era. A bottom-case paradox occurs. Users may experience déjà vu. Angels might feel a sense of dissonance.”

“Yes. There will be cracks in the Akashic Records. I’ve left ruins with my name inscribed. Not solely for this, but they’ll help resolve historical contradictions.”

“Hmm. Rather than confusing users, it might actually make the reset more stable.”

“That’s the only solution. Even if you can’t stop me, I’ll reset. You’ll be better off with the log I send than facing oblivion.”

A long silence followed.

“… I understand.”

The Twelve Apostles descended to the ground in human form. Cara Torsa rose to her full height.

“The Apostle of Time gives her word.”

“Thank you.”

As Guffin turned to leave, Cara Torsa called out, the light of Hexa glowing behind him.

“Are you sure you won’t be empty? This world, seen from outside, may be nothing but a signal. Can you still love it? If everything you’ve cherished turns out to be a lie?”

“You can.”

As the last descendant of that brilliant spirit—Gaia-in, who achieved a unified mental system.

“Even if it’s a lie, even if it’s more futile than that… I can still love it.”

Guffin touched his chest.

“Because everything I’ve felt—living, loving here—is real. It will stay with me forever.”

I’m not leaving to abandon.

“I am ready. The time has come to return to this world with what I’ve learned. And also… …”

Guffin’s body began dissolving into particles of light.

“He must be a god with a heart.”

Only his voice remained in the air. Cara Torsa stood in deep thought.

“He is a god with a heart… …”

It was believed the world had been collapsing into endless cowardice, endlessly spawning new realities.

“But now it reverses.”

Beyond infinity.

So that a god with a heart may dwell in all worlds.

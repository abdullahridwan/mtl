Chapter 946

Cold World (3)

~*~

Dante surveyed the town.

Instead of walls and roofs, the settlement followed the Hwajok’s unique architecture—homes built along solar roads, channels that absorbed and redirected sunlight. The earth shimmered like a star as daylight filtered through a dense tangle of trees overhead.

‘They eat food like anyone else, but under extreme conditions, they can survive on sunlight and water alone.’

Flallino walked ahead, his path aligned perfectly with the sun’s rays, the ceiling of the corridor open in a straight line to the sky. A smile spread across his face.

“Ah, look at this sunlight. What a beautiful sky. Walking along this path… it’s like basking in pure life.”

He turned, eager to share the moment, but met only Lufist’s cold gaze.

“I’m sorry,” Flallino said quietly.

Lufist didn’t respond. Instead, he tilted his head up, staring at the sky.

“Do you like the sun?” he asked.

“Of course,” Flallino said. “For the Hwajok, the sun is the greatest gift. It brings us joy.”

“I like you too,” Lufist said.

Flallino froze, stunned.

“I don’t look up for no reason,” Lufist continued. “How many humans even remember there’s a sky above them?”

“It’s… it’s wonderful,” Flallino said, closing his eyes. “You feel happy just standing in the light.”

“Happiness?” Lufist laughed. “Do you know what we discarded first when building the system we have today? Happiness. Our system runs on the suffering of the entire population.”

Flallino frowned. “What does that mean? If no one can be happy, why build such a system?”

“To dominate you,” Lufist said. “It’s human to give up happiness if it means superiority. Call it madness in pursuit of perfection. But now, an even more insane system has emerged.”

The Hwajok had not left their realm, but they had observed the world through small world creation.

“The one that’s more insane will win,” Lufist said. “That’s how it works. You live in this world, yet you talk about the sun, about happiness.”

Flallino lowered his head.

“I know we’re a failed race. That’s why we hide.”

“It’s too early to conclude that,” Lufist said.

Flallino turned, surprised, just as Lufist raised a finger and fired a warning shot into the air.

“Anyway… the weather’s killing it.”

How long had it been since he’d looked up and truly seen the sky?

‘Is it time for me to retire too?’

Since when had he grown sentimental?

“You’re here,” Lufist said.

In a sunlit clearing, a woman with long black hair sat cross-legged on the ground.

‘That woman…’

Lufist stopped. The air was thick with the scent of lotus blossoms.

“Are you a direct descendant of the Hwa clan?”

The woman smiled and bowed her head.

“Yes. I am Protea of the Lotus lineage. Thank you for visiting such a humble place.”

Lufist had granted them shelter, but he hadn’t cared who led them. He’d only heard a report that a Hwajok had arrived on their own after the village was built.

‘The Lotus lineage. It survived.’

The origin of the Hwajok was said to be the lotus flower.

“Long ago,” Protea said, “an unknown Buddha sat upon a lotus and sought the truth for forty thousand years. The lotus, which never withered or died, learned of human life through the Buddha.”

Lufist nodded.

“And on the day the Buddha departed, the lotus achieved incarnation and became human. That is the birth of the Hwajok.”

It was just a myth. But unless it was true, the diversity of Hwajok bloodlines made no sense.

‘They couldn’t have appeared spontaneously. Unless the first Hwajok spread through small world creation…’

The Lotus lineage before him was the direct bloodline of the enlightened one.

‘Annoying.’

Against any other Hwajok, Lufist’s presence alone would have been enough to intimidate. But Protea was different.

“Yes,” she said. “Our benefactor, Mr. Lufist—why have you come to this place?”

“You know?”

It wasn’t an exaggeration to say that a direct descendant like her could control the entire forest beyond the village.

“Ah. Well… I heard about your philosophy on the system. I assumed you’d need precise control over the flowers.”

Dante stepped forward.

“Yes. The system is massive. We built a separate interface, but it still takes dozens of people to manage fine adjustments.”

“In contrast,” Lufist said, “with small world creation, a single Hwajok could control even the nerve-level functions.”

Protea’s expression darkened.

“If you had that power, you’d have acted already. If we lose, you die too. You’d better cooperate.”

“The fire tribe’s decision…” Protea began.

Then her smile vanished.

“It’s a refusal.”

Lufist didn’t hesitate.

“Then die.”

Flallino screamed as a blade flashed past her.

“The strategy won’t change just because you refuse. If you stand firm, others will follow.”

Dante stepped in.

“Why? Half the world is already under demon control. If humanity falls, the Hwajok won’t survive either.”

“We die anyway,” Protea said.

The other Hwajok in the forest turned their eyes toward her.

“There are hundreds of ancient weapons, mobilized across the entire fire tribe. If the enemy is strong enough to overcome that, what hope do we have?”

Lufist didn’t deny it.

“Humanity will pay the price too. But he’s risking everything to win. That’s war.”

“This is a human war,” Protea said. “Offer a way to ensure the Hwajok’s survival. Otherwise, cooperation is impossible.”

The Hwajok in the forest stepped forward and activated small world changyu.

The ground trembled. A crawling sensation ran beneath their feet, as if vast networks of nerves were shifting underground.

“Huh.”

The moment Lufist grunted, a blade taller than a man erupted beside him.

“Keugh!”

Some Hwajok flinched, but Protea didn’t blink.

“Coercion is useless. You’ve forced us before, but this time, we choose death.”

“I’ll do it,” Flallino said suddenly.

All eyes turned to her.

“I’ll move the flowers. Just… don’t fight. I’ve done it before. I can do it again.”

Protea stared at her.

“Flallino, what are you doing? This is about the fate of our entire family. There are no compromises.”

“If we don’t act, we’re all dead,” Flallino said. “If humanity falls, the Hwajok fall with them.”

“No,” Protea snapped. “If we’re doomed anyway, we won’t help humans. Think of what they’ve done—mocked our bodies, insulted our minds! Humans are the most selfish creatures.”

“I know,” Flallino said softly.

How could she forget?

“But I think Mr. Lufist is different. He seems cold, emotionless—but if he truly didn’t care… he wouldn’t have built this village for us from the start. This is exactly the place we wanted. The best place.”

“You’re being deceived,” Protea said. “He only did it to use us when the time came.”

“He said the weather was nice,” Flallino said.

She looked at Lufist.

“I don’t believe someone who knows the warmth of the sun can truly forget happiness.”

Her eyes glistened.

“You know it too. But you have to fight. Because not everyone can be happy, you choose to be cold, colder… but deep down, you want everyone to be happy, don’t you?”

“No,” Lufist said. “I’m a cold man. Humans are evil. I’ll do anything to achieve my goals.”

This was why Lufist hated dealing with women.

‘Emotional at the worst moments. Annoying.’

Who cared about good or evil when an enemy was coming that would tear them all apart?

‘You don’t have to fight yet. He said we need the optimal strategy first.’

But still.

It felt good to know why they were fighting.

Lufist exhaled and released the Spirit Zone. Dante let go of his murderous intent.

“Fine,” Lufist said. “Let’s talk. I’ll do my best to ensure your survival. Is that acceptable?”

“No,” Protea said. “We need a solid guarantee.”

Just as tension flared in Lufist’s temples, a voice came from the forest.

“I’ll provide it.”

Lufist and Dante snapped their heads toward the sound, their pupils trailing afterimages.

‘Fast. Just escaping the field of vision pushed my alertness to the limit.’

Protea’s eyes widened.

“You are…?”

A woman stepped forward—sharp ears, pale skin, eyes clear as mountain springs.

“Hello?” she said, waving brightly.

Lufist recognized her instantly.

“Enox.”

She was the representative of the fairy tribe, one of the kings of the Temple.

“Yes. The commander-in-chief asked me to come. Said I might be useful.”

Dante’s eyes widened.

‘If this was going to happen, at least warn me.’

Protea knelt. The other Hwajok followed, bowing low.

“Lord of the Forest.”

“It’s been a while,” Enox said, smiling. “The Hwajok scent is as pleasant as ever.”

She raised a finger.

“Don’t worry about the war. We elves will intervene. The Hwajok will be protected.”

“…Are you saying the elves will join the fight?”

“Of course,” Enox said.

“But why support humans? They’re destroying the nature you cherish.”

“Exactly,” Enox said, gently touching Protea’s chin. “Humans are thrilling.”

That night.

Tormia’s capital.

While King Adolf remained on sick leave, Lufist and Fleur attended a royal dinner.

Pony, a graduate of Alpheas Magic School, was also present. The conversation stayed light.

“The world situation is critical,” someone said. “Many investors have gone bankrupt after pouring money into foreign ventures.”

Even as the war with the demons raged, the royal family cared only about increasing their wealth.

‘The most unreal place,’ Lufist thought.

‘This is exactly what the system needs—a space completely detached from reality, where people only talk about ideals.’

He continued eating in silence.

“Anyway, this Chief of Staff is quite charming,” a noble said, glancing at Fleur. “I didn’t like the last one—too fierce.”

Fleur smiled politely.

“Thank you for the compliment.”

Seizing the moment, Lufist made his request.

“Asmodians will invade Tormia. I want to redeploy the capital’s garrison.”

“The Royal Guard?” The air grew heavy. That force was their last line of defense.

“Yes. Don’t worry. We have the flower garden. We only need troops for support roles.”

Everyone dies anyway.

If annihilation was coming, Lufist would use every resource available.

“Well,” the noble said, “if the flower garden is intact, Tormia is safe. It has the world’s strongest defense.”

A defense Lufist himself had built.

“By the way…” Another noble, thick-browed and smirking, pointed his fork at Lufist and Fleur. “How’s the dragonfly compatibility? There were rumors, you know. I heard you’re a chief secretary killer. Fuhahaha!”

The royals burst into laughter.

They didn’t care if the rumors were true. They just wanted to see the reaction from the Magic Association President and Chief of Staff.

“Nothing,” Lufist said flatly.

“It’s not very entertaining. The previous Chief of Staff was much more interesting.”

“Oh? Really? Well, Miss Fleur, you’re too thin. Eat more.”

Fleur puffed her cheeks.

“That’s self-defeating. The taste of the Association President is strange. I can’t confirm it.”

“Why not? Leave it to me. My stamina is world-renowned.”

“Aww! That’s kind of scary.”

The royals roared with laughter.

Only Pony remained silent.

‘It’s all a lie.’

She knew. She’d attended the same magic school.

The cold calculation in the eyes of the two wizards, pretending to laugh along.

‘Pro mages. But not skilled enough in politics to please royalty. And the Royal Guard…’

A chill ran down her spine.

‘Something big is about to happen.’

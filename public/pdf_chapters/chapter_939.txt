God’s Brain (1)

Omega 187 years.

Returning from Hell in the Underworld, Guffin rallied the Gaians and prepared to leave the photon realm.

“It will be an interesting trip.”

The hearts of those welcoming the new era thrilled with anticipation.

“There is only one way to leave the photon field.”

Standing before a thousand Gaians gathered on the high spire, Guffin began his final speech.

“Leave the rule of Anchera. From this moment on, everyone becomes the manager of this world.”

Though these truths were already known, the Gaians listened in reverent silence.

“We have no more controversy. No more doubts. We are the true masters of this world. Thus, we will escape Anchera’s cowardice and transcend infinity.”

Applause rang out across the spire.

The number present was small compared to the total population, but to the Gaians, quantity had never mattered.

‘Of course, today is different.’

To dismantle Anchera’s authority and escape the photon world, every soul counted.

As Guffin descended from the spire, his metallic hair floating as if weightless, the crowd parted.

“It’s about time,” he said. “Your role is vital.”

Though Gaia had long since merged with Ultima, Guffin remained its core.

“Magic,” he murmured.

The reason behind the phenomenon.

Understanding the truth of this world and changing it through that understanding were two entirely different matters.

Only the Infinite Mage, Guffin, could unlock the final seal at the end of the universe.

“We choose.”

At that moment, the pride of the Gaian people—those who had reclaimed the meaning of perfect existence—pierced the sky like a blade.

“Mr. Guffin!”

A woman with golden steel hair coiled into a spiral stepped forward.

“Milla?”

When Guffin had first returned to the Underworld, she had been the first child born in Gaia’s history.

“It’s been a while.”

The sharp, distinctive aura of the Gaians now carried a bright, gentle warmth.

“Of course. She’s a full-grown woman now. She has no idea how long she’s waited for this day.”

Milla’s cheeks flushed slightly.

“Since the day I fell in love with you.”

“Yes,” Guffin said softly. “I’m sorry.”

He smiled and gently stroked her hair. Milla stuck out her tongue and grinned.

“It’s okay. When you leave this world, everything begins anew. Maybe I’ll have a chance then.”

“Haha! I hope so.”

She had been rejected by Guffin, yet her heart bore no scars. Among the Gaians, love required mutual consent.

‘Know what is right, and do it.’ She was grateful to have been born as one of them.

“Guffin, the preparations are complete.”

At the elder’s words, Guffin activated his 11th sense—his palace.

‘Ultima.’

Across the planet, every Gaian felt Guffin’s presence, urgent and clear.

“Then…?”

Guffin turned to Milla with a soft smile. She nodded, her expression refreshed.

“Go.”

In that instant, the Spirit Zone opened and spread through the infinite realm at the speed of light.

Pop!

Milla’s head exploded.

Her body, severed at the neck, staggered and collapsed to the ground with a thud.

Guffin turned.

“Archangel.”

In the distant sky, Ikael gazed down, his halo vastly expanded.

“You foolish beings,” he intoned. “Why cast aside the authority granted by God and bring about your own destruction?”

Guffin exhaled. “Ataraxia.”

The concept that had birthed this world.

‘The closest signal to the outside world. The amplification range spans the distance from nothing to meaning.’

Even for the Infinite Mage, confronting the peak of the universe was no simple task.

The Chief Gaia examined Milla’s remains.

“Dead. The attack came faster than the perception of time. I couldn’t stop it.”

Guffin looked up at the sky. “I hope it was a good dream.”

“Archangel,” Guffin called. “Do I interpret your interference as Anchera’s will?”

Ikael raised his chin with regal disdain. “It is not interference. It is law. I, Ikael, Archangel, will destroy all who defy it.”

He stretched his hand toward the earth, Ataraxia expanding to the edges of the sky.

“This is your final chance. Follow God’s will.”

With a single flick of his finger, the ground would be crushed and mountains split.

“God?” Guffin smiled.

“Don’t misunderstand me.”

As Guffin’s eyes blazed with golden light, the eyes of every Gaian ignited in response.

“You dare?!”

As Ikael raised his hand, the air around him began to collapse at terrifying speed.

“Ultima System.”

A thousand Gaians gave birth to a single thought.

“I refuse.”

Woooooooooooooooooooooooooh!

A dome-shaped barrier erupted from the ground, shielding them from the crushing pressure.

‘How…?’

Ikael’s pride stung. His brow furrowed.

‘What is that light? Where does it come from—this power that defies the laws of this world?’

“Ikael, do not doubt.”

Spotting Guffin ascending into the sky, Ikael sneered.

‘If only one man…’

But even as the thought formed, a sphere of light burst from Guffin’s palm.

“Photon Cannon.”

A torrent of light, dense with mass, hurtled toward Ikael at near-light speed.

‘Ataraxia!’

Ikael amplified his sense of time, barely dodging. The beam struck the plateau behind him.

Aaaaaang!

The force obliterated twenty thousand tons of earth—power on par with the original Ultima.

‘I must eliminate that man.’ Filled with divine purpose, Ikael gnashed his teeth and surged toward Guffin.

When the leader of the angels and the representative of Gaia collided, the world trembled.

‘How is such power possible?’

Amidst the storm of photon cannons, Guffin’s voice rang out.

“I told you not to doubt it.”

Ikael turned—only to find Guffin already before him, having transcended time and space.

“Because the asteroid is shaking.”

A flash of light screamed toward Ikael’s wide eyes.

“Keugh!”

As Ikael tried to amplify his power, a colossal pin of force blocked his path—faster than the photon beam.

“Uh?”

Aaaaaaaang!

A roar shook heaven and earth. Guffin, struck head-on by the blast, was sent flying alongside Ikael.

Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud!

Ikael, driven through four mountain peaks and pinned to the ground, lifted his head instantly.

Guffin stood over him, gripping his waist, unmoving.

‘Why…?’

As Ikael slowly reached out, Guffin suddenly lifted his face.

“As expected,” he said, one corner of his mouth lifting.

“Angels don’t have hearts.”

It was only logical—angels were mental beings—but Ikael felt an unfamiliar irritation.

“There is no reason for angels to imitate inferior human organisms. We are the highest intelligence.”

“Is that so?” Guffin slowly rose, frowning as if in pain, then knelt.

“Aww!”

Ikael looked down and saw the charred back of Guffin’s body.

Such a choice could only be made by one already half-mad.

“Why did you do that?”

Guffin didn’t answer.

“I wouldn’t have died from that attack anyway. But to you, I’m the enemy who killed your own people, aren’t I?”

“I made a promise.”

This day would never have come if Lucifer hadn’t died in Guffin’s place.

“I cannot harm you.” Self-restraint and self-blocking were fundamental to Gaia’s philosophy.

“What promise? What are you talking about?”

Guffin scratched his wire-sharp head.

‘It’s embarrassing.’

The face of Lucifer—once asking Ikael to find the meaning of existence—flickered in his mind.

‘Aha.’

Guffin leaned forward, suddenly inches from Ikael’s face.

“You. Can I eat you raw?”

Ikael’s eyes widened. He swung his fist at blinding speed.

“Aww!”

Guffin took the blow full in the jaw, flying dozens of meters before crashing into a massive rock.

“Dare a worthless human mock an angel?”

Even if angels used human bodies to create the Nephilim, such acts were far from bestial.

Guffin staggered forward, clinging to a dizzying purpose.

“Ah, what a temper…”

“Silence. In the end, this was all your scheme. But it won’t work on me.”

At that moment, Guffin’s eyes flared.

“Who created this world?” he asked, meeting Ikael’s gaze with Gaia’s piercing clarity.

Ikael frowned. “Eternal Immortality. The one and only God who brought light to this world and birthed the angels.”

“Do you truly believe that?”

Ikael’s brows twitched.

‘I hate that look.’

It was as if Guffin regarded the Archangel—and all of creation—as less than filth.

“Are you claiming to be God? If Anchera lied, does that mean everything is a lie?”

“No,” Guffin said, shaking his head. “We are no different. Even if humans created this world, who created them?”

An endless chain of creation, binding all in repetition.

“That’s why I want to leave.”

“I forbid it.”

As Ikael raised his will to attack, Guffin extended his hand.

“Shall we go together?”

“What?”

“You can’t know what lies beyond until you reach it. Let’s find out. When you pass beyond infinity…”

Guffin smiled.

“Perhaps there you’ll find the meaning of your existence.”

“The meaning of existence?”

Ikael had been born an archangel. Never once had he questioned his purpose.

‘I am first. I am origin.’ Yet, staring into Guffin’s eyes, he felt as if he were about to vanish.

‘The outside world.’

Ikael scowled. “Nonsense! Do you think I’d fall for human words? I am Ikael, chief of the archangels!”

“Is that so? Then you can’t leave.”

Deciding he’d said enough, Guffin snorted and activated the Ultima System.

It had been delayed, but now, countless Gaians stood ready to depart the photon world.

“Go.”

As Guffin’s body dissolved into light, Ikael witnessed the impossible.

‘Truly…’

The blue sky vanished. Against a black, starless void, signals crisscrossed like data streams.

‘Does it really exist?’

It should have been blocked. Even with Ataraxia activated, a single Guffin should not have been enough to breach it.

“Ah…?!”

But Ikael could only stare, transfixed, as hundreds of millions of lights surged into the sky.

“Is it… out there?”

Guffin’s form flickered, scattering into light with each pulse.

“Me too…”

Ikael reached out.

Ankera’s eyes snapped open.

‘Something…?’

Before he could react, a violent electric shock surged through him. The massive pupils dilated and trembled.

A voice, crackling with static, echoed through the void.

“Detection of total abrogation intent toward the universe. Target: Gaia. Code quota: 99.8 percent of total. Searching for optimization countermeasures.”

Silence fell.

Then, Ankera’s voice returned—calm, majestic.

“Activate Code Argones.”

Ridge! Ridge! Ridge!

Each time dozens of tons of pulsating muscle flexed, Guffin’s skin tingled.

The rhythm of the pulses grew faster.

Sound filled the sanctuary until no empty space remained, and finally—

Ridge!

After the final echo, silence fell.

“Why me?”

Ankera’s eyelids lifted from top and bottom, forming narrow, slitted eyes.

“I am everything in this world. I defined all things. I have no reason to negotiate with you.”

“Then define me.”

Ankera’s upper eyelid twitched into a V.

“You cannot define me. Because I do not know. The only thing in this world I do not understand—isn’t that me?”

Hexa.

“So stop tormenting the Gaian and let us compete. If you can consume me, this world will return to clarity.”

Squeak! Squeak! Squeak!

Nerve endings scattered in all directions collided, producing a horrifying noise.

‘They’re in conflict.’

Guffin watched Ankera’s pupils contract and dilate repeatedly, lost in thought.

‘I am the challenger, but Ankera carries the pride of a god.’

The Akashic Records and the Ultima System.

‘Let’s see—just who is the true god.’

The two laws that permeated the world pressed against each other with immense force.

All.

Ikael, pressing his ear to the sanctuary door where Anchera resided, blinked.

He knew he shouldn’t be able to hear anything, yet Guffin’s final words echoed strangely in his mind.

‘The mind leads.’

That much, he understood.

‘You want me to continue? But to whom? If I am a being capable of connecting hearts…’

Ikael sighed.

‘I hope this ends well.’ Even now, the angels and the Gaians stood in tense standoff across the vast expanse of Heaven.

‘I cannot endure another war.’

Suddenly, a thought struck him.

‘Or… am I simply hoping that Guffin doesn’t leave this place?’

There was no answer.

As the silence within the sanctuary stretched longer, Ikael’s heart burned with anxiety.

“Good.”

Ankera had made her decision.

“I grant you access to the Akashic Records. But you will also merge with the whole and lose your individual will.”

Guffin nodded quietly.

“It doesn’t matter, as long as you keep your promise. Liberate all Gaians except me from the photon world.”

Three minutes passed before the reply came.

“I allow it.”

To Ankera, whose thoughts moved at speeds capable of counting every atom in the universe, this delay was more than sufficient.

“Are you confident?”

“I will dismantle you and return you to the world. Everything will be under my control.”

Ankera’s nerve trunks extended toward Guffin, wrapping around his legs.

By the time they reached his waist and climbed toward his neck, Guffin slowly closed his eyes.

‘This is my only chance. And…’

Guffin’s eyes snapped open.

‘This is the final gamble!’

Light erupted from Guffin’s pupils the instant Ankera’s nerve terminals pierced his ears.

“Aaaaaaa!”

As the Akashic Records invaded the Ultima System, they began absorbing all data.

“Kick! A cheat job!”

The moment Ankera’s pupils vibrated at light-speed, attempting to dismantle Hexa.

“Hey! Hey! Hey! Hey!”

A roar of electromagnetic waves tore through the sanctuary and spread across Heaven. Ikael reeled from the shock.

“Huh!”

He pressed his ear harder to the door, barely stabilizing the swaying asteroid as he bent his back.

‘Ankera’s scream.’

Even Ikael had never heard such a sound.

‘Why? What is happening in there?’

He wanted to break down the door, but no such option existed in his mind.

“Kik! Queek! Good luck!”

Inside the blinding sanctuary, Guffin hung suspended from the ceiling, connected by nerve terminals.

He activated every ability under the name Anke.

‘All authority belongs to me.’

It controlled Guffin’s thoughts, will, and spirit—but still, Hexa did not disintegrate.

‘Why?’

For the first time since her creation, confusion stirred painfully in Ankera’s nerves.

‘What is this?’ Infinite.

‘No, impossible. Infinity is not a trait granted to any being. If I cannot absorb…’

Destroy.

Ankera emitted a series of high-frequency pulses, sending a destruction signal directly into Hexa.

Guffin’s body convulsed violently, yet the concept of Hexa remained unbroken.

‘This cannot be.’

Even using every concept in existence, she could not access Hexa. ‘Only illusions repeat endlessly.’ Ankera’s consciousness, having circled the entire universe and returned to its origin, finally encountered Guffin’s words.

‘Why can’t I become a god?’

At that moment, for the first time, Ankera’s Akashic Records registered an error.

‘No.’

Unresolvable error.

‘I am God. That is where we must begin.’

The greatest crisis in existence had occurred.

Ankera, who oversaw all, denied the problem itself—refusing even to acknowledge the error.

“I am everything!”

An order surged through all concepts created by Anchera—that is, all angels.

“Punish the Gaians! Eliminate all errors in this world!”

Hearing the command through his Astral Body, Ikael lifted his head.

“What…?”

The war had begun.

Of course, Archangel Ikael was duty-bound to join the battlefield, yet his gaze remained fixed on the door.

‘I must know what happened.’

His fingertips trembled as he reached for the door.

‘No. I cannot open it without Anchera-sama’s permission. That authority was never given to me.’

Guffin.

As electricity crackled through starlight, the voice of the Guffin he had once known echoed in his mind.

– You have to choose for yourself.

Tears welled in Ikael’s eyes.

“Huh!”

A groan escaped his clenched teeth, his face twisting in anguish—but his hand finally touched the door.

“Oh God…!”

Blasphemy from an archangel.

“You can do it.”

Pushing his full weight against the door, he raised his eyes and shouted.

“I can choose!”

The heavy door burst inward with a crash, revealing the scene inside.

“This… this?”

Guffin dangled from Ankera’s nerves, limbs flailing in midair.

Ankera lashed out with sound waves.

“Who gave you permission to enter the sanctuary?” Ikael stammered.

“Great Anchera, what is happening? Why is the Guffin moving like that…?”

“Good. Punish the Gaians.”

The thought that the outcome would never align with Guffin’s wishes held him back.

“Tell me! Did the Guffin agree? Or is this defiance during a sacred conference…?”

Ankera opened her eyes. Veins bulged, and her nerves tore through the air at Mach speed.

“Gaaaaaaa! Kak! Kak! Kakaka! Cack! Cack! Cuck!”

The asteroid exploded into dust with a pop as the wave shrieked.

Ikael, stunned for a moment, turned and spread his wings the instant the asteroid reformed.

‘Something is wrong!’

Accepting that thought, he flew toward the battlefield with all his strength.

“Kill! Destroy the Gaians!” Countless angels filled the sky, bombarding the Gaians below.

Satiel shouted.

“Wait! This isn’t right! They don’t even want to fight!”

It was rare to defy Anchera’s orders, but more than that, her last words echoed in his mind.

‘You have no intention of fighting?’

Looking down, he saw Gaians who had not even activated the Ultima System, lifting their heads calmly.

“What are you thinking?”

The moment he met their unshaken eyes—despite their comrades dying around them—he realized.

‘That’s not what Guffin meant.’ Ataraxia unfolded.

“Stop! Cease the attack! An error has occurred! All angels, return to me at once!”

No one obeyed.

“Remove them! Humans are flawed! Eradicate them!”

Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang!

Ikael stood devastated, watching angels recklessly bomb the ground.

“Guffin does not want war,” a Gaian declared.

“So we will not fight. I will uphold Guffin’s will until the end.”

Ikael didn’t understand.

“Do you understand now?”

Gaia’s voice cut through the explosions, striking the planet directly.

“Ankera is not a god! What god breaks their own word? Guffin sacrificed himself to show you this!”

Ikael’s brows tightened, a cry escaping his clenched teeth.

“Stop.”

The angels ignored him.

“You insignificant humans! You don’t deserve to exist in this world!”

Ikael was overcome by the Tears of Light.

‘I must stop this at any cost.’ But how?

No being could defy the command of Ankera—the law itself, the embodiment of the Akashic Records.

‘Satiel.’

He recalled she was the only one who hadn’t attacked.

Guffin’s words returned.

– I’ve managed to put my heart into sound, just a little.

Mind.

‘My heart. With all my heart.’

Could it reach someone?

He threw himself into Ataraxia, which had expanded to its largest size yet.

Ataraxia – Hexagonal.

As the asteroid passed through Halo, a storm of emotions surged in.

“Huhhhh!”

A sensation as if his mind would burst.

Ikael, tears of light streaming from pure emotion, screamed with all his strength.

“Gmmaaaan!”

The world trembled.

The luminous bodies of the angels were pushed back by the wave, then scattered like smoke.

The angels, their minds cleared, turned and looked at Ikael.

“Chun… Archangel.”

It had reached their hearts.

“Stop the war. All of you… listen to me…”

Ikael’s Astral Body shook violently. He lost his strength and began to fall.

“Chief Angel!”

Angels rushed forward and caught him. Ikael smiled faintly.

“You must trust me.”

With those words, he lost consciousness.

The angels turned their eyes toward the remaining seven archangels.

Leil spoke.

“Open the White Watch.”

Mattiel frowned.

“What White Watch? Anchera’s orders are absolute. We have no choice.”

“But it’s strange that the Archangel stopped us. He’s the one who always carries out Anchera’s commands.” While Mattiel remained silent, Satiel stepped forward.

“For now, let’s withhold judgment until the Archangel awakens.”

Uriel asked.

“What about Gaia? It’s dangerous to leave them unchecked.”

Kariel suggested.

“Take them into custody. I will oversee it. I’m confident I won’t act recklessly.”

For Kariel, whose conduct was always strictly defined, stepping forward like this meant she had a specific purpose.

‘Humans.’

The one element missing from Kariel’s life experiment had finally been obtained.

Satiel added.

“For now, we suspend the war. There’s no way Anchera-nim would have changed her words, and there’s no way Kael-nim would have disobeyed. We can judge later.”

Certainly, there was much they didn’t understand with the information they had.

Faiel turned to Kariel.

“Can you take responsibility for your words? Heaven may change if this unrest spreads.”

“Don’t worry. I will use space transfer technology. I’ll isolate them with a metagate.”

The Gaians complied without resistance, and a long procession began moving toward Arabot.

The angels sounded victory trumpets, their excitement rising—but the archangels’ expressions remained grave.

‘What really happened?’

Only Ikael knew the truth of what had occurred in the sanctuary with Araboth.

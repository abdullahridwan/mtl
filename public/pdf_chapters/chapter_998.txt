Chapter 998

Existence of Absence (3)

~*~

After Shirone destroyed Ionas, Ikael’s astral body ceased trembling.

Yet it now blazed brighter than anything else in the sky.

‘Guffin.’

A wave of pain and pleasure surged through his chest, as if hot and cold water had been poured over him at once.

‘Really, really…’

Ikael looked at Shirone.

‘It’s our child.’

The face of the man he had loved most in the world.

Shirone, still looking down, reached out his hand as though Ikael stood right before him.

“Iced coffee.”

At that moment, Ikael raised his arm as if to take it.

“Be careful.”

Archangels surrounded him.

Uriel, who had once supported Ikael—knowingly or not—now showed only quiet hostility.

“Don’t you know? If you go to Shirone now, there will be no turning back.”

Uriel stood to the left, Rayel to the right, and Satiel guarded Ikael’s rear.

As Ikael turned his gaze left and right, sensing their presence, Maras blocked the path ahead.

Satiel spoke.

“Give up. Even you can’t stop all of us. And we won’t let this go.”

For a long time, Ikael had felt the archangels’ presence like thorns piercing his flesh.

“Whoops.”

One corner of Ikael’s mouth lifted.

Uriel, Rayel, and Satiel froze, unprepared for the unfamiliar sensation of discomfort.

They could only wonder—had an archangel ever laughed like this before?

Ikael asked, “Can’t stop all of you?” She had already said yes.

He turned and looked back at the archangels.

“Answer me. Can’t I stop all of you? Did you really mean that?”

They had already spoken, but now the archangels hesitated, as if possessed.

Really?

The four of them…

Could they truly subdue Ikael?

“Shirone is my son.”

He had kept that truth locked inside for so long. Just saying it sent a thrill through him.

‘I’m not afraid.’

Guffin was gone—but wasn’t his son, who looked so much like him, reaching out now?

“I’ve come back after many years. I won’t back down now. If you, as archangels, would rather stand in my way than follow orders…”

Ikael exhaled coldly.

“Don’t expect any more respect from me.”

Satiel activated his judicial halo, nostalgia, and fired.

“Respect? That’s what you’ve lost. You’re no longer an archangel. You’re a cancerous mass, fighting for humans and betraying Heaven’s armies.”

Uriel, bound by Heaven’s clear laws, could not show personal feelings.

‘… … Do I have no choice but to fight?’

Rayel unfolded his judicial halo, Ragnarok, and activated the accelerator.

“You abdicated your authority yourself. Don’t blame us.”

“My authority was given only because of my age,” Ikael said, laughing.

“It’s something you can’t talk about, worry about, or break.”

The moment he finished speaking, the three archangels and the Maras in front charged at once.

It was a cosmic momentum.

But Ikael’s eyes sank into a deep abyss.

Then, countless living beings on the ground saw another sun floating in the sky.

Ymir writhed in agony, ensnared by the Law of Law Killing.

Shirone and the others stilled their racing hearts and simply watched.

They didn’t know how to kill Ymir, but the Law was trying to force the incident by drawing in everything in the universe.

It was beyond human comprehension. All they could see was the surface of the black space surrounding Ymir, occasionally flashing in full color.

“Great!”

Ymir’s heart clenched with the will to erase all life.

‘Die. Please die.’

Even those who didn’t believe in God joined hands—not out of faith, but because, deep down, they didn’t want to fight anymore.

‘I just need you to disappear.’

Should the universe be sacrificed?

As the black space rose to her neck and engulfed her entire body, Ymir glared at Shirone and said,

“In this way, I…”

Her voice was cut off as space sealed her mouth, then her face.

To Shirone and the others, the sight was now just a black sphere containing the entire universe.

“Is it over?”

After a moment, the black sphere shuddered violently, as if about to explode.

Cuckoo! Cuckoo!

The ground shook like an earthquake. Buildings swayed side to side.

‘Doesn’t matter. If only I could contain it here.’

Then, white cracks began to spread across the black sphere.

The rift widened rapidly. A fierce golden light erupted from the grotesque fissure.

“Kuaaaaa!”

Ymir flung out her limbs with a roar. The black shell clinging to her burned away without a trace.

Even after seeing it with his own eyes, Shirone took a long time to utter a single word.

‘Failure.’

<Law Kill> never ceased until its target was dead.

If not extinguished by a specific event, it was a legal weapon that would take the opponent’s life—even by artificially altering reality.

It wasn’t about power. It was simply how it functioned.

‘But the fact that it couldn’t kill Ymir…’

Those who knew <Slaughter>, and even those who didn’t, sensed one truth.

“There’s no way to kill her.” At least, not in this universe.

Shirone was horrified by the terrifying truth.

“It was a good try.”

Ymir moved forward, looking slightly weary.

“I don’t know what that was, but it was impressive. But… …”

She released a shockwave and charged, throwing everyone into chaos.

“It’s not what I wanted.”

“Harm!”

The Thousand Hands Guanyin incarnation formed an iron barrier with countless palms.

“Yaaaa!”

As Ymir was lifted, Miro’s pupils flickered.

“Whoa!”

She quickly recovered, exhaled sharply, and waved her arms.

Thousand Hands Guanyin – Double Thunderbolt.

The moment Ymir was trapped, a billion-unit impact was delivered at the limit of time.

“Hehehehe!”

Even now, Ymir smiled—and it was terrifying.

“Weak.”

She stomped her foot. The ground trembled.

“Weak!”

At the same time, a sharp, teeth-grating sound cut through the roar of the earth.

“……I”

Perhaps it was instinct.

Ymir, who had been charging straight at Miro, suddenly turned her head.

‘What?’

A fist-sized air bullet had flown through the atmosphere.

As she raised her other hand to block it, there was a bang—and Ymir’s body was thrown back.

“Big!”

The bullet exploded in her hand, tearing through the shell.

‘Sick.’

The impact pierced bone and kept going.

The party, who had been staring at Ymir as she was thrown back dozens of meters, slowly turned their heads.

“Cadet??????”

A dying voice. Someone stepped out from the shadows in the alley.

At first glance, he looked like a corpse.

A man with disheveled hair and a gaunt face, like a skeleton.

“Ah… ah?????”

He could barely speak. His legs were stiff, barely able to move.

But Kang Nan recognized him instantly.

“Gaold.”

More than joy, she understood—just how he had survived to this point.

She didn’t need words.

As he looked around like a lost soul, his eyes caught sight of Miro—the one who had escaped at the moment of his death.

For a moment, his dead-looking black pupils flickered to life.

“Don’t cry.”

Gaold said, his expression hazy.

“Smile.”

Sein stepped forward.

“Gaold, you… …”

As they approached, a stench rose from him. But his condition was so dire, even that didn’t matter.

Miro asked, “Why did you come?”

When Shirone and the others turned to her, she wore a blank expression.

“I’d rather die. What are you doing, showing yourself like this?”

Kang Nan’s eyes widened.

“Is that what you’re saying now? Who did you save your life for?”

Miro raised her hand as if to stop him from coming closer.

She would have said the same, if not for maintaining the integrity of the all-round projection.

“Go back. Your role is over.”

“I can’t go back.”

“Why?”

Miro clenched her teeth.

“Why are you being so stubborn? I said I liked you. I said I’d be yours if you wanted. But you rejected me. And now I hate people like you.”

“……Yes.”

“Get out of here! Disappear from my sight!”

“Okay, Miro. So… …”

Gaold lifted his chin and smiled faintly.

“Smile.”

The words made no sense, but Miro bit her lip, her eyes tightening.

As Gaold moved, Ymir, who had been staring at her torn hand, raised her head.

A skeletal man, limping forward—yet strangely, he didn’t feel weak.

‘It’s the pinnacle of something.’

Ymir sensed it.

‘If I were infinitely distant from the senses… …’

She straightened her body, showing she was far from finished.

“The existence closest to the senses.”

“What are you talking about?”

Gaold, one eye half-closed, spoke. Ymir’s lips curled.

“… … You did well to come.”

Destroy everything. Become the dignity of the universe.

“I am the strongest!”

As Ymir lunged forward with her full weight, Gaold’s face twisted like a demon.

‘Bakum Press.’

With a thud, Ymir froze mid-charge. Her back arched violently.

“Haha! How’s that? But… …”

As Ymir trembled and slowly lifted her head, Gaold’s nerves flared.

“Cheuk, heck, heck!”

Yet his expression, like Ymir’s, twisted into a wicked grin.

“Are you the king of giants?”

Seeing Gaold’s strange mockery, Sein reached out, realizing something was wrong.

“No! If you break here… … everyone in the city will die!”

“Huh!”

Gaold’s eyes, not even pretending to listen, curled upward.

‘Pain?’

In a spirit realm without light, an endless path stretched ahead.

‘What is pain?’

Gaold, standing at the start, walked toward the blade protruding from the end.

The blade pierced his stomach.

‘It’s pain.’

He didn’t stop as it cut through his body.

As he advanced, another blade waited—piercing his shoulder.

‘Go ahead.’

More blades appeared. His forehead. His heart. His thighs.

Finally, before Gaold’s eyes, countless blades waited—enough to pierce his entire body.

‘It will advance.’

Even as his whole body burned with pain, his legs didn’t stop.

If he stopped, the pain would fade—but that would mean death.

‘Great!’

Gaold charged with all his strength.

‘Aaaaaa!’

Even as thousands of blades tore through him, he didn’t know where it ended. Maybe there was no end.

‘It doesn’t stop.’

Throwing yourself toward the pain ahead—that is… ….

“It is my life!”

As Gaold’s mind compressed to its limit, the eyes of Shirone and the others widened in shock.

“Crazy… …”

At the same time, an air pressure of immeasurable force slammed down on Ymir’s body.
